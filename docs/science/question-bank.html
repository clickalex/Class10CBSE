Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (12)

SHORTS11 mark

1. Why must a chemical equation be balanced?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because of the law of conservation of mass — the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides, since atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Balance: HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + H2O

Show Answer

Ans:

2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O. This is also a neutralisation, hence a double displacement reaction.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is a precipitation reaction? Give one example with a balanced equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

A precipitation reaction forms an insoluble solid (the precipitate) when two aqueous solutions are mixed. Example: Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq), where the white precipitate is barium sulphate.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why is nitrogen flushed into bags of potato chips?

Show Answer

Ans:

Nitrogen is an unreactive (inert) gas. It displaces oxygen from the packet, so the fats and oils cannot be oxidised, which prevents rancidity and keeps the chips from changing smell and taste.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. To balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O, a student changes the formula of water to H₂O₂ and writes H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂. Why is this wrong? Give the correct balanced equation with state symbols.

Show Answer

Ans:

Changing a subscript changes the substance: H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide, not water. Only the coefficient in front of a formula may be changed.
Balanced equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (4 H and 2 O atoms on each side).
With state symbols: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l).

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. An iron nail is dipped in blue copper sulphate solution for about 20 minutes. State what you observe, write the balanced chemical equation and name the type of reaction.

Show Answer

Ans:

The nail becomes coated with a reddish-brown deposit of copper, and the blue colour of the solution fades to light green.
Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s).
Iron is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper from its salt solution: a displacement reaction.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Balance this equation and name the type of reaction: FeSO₄(s) → Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).

Show Answer

Ans:

Balanced: 2FeSO₄(s) → Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).
It is a thermal decomposition reaction — one substance breaks into three on heating.
It is also a redox reaction: Fe²⁺ is oxidised while part of the sulphur is reduced.

LONGL84 marks

8. In the reaction ZnO + C → Zn + CO, identify the substance oxidised and the substance reduced.

Show Answer

Ans:

Carbon is oxidised (it gains oxygen to form CO) and zinc oxide is reduced (it loses oxygen to form Zn). Carbon is the reducing agent; ZnO is the oxidising agent.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: A shopkeeper finds that a packet of chips left open for weeks smells and tastes bad, and that the iron gate outside his shop has turned reddish-brown. (a) Name the two processes. (b) Give the chemical formula of the reddish-brown substance on the gate. (c) Suggest one way to prevent each process.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The chips have turned rancid (oxidation of fats and oils); the gate has rusted (corrosion of iron).
(b) Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O.
(c) Rancidity: pack the chips in airtight bags flushed with nitrogen, or add antioxidants. Rusting: paint, oil or galvanise the iron so that air and moisture cannot reach it.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A student rubs a 3 cm magnesium ribbon with sandpaper and then holds it with tongs over a flame. It burns with a dazzling white flame and leaves a white powder. (a) Why is the ribbon cleaned before burning? (b) Name the white powder. (c) Write the balanced equation and name the type of reaction.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Sandpaper removes the dull layer of magnesium oxide that covers the ribbon, so the pure metal can burn.
(b) The white powder is magnesium oxide, MgO.
(c) 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Two substances combine to form one product, so it is a combination reaction (it is also an oxidation, and exothermic).

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. Describe an activity to show that a chemical reaction is accompanied by the evolution of a gas, and how you would identify the gas.

Show Answer

Ans:

Take a few zinc granules in a conical flask and add dilute sulphuric acid; fit a cork with a delivery tube.
Bubbles appear on the surface of the zinc, showing that a gas is being evolved.
Bring a burning splint near the mouth of the tube: the gas burns with a pop sound, which identifies it as hydrogen.
Equation: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑.

LONGL125 marks

12. A white salt on heating decomposes to give brown fumes and a residue. Name the salt and write the equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lead nitrate. The brown fumes are nitrogen dioxide. 2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2↑ + O2↑ — a thermal decomposition reaction.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why does dry HCl gas not change the colour of dry litmus paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because acidic behaviour requires the formation of H+(aq) ions, which needs water. Dry HCl gas has no water, so it does not ionise and cannot affect dry litmus.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A solution turns red litmus blue. Its pH is likely to be: 1, 4, 7 or 10. Justify.

Show Answer

Ans:

  1. Turning red litmus blue shows the solution is basic, and only pH 10 among the options is on the basic side of 7.
SHORTS32 marks

3. Fresh milk has a pH of 6. Explain why the pH falls as it turns into curd.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid. The acid increases the H+ ion concentration, so the pH drops below 6 and the milk proteins coagulate into curd.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What happens when a base reacts with a non-metal oxide? Give an equation and state what it tells you about non-metal oxides.

Show Answer

Ans:

A salt and water are formed, just as in neutralisation: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. It shows that non-metal oxides are acidic in nature.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why is plaster of Paris written as CaSO4·½H2O? Is it possible to have half a water molecule?

Show Answer

Ans:

The formula is written per formula unit of CaSO4. One molecule of water is shared between two CaSO4 units, so each unit effectively holds half a water molecule. Heating gypsum at 373 K drives out 1½ molecules of water.

SHORTS63 marks

6. A milkman adds a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk. Why does the milk take longer to set as curd?

Show Answer

Ans:

The baking soda makes the milk slightly alkaline, so the lactic acid produced by bacteria is first neutralised before the pH can fall low enough to coagulate the casein. Setting is therefore delayed.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes: “The setting of plaster of Paris into a hard mass is only a physical change because nothing new is made.” Correct the statement with the chemical equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Setting is a chemical change: plaster of Paris combines with water to form a new compound, gypsum.
CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O (gypsum, a hard solid mass).
This is why POP is stored in moisture-proof containers: damp air would make it set. Its uses include casting for fractured bones and making toys and decorative material.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. The soil in a field has a pH of 5.2. What should the farmer add to it, and why? Name one common material.

Show Answer

Ans:

A pH of 5.2 means the soil is acidic, while most crops grow best at about pH 6.5–7.5.
The farmer should add a base such as quicklime (CaO), slaked lime Ca(OH)₂ or chalk (CaCO₃).
The base neutralises the excess acid (acid + base → salt + water), which raises the pH towards neutral.

SHORTS93 marks

9. How is bleaching powder prepared? Write the equation and two uses.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bleaching powder is prepared by the action of chlorine gas on dry slaked lime: Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O.
Uses: bleaching cotton and linen in the textile industry and wood pulp in paper factories.
It is also used as an oxidising agent in chemical industries and to disinfect drinking water.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Four solutions P, Q, R and S have pH values 2, 6, 8 and 13 respectively. (a) Which is the strongest acid and which is the strongest base? (b) Which solution would you use to relieve a bee sting, and why not the strongest base? (c) How many times greater is the hydrogen-ion concentration of P than that of Q?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) P (pH 2) is the strongest acid; S (pH 13) is the strongest base.
(b) R (pH 8), a mild base like baking-soda solution, neutralises the acid (methanoic acid) of the sting. S would be too strong and would burn the skin.
(c) Each pH unit is a factor of 10, and P and Q differ by 4 units, so P has 10⁴ = 10,000 times the hydrogen-ion concentration of Q.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A student heats a few blue crystals of copper sulphate in a dry test tube. Water droplets appear on the cooler part of the tube and the crystals turn white. When a few drops of water are added to the white powder, the blue colour returns. (a) Write the formula of the blue crystals. (b) Why does the colour change on heating? (c) What is this water called?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) CuSO₄·5H₂O.
(b) Heating drives off the water held in the crystals, leaving white anhydrous copper sulphate, CuSO₄.
(c) It is the water of crystallisation: five water molecules per formula unit. Adding water restores it, so the blue colour returns.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Three unlabelled bottles contain an acidic, a basic and a neutral solution. How would you identify all three using only red and blue litmus paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Dip a strip of red litmus into each solution: the one that turns it blue is the basic solution.
Now dip a strip of blue litmus into the two remaining solutions: the one that turns it red is the acidic solution.
The solution that changes neither paper is neutral (pH ≈ 7).
Note that a single indicator is not enough — you must use both papers, because neutral and acidic solutions both leave red litmus unchanged.

LONGL135 marks

13. Explain the chlor-alkali process with an equation, and give two uses each of the products.

Show Answer

Ans:

Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (brine) gives sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen: 2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g).
Sodium hydroxide — making soaps and detergents, and in the paper industry.
Chlorine — disinfecting drinking water and making bleaching powder/PVC.
Hydrogen — as a fuel and in the manufacture of ammonia and margarine (hydrogenation of oils).

LONGL145 marks

14. How is baking soda prepared? What happens when it is heated? Write the equations and give two uses of baking soda and two uses of washing soda.

Show Answer

Ans:

Preparation: NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ + NH₃ → NH₄Cl + NaHCO₃ (sodium hydrogencarbonate, baking soda).
On heating: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. The sodium carbonate formed gives washing soda, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, on recrystallisation.
Uses of baking soda: an antacid; baking powder (with tartaric acid) to make cakes soft and spongy; soda-acid fire extinguishers.
Uses of washing soda: removing permanent hardness of water; making glass, soap and paper; household cleaning.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name a metal that is liquid at room temperature and a non-metal that is lustrous.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mercury (metal, liquid at room temperature) and iodine (non-metal, lustrous).

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?

Show Answer

Ans:

Sodium is so reactive that it reacts vigorously with oxygen, moisture and carbon dioxide in air, catching fire. Kerosene cuts off air and moisture, so the metal stays unreacted.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why does aluminium not corrode easily even though it is a reactive metal?

Show Answer

Ans:

It reacts with oxygen to form a thin, tightly adhering layer of aluminium oxide on its surface. This layer prevents further reaction with air or moisture, so the metal below is protected.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Give a reason why ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Oppositely charged ions in an ionic compound are held together by strong electrostatic forces in a crystal lattice, and a large amount of energy is needed to break these forces, so melting and boiling points are high.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why are food cans coated with tin and not with zinc?

Show Answer

Ans:

Zinc is more reactive than tin and would itself react with the acidic contents of the food, contaminating it. Tin is less reactive, so it protects the can without reacting.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Show the formation of MgCl2 by the transfer of electrons, and state two properties of the compound formed.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mg (2,8,2) loses 2 electrons → Mg2+; each Cl (2,8,7) gains one electron → Cl−. Mg2+ + 2Cl− → MgCl2, held by strong electrostatic force.
Properties: it is a hard crystalline solid with a high melting point, and it conducts electricity when molten or dissolved in water.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes: “Zinc carbonate is converted into zinc oxide by roasting in excess air.” Correct the statement and write the equations for converting a carbonate ore and a sulphide ore into oxides.

Show Answer

Ans:

A carbonate ore is converted into its oxide by calcination — heating strongly in a limited supply or absence of air. Roasting is used for sulphide ores, in excess air.
Calcination: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂.
Roasting: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. Iron nails are placed in copper sulphate solution, and a copper strip is placed in zinc sulphate solution. In which case will a reaction occur? Justify.

Show Answer

Ans:

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
Iron is more reactive than copper, so a reaction occurs: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu.
Copper is less reactive than zinc, so it cannot displace zinc, and there is no reaction in the second case.

SHORTS93 marks

9. How are metals low in the reactivity series extracted from their ores? Give equations.

Show Answer

Ans:

Metals low in the series (such as mercury and copper) are obtained by heating their ores alone — reduction with carbon is not needed.
Cinnabar (HgS) is first roasted in air: 2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂, then heated: 2HgO → 2Hg + O₂.
Copper from its sulphide ore: Cu₂S + 3O₂ → 2Cu₂O + 2SO₂, then 2Cu₂O + Cu₂S → 6Cu + SO₂.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A metal M reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid, giving bubbles of a gas that burns with a pop sound. M does not react with cold water but reacts with steam to form a metal oxide and the same gas. (a) Name the gas. (b) Suggest a metal that could be M. (c) Write the equation for the reaction of M with steam.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Hydrogen.
(b) Zinc (iron or aluminium would also fit).
(c) Zn + H₂O(g) → ZnO + H₂.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A student tests three samples. Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity, but molten sodium chloride and its aqueous solution do. An aqueous solution of glucose does not conduct either. (a) Why does solid NaCl not conduct? (b) Why does it conduct when molten or dissolved? (c) Why does the glucose solution not conduct?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) In the solid, the Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions are held rigidly in the crystal and cannot move.
(b) Melting or dissolving frees the ions, and moving ions carry the current.
(c) Glucose is a covalent compound made of neutral molecules; it forms no ions in water, so there are no charge carriers.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Describe the thermite reaction and give its use.

Show Answer

Ans:

The thermite reaction: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe + heat.
Aluminium is more reactive than iron, so it reduces iron(III) oxide to molten iron and a large amount of heat is released.
The molten iron produced is used to weld railway tracks and cracked machine parts without dismantling them.

LONGL135 marks

13. Explain electrolytic refining of copper.

Show Answer

Ans:

The impure copper is made the anode and a thin strip of pure copper the cathode, both dipped in acidified copper sulphate solution.
On passing current, copper from the anode dissolves as Cu2+ and an equal amount deposits on the cathode.
Soluble impurities go into the solution; insoluble impurities settle below the anode as anode mud (which contains the precious metals).

LONGL145 marks

14. Explain, with equations, how zinc is extracted from its sulphide ore. Why can sodium not be obtained from its oxide in the same way?

Show Answer

Ans:

Zinc blende (ZnS) is first concentrated, then converted into zinc oxide by roasting: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂.
The oxide is reduced to the metal by heating with carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO.
The crude zinc is purified by electrolytic refining.
Sodium is far above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide.
Highly reactive metals are obtained by electrolysis of their molten chlorides: at the cathode Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why do covalent compounds generally have low melting and boiling points?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the forces of attraction between their molecules are weak, even though the covalent bonds within a molecule are strong. Little energy is needed to separate the molecules.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Give a chemical test to distinguish between butter and cooking oil.

Show Answer

Ans:

The bromine water test (or the alkaline KMnO4 test). Cooking oil is unsaturated, so it decolorises bromine water/KMnO4; butter is largely saturated and does not.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why is a mixture of ethyne and pure oxygen used for welding instead of ethyne and air?

Show Answer

Ans:

In air, ethyne burns incompletely and gives a sooty flame with less heat. In pure oxygen combustion is complete, producing a clean flame at about 3000 °C (the oxy-acetylene flame), which is hot enough for welding.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Why is methane called a saturated hydrocarbon? Describe its electron-dot structure in words.

Show Answer

Ans:

In methane (CH₄) all four valencies of carbon are satisfied by four single covalent bonds with hydrogen atoms, so no more atoms can be added — the molecule is saturated. In the electron-dot structure, carbon sits at the centre and shares one pair of electrons with each of the four hydrogen atoms, giving every atom a stable octet (duplet for hydrogen).

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is a homologous series? Write the general formula and the third member of the alkane series.

Show Answer

Ans:

A homologous series is a group of compounds with the same general formula and similar chemical properties, in which successive members differ by a −CH2− unit (14 u).
Alkanes: CnH2n+2. Third member = propane, C3H8.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Explain the cleansing action of soap with the help of a diagram description.

Show Answer

Ans:

A soap molecule has a hydrophilic ionic head and a hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail.
In water, the tails attach to the oily dirt while the heads remain in water, forming a spherical cluster called a micelle with the oil trapped inside.
The micelle is washed away with the water, taking the dirt with it; agitation helps detach the oil.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes the formula of propene as C₃H₈, saying the general formula of alkenes is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Find the error and give the correct formula and structure of propene.

Show Answer

Ans:

Error: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ is the general formula of alkanes; alkenes have the general formula CₙH₂ₙ.
For n = 3, propene is C₃H₆, with the structure CH₃–CH=CH₂ (one C=C double bond).
C₃H₈ is propane, the saturated hydrocarbon with only single bonds.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. How can you distinguish between ethanol and ethanoic acid by adding sodium hydrogencarbonate to each? Write the equation for the reaction that occurs.

Show Answer

Ans:

Add a little sodium hydrogencarbonate to a sample of each liquid.
Ethanoic acid gives a brisk effervescence of carbon dioxide (the gas turns lime water milky); ethanol shows no reaction.
CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Write the equation for the combustion of ethanol and explain why ethanol burns with a clean blue flame.

Show Answer

Ans:

C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O + heat and light.
Ethanol already contains oxygen and has a low carbon-to-hydrogen ratio, so it burns almost completely.
Saturated hydrocarbons contain relatively more carbon and often burn incompletely, depositing unburnt carbon, which gives a sooty yellow flame.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Three compounds are: A = CH₃CH₂OH, B = CH₃COOH and C = CH₃COOCH₂CH₃. (a) Name A, B and C. (b) Which compound has a sweet, fruity smell, and how is it formed? (c) Write the equation for its formation.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) A is ethanol, B is ethanoic acid and C is ethyl ethanoate.
(b) C, the ester. It is formed when ethanol reacts with ethanoic acid in the presence of a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid — an esterification reaction.
(c) CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄ as catalyst).

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A chemist heats ethanol with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K and collects a colourless gas that decolourises bromine water. In another test, a small piece of sodium is dropped into ethanol; a gas bubbles out that burns with a pop sound. (a) Name the two gases. (b) Name the type of the first reaction. (c) Write the equation for the second reaction.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The first gas is ethene; the second is hydrogen.
(b) The first reaction is dehydration — conc. H₂SO₄ removes a molecule of water from ethanol: CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O.
(c) 2Na + 2CH₃CH₂OH → 2CH₃CH₂ONa + H₂ (sodium ethoxide and hydrogen).

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Why does soap not lather well in hard water, and what is used instead?

Show Answer

Ans:

Hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium salts, which react with soap to form an insoluble sticky precipitate (scum).
Soap is therefore wasted and lather forms only after all the Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions have been precipitated.
Detergents are used instead: their molecules are sulphonate or sulphate salts whose calcium and magnesium salts stay dissolved, so they lather even in hard water.

LONGL135 marks

13. With equations, describe three chemical properties of ethanoic acid.

Show Answer

Ans:

With a base (neutralisation): CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O.
With a metal carbonate: 2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2.
With a hydrogen carbonate: CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 (brisk effervescence).
Esterification (with ethanol, conc. H2SO4): CH3COOH + C2H5OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O — a sweet-smelling ester used in perfumes and flavourings.

LONGL145 marks

14. Why does carbon form covalent compounds? Describe the bonding in methane and in ethene with the help of electron-dot structures, in words.

Show Answer

Ans:

Carbon has four valence electrons. To gain four electrons (C⁴⁻) is difficult because six protons must hold ten electrons, and to lose four (C⁴⁺) needs a very large amount of energy. So carbon shares electrons and forms covalent bonds.
Methane, CH₄: the carbon atom shares one electron with each of four hydrogen atoms, forming four single bonds. Carbon gets an octet and each hydrogen gets a duplet.
Ethene, C₂H₄: each carbon shares two electrons with the other carbon (a double bond, two shared pairs) and one electron with each of two hydrogen atoms.
Each carbon in ethene has four shared pairs around it, so both carbons have an octet.

Ch 5 · Life Processes (14)

Save this chapter: Text (.txt) ·

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name the functional unit of the kidney and the pigment that absorbs light energy in plants.

Show Answer

Ans:

Nephron; chlorophyll.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like human beings?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because their cells are not all in direct contact with the environment, and the volume of the body is large, so the distance oxygen would have to diffuse is too great. Diffusion is too slow to supply every cell, which is why special respiratory and transport systems are needed.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Name the raw materials and the products of photosynthesis, and write the overall equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Raw materials: carbon dioxide from the air and water absorbed from the soil, using chlorophyll and sunlight. Products: glucose (stored as starch) and oxygen. Overall: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why is the rate of breathing in aquatic organisms much faster than in terrestrial organisms?

Show Answer

Ans:

13. Explain atmospheric refraction and describe any three phenomena based on it.

Show Answer

Ans:

11. A village panchayat plans to burn its collected plastic waste in the open. Advise them, giving reasons from what you have studied about ecosystems.

Show Answer

Ans:

Open burning of plastic releases poisonous gases such as hydrogen chloride and dioxins into the air, harming people, animals and crops.
Plastics are non-biodegradable, so if they are buried or dumped they persist in the soil for decades and choke drains and the guts of animals.
Better: segregate waste at source, send recyclable plastic for recycling, compost the biodegradable fraction, and replace single-use plastic with cloth or paper bags.
This keeps harmful chemicals out of the soil and water, so they cannot enter food chains and build up by biological magnification.

LONGL125 marks

12. What is biological magnification? Will the level of magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?

Show Answer

Ans:

Biological magnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of non-biodegradable harmful chemicals at successive trophic levels of a food chain.
Yes, the level differs. Because these chemicals are not metabolised or excreted, they pass on to the next level, and the amount of food consumed at each level is large, so the concentration keeps increasing.
Example: DDT sprayed on crops is absorbed by plants; grasshoppers eating many plants accumulate more; frogs eating many grasshoppers more still; and hawks at the top accumulate the highest concentration. Human beings, being at the top of many food chains, are among the most affected.

LONGL135 marks

13. What is a trophic level? Explain the flow of energy in an ecosystem and why it is unidirectional.

Show Answer

Ans:

A trophic level is a step in a food chain, for example producers, herbivores and carnivores.
The ultimate source of energy is the Sun; producers capture it through photosynthesis and pass it on when eaten.
At each step only about 10% of the energy is passed on to the next level (the 10% law); the rest is lost as heat in respiration and other life processes.
The energy lost as heat cannot be used again by the producers, so energy flows in one direction only, from the Sun to producers to consumers.
Because so little energy remains, food chains are short, and plants always occupy the first level.
