MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. In a monohybrid cross between a tall (TT) and a dwarf (tt) pea plant, the F2 phenotypic ratio is: (a) 1:1 (b) 3:1 (c) 9:3:3:1 (d) 1:2:1

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 3 tall : 1 dwarf. 1:2:1 is the genotypic ratio of the same cross.

MCQM21 mark

2. The sex of a human child is determined by: (a) the mother's egg (b) the father's sperm (c) both parents equally (d) the diet of the mother

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) the father's sperm. Eggs always carry X; a sperm carrying Y produces a boy and one carrying X produces a girl.

MCQM31 mark

3. A cross between F1 hybrid tall plants (Tt × Tt) gives dwarf plants in F2. The expected ratio of tall to dwarf is: (a) 1:3 (b) 3:1 (c) 1:1 (d) 2:2

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 3:1 — 25% of F2 are homozygous dwarf (tt).

MCQM61 mark

6. The phenotypic ratio obtained in a dihybrid cross between two F₁ hybrids is: (a) 3 : 1 (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 1 : 2 : 1

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Mendel obtained this for two independently assorting traits, such as seed shape and seed colour.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which of these traits can be inherited? (a) the cut tail of a mouse (b) large muscles built by exercise (c) eye colour (d) a scar

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.

MCQM81 mark

8. The number of chromosomes in a human zygote is: (a) 23 (b) 46 (c) 92 (d) 22

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 46. Each gamete carries 23 chromosomes and their fusion restores the 23 pairs.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. What is the sex chromosome combination in a human male, and what is the functional unit of inheritance?

Show Answer

Ans:

XY; the gene (a segment of DNA on a chromosome).

SHORTS22 marks

2. A man with blood group A marries a woman with blood group O and their daughter has blood group O. Is this enough to tell which trait is dominant? Why?

Show Answer

Ans:

No. From one family we cannot tell. If A were dominant, the father could be heterozygous (IAi) and the daughter could inherit i from both parents; but a single observation does not establish dominance, which requires studying many such crosses.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Distinguish between acquired and inherited traits, with one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

An acquired trait develops during an organism's lifetime due to experience or environment and is not passed on — for example, a wrestler's well-developed muscles or a pierced ear. An inherited trait is present from birth and is passed on through the DNA of germ cells — for example, the colour of eyes or free ear lobes.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Distinguish between genotype and phenotype with an example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Genotype is the genetic constitution of an organism (for example Tt), while phenotype is the observable character it produces (for example a tall plant). Because the tall allele is dominant, the genotypes TT and Tt both give the tall phenotype.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain Mendel's monohybrid cross with a Punnett square.

Show Answer

Ans:

Pure tall (TT) × pure dwarf (tt). Gametes: T and t. F1: all Tt — tall, because tall is dominant and dwarf is recessive.
Selfing F1 (Tt × Tt) gives gametes T and t from each parent. Punnett square → TT, Tt, Tt, tt.
Phenotype: 3 tall : 1 dwarf. Genotype: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. The dwarf character was only masked in F1, not lost.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How is the sex of a child determined in human beings? Show diagrammatically.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mother (XX) produces only X-bearing eggs; father (XY) produces X-bearing and Y-bearing sperms in equal numbers.
X egg + X sperm → XX = girl; X egg + Y sperm → XY = boy.
The probability of either is 50%, so the sex of the child is decided by chance and not by either parent's wish or action.

SHORTS93 marks

9. A tall pea plant (Tt) is crossed with a dwarf plant (tt). What proportion of the progeny will be tall?

Show Answer

Ans:

The tall parent produces two kinds of gametes, T and t; the dwarf parent produces only t.
Combining them gives Tt, Tt, tt and tt.
So half the progeny are Tt (tall) and half are tt (dwarf) — a 1 : 1 ratio. This cross with a recessive parent is called a test cross.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. A family blames the mother whenever a daughter is born. Using what you know about sex determination, write the reply you would give them.

Show Answer

Ans:

Human females have two X chromosomes, so every egg carries an X chromosome.
Human males have one X and one Y, so half their sperm carry X and half carry Y.
An X-bearing sperm fertilising the egg gives XX, a girl; a Y-bearing sperm gives XY, a boy. The egg always contributes an X, so it is the sperm — that is, the father — that decides the sex of the child.
Repeated daughters in a family are a matter of chance, exactly like repeated tails when a coin is tossed; there is no biological basis for blaming the mother.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe Mendel's dihybrid cross and state the conclusion drawn from it.

Show Answer

Ans:

He crossed pea plants with round-yellow seeds (RRYY) with plants having wrinkled-green seeds (rryy).
F1: all plants had round-yellow seeds (RrYy) — round and yellow are dominant.
On self-pollinating F1, F2 gave four phenotypes: 9 round-yellow : 3 round-green : 3 wrinkled-yellow : 1 wrinkled-green (16 combinations in the 4×4 Punnett square).
Conclusion: the two pairs of characters are inherited independently of each other, which is why new combinations (round-green and wrinkled-green) appear in F2 that were absent in the parents. This is the law of independent assortment.

LONGL145 marks

14. Discuss “Heredity” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The basis of heredity. Heredity is the transmission of characters from parents to offspring; the functional unit of inheritance is the gene, a segment of DNA on a chromosome. Each character has a pair of alleles, one from each parent. A dominant allele expresses itself in the F1 generation; the recessive allele is masked and reappears in F2. Genotype is the genetic make-up (TT, Tt, tt); phenotype is what appears (tal.
