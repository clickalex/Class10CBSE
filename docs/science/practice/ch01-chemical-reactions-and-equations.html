MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The reaction Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe is an example of: (a) combination (b) decomposition (c) displacement (d) double displacement

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which of the following is an endothermic process? (a) respiration (b) decomposition of vegetable matter into compost (c) process of decomposition of ferrous sulphate (d) quicklime + water

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.

MCQM31 mark

3. The green coating on copper and the black coating on silver are respectively: (a) basic copper carbonate and Ag2S (b) CuO and Ag2O (c) CuCO3 and AgS2 (d) Cu(OH)2 and AgO

Show Answer

Ans:

(a). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Chemical Reactions and Equations” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Chemical Reactions and Equations” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is a combination reaction? (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (b) 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (c) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (d) AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.

MCQM71 mark

7. The brown fumes evolved when lead nitrate is heated are of: (a) NO₂ (b) O₂ (c) NO (d) PbO

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) NO₂. 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂; nitrogen dioxide is brown, the residue lead oxide is yellow.

MCQM81 mark

8. Rancidity of fat-containing food is prevented by: (a) storing it in the open (b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen (c) adding oxidising agents (d) heating it repeatedly

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.

MCQM91 mark

9. When the equation Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂ is balanced, the coefficient of steam (H₂O) is: (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.

MCQM101 mark

10. A shiny brown element X becomes black when heated in air. X and the black compound are: (a) Cu and CuO (b) Fe and Fe₂O₃ (c) Ag and Ag₂S (d) Al and Al₂O₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Cu and CuO. Copper is reddish-brown and forms black copper(II) oxide on heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.

MCQM111 mark

11. The reaction 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂ used in black-and-white photography is a: (a) thermal decomposition (b) photochemical decomposition (c) displacement (d) combination reaction

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Why must a chemical equation be balanced?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because of the law of conservation of mass — the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides, since atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Balance: HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + H2O

Show Answer

Ans:

2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O. This is also a neutralisation, hence a double displacement reaction.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is a precipitation reaction? Give one example with a balanced equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

A precipitation reaction forms an insoluble solid (the precipitate) when two aqueous solutions are mixed. Example: Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq), where the white precipitate is barium sulphate.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why is nitrogen flushed into bags of potato chips?

Show Answer

Ans:

Nitrogen is an unreactive (inert) gas. It displaces oxygen from the packet, so the fats and oils cannot be oxidised, which prevents rancidity and keeps the chips from changing smell and taste.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Riya is revising “Chemical Reactions and Equations” (Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Changing a subscript to balance (H2O → H2O2). Only the coefficient in front of a formula may be changed. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Balancing order: start with the element that appears in the largest number of atoms, leave free elements (O2, H2) for last, then re-check oxygen.
Why the mistake costs marks: Changing a subscript to balance (H2O → H2O2). Only the coefficient in front of a formula may be changed.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Chemical Reactions and Equations”: “Why must a chemical equation be balanced?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity..
Because of the law of conservation of mass — the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides, since atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Balance this equation and name the type of reaction: FeSO₄(s) → Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).

Show Answer

Ans:

Balanced: 2FeSO₄(s) → Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).
It is a thermal decomposition reaction — one substance breaks into three on heating.
It is also a redox reaction: Fe²⁺ is oxidised while part of the sulphur is reduced.

LONGL84 marks

8. In the reaction ZnO + C → Zn + CO, identify the substance oxidised and the substance reduced.

Show Answer

Ans:

Carbon is oxidised (it gains oxygen to form CO) and zinc oxide is reduced (it loses oxygen to form Zn). Carbon is the reducing agent; ZnO is the oxidising agent.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: A Class 10 student is preparing “Chemical Reactions and Equations”. Their note says: Recognising a chemical reaction. Four observable clues: change of state, change of colour, evolution of a gas, and change in temperature. If a new substance with new properties forms, a chemical reaction has taken place. The chapter is described as: Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity.. (a) Name the key idea. (b) How is this usually asked in the paper? (c) State one mistake that would cost a mark.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity.
(b) –4 marks every year, usually as one balancing question, one reaction-type identification and one reasoning question on corrosion or rancidity.
(c) Changing a subscript to balance (H2O → H2O2). Only the coefficient in front of a formula may be changed.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Two students attempt: “Why must a chemical equation be balanced?” Student A writes one line. Student B writes points, an example and a check. (a) Which answer frame should you use for full marks? (b) Write the model points. (c) Add one memory trick you would use on exam morning.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Statement → working or reasons in points → example or check. One-line answers lose step marks.
(b) Because of the law of conservation of mass — the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides, since atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
(c) Balancing order: start with the element that appears in the largest number of atoms, leave free elements (O2, H2) for last, then re-check oxygen.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. Describe an activity to show that a chemical reaction is accompanied by the evolution of a gas, and how you would identify the gas.

Show Answer

Ans:

Take a few zinc granules in a conical flask and add dilute sulphuric acid; fit a cork with a delivery tube.
Bubbles appear on the surface of the zinc, showing that a gas is being evolved.
Bring a burning splint near the mouth of the tube: the gas burns with a pop sound, which identifies it as hydrogen.
Equation: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑.

LONGL125 marks

12. A white salt on heating decomposes to give brown fumes and a residue. Name the salt and write the equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lead nitrate. The brown fumes are nitrogen dioxide. 2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2↑ + O2↑ — a thermal decomposition reaction.
