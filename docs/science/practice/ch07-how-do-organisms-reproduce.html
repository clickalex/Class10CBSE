MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Binary fission in amoeba begins with the division of the: (a) cytoplasm (b) nucleus (c) cell membrane (d) pseudopodia

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) nucleus. The nucleus divides first, followed by the division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells.

MCQM21 mark

2. The anther contains: (a) sepals (b) ovules (c) carpel (d) pollen grains

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) pollen grains. The anther is the male reproductive part; ovules are inside the ovary.

MCQM31 mark

3. Which of the following is a barrier method of contraception? (a) oral pill (b) copper-T (c) condom (d) vasectomy

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) condom. It physically prevents the sperm from meeting the egg and also reduces the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.

MCQM41 mark

4. In human females, the embryo implants in the: (a) fallopian tube (b) ovary (c) uterus (d) vagina

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) uterus. The zygote develops into an embryo that embeds in the thick, blood-rich lining of the uterus.

MCQM51 mark

5. Spirogyra reproduces asexually by: (a) budding (b) fragmentation (c) spore formation (d) regeneration

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) fragmentation. A Spirogyra filament breaks into pieces, and each piece grows into a new filament.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is an asexual mode of reproduction? (a) budding in yeast (b) pollination (c) fertilisation (d) seed germination

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) budding in yeast. Asexual modes include budding, binary fission, fragmentation, spore formation and vegetative propagation.

MCQM71 mark

7. Sperms are produced in the: (a) penis (b) testes (c) vas deferens (d) seminal vesicles

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) testes. They are formed in the seminiferous tubules, where the temperature is about 2–3 °C below body temperature.

MCQM81 mark

8. Sterilisation in human beings means: (a) preventing ovulation with pills (b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts (c) killing germs (d) using a condom

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts. Vasectomy in males and tubectomy in females prevent gametes from meeting.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Where does fertilisation occur in human beings, and which gland secretes testosterone?

Show Answer

Ans:

Fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct); testosterone is secreted by the testes.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why are the testes located outside the abdominal cavity in the scrotum?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because sperm formation requires a temperature 2–3 °C lower than the normal body temperature. The scrotum, being outside the abdomen, maintains this lower temperature.

SHORTS32 marks

3. List two advantages of vegetative propagation.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is a quick and cheap method of multiplying plants, and the new plants are genetically identical to the parent, so desirable qualities such as fruit size and taste are retained. Plants that do not produce viable seeds, like banana and rose, can also be propagated this way.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Why is variation useful to a species, and how does sexual reproduction produce it?

Show Answer

Ans:

Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two different individuals, so offspring differ from both parents. Variation means that when the environment changes or a disease strikes, some individuals are likely to survive, the population is not wiped out, and natural selection has raw material to work on.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain regeneration in Planaria with a diagram description.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a Planaria is cut into several pieces, each piece can grow into a complete organism.
Specialised cells at the wound surface proliferate (multiply) to form a mass of cells.
These cells then move to their proper positions and redifferentiate into the various tissues and organs needed, so a whole new organism develops from the fragment.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Distinguish between pollination and fertilisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same or another flower of the same species; it needs an external agent such as wind, water or insects.
Fertilisation is the fusion of the male gamete from the pollen tube with the female gamete (egg) inside the ovule, forming a zygote. It occurs inside the ovary and needs no external agent.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes: “The egg is fertilised in the uterus, where the zygote also settles and grows.” Correct the error and state where fertilisation and implantation each occur.

Show Answer

Ans:

Fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct), not in the uterus.
The zygote divides as it moves down the oviduct and forms a ball of cells.
This embryo then implants in the thick lining of the uterus, where the placenta forms to nourish it.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. Name the mode of reproduction in (i) Hydra, (ii) Spirogyra, (iii) Rhizopus and (iv) Bryophyllum, and explain how any one of them takes place.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) Hydra — budding; (ii) Spirogyra — fragmentation; (iii) Rhizopus — spore formation; (iv) Bryophyllum — vegetative propagation by leaves.
In Hydra, regenerative cells divide repeatedly to form a bud on the parent’s body.
The bud grows into a small Hydra with its own mouth and tentacles and then detaches to live independently.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Describe what happens in a flower after fertilisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

The male gamete fuses with the egg to form a zygote, which develops into an embryo.
The ovule becomes the seed and its wall becomes the seed coat.
The ovary grows into the fruit, while the petals, sepals and stamens wither and fall off.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: At a health camp, a doctor describes three birth-control methods: a condom, oral pills and the copper-T. (a) Which method physically stops the sperm from reaching the egg? (b) Which method changes the hormonal balance so that eggs are not released? (c) Which of these also lowers the risk of sexually transmitted diseases?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The condom, a barrier method.
(b) Oral contraceptive pills.
(c) The condom. (The copper-T prevents implantation but gives no protection against infection.)

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A student studies two flowers: a hibiscus, which has both stamens and carpels, and a papaya flower, which has only one of them. (a) What are bisexual and unisexual flowers? (b) Can a papaya flower pollinate itself? Why? (c) Which kind of pollination gives more variation?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) A bisexual flower (hibiscus) has both stamens and carpels; a unisexual flower (papaya) has only stamens or only carpels.
(b) No. A unisexual flower has only one sex, so pollen must be carried to a flower on another plant: cross-pollination.
(c) Cross-pollination, because it combines genes from two different plants.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Describe the menstrual cycle and state what happens when the egg is not fertilised.

Show Answer

Ans:

Roughly once every 28 days one egg matures in an ovary and is released around day 14 (ovulation).
Meanwhile the lining of the uterus thickens and develops blood vessels to receive a fertilised egg.
If the egg is not fertilised, the thickened lining is no longer needed, breaks down, and is shed along with blood through the vagina for about three to five days — menstruation.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe the structure and working of the human female reproductive system.

Show Answer

Ans:

Ovaries — a pair of glands that produce the female gamete (ovum) and the hormone oestrogen.
Fallopian tubes (oviducts) — tubes with finger-like ends near the ovary and ciliated walls; the egg is captured here and fertilisation takes place in the tube.
Uterus — a muscular bag where the fertilised egg implants and the embryo develops.
Cervix and vagina — the cervix is the narrow opening of the uterus; the vagina is the passage through which sperm enter and the baby is delivered.
Every month the uterus prepares a thick, spongy lining to receive a fertilised egg. If no fertilisation occurs, the lining breaks down and is discharged as blood and mucus through the vagina — this is menstruation. After fertilisation, the embryo is nourished by the mother's blood through the placenta.

LONGL145 marks

14. Describe the human male reproductive system, naming the organs and the function of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Testes: lie in the scrotum, outside the abdomen, where the lower temperature suits sperm production; they also secrete testosterone.
Vas deferens: carries sperms from the testes towards the urethra.
Seminal vesicles and prostate gland: add fluids to the sperms, which nourish them and make transport easier; together with the sperms this forms semen.
Urethra: a common passage for semen and urine, which runs through the penis.
Penis: the organ that delivers sperms into the female body during mating.
