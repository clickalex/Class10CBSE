MCQs

Binary fission in amoeba begins with the division of the: (a) cytoplasm (b) nucleus (c) cell membrane (d) pseudopodia

(b) nucleus. The nucleus divides first, followed by the division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells.

The anther contains: (a) sepals (b) ovules (c) carpel (d) pollen grains

(d) pollen grains. The anther is the male reproductive part; ovules are inside the ovary.

Which of the following is a barrier method of contraception? (a) oral pill (b) copper-T (c) condom (d) vasectomy

(c) condom. It physically prevents the sperm from meeting the egg and also reduces the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.

Written questions

1 marks Where does fertilisation occur in human beings, and which gland secretes testosterone?

Fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct); testosterone is secreted by the testes.

2 marks Why are the testes located outside the abdominal cavity in the scrotum?

Because sperm formation requires a temperature 2–3 °C lower than the normal body temperature. The scrotum, being outside the abdomen, maintains this lower temperature.

3 marks Explain regeneration in Planaria with a diagram description.

If a Planaria is cut into several pieces, each piece can grow into a complete organism.
Specialised cells at the wound surface proliferate (multiply) to form a mass of cells.
These cells then move to their proper positions and redifferentiate into the various tissues and organs needed, so a whole new organism develops from the fragment.

3 marks Distinguish between pollination and fertilisation.

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same or another flower of the same species; it needs an external agent such as wind, water or insects.
Fertilisation is the fusion of the male gamete from the pollen tube with the female gamete (egg) inside the ovule, forming a zygote. It occurs inside the ovary and needs no external agent.

5 marks Describe the structure and working of the human female reproductive system.

Ovaries — a pair of glands that produce the female gamete (ovum) and the hormone oestrogen.
Fallopian tubes (oviducts) — tubes with finger-like ends near the ovary and ciliated walls; the egg is captured here and fertilisation takes place in the tube.
Uterus — a muscular bag where the fertilised egg implants and the embryo develops.
Cervix and vagina — the cervix is the narrow opening of the uterus; the vagina is the passage through which sperm enter and the baby is delivered.
Every month the uterus prepares a thick, spongy lining to receive a fertilised egg. If no fertilisation occurs, the lining breaks down and is discharged as blood and mucus through the vagina — this is menstruation. After fertilisation, the embryo is nourished by the mother's blood through the placenta.

2 marks List two advantages of vegetative propagation.

It is a quick and cheap method of multiplying plants, and the new plants are genetically identical to the parent, so desirable qualities such as fruit size and taste are retained. Plants that do not produce viable seeds, like banana and rose, can also be propagated this way.
