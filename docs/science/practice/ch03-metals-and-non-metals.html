MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. A copper coin placed in silver nitrate solution turns silvery and the solution turns blue. This shows copper is: (a) less reactive than silver (b) more reactive than silver (c) equally reactive (d) an inert metal

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which pair is amphoteric? (a) Na2O and CaO (b) Al2O3 and ZnO (c) CO2 and SO2 (d) CuO and FeO

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Al2O3 and ZnO — they react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.

MCQM31 mark

3. The method used to extract sodium from molten NaCl is: (a) reduction with carbon (b) self reduction (c) electrolytic reduction (d) roasting

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name a metal that is liquid at room temperature and a non-metal that is lustrous.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mercury (metal, liquid at room temperature) and iodine (non-metal, lustrous).

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?

Show Answer

Ans:

Sodium is so reactive that it reacts vigorously with oxygen, moisture and carbon dioxide in air, catching fire. Kerosene cuts off air and moisture, so the metal stays unreacted.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why are food cans coated with tin and not with zinc?

Show Answer

Ans:

Zinc is more reactive than tin and would itself react with the acidic contents of the food, contaminating it. Tin is less reactive, so it protects the can without reacting.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Show the formation of MgCl2 by the transfer of electrons, and state two properties of the compound formed.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mg (2,8,2) loses 2 electrons → Mg2+; each Cl (2,8,7) gains one electron → Cl−. Mg2+ + 2Cl− → MgCl2, held by strong electrostatic force.
Properties: it is a hard crystalline solid with a high melting point, and it conducts electricity when molten or dissolved in water.

LONGL55 marks

5. Explain electrolytic refining of copper.

Show Answer

Ans:

The impure copper is made the anode and a thin strip of pure copper the cathode, both dipped in acidified copper sulphate solution.
On passing current, copper from the anode dissolves as Cu2+ and an equal amount deposits on the cathode.
Soluble impurities go into the solution; insoluble impurities settle below the anode as anode mud (which contains the precious metals).

SHORTS62 marks

6. Why does aluminium not corrode easily even though it is a reactive metal?

Show Answer

Ans:

It reacts with oxygen to form a thin, tightly adhering layer of aluminium oxide on its surface. This layer prevents further reaction with air or moisture, so the metal below is protected.

LONGL75 marks

7. Discuss “Metals and Non-metals” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Physical properties. Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous and good conductors of heat and electricity; sodium and potassium are soft enough to cut with a knife, mercury is the only liquid metal, gallium and caesium melt in your palm. Non-metals are the opposite: dull, brittle, poor conductors — except graphite, which conducts, and iodine, a lustrous non-metal.
Marks lens: –4 marks. The reactivity series and displacement reactions are asked every year; ionic bond formation and the extraction of a metal (usually copper or zinc) fill the 3-mark slot.
Method / memory trick: Reactivity series mnemonic: Please Send Charlie's Monkey And Zebra Into Lions' Pens, Come Home Monkeys, All Safe And sound (K Na Ca Mg Al Zn Fe Pb H Cu Hg Ag Au).
Do not: Saying metals are oxidised by nitric acid to give H2 — HNO3 is an oxidising acid, so it oxidises the H2 formed to water.
