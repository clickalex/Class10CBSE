Show Answer

MCQM21 mark

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) C2H4 (ethene). Only unsaturated compounds with a double bond undergo addition with bromine.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why do covalent compounds generally have low melting and boiling points?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the forces of attraction between their molecules are weak, even though the covalent bonds within a molecule are strong. Little energy is needed to separate the molecules.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Give a chemical test to distinguish between butter and cooking oil.

Show Answer

Ans:

The bromine water test (or the alkaline KMnO4 test). Cooking oil is unsaturated, so it decolorises bromine water/KMnO4; butter is largely saturated and does not.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What is a homologous series? Write the general formula and the third member of the alkane series.

Show Answer

Ans:

A homologous series is a group of compounds with the same general formula and similar chemical properties, in which successive members differ by a −CH2− unit (14 u).
Alkanes: CnH2n+2. Third member = propane, C3H8.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Explain the cleansing action of soap with the help of a diagram description.

Show Answer

Ans:

A soap molecule has a hydrophilic ionic head and a hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail.
In water, the tails attach to the oily dirt while the heads remain in water, forming a spherical cluster called a micelle with the oil trapped inside.
The micelle is washed away with the water, taking the dirt with it; agitation helps detach the oil.

LONGL55 marks

5. With equations, describe three chemical properties of ethanoic acid.

Show Answer

Ans:

With a base (neutralisation): CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O.
With a metal carbonate: 2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2.
With a hydrogen carbonate: CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 (brisk effervescence).
Esterification (with ethanol, conc. H2SO4): CH3COOH + C2H5OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O — a sweet-smelling ester used in perfumes and flavourings.

SHORTS62 marks

6. Why is a mixture of ethyne and pure oxygen used for welding instead of ethyne and air?

Show Answer

Ans:

In air, ethyne burns incompletely and gives a sooty flame with less heat. In pure oxygen combustion is complete, producing a clean flame at about 3000 °C (the oxy-acetylene flame), which is hot enough for welding.

LONGL75 marks

7. Discuss “Carbon and its Compounds” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Why carbon is special. Carbon has four valence electrons; gaining or losing four electrons is energetically impossible, so it shares electrons and forms covalent bonds. Two properties make it unique: catenation (bonding to itself in long chains, branches and rings) and tetravalency. Covalent compounds have low melting and boiling points (weak intermolecular forces), are usually insoluble in water, and do not conduct.
Marks lens: –5 marks. One naming question (IUPAC name or structure), one comparison of soap and detergent or of ethanol and ethanoic acid, and one reaction of carbon compounds.
Method / memory trick: Carbon counts: Meth-1, Eth-2, Prop-3, But-4, Pent-5, Hex-6. The suffix gives the bond: -ane single, -ene double, -yne triple.
Do not: Writing the general formula of alkenes as CnH2n+2. Check by counting ethene: C2H4 fits CnH2n.
