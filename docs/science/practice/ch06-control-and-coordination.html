MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The gap between two neurons is called: (a) dendrite (b) synapse (c) axon (d) impulse

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) synapse — the junction where a neurotransmitter carries the signal across.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which plant hormone is a growth inhibitor? (a) auxin (b) gibberellin (c) cytokinin (d) abscisic acid

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) abscisic acid — it inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves and the closing of stomata.

MCQM31 mark

3. The part of the brain that maintains the posture and balance of the body is: (a) cerebrum (b) cerebellum (c) pons (d) medulla

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) cerebellum. It also makes voluntary movements precise; alcohol affects it, which is why a drunk person staggers.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Control and Coordination” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Nervous system, reflex arc, plant hormones and animal hormones..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Control and Coordination” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

MCQM61 mark

6. The hormone that regulates the body's metabolic rate is: (a) insulin (b) thyroxine (c) growth hormone (d) adrenaline

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) thyroxine. Secreted by the thyroid gland; iodine is needed to make it, and its deficiency causes goitre.

MCQM71 mark

7. The part of a neuron that receives the impulse is the: (a) axon (b) dendrite (c) cell body (d) myelin sheath

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) dendrite. Dendrites receive signals; the axon carries the impulse away towards the next neuron.

MCQM81 mark

8. In a reflex action the impulse is processed in the: (a) cerebrum (b) spinal cord (c) cerebellum (d) medulla only

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) spinal cord. Because the brain is not involved in the decision, the response is quick and automatic.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name the largest part of the human brain and the hormone secreted by the pancreas.

Show Answer

Ans:

Cerebrum; insulin.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?

Show Answer

Ans:

Iodine is essential for the synthesis of thyroxin by the thyroid gland. A shortage of iodine causes the gland to swell, producing goitre, and can lead to mental and physical disability in children, so iodised salt ensures an adequate intake.

SHORTS32 marks

3. How does phototropism occur in plants?

Show Answer

Ans:

Auxin is synthesised at the tip of the shoot and diffuses to the shaded side of the stem. There it causes the cells to elongate more than on the illuminated side, so the stem bends toward the light.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Name the plant hormone responsible for cell elongation and the one that ripens fruit.

Show Answer

Ans:

Auxin causes cell elongation and is responsible for the bending of a shoot towards light. Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that promotes the ripening of fruits.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Draw a labelled diagram of a neuron, or explain how a nerve impulse travels across a synapse.

Show Answer

Ans:

A neuron has a cell body with dendrites, a long axon, and branching nerve endings.
When the impulse reaches the nerve ending, an electrical signal is converted into a chemical — a neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic gap.
The chemical diffuses across the gap and triggers a new electrical impulse in the next neuron's dendrite, so the impulse travels in one direction only.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is a reflex arc? Why are reflex actions important?

Show Answer

Ans:

A reflex arc is the path followed by a nerve impulse during a reflex action: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord (relay neuron) → motor neuron → effector.
They are important because they are immediate and do not require the brain to think, so they protect the body from sudden injury such as heat, sharp objects or bright light.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Explain how the nervous and endocrine systems work together, using adrenaline as the example.

Show Answer

Ans:

The nervous system gives a fast, point-to-point electrical message; hormones give a slower, longer-lasting chemical message.
In a frightening situation the brain signals the adrenal medulla, which releases adrenaline into the blood.
Adrenaline increases heart rate and breathing, and diverts blood to the skeletal muscles — the ‘fight or flight’ response — so the whole body reacts, not just one muscle.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Describe the feedback mechanism that keeps blood sugar level steady.

Show Answer

Ans:

When the glucose level in blood rises, the pancreas releases insulin, which makes body cells take up glucose and the liver convert the excess into glycogen.
As the level falls, the pancreas reduces insulin and secretes glucagon, which converts the stored glycogen back into glucose.
This is a negative feedback loop. If insulin secretion fails, the blood sugar stays high — the condition called diabetes.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe the parts of the human brain and their functions.

Show Answer

Ans:

Forebrain — contains the cerebrum, the main thinking part, which controls voluntary actions, memory, intelligence and the senses; it also contains centres for hunger and smell.
Midbrain — controls involuntary actions such as the change in the size of the pupil.
Hindbrain — pons helps regulate respiration; the medulla controls involuntary actions such as blood pressure, salivation and vomiting; the cerebellum maintains posture and balance and makes voluntary movements precise, such as walking in a straight line or picking up a pencil.
The whole brain is protected by the cranium, meninges and the shock-absorbing cerebrospinal fluid.

LONGL145 marks

14. Discuss “Control and Coordination” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Two ways of coordinating. Nervous coordination uses electrical impulses and is fast but short-lived and reaches only selected cells; chemical (hormonal) coordination is slower, slower to act but longer-lasting and reaches every cell through the blood. Plants rely mainly on chemical coordination and on growth movements.
Marks lens: –3 marks. Reflex arc and the hormone tables are the recurring questions; a labelled diagram of the neuron or the reflex arc is common.
Method / memory trick: Reflex arc order: Receptor → Sensory → (Spine) → Motor → Effector. 'Red Sun Shines More Energetically'.
Do not: Drawing the reflex arc through the brain. It passes through the spinal cord only.
