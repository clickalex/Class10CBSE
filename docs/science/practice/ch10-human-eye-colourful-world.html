MCQs

The change in focal length of the eye lens is caused by the action of the: (a) pupil (b) retina (c) ciliary muscles (d) iris

(c) ciliary muscles — they change the curvature of the crystalline lens, giving the eye its power of accommodation.

The danger signals installed at the top of tall buildings are red because red light: (a) is scattered the most by smoke or fog (b) is scattered the least by smoke or fog (c) is absorbed the most (d) moves fastest in air

(b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.

A person cannot read a newspaper without glasses. He is probably suffering from: (a) myopia (b) hypermetropia (c) astigmatism (d) night blindness

(b) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.

Written questions

1 marks Name the part of the eye that controls the amount of light entering it, and the least distance of distinct vision for a normal eye.

The iris (which adjusts the size of the pupil); 25 cm.

2 marks Why do stars twinkle but planets do not?

Starlight passes through layers of air of changing refractive index, so its path keeps changing slightly and the apparent brightness and position fluctuate — the star twinkles. Planets are much nearer and behave as a collection of many point sources, so the fluctuations from different parts cancel out and the total light remains steady.

3 marks What is myopia? State its two causes and its correction.

Myopia (short-sightedness) is the defect in which a person can see nearby objects clearly but cannot see distant objects distinctly; the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina.
Causes: (i) excessive curvature of the eye lens, (ii) elongation of the eyeball.
Correction: a concave lens of suitable power, which diverges the light so that the image is formed exactly on the retina.

3 marks Explain the formation of a rainbow in the sky.

After rain, tiny water droplets remain suspended in the air and act as small glass prisms.
Sunlight entering a droplet is first refracted and dispersed into its constituent colours, then undergoes total internal reflection at the inner surface of the droplet, and is refracted again as it leaves.
Because different colours emerge at slightly different angles, the observer sees a coloured arc — a rainbow — with the sun always behind them.

5 marks Explain atmospheric refraction and describe any three phenomena based on it.

Atmospheric refraction is the bending of light as it passes through layers of air of different densities; the refractive index of air decreases as we go up, so light from a celestial body bends gradually toward the normal.
(1) Twinkling of stars: the continuously changing refractive index makes the apparent position and brightness of a star fluctuate.
(2) Advance of sunrise and delay of sunset: light from the sun below the horizon is refracted toward the earth, so the sun becomes visible about two minutes before actual sunrise and remains visible about two minutes after sunset — the day is lengthened by about 4 minutes.
(3) Apparent flattening of the sun's disc at sunrise and sunset, and stars appearing slightly higher than their true position, are also caused by the same refraction.

2 marks Why does the sun appear reddish early in the morning?

Because near the horizon sunlight travels through a much thicker layer of air. The shorter blue wavelengths are scattered away, and mainly the longer red wavelengths reach our eyes, so the sun looks red. At noon the path is short, little blue light is scattered, and the sun looks white.
