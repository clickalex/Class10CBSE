MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. A solution reacts with crushed egg-shell to give a gas that turns lime water milky. The solution contains: (a) NaCl (b) HCl (c) LiCl (d) KCl

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which one is a strong base? (a) NH4OH (b) Mg(OH)2 (c) NaOH (d) Cu(OH)2

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) NaOH. Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases and ionise completely; the others are weak.

MCQM31 mark

3. The pH of gastric juice released during digestion is: (a) less than 7 (b) more than 7 (c) equal to 7 (d) equal to 0

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.

MCQM41 mark

4. Which of the following salts gives a neutral solution (pH about 7) in water? (a) NaCl (b) Na₂CO₃ (c) NH₄Cl (d) CH₃COONa

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) NaCl. NaCl is the salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH), so its solution is neutral.

MCQM51 mark

5. When carbon dioxide is passed through lime water, it turns milky because of the formation of: (a) calcium chloride (b) calcium hydroxide (c) calcium carbonate (d) calcium oxide

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) calcium carbonate. Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O; calcium carbonate is insoluble in water, so the lime water turns milky.

MCQM61 mark

6. A solution turns colourless phenolphthalein pink. Its pH is likely to be: (a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 10

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 10. Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution, whose pH is above 7.

MCQM71 mark

7. The salt used to remove permanent hardness of water is: (a) sodium chloride (b) sodium carbonate (c) calcium sulphate (d) ammonium chloride

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.

MCQM81 mark

8. Dilute acid added to an active metal produces: (a) oxygen (b) hydrogen (c) carbon dioxide (d) nitrogen

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) hydrogen. Metal + dilute acid → salt + H₂, which burns with a pop sound.

MCQM91 mark

9. The chemical formula of Plaster of Paris is: (a) CaSO₄·2H₂O (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O (c) CaSO₄ (d) CaCO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.

MCQM101 mark

10. Baking soda is: (a) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (b) NaHCO₃ (c) NaOH (d) Na₂CO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.

MCQM111 mark

11. Bleaching powder is manufactured by passing chlorine gas over: (a) dry slaked lime (b) quicklime (c) limestone (d) gypsum

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) dry slaked lime. Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O. Quicklime is CaO, limestone is CaCO₃, gypsum is a sulphate.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why does dry HCl gas not change the colour of dry litmus paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because acidic behaviour requires the formation of H+(aq) ions, which needs water. Dry HCl gas has no water, so it does not ionise and cannot affect dry litmus.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A solution turns red litmus blue. Its pH is likely to be: 1, 4, 7 or 10. Justify.

Show Answer

Ans:

  1. Turning red litmus blue shows the solution is basic, and only pH 10 among the options is on the basic side of 7.
SHORTS32 marks

3. Fresh milk has a pH of 6. Explain why the pH falls as it turns into curd.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid. The acid increases the H+ ion concentration, so the pH drops below 6 and the milk proteins coagulate into curd.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What happens when a base reacts with a non-metal oxide? Give an equation and state what it tells you about non-metal oxides.

Show Answer

Ans:

A salt and water are formed, just as in neutralisation: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. It shows that non-metal oxides are acidic in nature.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why is plaster of Paris written as CaSO4·½H2O? Is it possible to have half a water molecule?

Show Answer

Ans:

The formula is written per formula unit of CaSO4. One molecule of water is shared between two CaSO4 units, so each unit effectively holds half a water molecule. Heating gypsum at 373 K drives out 1½ molecules of water.

SHORTS63 marks

6. A milkman adds a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk. Why does the milk take longer to set as curd?

Show Answer

Ans:

The baking soda makes the milk slightly alkaline, so the lactic acid produced by bacteria is first neutralised before the pH can fall low enough to coagulate the casein. Setting is therefore delayed.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes: “The setting of plaster of Paris into a hard mass is only a physical change because nothing new is made.” Correct the statement with the chemical equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Setting is a chemical change: plaster of Paris combines with water to form a new compound, gypsum.
CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O (gypsum, a hard solid mass).
This is why POP is stored in moisture-proof containers: damp air would make it set. Its uses include casting for fractured bones and making toys and decorative material.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. The soil in a field has a pH of 5.2. What should the farmer add to it, and why? Name one common material.

Show Answer

Ans:

A pH of 5.2 means the soil is acidic, while most crops grow best at about pH 6.5–7.5.
The farmer should add a base such as quicklime (CaO), slaked lime Ca(OH)₂ or chalk (CaCO₃).
The base neutralises the excess acid (acid + base → salt + water), which raises the pH towards neutral.

SHORTS93 marks

9. How is bleaching powder prepared? Write the equation and two uses.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bleaching powder is prepared by the action of chlorine gas on dry slaked lime: Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O.
Uses: bleaching cotton and linen in the textile industry and wood pulp in paper factories.
It is also used as an oxidising agent in chemical industries and to disinfect drinking water.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Four solutions P, Q, R and S have pH values 2, 6, 8 and 13 respectively. (a) Which is the strongest acid and which is the strongest base? (b) Which solution would you use to relieve a bee sting, and why not the strongest base? (c) How many times greater is the hydrogen-ion concentration of P than that of Q?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) P (pH 2) is the strongest acid; S (pH 13) is the strongest base.
(b) R (pH 8), a mild base like baking-soda solution, neutralises the acid (methanoic acid) of the sting. S would be too strong and would burn the skin.
(c) Each pH unit is a factor of 10, and P and Q differ by 4 units, so P has 10⁴ = 10,000 times the hydrogen-ion concentration of Q.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A student heats a few blue crystals of copper sulphate in a dry test tube. Water droplets appear on the cooler part of the tube and the crystals turn white. When a few drops of water are added to the white powder, the blue colour returns. (a) Write the formula of the blue crystals. (b) Why does the colour change on heating? (c) What is this water called?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) CuSO₄·5H₂O.
(b) Heating drives off the water held in the crystals, leaving white anhydrous copper sulphate, CuSO₄.
(c) It is the water of crystallisation: five water molecules per formula unit. Adding water restores it, so the blue colour returns.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Three unlabelled bottles contain an acidic, a basic and a neutral solution. How would you identify all three using only red and blue litmus paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Dip a strip of red litmus into each solution: the one that turns it blue is the basic solution.
Now dip a strip of blue litmus into the two remaining solutions: the one that turns it red is the acidic solution.
The solution that changes neither paper is neutral (pH ≈ 7).
Note that a single indicator is not enough — you must use both papers, because neutral and acidic solutions both leave red litmus unchanged.

LONGL135 marks

13. Explain the chlor-alkali process with an equation, and give two uses each of the products.

Show Answer

Ans:

Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (brine) gives sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen: 2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g).
Sodium hydroxide — making soaps and detergents, and in the paper industry.
Chlorine — disinfecting drinking water and making bleaching powder/PVC.
Hydrogen — as a fuel and in the manufacture of ammonia and margarine (hydrogenation of oils).

LONGL145 marks

14. How is baking soda prepared? What happens when it is heated? Write the equations and give two uses of baking soda and two uses of washing soda.

Show Answer

Ans:

Preparation: NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ + NH₃ → NH₄Cl + NaHCO₃ (sodium hydrogencarbonate, baking soda).
On heating: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. The sodium carbonate formed gives washing soda, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, on recrystallisation.
Uses of baking soda: an antacid; baking powder (with tartaric acid) to make cakes soft and spongy; soda-acid fire extinguishers.
Uses of washing soda: removing permanent hardness of water; making glass, soap and paper; household cleaning.
