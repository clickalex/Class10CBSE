MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. A solution reacts with crushed egg-shell to give a gas that turns lime water milky. The solution contains: (a) NaCl (b) HCl (c) LiCl (d) KCl

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which one is a strong base? (a) NH4OH (b) Mg(OH)2 (c) NaOH (d) Cu(OH)2

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) NaOH. Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases and ionise completely; the others are weak.

MCQM31 mark

3. The pH of gastric juice released during digestion is: (a) less than 7 (b) more than 7 (c) equal to 7 (d) equal to 0

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why does dry HCl gas not change the colour of dry litmus paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because acidic behaviour requires the formation of H+(aq) ions, which needs water. Dry HCl gas has no water, so it does not ionise and cannot affect dry litmus.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A solution turns red litmus blue. Its pH is likely to be: 1, 4, 7 or 10. Justify.

Show Answer

Ans:

  1. Turning red litmus blue shows the solution is basic, and only pH 10 among the options is on the basic side of 7.
SHORTS33 marks

3. Why is plaster of Paris written as CaSO4·½H2O? Is it possible to have half a water molecule?

Show Answer

Ans:

The formula is written per formula unit of CaSO4. One molecule of water is shared between two CaSO4 units, so each unit effectively holds half a water molecule. Heating gypsum at 373 K drives out 1½ molecules of water.

SHORTS43 marks

4. A milkman adds a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk. Why does the milk take longer to set as curd?

Show Answer

Ans:

The baking soda makes the milk slightly alkaline, so the lactic acid produced by bacteria is first neutralised before the pH can fall low enough to coagulate the casein. Setting is therefore delayed.

LONGL55 marks

5. Explain the chlor-alkali process with an equation, and give two uses each of the products.

Show Answer

Ans:

Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (brine) gives sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen: 2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g).
Sodium hydroxide — making soaps and detergents, and in the paper industry.
Chlorine — disinfecting drinking water and making bleaching powder/PVC.
Hydrogen — as a fuel and in the manufacture of ammonia and margarine (hydrogenation of oils).

SHORTS62 marks

6. Fresh milk has a pH of 6. Explain why the pH falls as it turns into curd.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid. The acid increases the H+ ion concentration, so the pH drops below 6 and the milk proteins coagulate into curd.

LONGL75 marks

7. Discuss “Acids, Bases and Salts” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

What makes an acid an acid. Acids produce H+(aq) ions in water; bases produce OH−(aq) ions. Distillation water does not conduct electricity, but adding an acid or base makes it conduct, because the ions are the charge carriers. Note: dry HCl gas does not turn dry blue litmus red — water is needed for ionisation.
Marks lens: –5 marks. A pH/reasoning question (2), a salt-family question such as bleaching powder or plaster of Paris (2–3), and one indicator or neutralisation question.
Method / memory trick: pH memory: the scale counts down as acidity goes up. pH 2 is a hundred times more acidic than pH 4 (each unit is a factor of ten).
Do not: Saying 'an acid gives H+ ions' without (aq). Ionisation needs water — dry HCl is not acidic.
