MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The direction of the magnetic field produced by a straight current-carrying wire is given by: (a) Fleming's left-hand rule (b) Fleming's right-hand rule (c) Lenz's law (d) the right-hand thumb rule

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.

MCQM21 mark

2. The essential difference between an AC generator and a DC generator is: (a) AC generator has an electromagnet (b) DC generator has a commutator (c) AC generator has slip rings while DC generator has a commutator (d) AC generator produces more power

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). The slip rings of an AC generator allow the output to reverse, while the split-ring commutator of a DC generator keeps the external current in one direction.

MCQM31 mark

3. The frequency of AC supply in India is 50 Hz. The direction of the current changes: (a) 50 times per second (b) 25 times per second (c) 200 times per second (d) 100 times per second

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 100 times per second — the direction reverses twice in each cycle.

MCQM41 mark

4. Outside a bar magnet, the magnetic field lines run: (a) from the south pole to the north pole (b) in straight lines parallel to the axis only (c) from the centre to both poles (d) from the north pole to the south pole

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) from the north pole to the south pole. By convention the field lines emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole outside the magnet, and continue from south to north inside it.

MCQM51 mark

5. A straight conductor carrying current is placed in a magnetic field. The force on it is greatest when the current is: (a) parallel to the field (b) at 45° to the field (c) perpendicular to the field (d) zero

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) perpendicular to the field. The force F = BIl sin θ is maximum at θ = 90°, and zero when the conductor is parallel to the field.

MCQM61 mark

6. The magnetic field lines inside a long current-carrying solenoid are: (a) concentric circles (b) parallel straight lines (c) diverging curves (d) absent

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.

MCQM71 mark

7. An electric motor converts: (a) electrical energy into mechanical energy (b) mechanical energy into electrical energy (c) heat into light (d) chemical energy into sound

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator does the reverse conversion.

MCQM81 mark

8. The insulation on the earth wire in a domestic circuit is: (a) green (b) red (c) black (d) blue

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) green. Live is red or brown, neutral is black or blue, and earth is green or green-yellow.

MCQM91 mark

9. The magnetic field at a point near a long straight current-carrying wire: (a) increases as you move away from the wire (b) does not depend on the current (c) decreases as you move away from the wire (d) is zero everywhere around the wire

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) decreases as you move away from the wire. The concentric field lines get farther apart with distance; the field is stronger for a larger current and closer to the wire.

MCQM101 mark

10. Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction of the: (a) induced current in a coil (b) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (c) magnetic field due to a current (d) current produced by a generator

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.

MCQM111 mark

11. The production of a current in a closed coil by a changing magnetic field is called: (a) electromagnetic induction (b) magnetic effect of current (c) electrolysis (d) electrostatic induction

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) electromagnetic induction. Faraday's discovery — the basis of the electric generator; the induced current direction is given by Fleming's right-hand rule.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. State Fleming's right-hand rule.

Show Answer

Ans:

Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the right hand so that they are mutually perpendicular. If the forefinger points in the direction of the magnetic field and the thumb in the direction of motion of the conductor, the middle finger gives the direction of the induced current.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect each other?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because at the point of intersection there would be two directions of the magnetic field for a single point, which is impossible — a compass needle placed there could not point in two directions at once.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Differentiate between an AC generator and a DC generator.

Show Answer

Ans:

An AC generator uses two complete slip rings, and the current it produces reverses direction periodically (50 Hz in India). A DC generator uses a split-ring commutator, so although the current in the coil reverses, the current in the external circuit always flows in one direction.

SHORTS42 marks

4. State the rule used to find the direction of the magnetic field around a straight current-carrying conductor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Right-hand thumb rule: imagine holding the conductor in your right hand with the thumb pointing along the current; the direction in which your fingers curl around the conductor is the direction of the magnetic field lines, which are concentric circles around the wire.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain the principle and working of an electric motor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Principle: a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force, given by Fleming's left-hand rule.
Working: a rectangular coil ABCD is placed between the poles of a magnet. When current flows, one arm is pushed up and the other down, so the coil rotates.
The split-ring commutator reverses the direction of current in the coil after every half rotation, so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction.
Uses: electric fans, mixers, washing machines and pumps.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is electromagnetic induction? Describe an activity to demonstrate it.

Show Answer

Ans:

When the magnetic field linked with a coil changes, a current is induced in the coil — this is electromagnetic induction.
Activity: connect a galvanometer to a coil. Push a bar magnet's north pole into the coil and the galvanometer needle deflects momentarily in one direction; pull it out and it deflects in the opposite direction; hold it still inside and there is no deflection.
The deflection shows that a current is induced only when the magnetic flux through the coil is changing, i.e. only while the magnet is in relative motion.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student draws the field lines of a bar magnet as coming out of the south pole and entering the north pole outside the magnet. Correct the statement and state two properties of magnetic field lines.

Show Answer

Ans:

Outside the magnet, field lines run from the north pole to the south pole; inside the magnet they run from south to north, so each line is a closed loop.
Property 1: the lines are closed curves that never cross one another.
Property 2: the field is strongest where the lines are closest together, i.e. near the poles.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. A coil of wire is connected to a galvanometer. When a bar magnet is pushed into the coil, the needle deflects. What happens (i) when the magnet is held still inside the coil, (ii) when it is pulled out, and (iii) how can the deflection be increased?

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) No deflection: a current is induced only while the magnetic field linked with the coil is changing.
(ii) The needle deflects in the opposite direction, because the change in the field is reversed.
(iii) Move the magnet faster, use a stronger magnet or use a coil with more turns. This is electromagnetic induction.

SHORTS93 marks

9. How does the strength of the magnetic field at the centre of a current-carrying circular coil depend on the current, the number of turns and the radius of the coil?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is directly proportional to the current flowing through the coil.
It is directly proportional to the number of turns, because the field of each turn adds up.
It is inversely proportional to the radius of the coil, so a smaller coil gives a stronger field. This is the principle behind electromagnets and solenoids.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A student winds insulated copper wire round an iron nail and connects the ends to a battery. The nail picks up small iron pins, but drops them when the battery is disconnected. (a) Name this device. (b) Why does it hold the pins only while the current flows? (c) State two ways to make it stronger.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) An electromagnet (a solenoid with a soft iron core).
(b) The current-carrying coil magnetises the iron core; soft iron loses its magnetism as soon as the current stops, so the pins fall.
(c) Increase the number of turns of the coil, or increase the current (a soft iron core also strengthens it).

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A socket in a house is protected by a 5 A fuse, and the supply is 220 V. (a) Find the current drawn by a 1 kW heater. (b) Will the fuse blow if a 2 kW geyser is plugged in instead? Justify. (c) State the purpose of the fuse and of the earth wire.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) I = P/V = 1000/220 ≈ 4.5 A, which is below 5 A.
(b) For the geyser, I = 2000/220 ≈ 9.1 A, which is above 5 A, so the fuse wire heats up, melts and breaks the circuit.
(c) The fuse protects appliances and wiring from overloading and short circuits. The earth wire joins the metal body of an appliance to the ground, so any leakage current flows to earth and does not give a shock.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Explain the working of an electric generator, including the role of slip rings and the commutator.

Show Answer

Ans:

A coil is rotated mechanically in a magnetic field, so the magnetic flux linked with it keeps changing and an induced current is produced (electromagnetic induction).
The direction of the induced current reverses every half rotation, which is found by Fleming's right-hand rule.
In an AC generator two slip rings keep the coil connected to the external circuit without reversing the connections, so the output is alternating current.
A DC generator replaces them with a split-ring commutator, which reverses the connection to the external circuit every half turn so the output flows in one direction.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe a domestic electric circuit and explain the function of the earth wire and the fuse.

Show Answer

Ans:

The supply has three wires: live (red, at about 220 V), neutral (black, at zero potential) and earth (green).
Appliances are connected in parallel so that each receives the full supply voltage, each has its own switch, and the failure of one does not affect the others. Separate 15 A and 5 A circuits are provided for heavy and light appliances.
Earth wire: the metallic body of an appliance is connected to a metal plate buried in the earth, so if the live wire touches the body the leakage current flows safely into the ground instead of through a person — preventing a fatal shock.
Fuse: a short piece of wire of low melting point connected in series with the appliance. If the current exceeds the safe value, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit, protecting the appliance from damage due to overloading or short circuiting.

LONGL145 marks

14. Describe the magnetic field produced by a current-carrying solenoid and explain how an electromagnet is made from it.

Show Answer

Ans:

A solenoid is a coil of many closely wound circular turns of insulated copper wire.
The field lines inside the solenoid are parallel straight lines, so the field there is uniform; outside, the pattern is like that of a bar magnet.
One end of the solenoid behaves as a north pole and the other as a south pole.
If a piece of soft iron is placed inside the coil, the strong field magnetises it, and the combination is an electromagnet.
It is a temporary magnet: it is magnetised only while the current flows. It is used in cranes for lifting iron, in electric bells and in MRI machines.
