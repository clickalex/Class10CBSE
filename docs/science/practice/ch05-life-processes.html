MCQM11 mark

1. The kidneys in human beings are a part of the system for: (a) nutrition (b) respiration (c) excretion (d) transportation

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which of the following is not a function of the stomach? (a) killing bacteria (b) digestion of protein (c) absorption of digested food (d) protection of the stomach wall by mucus

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). Absorption of digested food occurs mainly in the small intestine through the villi.

MCQM31 mark

3. The breakdown of pyruvate to give CO2, water and energy takes place in the: (a) cytoplasm (b) mitochondria (c) chloroplast (d) nucleus

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) mitochondria. Glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate) occurs in the cytoplasm; the further oxidation of pyruvate is mitochondrial.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why is the rate of breathing in aquatic organisms much faster than in terrestrial organisms?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the amount of dissolved oxygen in water is far lower than the oxygen in air. Aquatic animals must therefore pass a larger volume of water over their gills per unit time to obtain enough oxygen, which makes the breathing rate faster.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? Name two organisms that use anaerobic respiration.

Show Answer

Ans:

Aerobic occurs in the presence of oxygen, in the mitochondria, giving CO2 and H2O and a large amount of energy.
Anaerobic occurs without oxygen in the cytoplasm, giving ethanol and CO2 (yeast) or lactic acid (muscles), and much less energy.
Organisms: yeast, some bacteria and parasitic worms such as Taenia; human muscle cells respire anaerobically temporarily.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Draw a labelled diagram of the human excretory system, or describe the working of a nephron.

Show Answer

Ans:

Kidneys → ureters → urinary bladder → urethra.
Nephron: blood enters the glomerulus under pressure inside Bowman's capsule, where water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea are filtered into the tubule (ultrafiltration).
As the filtrate moves down the tubule, useful substances and most water are selectively reabsorbed; the remaining fluid, now urine, collects in the collecting duct and passes into the ureter.

LONGL55 marks

5. Explain the process of nutrition in human beings, from ingestion to egestion.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mouth: teeth chew the food; salivary amylase begins the breakdown of starch into simpler sugars.
Oesophagus: peristaltic movements push the food to the stomach.
Stomach: HCl makes the medium acidic for pepsin to digest proteins, kills germs, and mucus protects the stomach wall.
Small intestine: the site of complete digestion — bile from the liver emulsifies fat, pancreatic trypsin acts on proteins, lipase on fats, and intestinal enzymes convert carbohydrates to glucose and proteins to amino acids; the finger-like villi absorb the digested food into the blood.
Large intestine and anus: water is absorbed from the undigested food and the waste is egested.

SHORTS62 marks

6. Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like human beings?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because their cells are not all in direct contact with the environment, and the volume of the body is large, so the distance oxygen would have to diffuse is too great. Diffusion is too slow to supply every cell, which is why special respiratory and transport systems are needed.

LONGL75 marks

7. Discuss “Life Processes” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Maintenance. Living organisms constantly repair and renew their structures using energy from outside. The processes that maintain life are nutrition, respiration, transport and excretion. Growth and movement are not by themselves proof of life — a breathing person in a coma is alive; a growing crystal is not.
Marks lens: –5 marks. Almost always a labelled diagram (human respiratory or excretory system, or the nephron) plus one 3-mark reasoning question.
Method / memory trick: Four systems, four words: Eat (nutrition), Burn (respiration), Carry (transport), Throw out (excretion).
Do not: Writing the photosynthesis equation with 6H2O on the left. NCERT uses 12H2O.
