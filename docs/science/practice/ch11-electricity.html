MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The least resistance that can be obtained by combining 2 Ω, 4 Ω, 1 Ω and 100 Ω is: (a) more than 100 Ω (b) less than 1 Ω (c) 107 Ω (d) between 2 Ω and 100 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.

MCQM21 mark

2. Two wires of the same material and length but different cross-sectional areas are compared. The thicker wire has: (a) more resistance (b) less resistance (c) the same resistance (d) no resistance

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) less resistance, since R = ρl/A and a larger area of cross-section reduces the resistance.

MCQM31 mark

3. The commercial unit of electrical energy is: (a) joule (b) watt (c) kilowatt-hour (d) volt

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) kilowatt-hour (kWh), equal to 3.6 × 10⁶ J, and commonly called a 'unit'.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Electricity” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, heating effect and power..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Electricity” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. The resistance of a wire doubles when: (a) its length is doubled (b) its area of cross-section is doubled (c) its length is halved (d) its diameter is doubled

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.

MCQM71 mark

7. One kilowatt hour equals: (a) 3.6 × 10⁶ J (b) 3.6 × 10³ J (c) 360 J (d) 6 × 10µ J

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J.

MCQM81 mark

8. An ammeter is connected in a circuit: (a) in series (b) in parallel (c) across the supply (d) either way

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) in series. It has very low resistance so it does not change the current; a voltmeter is connected in parallel.

MCQM91 mark

9. A 12 V battery drives a current of 2 A through a resistor. The resistance of the resistor is: (a) 24 Ω (b) 6 Ω (c) 0.17 Ω (d) 14 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.

MCQM101 mark

10. Three 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is: (a) 18 Ω (b) 6 Ω (c) 2 Ω (d) 3 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 2 Ω. 1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6, so R = 2 Ω — equal resistors in parallel give R/n.

MCQM111 mark

11. A 100 W bulb is used for 10 hours every day. The electrical energy it consumes in 30 days is: (a) 3 kWh (b) 30 kWh (c) 300 kWh (d) 3000 kWh

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Define one ohm and name the device used to measure potential difference.

Show Answer

Ans:

One ohm is the resistance of a conductor when a potential difference of 1 volt across its ends produces a current of 1 ampere through it. Potential difference is measured with a voltmeter, connected in parallel.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why are the coils of electric toasters and electric irons made of an alloy rather than a pure metal?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because alloys have higher resistivity than the pure metals, so they produce more heat for the same current, and they do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures.

SHORTS32 marks

3. An electric iron of rating 750 W, 220 V is used for 2 hours daily. Calculate the cost of using it for 30 days at ₹5 per unit.

Show Answer

Ans:

Energy per day = P × t = 750 W × 2 h = 1500 Wh = 1.5 kWh.
Energy for 30 days = 1.5 × 30 = 45 kWh = 45 units.
Cost = 45 × ₹5 = ₹225.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Why are household appliances connected in parallel and not in series?

Show Answer

Ans:

In a parallel circuit every appliance gets the full supply voltage and works independently, so switching one off does not affect the others and a fault in one branch does not stop the rest — none of which is possible in a series circuit.

SHORTS53 marks

5. State Ohm's law and draw the V–I graph for an ohmic conductor.

Show Answer

Ans:

At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends: V = IR, where R is the resistance.
The graph of V against I is a straight line passing through the origin; its slope (V/I) gives the resistance of the conductor.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.

Show Answer

Ans:

1/Rp = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = (3 + 2 + 1)/6 = 6/6 = 1.
So Rp = 1 Ω — less than the smallest resistor (2 Ω), as expected for a parallel combination.

A classmate lost marks by doing this: Connecting the ammeter in parallel and the voltmeter in series in a circuit diagram — this is a marked error. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Series = same current, voltage divides. Parallel = same voltage, current divides. Read that sentence before every circuit question.
Why the mistake costs marks: Connecting the ammeter in parallel and the voltmeter in series in a circuit diagram — this is a marked error.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Resistors of 4 Ω and 12 Ω are connected in parallel and the combination is joined to a 6 V battery. Find the equivalent resistance and the current drawn from the battery.

Show Answer

Ans:

1/Rₚ = 1/4 + 1/12 = (3 + 1)/12 = 4/12, so Rₚ = 3 Ω.
Current I = V/R = 6/3 = 2 A.
Check: the branch currents are 6/4 = 1.5 A and 6/12 = 0.5 A, which add to 2 A. Note the equivalent resistance is less than the smaller resistor.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. A 100 W bulb and a 60 W bulb are both rated at 220 V. Which has the higher resistance, and what current does each draw?

Show Answer

Ans:

Using R = V²/P: R₁₀₀ = 220²/100 = 484 Ω and R₆₀ = 220²/60 ≈ 806.7 Ω.
So the 60 W bulb has the higher resistance — the lower-power bulb resists the current more.
Currents: I = P/V, so the 100 W bulb draws 100/220 ≈ 0.45 A and the 60 W bulb draws 60/220 ≈ 0.27 A.

LONGL135 marks

13. A 4 Ω resistor and a 6 Ω resistor are connected in series with a 20 V battery. Find the total resistance, the current, the potential difference across each resistor, and the heat produced in the 4 Ω resistor in 1 minute.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total resistance Rs = 4 + 6 = 10 Ω.
Current I = V/R = 20/10 = 2 A — the same through both resistors.
Potential differences: across 4 Ω, V = 2 × 4 = 8 V; across 6 Ω, V = 2 × 6 = 12 V. (8 + 12 = 20 V ✓)
Heat in the 4 Ω resistor H = I²Rt = (2)² × 4 × 60 = 960 J.

LONGL145 marks

14. Discuss “Electricity” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Electric current and potential difference. Current I = Q/t, measured in amperes with an ammeter connected in series. Potential difference V = W/Q, measured in volts with a voltmeter connected in parallel; 1 volt = 1 joule per coulomb. By convention current flows from the positive to the negative terminal, opposite to the flow of electrons. A cell maintains the potential difference that drives the charge.
Marks lens: –4 marks. A series or parallel circuit numerical is almost certain, plus one question on the heating effect or on power rating.
Method / memory trick: Series = same current, voltage divides. Parallel = same voltage, current divides. Read that sentence before every circuit question.
Do not: Connecting the ammeter in parallel and the voltmeter in series in a circuit diagram — this is a marked error.
