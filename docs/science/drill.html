Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The reaction Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe is an example of: (a) combination (b) decomposition (c) double displacement (d) displacement

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which of the following is an endothermic process? (a) process of decomposition of ferrous sulphate (b) respiration (c) decomposition of vegetable matter into compost (d) quicklime + water

Show Answer

Ans:

(a). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.

MCQM31 mark

3. The green coating on copper and the black coating on silver are respectively: (a) CuO and Ag2O (b) CuCO3 and AgS2 (c) basic copper carbonate and Ag2S (d) Cu(OH)2 and AgO

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).

MCQM41 mark

4. Which of the following is a displacement reaction? (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (b) 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (c) 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (d) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu. Zinc, being more reactive, replaces copper from its salt solution. The others are combination, decomposition and combination reactions.

MCQM51 mark

5. Quicklime reacts vigorously with water to form slaked lime and a lot of heat. This reaction is: (a) endothermic and a combination reaction (b) exothermic and a combination reaction (c) exothermic and a decomposition reaction (d) endothermic and a displacement reaction

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) exothermic and a combination reaction. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat: two substances join to form one product, and heat is released.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is a combination reaction? (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (b) 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (c) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (d) AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.

MCQM71 mark

7. The brown fumes evolved when lead nitrate is heated are of: (a) O₂ (b) NO₂ (c) NO (d) PbO

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) NO₂. 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂; nitrogen dioxide is brown, the residue lead oxide is yellow.

MCQM81 mark

8. Rancidity of fat-containing food is prevented by: (a) storing it in the open (b) adding oxidising agents (c) heating it repeatedly (d) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.

MCQM91 mark

9. When the equation Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂ is balanced, the coefficient of steam (H₂O) is: (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.

MCQM101 mark

10. A shiny brown element X becomes black when heated in air. X and the black compound are: (a) Fe and Fe₂O₃ (b) Ag and Ag₂S (c) Cu and CuO (d) Al and Al₂O₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Cu and CuO. Copper is reddish-brown and forms black copper(II) oxide on heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.

MCQM111 mark

11. The reaction 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂ used in black-and-white photography is a: (a) thermal decomposition (b) photochemical decomposition (c) displacement (d) combination reaction

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.

Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. A solution reacts with crushed egg-shell to give a gas that turns lime water milky. The solution contains: (a) NaCl (b) HCl (c) LiCl (d) KCl

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which one is a strong base? (a) NaOH (b) NH4OH (c) Mg(OH)2 (d) Cu(OH)2

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) NaOH. Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases and ionise completely; the others are weak.

MCQM31 mark

3. The pH of gastric juice released during digestion is: (a) less than 7 (b) more than 7 (c) equal to 7 (d) equal to 0

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.

MCQM41 mark

4. Which of the following salts gives a neutral solution (pH about 7) in water? (a) Na₂CO₃ (b) NH₄Cl (c) NaCl (d) CH₃COONa

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) NaCl. NaCl is the salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH), so its solution is neutral.

MCQM51 mark

5. When carbon dioxide is passed through lime water, it turns milky because of the formation of: (a) calcium chloride (b) calcium carbonate (c) calcium hydroxide (d) calcium oxide

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) calcium carbonate. Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O; calcium carbonate is insoluble in water, so the lime water turns milky.

MCQM61 mark

6. A solution turns colourless phenolphthalein pink. Its pH is likely to be: (a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 10

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 10. Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution, whose pH is above 7.

MCQM71 mark

7. The salt used to remove permanent hardness of water is: (a) sodium chloride (b) calcium sulphate (c) sodium carbonate (d) ammonium chloride

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.

MCQM81 mark

8. Dilute acid added to an active metal produces: (a) hydrogen (b) oxygen (c) carbon dioxide (d) nitrogen

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) hydrogen. Metal + dilute acid → salt + H₂, which burns with a pop sound.

MCQM91 mark

9. The chemical formula of Plaster of Paris is: (a) CaSO₄·2H₂O (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O (c) CaSO₄ (d) CaCO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.

MCQM101 mark

10. Baking soda is: (a) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (b) NaOH (c) Na₂CO₃ (d) NaHCO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.

MCQM111 mark

11. Bleaching powder is manufactured by passing chlorine gas over: (a) quicklime (b) limestone (c) dry slaked lime (d) gypsum

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) dry slaked lime. Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O. Quicklime is CaO, limestone is CaCO₃, gypsum is a sulphate.

Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. A copper coin placed in silver nitrate solution turns silvery and the solution turns blue. This shows copper is: (a) less reactive than silver (b) equally reactive (c) an inert metal (d) more reactive than silver

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which pair is amphoteric? (a) Na2O and CaO (b) Al2O3 and ZnO (c) CO2 and SO2 (d) CuO and FeO

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Al2O3 and ZnO — they react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.

MCQM31 mark

3. The method used to extract sodium from molten NaCl is: (a) reduction with carbon (b) self reduction (c) electrolytic reduction (d) roasting

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.

MCQM41 mark

4. Which of the following metals will displace copper from copper sulphate solution? (a) Ag (b) Zn (c) Hg (d) Au

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Zn. Only a metal above copper in the reactivity series can displace it; zinc is above copper, while silver, mercury and gold are below it.

MCQM51 mark

5. An element forms an oxide that is acidic in nature. The element is most likely a: (a) non-metal (b) metal (c) alkali metal (d) alloy

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) non-metal. Non-metals form acidic (or neutral) oxides, while metals generally form basic oxides.

MCQM61 mark

6. The best conductor of electricity among these is: (a) copper (b) silver (c) aluminium (d) iron

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) silver. Silver conducts best, then copper; silver is too costly for wiring, so copper is used.

MCQM71 mark

7. Brass is an alloy of: (a) copper and zinc (b) copper and tin (c) lead and tin (d) mercury and zinc

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) copper and zinc. Bronze is copper and tin, solder is lead and tin, and an amalgam contains mercury.

MCQM81 mark

8. Which metal reacts vigorously with cold water? (a) iron (b) zinc (c) copper (d) sodium

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) sodium. It reacts violently with cold water; iron and zinc react only with steam, and copper does not react at all.

MCQM91 mark

9. The correct decreasing order of reactivity is: (a) Zn > Mg > Cu (b) Cu > Zn > Mg (c) Zn > Cu > Mg (d) Mg > Zn > Cu

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Mg > Zn > Cu. In the reactivity series magnesium lies above zinc, and copper lies below hydrogen, so it is the least reactive of the three.

MCQM101 mark

10. Galvanisation protects iron from rusting by coating it with a thin layer of: (a) tin (b) chromium (c) zinc (d) copper

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference to the iron even if the coating is scratched.

MCQM111 mark

11. Magnesium (2, 8, 2) combines with chlorine (2, 8, 7) to form an ionic compound with the formula: (a) MgCl₂ (b) MgCl (c) Mg₂Cl (d) MgCl₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.

Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The number of covalent bonds in a molecule of ethane (C2H6) is: (a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 8

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 7 — six C–H bonds plus one C–C bond.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which compound decolorises bromine water? (a) C2H4 (b) CH4 (c) C2H6 (d) C3H8

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) C2H4 (ethene). Only unsaturated compounds with a double bond undergo addition with bromine.

MCQM31 mark

3. Vinegar is a solution of: (a) 50–80% ethanoic acid (b) 5–8% ethanoic acid in water (c) pure ethanoic acid (d) ethanoic acid in alcohol

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 5–8% ethanoic acid in water. Pure (glacial) acetic acid freezes at 290 K, which is why it is not used directly in kitchens.

MCQM41 mark

4. The next higher homologue of ethanol, C₂H₅OH, is: (a) C₃H₇OH (b) C₃H₈ (c) CH₃OH (d) C₂H₅COOH

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) C₃H₇OH. Successive members of a homologous series differ by a –CH₂– unit; ethanol → propanol (C₃H₇OH).

MCQM51 mark

5. Which of the following burns with a sooty flame? (a) methane (CH₄) (b) ethane (C₂H₆) (c) ethyne (C₂H₂) (d) ethanol (C₂H₅OH)

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) ethyne (C₂H₂). Unsaturated hydrocarbons such as ethyne have a high proportion of carbon, which does not burn completely and forms soot.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which compound decolourises alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent)? (a) C₂H₆ (b) C₂H₄ (c) CH₄ (d) C₃H₈

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) C₂H₄. Ethene is unsaturated; only double or triple bonds add on and decolourise the reagent.

MCQM71 mark

7. The functional group present in an alcohol is: (a) –CHO (b) –COOH (c) –CO– (d) –OH

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.

MCQM81 mark

8. The gas evolved when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate is: (a) H₂ (b) O₂ (c) CO (d) CO₂

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) CO₂. 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂; the gas turns lime water milky.

MCQM91 mark

9. The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH₂–CHO is: (a) propanal (b) propanol (c) propanone (d) propanoic acid

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) propanal. Three carbons (prop-) with an aldehyde group –CHO (-al). Propanone is a ketone, propanol an alcohol, propanoic acid a carboxylic acid.

MCQM101 mark

10. Which pair belongs to the same homologous series? (a) CH₄ and C₂H₄ (b) C₂H₄ and C₂H₂ (c) CH₃OH and CH₃COOH (d) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.

MCQM111 mark

11. Which hydrocarbon undergoes an addition reaction with hydrogen in the presence of a nickel catalyst? (a) C₃H₈ (b) C₂H₆ (c) C₂H₄ (d) C₄H₁₀

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) C₂H₄. Only an unsaturated hydrocarbon (ethene, with a C=C double bond) can add hydrogen; the other three are saturated alkanes.

Ch 5 · Life Processes (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. The kidneys in human beings are a part of the system for: (a) excretion (b) nutrition (c) respiration (d) transportation

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which of the following is not a function of the stomach? (a) killing bacteria (b) digestion of protein (c) absorption of digested food (d) protection of the stomach wall by mucus

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). Absorption of digested food occurs mainly in the small intestine through the villi.

MCQM31 mark

3. The breakdown of pyruvate to give CO2, water and energy takes place in the: (a) mitochondria (b) cytoplasm (c) chloroplast (d) nucleus

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) mitochondria. Glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate) occurs in the cytoplasm; the further oxidation of pyruvate is mitochondrial.

MCQM41 mark

4. Which of the following gives the correct path of food in the human alimentary canal? (a) mouth → stomach → oesophagus → small intestine → large intestine (b) mouth → oesophagus → small intestine → stomach → large intestine (c) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → large intestine → small intestine (d) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. Food is swallowed into the oesophagus, digested in the stomach, absorbed in the small intestine, and the undigested part passes to the large intestine.

MCQM51 mark

5. The opening and closing of stomata is controlled by: (a) epidermal cells (b) mesophyll cells (c) xylem vessels (d) guard cells

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) guard cells. Guard cells swell or shrink with water, changing the size of the stomatal pore.

MCQM61 mark

6. Photosynthesis in a leaf takes place inside the: (a) mitochondria (b) chloroplast (c) ribosome (d) vacuole

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) chloroplast. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which of these is an autotroph? (a) mushroom (b) Cuscuta (c) green plant (d) human being

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) green plant. It prepares its own food by photosynthesis; Cuscuta is a parasite and fungi are saprotrophs.

MCQM81 mark

8. The main nitrogenous waste filtered out by the kidneys is: (a) carbon dioxide (b) urea (c) glucose (d) glycogen

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) urea. It is filtered out of blood in the nephrons along with water and salts to form urine.

Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. The gap between two neurons is called: (a) synapse (b) dendrite (c) axon (d) impulse

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) synapse — the junction where a neurotransmitter carries the signal across.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which plant hormone is a growth inhibitor? (a) auxin (b) gibberellin (c) cytokinin (d) abscisic acid

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) abscisic acid — it inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves and the closing of stomata.

MCQM31 mark

3. The part of the brain that maintains the posture and balance of the body is: (a) cerebrum (b) pons (c) cerebellum (d) medulla

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) cerebellum. It also makes voluntary movements precise; alcohol affects it, which is why a drunk person staggers.

MCQM41 mark

4. Which part of the brain controls involuntary actions such as blood pressure, salivation and vomiting? (a) cerebrum (b) cerebellum (c) pituitary (d) medulla

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) medulla. The medulla in the hindbrain controls involuntary actions; the cerebellum controls posture and balance.

MCQM51 mark

5. The hormone that prepares the body for “fight or flight” is: (a) insulin (b) adrenaline (c) thyroxin (d) growth hormone

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) adrenaline. Adrenaline from the adrenal gland raises heart rate and breathing rate and diverts blood to the muscles.

MCQM61 mark

6. The hormone that regulates the body's metabolic rate is: (a) thyroxine (b) insulin (c) growth hormone (d) adrenaline

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) thyroxine. Secreted by the thyroid gland; iodine is needed to make it, and its deficiency causes goitre.

MCQM71 mark

7. The part of a neuron that receives the impulse is the: (a) axon (b) cell body (c) dendrite (d) myelin sheath

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) dendrite. Dendrites receive signals; the axon carries the impulse away towards the next neuron.

MCQM81 mark

8. In a reflex action the impulse is processed in the: (a) cerebrum (b) spinal cord (c) cerebellum (d) medulla only

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) spinal cord. Because the brain is not involved in the decision, the response is quick and automatic.

Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. Binary fission in amoeba begins with the division of the: (a) cytoplasm (b) cell membrane (c) nucleus (d) pseudopodia

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) nucleus. The nucleus divides first, followed by the division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells.

MCQM21 mark

2. The anther contains: (a) sepals (b) ovules (c) carpel (d) pollen grains

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) pollen grains. The anther is the male reproductive part; ovules are inside the ovary.

MCQM31 mark

3. Which of the following is a barrier method of contraception? (a) condom (b) oral pill (c) copper-T (d) vasectomy

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) condom. It physically prevents the sperm from meeting the egg and also reduces the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.

MCQM41 mark

4. In human females, the embryo implants in the: (a) fallopian tube (b) ovary (c) uterus (d) vagina

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) uterus. The zygote develops into an embryo that embeds in the thick, blood-rich lining of the uterus.

MCQM51 mark

5. Spirogyra reproduces asexually by: (a) budding (b) fragmentation (c) spore formation (d) regeneration

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) fragmentation. A Spirogyra filament breaks into pieces, and each piece grows into a new filament.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is an asexual mode of reproduction? (a) budding in yeast (b) pollination (c) fertilisation (d) seed germination

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) budding in yeast. Asexual modes include budding, binary fission, fragmentation, spore formation and vegetative propagation.

MCQM71 mark

7. Sperms are produced in the: (a) penis (b) vas deferens (c) seminal vesicles (d) testes

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) testes. They are formed in the seminiferous tubules, where the temperature is about 2–3 °C below body temperature.

MCQM81 mark

8. Sterilisation in human beings means: (a) preventing ovulation with pills (b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts (c) killing germs (d) using a condom

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts. Vasectomy in males and tubectomy in females prevent gametes from meeting.

Ch 8 · Heredity (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. In a monohybrid cross between a tall (TT) and a dwarf (tt) pea plant, the F2 phenotypic ratio is: (a) 1:1 (b) 9:3:3:1 (c) 3:1 (d) 1:2:1

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 3 tall : 1 dwarf. 1:2:1 is the genotypic ratio of the same cross.

MCQM21 mark

2. The sex of a human child is determined by: (a) the father's sperm (b) the mother's egg (c) both parents equally (d) the diet of the mother

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the father's sperm. Eggs always carry X; a sperm carrying Y produces a boy and one carrying X produces a girl.

MCQM31 mark

3. A cross between F1 hybrid tall plants (Tt × Tt) gives dwarf plants in F2. The expected ratio of tall to dwarf is: (a) 1:3 (b) 3:1 (c) 1:1 (d) 2:2

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 3:1 — 25% of F2 are homozygous dwarf (tt).

MCQM41 mark

4. In a cross between two plants with genotype Tt × Tt, the proportion of the progeny with genotype Tt is: (a) 1/4 (b) 3/4 (c) 1 (d) 1/2

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1/2. The Punnett square gives TT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1, so Tt makes up 2 of 4, i.e. 1/2.

MCQM51 mark

5. Mendel chose the garden pea for his experiments mainly because it: (a) has easily visible contrasting traits and a short life cycle (b) has no variation among its plants (c) cannot be self-pollinated (d) produces only one seed per plant

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) has easily visible contrasting traits and a short life cycle. Pea plants have clear contrasting traits (tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled), grow quickly, and can be both self- and cross-pollinated.

MCQM61 mark

6. The phenotypic ratio obtained in a dihybrid cross between two F₁ hybrids is: (a) 3 : 1 (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 1 : 2 : 1

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Mendel obtained this for two independently assorting traits, such as seed shape and seed colour.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which of these traits can be inherited? (a) the cut tail of a mouse (b) large muscles built by exercise (c) eye colour (d) a scar

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.

MCQM81 mark

8. The number of chromosomes in a human zygote is: (a) 23 (b) 92 (c) 22 (d) 46

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 46. Each gamete carries 23 chromosomes and their fusion restores the 23 pairs.

Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The magnification produced by a rear-view mirror fitted in vehicles is: (a) less than one (b) more than one (c) equal to one (d) more than one or less than one depending on the position of the object

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) less than one. A convex mirror always forms a diminished, virtual and erect image.

MCQM21 mark

2. Where should an object be placed in front of a convex lens to get a real image of the same size? (a) at F (b) at infinity (c) at 2F (d) between F and 2F

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.

MCQM31 mark

3. The refractive index of water is 1.33. The speed of light in water is: (a) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (b) 1.33 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 4 × 10⁸ m/s (d) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.33.

MCQM41 mark

4. A concave mirror of focal length 15 cm forms a real image of the same size as the object. The object is placed at: (a) 15 cm from the mirror (b) 30 cm from the mirror (c) 45 cm from the mirror (d) 7.5 cm from the mirror

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 30 cm from the mirror. A real image of the same size is formed when the object is at the centre of curvature, C = 2f = 30 cm.

MCQM51 mark

5. The lens formula is: (a) 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (b) 1/u − 1/v = 1/f (c) v − u = f (d) 1/v − 1/u = 1/f

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. With the Cartesian sign convention, the lens formula is 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; the mirror formula has + between 1/v and 1/u.

MCQM61 mark

6. The power of a convex lens of focal length 50 cm is: (a) −2 D (b) +2 D (c) +0.5 D (d) −0.5 D

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) +2 D. P = 1/f in metres = 1/0.5 = +2 D; a converging lens always has positive power.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which mirror always forms an erect and diminished image? (a) plane (b) concave (c) parabolic (d) convex

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.

MCQM81 mark

8. When a ray of light goes from glass into air, it: (a) bends towards the normal (b) bends away from the normal (c) does not bend (d) is totally absorbed

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.

MCQM91 mark

9. An object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. The image is formed: (a) 10 cm in front of the mirror (b) 20 cm behind the mirror (c) 20 cm in front of the mirror (d) at infinity

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.

MCQM101 mark

10. A ray of light enters a denser medium along the normal (angle of incidence 0°). It: (a) passes undeviated (b) bends towards the normal (c) bends away from the normal (d) is totally reflected

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) passes undeviated. Refraction changes the speed but there is no bending when the ray is along the normal, because sin 0° = 0 on both sides of Snell's law.

MCQM111 mark

11. The image formed by a concave lens of a real object is always: (a) real and inverted (b) virtual, erect and magnified (c) virtual, erect and diminished (d) real and magnified

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.

Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The change in focal length of the eye lens is caused by the action of the: (a) pupil (b) retina (c) iris (d) ciliary muscles

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) ciliary muscles — they change the curvature of the crystalline lens, giving the eye its power of accommodation.

MCQM21 mark

2. The danger signals installed at the top of tall buildings are red because red light: (a) is scattered the most by smoke or fog (b) is scattered the least by smoke or fog (c) is absorbed the most (d) moves fastest in air

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.

MCQM31 mark

3. A person cannot read a newspaper without glasses. He is probably suffering from: (a) myopia (b) astigmatism (c) hypermetropia (d) night blindness

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.

MCQM41 mark

4. The eye defect hypermetropia is corrected by using a: (a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) cylindrical lens (d) bifocal lens with a concave upper part

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) convex lens. In hypermetropia the image forms behind the retina, so a converging (convex) lens is used.

MCQM51 mark

5. The part of the eye that contains the light-sensitive cells is the: (a) retina (b) cornea (c) iris (d) pupil

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) retina. The retina holds the rods and cones that convert light into electrical signals.

MCQM61 mark

6. The eye defect in which the far point is nearer than infinity is: (a) hypermetropia (b) myopia (c) presbyopia (d) cataract

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.

MCQM71 mark

7. The splitting of white light into its component colours is called: (a) dispersion (b) scattering (c) total internal reflection (d) polarisation

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) dispersion. Different wavelengths bend by different amounts inside a prism, so the colours separate.

MCQM81 mark

8. Stars twinkle mainly because of: (a) dispersion (b) reflection from clouds (c) their own flickering (d) atmospheric refraction

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.

MCQM91 mark

9. The least distance of distinct vision for a young adult with normal vision is about: (a) 2.5 cm (b) 25 m (c) 25 cm (d) infinity

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 25 cm. This is the near point; the far point of a normal eye is at infinity.

MCQM101 mark

10. The clear sky appears blue because: (a) blue light is absorbed the most (b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules (c) the sea reflects blue light upwards (d) the atmosphere refracts blue light towards the eye

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules. Fine particles scatter blue light far more than red, so scattered blue reaches the eye from every direction.

MCQM111 mark

11. A student with myopia is prescribed a lens of power −2.0 D. The lens is: (a) convex, focal length 50 cm (b) concave, focal length 2 m (c) concave, focal length 50 cm (d) convex, focal length 2 m

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) concave, focal length 50 cm. f = 1/P = 1/(−2) m = −0.5 m; the negative sign means a concave (diverging) lens, which corrects myopia.

Ch 11 · Electricity (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The least resistance that can be obtained by combining 2 Ω, 4 Ω, 1 Ω and 100 Ω is: (a) more than 100 Ω (b) less than 1 Ω (c) 107 Ω (d) between 2 Ω and 100 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.

MCQM21 mark

2. Two wires of the same material and length but different cross-sectional areas are compared. The thicker wire has: (a) more resistance (b) the same resistance (c) less resistance (d) no resistance

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) less resistance, since R = ρl/A and a larger area of cross-section reduces the resistance.

MCQM31 mark

3. The commercial unit of electrical energy is: (a) joule (b) watt (c) volt (d) kilowatt-hour

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) kilowatt-hour (kWh), equal to 3.6 × 10⁶ J, and commonly called a 'unit'.

MCQM41 mark

4. The electric power consumed by a resistor R connected to a potential difference V is given by: (a) P = V²/R (b) P = VR² (c) P = R/V² (d) P = V/R²

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) P = V²/R. P = VI and I = V/R, so P = V × (V/R) = V²/R.

MCQM51 mark

5. The SI unit of resistivity is: (a) ohm (Ω) (b) ohm per metre (Ω/m) (c) siemens (S) (d) ohm metre (Ω m)

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) ohm metre (Ω m). From R = ρl/A, ρ = RA/l, whose unit is Ω × m²/m = Ω m.

MCQM61 mark

6. The resistance of a wire doubles when: (a) its area of cross-section is doubled (b) its length is doubled (c) its length is halved (d) its diameter is doubled

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.

MCQM71 mark

7. One kilowatt hour equals: (a) 3.6 × 10³ J (b) 360 J (c) 6 × 10µ J (d) 3.6 × 10⁶ J

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J.

MCQM81 mark

8. An ammeter is connected in a circuit: (a) in series (b) in parallel (c) across the supply (d) either way

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) in series. It has very low resistance so it does not change the current; a voltmeter is connected in parallel.

MCQM91 mark

9. A 12 V battery drives a current of 2 A through a resistor. The resistance of the resistor is: (a) 6 Ω (b) 24 Ω (c) 0.17 Ω (d) 14 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.

MCQM101 mark

10. Three 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is: (a) 18 Ω (b) 6 Ω (c) 2 Ω (d) 3 Ω

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 2 Ω. 1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6, so R = 2 Ω — equal resistors in parallel give R/n.

MCQM111 mark

11. A 100 W bulb is used for 10 hours every day. The electrical energy it consumes in 30 days is: (a) 3 kWh (b) 30 kWh (c) 300 kWh (d) 3000 kWh

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.

Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The direction of the magnetic field produced by a straight current-carrying wire is given by: (a) Fleming's left-hand rule (b) Fleming's right-hand rule (c) Lenz's law (d) the right-hand thumb rule

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.

MCQM21 mark

2. The essential difference between an AC generator and a DC generator is: (a) AC generator has an electromagnet (b) DC generator has a commutator (c) AC generator has slip rings while DC generator has a commutator (d) AC generator produces more power

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). The slip rings of an AC generator allow the output to reverse, while the split-ring commutator of a DC generator keeps the external current in one direction.

MCQM31 mark

3. The frequency of AC supply in India is 50 Hz. The direction of the current changes: (a) 50 times per second (b) 25 times per second (c) 200 times per second (d) 100 times per second

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 100 times per second — the direction reverses twice in each cycle.

MCQM41 mark

4. Outside a bar magnet, the magnetic field lines run: (a) from the south pole to the north pole (b) in straight lines parallel to the axis only (c) from the centre to both poles (d) from the north pole to the south pole

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) from the north pole to the south pole. By convention the field lines emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole outside the magnet, and continue from south to north inside it.

MCQM51 mark

5. A straight conductor carrying current is placed in a magnetic field. The force on it is greatest when the current is: (a) parallel to the field (b) at 45° to the field (c) perpendicular to the field (d) zero

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) perpendicular to the field. The force F = BIl sin θ is maximum at θ = 90°, and zero when the conductor is parallel to the field.

MCQM61 mark

6. The magnetic field lines inside a long current-carrying solenoid are: (a) concentric circles (b) parallel straight lines (c) diverging curves (d) absent

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.

MCQM71 mark

7. An electric motor converts: (a) electrical energy into mechanical energy (b) mechanical energy into electrical energy (c) heat into light (d) chemical energy into sound

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator does the reverse conversion.

MCQM81 mark

8. The insulation on the earth wire in a domestic circuit is: (a) green (b) red (c) black (d) blue

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) green. Live is red or brown, neutral is black or blue, and earth is green or green-yellow.

MCQM91 mark

9. The magnetic field at a point near a long straight current-carrying wire: (a) increases as you move away from the wire (b) does not depend on the current (c) decreases as you move away from the wire (d) is zero everywhere around the wire

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) decreases as you move away from the wire. The concentric field lines get farther apart with distance; the field is stronger for a larger current and closer to the wire.

MCQM101 mark

10. Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction of the: (a) induced current in a coil (b) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (c) magnetic field due to a current (d) current produced by a generator

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.

MCQM111 mark

11. The production of a current in a closed coil by a changing magnetic field is called: (a) electromagnetic induction (b) magnetic effect of current (c) electrolysis (d) electrostatic induction

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) electromagnetic induction. Faraday's discovery — the basis of the electric generator; the induced current direction is given by Fleming's right-hand rule.

(b) 100 J. Grass 10,000 J → deer 1,000 J → lion 100 J (10% at each transfer).

(b) a plastic bag. Microorganisms cannot break down plastics, so they persist in the environment for a very long time.

(c) frog. The grasshopper is the primary consumer and the frog, which eats it, is the secondary consumer.

Ans:

(a) energy. Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is stored in the next; the rest is lost as heat and in life processes.

(c) sunlight. Coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels that take millions of years to form.
