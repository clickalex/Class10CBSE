Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. The reaction Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe is an example of: (a) combination (b) decomposition (c) displacement (d) double displacement

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which of the following is an endothermic process? (a) respiration (b) decomposition of vegetable matter into compost (c) process of decomposition of ferrous sulphate (d) quicklime + water

Show Answer

Ans:

(c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.

MCQM31 mark

3. The green coating on copper and the black coating on silver are respectively: (a) basic copper carbonate and Ag2S (b) CuO and Ag2O (c) CuCO3 and AgS2 (d) Cu(OH)2 and AgO

Show Answer

Ans:

(a). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Chemical Reactions and Equations” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Balancing, the five reaction types, corrosion and rancidity..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Chemical Reactions and Equations” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is a combination reaction? (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (b) 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (c) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (d) AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.

MCQM71 mark

7. The brown fumes evolved when lead nitrate is heated are of: (a) NO₂ (b) O₂ (c) NO (d) PbO

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) NO₂. 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂; nitrogen dioxide is brown, the residue lead oxide is yellow.

MCQM81 mark

8. Rancidity of fat-containing food is prevented by: (a) storing it in the open (b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen (c) adding oxidising agents (d) heating it repeatedly

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.

MCQM91 mark

9. When the equation Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂ is balanced, the coefficient of steam (H₂O) is: (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.

MCQM101 mark

10. A shiny brown element X becomes black when heated in air. X and the black compound are: (a) Cu and CuO (b) Fe and Fe₂O₃ (c) Ag and Ag₂S (d) Al and Al₂O₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Cu and CuO. Copper is reddish-brown and forms black copper(II) oxide on heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.

MCQM111 mark

11. The reaction 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂ used in black-and-white photography is a: (a) thermal decomposition (b) photochemical decomposition (c) displacement (d) combination reaction

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.

Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. A solution reacts with crushed egg-shell to give a gas that turns lime water milky. The solution contains: (a) NaCl (b) HCl (c) LiCl (d) KCl

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which one is a strong base? (a) NH4OH (b) Mg(OH)2 (c) NaOH (d) Cu(OH)2

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) NaOH. Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases and ionise completely; the others are weak.

MCQM31 mark

3. The pH of gastric juice released during digestion is: (a) less than 7 (b) more than 7 (c) equal to 7 (d) equal to 0

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Acids, Bases and Salts” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is pH scale, indicators, and the four commercially important salts..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Acids, Bases and Salts” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

MCQM61 mark

6. A solution turns colourless phenolphthalein pink. Its pH is likely to be: (a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 10

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 10. Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution, whose pH is above 7.

MCQM71 mark

7. The salt used to remove permanent hardness of water is: (a) sodium chloride (b) sodium carbonate (c) calcium sulphate (d) ammonium chloride

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.

MCQM81 mark

8. Dilute acid added to an active metal produces: (a) oxygen (b) hydrogen (c) carbon dioxide (d) nitrogen

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) hydrogen. Metal + dilute acid → salt + H₂, which burns with a pop sound.

MCQM91 mark

9. The chemical formula of Plaster of Paris is: (a) CaSO₄·2H₂O (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O (c) CaSO₄ (d) CaCO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.

MCQM101 mark

10. Baking soda is: (a) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (b) NaHCO₃ (c) NaOH (d) Na₂CO₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.

MCQM111 mark

11. Bleaching powder is manufactured by passing chlorine gas over: (a) dry slaked lime (b) quicklime (c) limestone (d) gypsum

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) dry slaked lime. Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O. Quicklime is CaO, limestone is CaCO₃, gypsum is a sulphate.

Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (11)

MCQM11 mark

1. A copper coin placed in silver nitrate solution turns silvery and the solution turns blue. This shows copper is: (a) less reactive than silver (b) more reactive than silver (c) equally reactive (d) an inert metal

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which pair is amphoteric? (a) Na2O and CaO (b) Al2O3 and ZnO (c) CO2 and SO2 (d) CuO and FeO

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Al2O3 and ZnO — they react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.

MCQM31 mark

3. The method used to extract sodium from molten NaCl is: (a) reduction with carbon (b) self reduction (c) electrolytic reduction (d) roasting

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Metals and Non-metals” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Physical properties, the reactivity series and extraction of metals..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Metals and Non-metals” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. The best conductor of electricity among these is: (a) copper (b) silver (c) aluminium (d) iron

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) silver. Silver conducts best, then copper; silver is too costly for wiring, so copper is used.

MCQM71 mark

7. Brass is an alloy of: (a) copper and tin (b) copper and zinc (c) lead and tin (d) mercury and zinc

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) copper and zinc. Bronze is copper and tin, solder is lead and tin, and an amalgam contains mercury.

MCQM81 mark

8. Which metal reacts vigorously with cold water? (a) iron (b) zinc (c) sodium (d) copper

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) sodium. It reacts violently with cold water; iron and zinc react only with steam, and copper does not react at all.

MCQM91 mark

9. The correct decreasing order of reactivity is: (a) Zn > Mg > Cu (b) Mg > Zn > Cu (c) Cu > Zn > Mg (d) Zn > Cu > Mg

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Mg > Zn > Cu. In the reactivity series magnesium lies above zinc, and copper lies below hydrogen, so it is the least reactive of the three.

MCQM101 mark

10. Galvanisation protects iron from rusting by coating it with a thin layer of: (a) tin (b) chromium (c) zinc (d) copper

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference to the iron even if the coating is scratched.

MCQM111 mark

11. Magnesium (2, 8, 2) combines with chlorine (2, 8, 7) to form an ionic compound with the formula: (a) MgCl (b) MgCl₂ (c) Mg₂Cl (d) MgCl₃

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which compound decolourises alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent)? (a) C₂H₆ (b) C₂H₄ (c) CH₄ (d) C₃H₈

Show Answer

Ans:

MCQM71 mark

7. The functional group present in an alcohol is: (a) –CHO (b) –COOH (c) –OH (d) –CO–

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.

MCQM81 mark

8. The gas evolved when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate is: (a) H₂ (b) O₂ (c) CO₂ (d) CO

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) CO₂. 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂; the gas turns lime water milky.

MCQM91 mark

9. The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH₂–CHO is: (a) propanol (b) propanal (c) propanone (d) propanoic acid

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) propanal. Three carbons (prop-) with an aldehyde group –CHO (-al). Propanone is a ketone, propanol an alcohol, propanoic acid a carboxylic acid.

MCQM101 mark

10. Which pair belongs to the same homologous series? (a) CH₄ and C₂H₄ (b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈ (c) C₂H₄ and C₂H₂ (d) CH₃OH and CH₃COOH

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.

MCQM111 mark

11. Which hydrocarbon undergoes an addition reaction with hydrogen in the presence of a nickel catalyst? (a) C₃H₈ (b) C₂H₆ (c) C₄H₁₀ (d) C₂H₄

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) C₂H₄. Only an unsaturated hydrocarbon (ethene, with a C=C double bond) can add hydrogen; the other three are saturated alkanes.

MCQM71 mark

MCQM81 mark

(c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.

MCQM81 mark

8. When a ray of light goes from glass into air, it: (a) bends towards the normal (b) bends away from the normal (c) does not bend (d) is totally absorbed

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.

MCQM91 mark

9. An object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. The image is formed: (a) 10 cm in front of the mirror (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror (c) 20 cm behind the mirror (d) at infinity

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.

MCQM101 mark

10. A ray of light enters a denser medium along the normal (angle of incidence 0°). It: (a) bends towards the normal (b) bends away from the normal (c) passes undeviated (d) is totally reflected

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) passes undeviated. Refraction changes the speed but there is no bending when the ray is along the normal, because sin 0° = 0 on both sides of Snell's law.

MCQM111 mark

11. The image formed by a concave lens of a real object is always: (a) real and inverted (b) virtual, erect and diminished (c) virtual, erect and magnified (d) real and magnified

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.
