🎯 Marks lens

4–5 marks. Almost always a labelled diagram (human respiratory or excretory system, or the nephron) plus one 3-mark reasoning question.
Case-based questions love the 10% energy transfer or the transpiration pull idea, and the difference between aerobic and anaerobic respiration appears nearly every year.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Maintenance. Living organisms constantly repair and renew their structures using energy from outside. The processes that maintain life are nutrition, respiration, transport and excretion. Growth and movement are not by themselves proof of life — a breathing person in a coma is alive; a growing crystal is not.
2. Nutrition. Autotrophic (green plants: 6CO2 + 12H2O →(chlorophyll, sunlight) C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O — three events: absorption of light by chlorophyll, conversion of light energy to chemical energy with the splitting of water, and reduction of CO2 to carbohydrate). Heterotrophic: holozoic (amoeba — pseudopodia, food vacuole), parasitic (Cuscuta, ticks, tapeworm), saprophytic (fungi, yeast).
3. Nutrition in human beings. Alimentary canal: mouth (salivary amylase starts starch digestion) → oesophagus (peristaltic movements) → stomach (HCl kills germs, pepsin digests protein in acidic medium, mucus protects the lining) → small intestine (bile from the liver emulsifies fat; trypsin acts on protein, lipase on fat; villi increase absorption area) → large intestine (water absorbed) → anus. Ruminants have a caecum for cellulose; amoeba uses pseudopodia.
4. Respiration. Aerobic (in mitochondria): glucose → pyruvate → CO2 + H2O + a large amount of energy. Anaerobic in yeast: glucose → ethanol + CO2 + less energy (fermentation). In our muscles when oxygen is scarce: glucose → lactic acid + energy, which causes cramps. Fish have gills with a large surface area; the rate of breathing in aquatic animals is much faster because dissolved oxygen is low.
5. Transport in human beings. Double circulation — blood passes through the heart twice in one cycle. Right auricle ← deoxygenated blood from the body (vena cava); right ventricle → lungs (pulmonary artery); left auricle ← oxygenated blood (pulmonary vein); left ventricle → body (aorta). Arteries have thick elastic walls and carry blood away from the heart; veins have valves and thin walls; capillaries are one cell thick for exchange. Platelets clot blood. In plants, xylem carries water and minerals upward (driven by root pressure at night and transpiration pull by day); phloem carries food by translocation using ATP, in both directions.
6. Excretion. The pair of kidneys contain nephrons — the functional units: Bowman's capsule with the glomerulus filters the blood under pressure (ultrafiltration), then useful glucose, amino acids, salts and most water are reabsorbed in the tubule, leaving urine (urea, uric acid, excess water) in the collecting duct. Artificial removal of nitrogenous waste is haemodialysis (dialyser). Plants excrete through oxygen release, transpiration, shedding leaves, resins and gums, and storage in cellular vacuoles.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • 6CO2 + 12H2O →(chlorophyll, sunlight) C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O
  • C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy (aerobic)
  • C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2 + energy (yeast)
  • C6H12O6 → 2 lactic acid + energy (muscle)

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Four systems, four words: Eat (nutrition), Burn (respiration), Carry (transport), Throw out (excretion).
  • Artery = Away, thick wall; Vein = in, Valves. The pulmonary artery and vein are the exceptions to the 'oxygenated blood' rule.
  • Xylem = water = one direction (up); Phloem = food = two directions. Both are living except xylem vessels and tracheids.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing the photosynthesis equation with 6H2O on the left. NCERT uses 12H2O.
  • Saying bile contains enzymes. Bile contains no digestive enzyme — it only emulsifies fat and makes the medium alkaline.
  • Labelling the diagram wrong: the left side of the heart carries oxygenated blood (drawn on the right of your page in most diagrams).
  • Confusing transpiration pull with root pressure — root pressure matters at night; transpiration pull is the main daytime force.

❓ Exam Q&A

1 marks Name the functional unit of the kidney and the pigment that absorbs light energy in plants.

Nephron; chlorophyll.

3 marks Why is the rate of breathing in aquatic organisms much faster than in terrestrial organisms?

Because the amount of dissolved oxygen in water is far lower than the oxygen in air. Aquatic animals must therefore pass a larger volume of water over their gills per unit time to obtain enough oxygen, which makes the breathing rate faster.

3 marks What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? Name two organisms that use anaerobic respiration.

Aerobic occurs in the presence of oxygen, in the mitochondria, giving CO2 and H2O and a large amount of energy.
Anaerobic occurs without oxygen in the cytoplasm, giving ethanol and CO2 (yeast) or lactic acid (muscles), and much less energy.
Organisms: yeast, some bacteria and parasitic worms such as Taenia; human muscle cells respire anaerobically temporarily.

3 marks Draw a labelled diagram of the human excretory system, or describe the working of a nephron.

Kidneys → ureters → urinary bladder → urethra.
Nephron: blood enters the glomerulus under pressure inside Bowman's capsule, where water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea are filtered into the tubule (ultrafiltration).
As the filtrate moves down the tubule, useful substances and most water are selectively reabsorbed; the remaining fluid, now urine, collects in the collecting duct and passes into the ureter.

5 marks Explain the process of nutrition in human beings, from ingestion to egestion.

Mouth: teeth chew the food; salivary amylase begins the breakdown of starch into simpler sugars.
Oesophagus: peristaltic movements push the food to the stomach.
Stomach: HCl makes the medium acidic for pepsin to digest proteins, kills germs, and mucus protects the stomach wall.
Small intestine: the site of complete digestion — bile from the liver emulsifies fat, pancreatic trypsin acts on proteins, lipase on fats, and intestinal enzymes convert carbohydrates to glucose and proteins to amino acids; the finger-like villi absorb the digested food into the blood.
Large intestine and anus: water is absorbed from the undigested food and the waste is egested.

2 marks Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like human beings?

Because their cells are not all in direct contact with the environment, and the volume of the body is large, so the distance oxygen would have to diffuse is too great. Diffusion is too slow to supply every cell, which is why special respiratory and transport systems are needed.
