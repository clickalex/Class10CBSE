Chapter 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations

🎯 Marks lens

3–4 marks every year, usually as one balancing question, one reaction-type identification and one reasoning question on corrosion or rancidity.
Case-based questions open with a laboratory observation — a colour change, a gas, a precipitate — and ask you to name the reaction type and write the equation.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Recognising a chemical reaction. Four observable clues: change of state, change of colour, evolution of a gas, and change in temperature. If a new substance with new properties forms, a chemical reaction has taken place.
2. From word equation to balanced equation. Write the word equation, substitute formulas to get the skeletal equation, then balance atoms on both sides. Balancing is the direct application of the law of conservation of mass — mass can neither be created nor destroyed.

3. Decomposition reactions always need energy — heat (thermal: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2), electricity (electrolytic: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2) or light (photolytic: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 in sunlight, which is why silver salts are used in photography).
4. Oxidation and reduction travel together. Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen = oxidation; loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen = reduction. In CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, CuO is reduced and H2 is oxidised; the substance supplying oxygen is the oxidising agent.
5. The two everyday reactions the board loves. Corrosion — metals are slowly eaten away by moisture and acids; rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe2O3·xH2O, silver turns black (Ag2S) and copper turns green (basic copper carbonate). Rancidity — fats and oils oxidise and smell; prevented by antioxidants, airtight containers, refrigeration, and flushing chip packets with nitrogen.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Fe2O3 + 3H2 → 2Fe + 3H2O (displacement / reduction)
  • 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br2 (photolytic decomposition)
  • CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + heat (exothermic combination)
  • Pb(NO3)2 + 2KI → PbI2↓ + 2KNO3 (double displacement, yellow precipitate)
  • Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2↑

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Balancing order: start with the element that appears in the largest number of atoms, leave free elements (O2, H2) for last, then re-check oxygen.
  • Type in one look: 2 reactants → 1 product = combination; 1 → 2 = decomposition; element + compound → element + compound = displacement; two ionic compounds swapping partners = double displacement.
  • Rancidity answer = nitrogen + airtight + antioxidant. Any two of these three score full marks.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Changing a subscript to balance (H2O → H2O2). Only the coefficient in front of a formula may be changed.
  • Omitting state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq) when the question asks for a complete equation.
  • Writing rust as plain Fe2O3. The marking scheme expects hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe2O3·xH2O.
  • Naming only the substance oxidised when the question asks for the oxidising and reducing agents.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Why must a chemical equation be balanced?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because of the law of conservation of mass — the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides, since atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Balance: HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + H2O

Show Answer

Ans:

2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O. This is also a neutralisation, hence a double displacement reaction.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why is nitrogen flushed into bags of potato chips?

Show Answer

Ans:

Nitrogen is an unreactive (inert) gas. It displaces oxygen from the packet, so the fats and oils cannot be oxidised, which prevents rancidity and keeps the chips from changing smell and taste.

LONGL44 marks

4. In the reaction ZnO + C → Zn + CO, identify the substance oxidised and the substance reduced.

Show Answer

Ans:

Carbon is oxidised (it gains oxygen to form CO) and zinc oxide is reduced (it loses oxygen to form Zn). Carbon is the reducing agent; ZnO is the oxidising agent.

LONGL55 marks

5. A white salt on heating decomposes to give brown fumes and a residue. Name the salt and write the equation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lead nitrate. The brown fumes are nitrogen dioxide. 2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2↑ + O2↑ — a thermal decomposition reaction.
