Chapter 13 · Our Environment

🎯 Marks lens

3–5 marks. A food-chain question with the 10% energy calculation, one on ozone depletion, and one on biodegradable waste or the two environmental problems.
Case-based questions give a food chain with an energy value at one level and ask you to work out the others.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Ecosystem. All the living organisms (biotic) in an area together with the non-living (abiotic) components — temperature, rainfall, wind, soil and minerals — form an ecosystem. Producers (green plants and blue-green algae) make their own food; consumers depend on them (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites); decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead organic matter and return nutrients to the soil. A pond and a forest are natural ecosystems; a crop field and an aquarium are artificial ones.
2. Food chain and trophic levels. A food chain shows who eats whom for the transfer of food energy: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk. Each step is a trophic level; producers occupy the first level. Energy flow in a food chain is always unidirectional — it passes from one level to the next and is never returned to the sun. A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.
3. The 10% law. Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next; the remaining 90% is lost as heat, used in metabolism, or left in undigested matter. So if producers have 10,000 J, the herbivore gets 1,000 J, the next level 100 J, and the top carnivore only 10 J. This is why food chains rarely have more than four levels, and why a vegetarian diet supports a larger population.
4. Biological magnification. Harmful chemicals such as DDT and mercury are non-biodegradable and accumulate at each trophic level, so their concentration is maximum in the top carnivore — including human beings, who sit at the top of many food chains.
5. Ozone layer depletion. Ozone (O3) in the upper atmosphere shields the earth from harmful ultraviolet radiation. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), used in refrigerators and air conditioners and in fire extinguishers, destroy ozone molecules, thinning the layer. Increased UV causes skin cancer and damage to crops and marine life. The Montreal Protocol (1987) froze CFC production at 1986 levels to protect the ozone layer.
6. Waste management. Biodegradable waste (paper, vegetable peels, wood) is broken down by decomposers; non-biodegradable waste (plastic, glass, DDT, aluminium) is not. Problems caused by non-biodegradable waste: it does not decompose, pollutes soil and water, blocks drainage, and can enter food chains and biomagnify. Even biodegradable waste in excess causes foul smell, breeds mosquitoes and disease, and produces gases. Methods of disposal: segregating waste, recycling (paper, plastic, glass, metal), composting and vermicomposting, sewage treatment before releasing water into rivers, and burying waste in sanitary landfills. The 5 R's: Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose, Recycle.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Energy at level n+1 = 10% of energy at level n
  • O3 formation: O2 + O →(UV) O3 ; O3 + CFC → destruction of ozone

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 10% calculation: move the decimal one place to the left at each level going up, and one place to the right going down.
  • Biomagnification = maximum at the top. If the question asks which organism has the highest concentration, pick the top carnivore.
  • Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable is decided by whether micro-organisms can break it down — not by whether it looks natural.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Drawing arrows in a food chain from predator to prey. Arrows always point toward the organism that eats — i.e. in the direction of energy flow.
  • Applying 10% in the wrong direction. Going down the chain, multiply by 10 at each step, not by 0.1.
  • Saying the ozone layer protects us from infrared radiation. It is ultraviolet radiation.
  • Confusing biological magnification with biodegradability — they are different concepts entirely.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name the organisms that always occupy the first trophic level of a food chain, and define an ecosystem.

Show Answer

Ans:

Green plants and blue-green algae (producers). An ecosystem is a self-sustaining system in which all the living organisms of an area interact with each other and with the non-living (abiotic) components such as soil, water and air.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why are some substances biodegradable and some non-biodegradable?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because micro-organisms such as bacteria and fungi produce enzymes that can break down only certain substances like paper, wood and vegetable waste. Substances such as plastic, glass and DDT cannot be acted upon by these enzymes, so they remain in the environment for a very long time and are called non-biodegradable.

SHORTS33 marks

3. In a food chain, if 10,000 joules of solar energy is available to the producers, how much energy will be available to the secondary consumer?

Show Answer

Ans:

Producers convert about 1% of the solar energy into food energy, but taking 10,000 J as the energy in the producers: primary consumer receives 10% = 1,000 J.
Secondary consumer receives 10% of 1,000 J = 100 J.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is ozone? How is it formed, and why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern?

Show Answer

Ans:

Ozone is a molecule of oxygen with three atoms, O3, found in the upper atmosphere.
Formation: ultraviolet radiation splits an oxygen molecule into free oxygen atoms, O2 →(UV) 2O; each free atom combines with an oxygen molecule to form ozone, O + O2 → O3.
Concern: the ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun. Its depletion, caused mainly by CFCs from refrigerators, air conditioners and aerosols, allows more UV to reach the earth, causing skin cancer, damage to the eyes, reduced crop yields and harm to marine organisms. The Montreal Protocol of 1987 froze CFC production to protect it.

LONGL55 marks

5. What is biological magnification? Will the level of magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?

Show Answer

Ans:

Biological magnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of non-biodegradable harmful chemicals at successive trophic levels of a food chain.
Yes, the level differs. Because these chemicals are not metabolised or excreted, they pass on to the next level, and the amount of food consumed at each level is large, so the concentration keeps increasing.
Example: DDT sprayed on crops is absorbed by plants; grasshoppers eating many plants accumulate more; frogs eating many grasshoppers more still; and hawks at the top accumulate the highest concentration. Human beings, being at the top of many food chains, are among the most affected.

SHORTS112 marks

11. Distinguish between a food chain and a food web.

Show Answer

Ans:

A food chain is a single linear sequence showing who eats whom, for example grass → grasshopper → frog → snake. A food web is a network of many interconnected food chains in an ecosystem; because each organism can feed at more than one level, the web is far more stable than a single chain.

SHORTS123 marks

12. Explain the role of decomposers in an ecosystem.

Show Answer

Ans:

Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down the dead bodies of plants and animals and their wastes into simple inorganic substances.
These substances — mineral salts, carbon dioxide and water — go back into the soil and air and are reused by the producers.
They also clean up the environment. Without decomposers nutrients would stay locked in dead matter and the cycling of matter would stop.

COMPETENCYC134 marks

13. A village panchayat plans to burn its collected plastic waste in the open. Advise them, giving reasons from what you have studied about ecosystems.

Show Answer

Ans:

Open burning of plastic releases poisonous gases such as hydrogen chloride and dioxins into the air, harming people, animals and crops.
Plastics are non-biodegradable, so if they are buried or dumped they persist in the soil for decades and choke drains and the guts of animals.
Better: segregate waste at source, send recyclable plastic for recycling, compost the biodegradable fraction, and replace single-use plastic with cloth or paper bags.
This keeps harmful chemicals out of the soil and water, so they cannot enter food chains and build up by biological magnification.

✍️ Hands-on task

Survey your own household waste for three days and sort it into biodegradable and non-biodegradable. Weigh or count each group, then list two things you could refuse or reuse that would remove the largest share of the non-biodegradable waste from the bin.
