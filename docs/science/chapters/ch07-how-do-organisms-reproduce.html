Chapter 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce?

Chapter 7 of 13. Half a page of asexual modes, half a page of the human system. Diagrams of binary fission in amoeba and the female reproductive system are standard.

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

2–3 marks. One asexual reproduction comparison and one question on the human reproductive system or contraception.
Diagrams asked: binary fission in amoeba, budding in yeast, longitudinal section of a flower, and the human female reproductive system.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Why DNA copying matters. Reproduction produces a new organism from an existing one, and the DNA in the nucleus is copied first. Copying is not perfectly accurate, so variations arise. Small variations help a species survive changing conditions; large ones may be lethal. Reproduction is therefore essential for the species, not for the survival of the individual organism.
2. Asexual reproduction. Fission — binary (amoeba, Leishmania, Paramecium: the nucleus divides first, then the cytoplasm) and multiple (Plasmodium). Fragmentation (Spirogyra breaks into pieces, each growing into a new organism). Regeneration (Planaria, Hydra — specialised cells proliferate and redifferentiate). Budding (Hydra, yeast — a small outgrowth detaches; in yeast it may stay attached and form chains). Vegetative propagation (Bryophyllum leaf buds, potato eyes, and artificial methods: cutting, layering, grafting — quick, cheap, and the new plant is a genetic copy so fruit quality is retained). Spore formation (Rhizopus — sporangia with thick walls that survive unfavourable conditions).
3. Sexual reproduction in flowering plants. Flower parts: calyx (sepals, protective), corolla (petals), androecium (stamen = anther + filament; anther contains pollen), gynocoecium (pistil = stigma, style, ovary; ovary contains ovules with egg cells). Pollination (self or cross — by wind, water, insects) is the transfer of pollen to the stigma; fertilisation is the fusion of the male gamete with the egg in the ovule. The pollen tube grows down the style. After fertilisation the ovule becomes the seed and the ovary becomes the fruit; the sepals and petals fall off.
4. The human male reproductive system. Testes (produce sperm and testosterone) lie outside the abdominal cavity in the scrotum, which keeps them 2–3 °C below body temperature for sperm formation. Vas deferens → urethra → urethral opening. Accessory glands — prostate and seminal vesicles — add fluid that nourishes the sperm and makes transport easier; this fluid plus sperm is semen.
5. The human female reproductive system. Pair of ovaries (produce ova and oestrogen), fallopian tubes/oviducts (site of fertilisation; cilia move the egg), uterus (where the embryo implants and develops), cervix, vagina. If the egg is not fertilised, the thickened uterine lining breaks down and is discharged as blood and mucus — menstruation, roughly every 28 days. After fertilisation the embryo gets nutrition from the mother's blood through the placenta, a disc embedded in the uterine wall with villi on the embryo's side.
6. Reproductive health. Contraception — mechanical barrier (condom, which also prevents sexually transmitted diseases; diaphragm, copper-T), chemical (oral pills that change the hormonal balance, so the ovum is not released), and surgical (vasectomy in males, tubectomy in females). Sexually transmitted infections — bacterial: gonorrhoea, syphilis; viral: warts, HIV-AIDS. Unwanted pregnancy and unsafe abortion lead to maternal illness and death; a healthy society needs a balance between population and resources.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • DNA + histone protein → chromosome; a gene is a segment of DNA
  • Binary fission: one cell → two identical cells (Amoeba)
  • Zygote → embryo → foetus; gestation in humans ≈ 9 months (about 280 days)
  • Vegetative propagation: cutting, layering, grafting — offspring are clones of the parent

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Decide whether the question is about asexual methods, sexual reproduction, or reproductive health — each has a fixed vocabulary.
  2. For asexual reproduction, name the method and one example: binary fission in Amoeba, budding in Hydra, fragmentation in Spirogyra, regeneration in Planaria, spore formation in Rhizopus, vegetative propagation in potato/bryophyllum.
  3. For sexual reproduction in plants, draw the flower diagram in words: sepals, petals, stamen (anther + filament), carpel (stigma, style, ovary) — then pollination, fertilisation, zygote, embryo, seed, fruit.
  4. For the human system, give the male and female parts with their two functions each — testis makes sperm and testosterone; ovary makes eggs and oestrogen/progesterone — and end with one point on contraception or menstrual hygiene.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Fission vs fragmentation vs regeneration: one cell splits = fission; body breaks into pieces = fragmentation; a cut part grows into a whole = regeneration.
  • Ovule → seed, ovary → fruit. Both start with 'o'.
  • Scrotum is outside because sperm need it cooler. That single sentence scores the 2-mark reasoning question.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying fertilisation occurs in the uterus. It occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct); implantation happens in the uterus.
  • Confusing pollination with fertilisation — pollination is only the transfer of pollen to the stigma.
  • Saying condoms protect against pregnancy only. They also reduce the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.
  • Drawing binary fission in amoeba without showing the nucleus dividing first.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Where does fertilisation occur in human beings, and which gland secretes testosterone?

Show Answer

Ans:

Fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct); testosterone is secreted by the testes.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why are the testes located outside the abdominal cavity in the scrotum?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because sperm formation requires a temperature 2–3 °C lower than the normal body temperature. The scrotum, being outside the abdomen, maintains this lower temperature.

SHORTS32 marks

3. List two advantages of vegetative propagation.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is a quick and cheap method of multiplying plants, and the new plants are genetically identical to the parent, so desirable qualities such as fruit size and taste are retained. Plants that do not produce viable seeds, like banana and rose, can also be propagated this way.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Why is variation useful to a species, and how does sexual reproduction produce it?

Show Answer

Ans:

Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two different individuals, so offspring differ from both parents. Variation means that when the environment changes or a disease strikes, some individuals are likely to survive, the population is not wiped out, and natural selection has raw material to work on.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain regeneration in Planaria with a diagram description.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a Planaria is cut into several pieces, each piece can grow into a complete organism.
Specialised cells at the wound surface proliferate (multiply) to form a mass of cells.
These cells then move to their proper positions and redifferentiate into the various tissues and organs needed, so a whole new organism develops from the fragment.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Distinguish between pollination and fertilisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same or another flower of the same species; it needs an external agent such as wind, water or insects.
Fertilisation is the fusion of the male gamete from the pollen tube with the female gamete (egg) inside the ovule, forming a zygote. It occurs inside the ovary and needs no external agent.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Describe what happens in a flower after fertilisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

The male gamete fuses with the egg to form a zygote, which develops into an embryo.
The ovule becomes the seed and its wall becomes the seed coat.
The ovary grows into the fruit, while the petals, sepals and stamens wither and fall off.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Describe the menstrual cycle and state what happens when the egg is not fertilised.

Show Answer

Ans:

Roughly once every 28 days one egg matures in an ovary and is released around day 14 (ovulation).
Meanwhile the lining of the uterus thickens and develops blood vessels to receive a fertilised egg.
If the egg is not fertilised, the thickened lining is no longer needed, breaks down, and is shed along with blood through the vagina for about three to five days — menstruation.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe the structure and working of the human female reproductive system.

Show Answer

Ans:

Ovaries — a pair of glands that produce the female gamete (ovum) and the hormone oestrogen.
Fallopian tubes (oviducts) — tubes with finger-like ends near the ovary and ciliated walls; the egg is captured here and fertilisation takes place in the tube.
Uterus — a muscular bag where the fertilised egg implants and the embryo develops.
Cervix and vagina — the cervix is the narrow opening of the uterus; the vagina is the passage through which sperm enter and the baby is delivered.
Every month the uterus prepares a thick, spongy lining to receive a fertilised egg. If no fertilisation occurs, the lining breaks down and is discharged as blood and mucus through the vagina — this is menstruation. After fertilisation, the embryo is nourished by the mother's blood through the placenta.
