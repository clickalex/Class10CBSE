🎯 Marks lens

2–3 marks. Fleming's left-hand and right-hand rules and the difference between AC and DC are asked repeatedly.
Domestic circuits and the role of the earth wire or fuse are a favourite case-based topic.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Magnetic field and field lines. A magnetic field is the region around a magnet in which its force can be detected. Field lines are closed continuous curves — outside a bar magnet they run from the north to the south pole and inside from south to north. Two field lines never intersect (that would mean two directions of force at one point). The relative strength is shown by how close the lines are.
2. Field around a current-carrying conductor. A straight wire produces concentric circles around it; increasing the current or decreasing the distance from the wire strengthens the field. A circular loop produces concentric circles that are nearly parallel at the centre; a solenoid produces a field like a bar magnet, with one end behaving as the north pole — this is how an electromagnet is made. Oersted's experiment showed that a compass needle placed near a current-carrying wire is deflected, so an electric current produces a magnetic field.
3. Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. A conductor carrying current placed in a magnetic field experiences a force, which is maximum when the conductor is perpendicular to the field. Fleming's left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular; if the forefinger points in the direction of the magnetic field and the middle finger in the direction of the current, then the thumb gives the direction of the force (motion). This is the principle of the electric motor.
4. The electric motor. A rectangular coil placed between the poles of a magnet; when current flows, the coil experiences a torque and rotates. The split ring (commutator) reverses the direction of current in the coil every half rotation, so the rotation continues in one direction. Uses: fans, mixers, washing machines, pumps. Electromagnetic induction: when the magnetic flux through a coil changes, a current is induced in it — Fleming's right-hand rule gives the direction of the induced current (thumb = motion, forefinger = field, middle finger = induced current).
5. The electric generator. It converts mechanical energy into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction. A DC generator uses a split-ring commutator and gives current in one direction; an AC generator uses two slip rings and the current reverses direction periodically. In India, AC has a frequency of 50 Hz, so its direction changes 100 times in one second.
6. Domestic electric circuits. Live wire (red, 220 V), neutral (black, 0 V) and earth wire (green, connected to a metal plate deep in the earth). Appliances with metallic bodies — geysers, irons, toasters — are earthed so that leakage current flows to the earth instead of through the user. Short circuit — live and neutral touch, resistance becomes almost zero, current rises enormously. Overloading — too many appliances on one circuit. Fuse and MCB protect the circuit; separate circuits of 15 A and 5 A are provided.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Left hand = Motor, Right hand = Generator. 'M' for motor and 'M' is in the word 'Motion' — but remember: the left hand has the thumb as Force, so Motor = Left.
  • Field lines never intersect — the standard one-mark reason question.
  • AC 50 Hz changes direction 100 times a second (twice per cycle).

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying field lines run from south to north outside a magnet. Outside it is north to south.
  • Mixing up Fleming's left-hand rule (force/motor) with the right-hand rule (induced current/generator).
  • Stating that the split ring makes the motor faster. It reverses the current every half rotation so that rotation continues in one direction.
  • Forgetting that the earth wire is connected to the metallic body of the appliance, not to the live wire.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect each other?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because at the point of intersection there would be two directions of the magnetic field for a single point, which is impossible — a compass needle placed there could not point in two directions at once.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Explain the principle and working of an electric motor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Principle: a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force, given by Fleming's left-hand rule.
Working: a rectangular coil ABCD is placed between the poles of a magnet. When current flows, one arm is pushed up and the other down, so the coil rotates.
The split-ring commutator reverses the direction of current in the coil after every half rotation, so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction.
Uses: electric fans, mixers, washing machines and pumps.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is electromagnetic induction? Describe an activity to demonstrate it.

Show Answer

Ans:

When the magnetic field linked with a coil changes, a current is induced in the coil — this is electromagnetic induction.
Activity: connect a galvanometer to a coil. Push a bar magnet's north pole into the coil and the galvanometer needle deflects momentarily in one direction; pull it out and it deflects in the opposite direction; hold it still inside and there is no deflection.
The deflection shows that a current is induced only when the magnetic flux through the coil is changing, i.e. only while the magnet is in relative motion.

LONGL55 marks

5. Describe a domestic electric circuit and explain the function of the earth wire and the fuse.

Show Answer

Ans:

The supply has three wires: live (red, at about 220 V), neutral (black, at zero potential) and earth (green).
Appliances are connected in parallel so that each receives the full supply voltage, each has its own switch, and the failure of one does not affect the others. Separate 15 A and 5 A circuits are provided for heavy and light appliances.
Earth wire: the metallic body of an appliance is connected to a metal plate buried in the earth, so if the live wire touches the body the leakage current flows safely into the ground instead of through a person — preventing a fatal shock.
Fuse: a short piece of wire of low melting point connected in series with the appliance. If the current exceeds the safe value, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit, protecting the appliance from damage due to overloading or short circuiting.

SHORTS62 marks

6. Differentiate between an AC generator and a DC generator.

Show Answer

Ans:

An AC generator uses two complete slip rings, and the current it produces reverses direction periodically (50 Hz in India). A DC generator uses a split-ring commutator, so although the current in the coil reverses, the current in the external circuit always flows in one direction.

✍️ Hands-on task

Build a simple electromagnet: wind 40 turns of insulated copper wire around an iron nail, connect it to two cells in series with a switch, and test how many pins it picks up. Then double the number of turns and repeat — the number of pins picked up shows that the field strength depends on the number of turns and the current.
