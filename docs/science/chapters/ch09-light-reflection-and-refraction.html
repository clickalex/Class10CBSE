🎯 Marks lens

3–4 marks. One ray diagram, one numerical on the mirror or lens formula, and one question on magnification or power.
Case-based questions give a practical situation — a dentist's mirror, a vehicle's rear-view mirror, a torch reflector — and ask which mirror is used and why.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Reflection. Light travelling in a straight line bounces back on striking a polished surface. Laws of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane. A plane mirror always forms a virtual, erect image, the same size as the object, at the same distance behind the mirror, and laterally inverted.
2. Spherical mirrors. Concave (converging, reflecting surface curves inwards) and convex (diverging). Key points: P (pole), C (centre of curvature), F (principal focus, midway between P and C), R = 2f. Aperture is the diameter of the reflecting surface.

3. Refraction. The bending of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium into another, caused by the change in its speed. Light bends toward the normal going from a rarer to a denser medium and away from the normal going the other way. It does not bend when it strikes the surface normally. Laws: (i) the incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in the same plane; (ii) Snell's law — the ratio sin i / sin r is constant for a given pair of media; this constant is the refractive index.
4. Refractive index. n = c/v (speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed in the medium); it has no unit. Relative refractive index n21 = n2/n1 = v1/v2. Air ≈ 1.0003, water 1.33, glass 1.5, diamond 2.42 — diamond's high value gives it brilliance through total internal reflection.
5. Lenses. Convex (converging, thicker at the centre) and concave (diverging). A convex lens forms a real, inverted image for objects beyond F, and a virtual, erect, magnified image when the object is between F and the optical centre (this is why it is used as a magnifying glass). A concave lens always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image.
6. Power. P = 1/f where f is in metres; the unit is the dioptre (D). Convex lens power is positive, concave lens power negative. Powers of lenses in contact add up: P = P1 + P2 + P3. Prescriptions read +2.0 D (convex, for hypermetropia) or −1.5 D (concave, for myopia).

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
  • Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
  • R = 2f
  • Magnification m = h'/h = −v/u (mirror) and m = h'/h = v/u (lens)
  • n = c/v ; n21 = n2/n1 = v1/v2
  • Power P = 1/f (f in metres), unit dioptre (D)

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Sign convention: distances measured against the direction of the incident light (to the left of the pole) are negative. Object distance u is always negative. For a concave mirror f is negative; for a convex mirror and a convex lens f is positive; a concave lens has negative f.
  • Nature from the sign of v: for a mirror, negative v = real image (in front); positive v = virtual (behind). For a lens, positive v = real (on the other side); negative v = virtual (same side).
  • 'Convex mirror = always virtual, erect, diminished' — the reason it is used as a rear-view mirror, giving a wider field of view.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Using the mirror formula for a lens. Lens formula has −v and +u in the same form: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.
  • Forgetting to convert f from cm to m before computing power. f = 20 cm must be written as 0.2 m.
  • Dropping the negative sign of m for a real, inverted image.
  • Drawing the ray diagram without arrowheads on the rays and without marking C, F and P.

❓ Exam Q&A

1 marks State the mirror formula and the SI unit of the power of a lens.

1/v + 1/u = 1/f. The SI unit of power is the dioptre (D), equal to 1 m⁻¹.

2 marks Why is a convex mirror preferred as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?

Because it always gives an erect, virtual and diminished image, and it has a wider field of view than a plane mirror, so the driver can see a much larger area of the traffic behind.

3 marks An object 4 cm high is placed 25 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the position, nature and size of the image.

u = −25 cm, f = −15 cm. Using 1/v + 1/u = 1/f: 1/v = 1/(−15) − 1/(−25) = (−5 + 3)/75 = −2/75.
So v = −37.5 cm — the image forms 37.5 cm in front of the mirror, hence real and inverted.
m = −v/u = −(−37.5)/(−25) = −1.5, so h' = −1.5 × 4 = −6 cm: the image is 6 cm tall, inverted, and magnified.

3 marks Draw a ray diagram for an object placed between C and F of a concave mirror and state the nature of the image.

Draw the principal axis with P, F and C marked. Place the object (an arrow) between C and F.
Ray 1: parallel to the axis, reflected through F. Ray 2: through F, reflected parallel to the axis. (A third ray through C retraces its path.)
The rays meet beyond C, so the image is real, inverted and magnified. This is the arrangement used in a shaving or makeup mirror when the object is close.

5 marks State the laws of refraction and solve: light enters from air into glass (n = 1.5) with an angle of incidence of 30°. Find the angle of refraction and the speed of light in glass.

Laws: (i) the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane; (ii) for a given pair of media and a given colour, sin i / sin r is constant (Snell's law).
Angle of refraction: sin r = sin i / n = 0.5/1.5 = 0.3333, so r ≈ 19.5° — the ray bends toward the normal.
Speed in glass: v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.5 = 2 × 10⁸ m/s.

2 marks A lens of power −4.0 D is placed in the path of light. Find its focal length and identify it.

P = 1/f, so f = 1/(−4.0) = −0.25 m = −25 cm. Negative focal length means it is a concave (diverging) lens.

✍️ Hands-on task

Using a concave mirror (or a spoon's inner surface) and a candle, find the focal length by focusing the sun or a distant window on a screen. Then place the candle at 2f and measure the image distance and size — check that v ≈ 2f and that the image is the same size and inverted.
