Chapter 6 · Control and Coordination

Chapter 6 of 13. Two tables to memorise — the brain parts and the plant/animal hormones — and this chapter is finished.

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

2–3 marks. Reflex arc and the hormone tables are the recurring questions; a labelled diagram of the neuron or the reflex arc is common.
Case-based questions often present a hormone-deficiency symptom and ask you to name the hormone and the gland.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Two ways of coordinating. Nervous coordination uses electrical impulses and is fast but short-lived and reaches only selected cells; chemical (hormonal) coordination is slower, slower to act but longer-lasting and reaches every cell through the blood. Plants rely mainly on chemical coordination and on growth movements.
2. The neuron. Cell body with nucleus, dendrites receive information, the axon carries the impulse away, and the nerve ending releases a chemical (neurotransmitter) into the synapse, so the impulse always travels one way. An impulse is generated by a change in the electrical potential difference across the cell membrane caused by ion movement.
3. The reflex arc. Receptor (skin) → sensory neuron → spinal cord (relay/interneuron) → motor neuron → effector (muscle or gland). Reflex actions are sudden and involuntary; they do not wait for the brain, which is why you withdraw your hand before you feel the pain. The brain is still informed afterwards.
4. The human nervous system. Central: brain + spinal cord, protected by the skull, vertebral column, three meninges and cerebrospinal fluid (shock absorber). Brain parts: forebrain — cerebrum (thinking, memory, voluntary action; the largest part), olfactory centre, hunger centre; midbrain — involuntary actions such as change in pupil size; hindbrain — pons (respiration), medulla (involuntary actions like blood pressure, salivation, vomiting), cerebellum (posture, balance, precision of voluntary movement — alcohol affects it). The spinal cord runs in the vertebral column and carries reflexes.
5. Coordination in plants. Tropic movements are directional growth responses: phototropism (shoot toward light), geotropism (root toward gravity), hydrotropism (root toward water), chemotropism (pollen tube growing toward the ovule), thigmotropism (tendrils). Nastic movements are non-directional, such as the closing of Mimosa (touch-me-not) leaves, caused by a change in the water content of cells. Phytohormones: auxin (cell elongation, phototropism, made at the shoot tip), gibberellins (stem growth), cytokinins (cell division, fruit and seed growth), abscisic acid (growth inhibitor — wilting of leaves, closing of stomata).
6. Hormones in animals. Endocrine glands are ductless and pour hormones directly into the blood. Thyroxin (thyroid) regulates carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism — iodine deficiency causes goitre. Growth hormone (pituitary) — deficiency causes dwarfism, excess causes gigantism. Testosterone (testes) and oestrogen (ovaries) control puberty. Insulin (pancreas) regulates blood sugar — its deficiency causes diabetes. Adrenaline (adrenal gland) is the emergency hormone: it increases heart rate, breathing rate and blood supply to muscles, preparing the body for fight or flight. Feedback regulation controls hormone secretion.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Reflex arc: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → effector
  • Synapse: electrical impulse → chemical (neurotransmitter) → electrical impulse
  • Plant movements: tropism = growth response; nastic = non-directional response
  • Phototropism: auxin accumulates on the shaded side → that side grows faster → bend towards light

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Split the answer into the nervous route and the hormonal route; most questions ask for one of the two or a comparison.
  2. For nervous control, trace the reflex arc in order: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → effector. Name the part that is damaged if a reflex is lost.
  3. For hormones, name the gland, the hormone and the effect: pituitary → growth hormone, thyroid → thyroxine, pancreas → insulin, adrenals → adrenaline, testes/ovaries → testosterone/oestrogen.
  4. Finish with the advantage: nervous control is fast and short-lived, hormonal control is slow but long-lasting. In plants, name the hormone — auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin or abscisic acid.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Reflex arc order: Receptor → Sensory → (Spine) → Motor → Effector. 'Red Sun Shines More Energetically'.
  • Hindbrain trio: Pons = breathing, Medulla = involuntary, Cerebellum = balance. Remember 'PMC — Pump, Medulla control, Coordination'.
  • Auxin is the growth hormone, abscisic acid is the anti-growth hormone. Everything else promotes.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Drawing the reflex arc through the brain. It passes through the spinal cord only.
  • Saying the pituitary secretes insulin. Insulin comes from the pancreas.
  • Calling the closing of Mimosa leaves a tropic movement. It is a nastic (non-directional) movement.
  • Writing 'thyroxin controls growth'. It controls metabolism; growth hormone controls growth.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name the largest part of the human brain and the hormone secreted by the pancreas.

Show Answer

Ans:

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?

Show Answer

Ans:

Iodine is essential for the synthesis of thyroxin by the thyroid gland. A shortage of iodine causes the gland to swell, producing goitre, and can lead to mental and physical disability in children, so iodised salt ensures an adequate intake.

SHORTS32 marks

3. How does phototropism occur in plants?

Show Answer

Ans:

Auxin is synthesised at the tip of the shoot and diffuses to the shaded side of the stem. There it causes the cells to elongate more than on the illuminated side, so the stem bends toward the light.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Name the plant hormone responsible for cell elongation and the one that ripens fruit.

Show Answer

Ans:

Auxin causes cell elongation and is responsible for the bending of a shoot towards light. Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that promotes the ripening of fruits.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Draw a labelled diagram of a neuron, or explain how a nerve impulse travels across a synapse.

Show Answer

Ans:

A neuron has a cell body with dendrites, a long axon, and branching nerve endings.
When the impulse reaches the nerve ending, an electrical signal is converted into a chemical — a neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic gap.
The chemical diffuses across the gap and triggers a new electrical impulse in the next neuron's dendrite, so the impulse travels in one direction only.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is a reflex arc? Why are reflex actions important?

Show Answer

Ans:

A reflex arc is the path followed by a nerve impulse during a reflex action: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord (relay neuron) → motor neuron → effector.
They are important because they are immediate and do not require the brain to think, so they protect the body from sudden injury such as heat, sharp objects or bright light.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Explain how the nervous and endocrine systems work together, using adrenaline as the example.

Show Answer

Ans:

The nervous system gives a fast, point-to-point electrical message; hormones give a slower, longer-lasting chemical message.
In a frightening situation the brain signals the adrenal medulla, which releases adrenaline into the blood.
Adrenaline increases heart rate and breathing, and diverts blood to the skeletal muscles — the ‘fight or flight’ response — so the whole body reacts, not just one muscle.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. Describe the feedback mechanism that keeps blood sugar level steady.

Show Answer

Ans:

When the glucose level in blood rises, the pancreas releases insulin, which makes body cells take up glucose and the liver convert the excess into glycogen.
As the level falls, the pancreas reduces insulin and secretes glucagon, which converts the stored glycogen back into glucose.
This is a negative feedback loop. If insulin secretion fails, the blood sugar stays high — the condition called diabetes.

LONGL135 marks

13. Describe the parts of the human brain and their functions.

Show Answer

Ans:

Forebrain — contains the cerebrum, the main thinking part, which controls voluntary actions, memory, intelligence and the senses; it also contains centres for hunger and smell.
Midbrain — controls involuntary actions such as the change in the size of the pupil.
Hindbrain — pons helps regulate respiration; the medulla controls involuntary actions such as blood pressure, salivation and vomiting; the cerebellum maintains posture and balance and makes voluntary movements precise, such as walking in a straight line or picking up a pencil.
The whole brain is protected by the cranium, meninges and the shock-absorbing cerebrospinal fluid.
