Chapter 4 · Mobile & Wireless Technology

🎯 Marks lens

Compare generations, name short-range technologies and their uses.
Generations are a table question — memorise the table.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Mobile generations. 1G was analog and voice-only. 2G went digital and added SMS and basic data through GPRS. 3G made mobile internet genuinely usable — video calls and browsing at a few Mbps. 4G/LTE is all-IP and built for data, giving tens of Mbps and making video streaming normal. 5G targets speeds up to around 20 Gbps with latency under a millisecond, which is what makes remote surgery, autonomous vehicles and dense IoT possible.
2. Short-range wireless. Bluetooth joins devices a few metres apart — headphones, keyboards, file sharing. Wi-Fi covers a building using the IEEE 802.11 family of standards. Infrared needs a clear line of sight and is used in remote controls. NFC works within a few centimetres and is what contactless payments and transit cards use.
3. Mobile data plumbing. A phone talks to a cell tower; the area one tower covers is a cell, and handover moves a call from one cell to the next without dropping it. Roaming is what lets a phone work on another operator's network when you travel.
4. Why it matters. Each generation did not just get faster — it changed what phones could do. 2G added text, 3G added the web, 4G added video, 5G adds machines talking to machines.

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Identify the generation or technology: 1G voice, 2G voice + SMS, 3G data, 4G/LTE broadband, 5G low latency.
  2. Name the access method — cellular towers, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or satellite.
  3. Note the handset requirements — a SIM, a radio for the band, and enough battery for the duty cycle.
  4. Close with the trade-off: range against speed, and speed against battery and cost.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 1 voice → 2 text → 3 web → 4 video → 5 machines. Five words for five generations.
  • Bluetooth = metres, Wi-Fi = a building, cell tower = kilometres.
  • NFC needs centimetres — that is why you tap, not wave.
  • Handover keeps a call alive as you drive between cells.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying 4G is faster only — it is also all-IP, which is the point.
  • Confusing Bluetooth and NFC ranges: Bluetooth is metres, NFC is centimetres.
  • Forgetting that infrared needs line of sight, unlike Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is the difference between Bluetooth and NFC?

Show Answer

Ans:

Bluetooth works over a range of a few metres and is used for continuous connections such as headphones, speakers and keyboards. NFC (Near Field Communication) works only within a few centimetres and is used for a single quick exchange — contactless payments, transit cards, pairing by tapping. Bluetooth needs pairing; NFC works by simply bringing two devices together.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is a cell and what is handover?

Show Answer

Ans:

A cell is the geographic area covered by one mobile tower. A whole city is divided into many cells, which is why mobile networks are called cellular. Handover is the automatic process of moving an ongoing call or data session from one cell's tower to the next as the user travels, so the connection does not drop.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why does infrared need line of sight but Wi-Fi does not?

Show Answer

Ans:

Infrared uses light waves, which cannot pass through solid objects, so the transmitter and receiver must see each other directly — that is why a TV remote only works when pointed at the TV. Wi-Fi uses radio waves, which pass through walls and furniture, so it works throughout a building without a direct line of sight.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What does 4G mean and how is it better than 3G?

Show Answer

Ans:

4G is the fourth generation of mobile network technology, designed for data rather than voice, with download speeds of up to about 100 Mbps, smooth video streaming and low delay. 3G was built mainly to improve voice calls and gave only modest data speeds, so tasks such as video calling were slow or impossible.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Compare 3G, 4G and 5G.

Show Answer

Ans:

3G made mobile internet practical, offering speeds up to about 2 Mbps, which allowed video calling and basic browsing. 4G (LTE) is an all-IP network designed around data rather than voice, reaching around 100 Mbps and making HD video streaming and VoIP everyday features. 5G targets up to 20 Gbps with latency below one millisecond; its real significance is not faster phones but support for massive numbers of connected devices — IoT sensors, autonomous vehicles and remote medical procedures.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Name four short-range wireless technologies and give one use of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bluetooth connects a phone to wireless earphones, a keyboard or a speaker within about ten metres.
Wi-Fi connects devices to a local network and through it to the internet, at home, in school or in a cafe.
NFC allows contactless payments and data exchange when two devices are touched or held a few centimetres apart.
Infrared needs a direct line of sight and is still used in television remote controls.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Why is 5G described as ‘for machines’ rather than just ‘faster phones’? Explain with reference to IoT and latency.

Show Answer

Ans:

Speed alone would only improve video on phones, which 4G already handles well.
5G's real change is low latency plus massive device density: millions of sensors, vehicles and appliances can talk to each other in milliseconds.
That is what the Internet of Things needs — a driverless car or a factory robot cannot wait for a slow round-trip. Hence: 1 voice, 2 text, 3 web, 4 video, 5 machines.
