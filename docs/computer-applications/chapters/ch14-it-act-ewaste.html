Chapter 14 · The IT Act, Digital Footprint & e-Waste

Chapter 14 of 14. The closing chapter. Sections, footprints and e-waste — three short topics.

🎯 Marks lens

Name IT Act sections and their offences, explain a digital footprint, describe e-waste handling.
Section numbers are recall marks.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The IT Act, 2000. The Information Technology Act, 2000 is India's principal law for electronic commerce and cybercrime; it was amended in 2008. It gives legal recognition to electronic documents and digital signatures, making an online contract as valid as a paper one, and it defines punishments for cyber offences. Key sections: Section 65 — tampering with computer source documents; Section 66 — computer-related offences including hacking; Section 66C — identity theft; Section 66D — cheating by personation using a computer resource; Section 67 — publishing obscene material in electronic form; Section 72 — breach of confidentiality and privacy.
2. Digital citizenship. A digital citizen uses technology responsibly, respectfully and safely. That means respecting other people's privacy and intellectual property, communicating without abusing or bullying, verifying information before sharing it, and reporting abuse instead of spreading it.
3. Digital footprint. A digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind online — accounts, posts, search history, photographs, comments and the metadata attached to them. Footprints are of two kinds: active, which you create deliberately by posting or filling a form, and passive, which is collected without your direct action, such as your IP address, location and browsing history. Anything posted can be copied, screenshotted and remain searchable for years, and it is routinely checked by colleges and employers.
4. e-Waste. e-Waste is discarded electronic equipment — old phones, batteries, chargers, monitors, circuit boards. It is dangerous because it contains lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants, which poison soil and groundwater when dumped, and because burning it releases toxic fumes. It is also valuable, since circuit boards contain recoverable gold, silver and copper. The correct handling is the 3R approach — reduce, reuse, recycle — by handing devices to authorised recyclers or e-waste collection centres rather than to ordinary waste collection.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • IT Act 2000 (amended 2008) — the Indian law on electronic transactions and cyber crime
  • Digital footprint = active (what you post) + passive (what sites collect about you)
  • E-waste rule: never in the household bin — hand it to an authorised recycler
  • Right to privacy is a fundamental right; ask what data an app collects and why

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. List what you have posted or shared — your active footprint.
  2. Work out what is collected silently — location, contacts, cookies, device ID: your passive footprint.
  3. Tighten the privacy settings, delete what you no longer need, and close unused accounts.
  4. Dispose of old devices securely: wipe the data first, then hand them to an authorised recycler.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 65 tampering, 66 hacking, 66C identity theft, 67 obscenity.
  • IT Act 2000, amended 2008.
  • Active footprint = you posted it; passive footprint = it was collected.
  • e-Waste = 3R: reduce, reuse, recycle.
  • The Act made electronic documents legally valid — that is its most-asked point.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting the 2008 amendment when asked about the IT Act.
  • Confusing active and passive digital footprints.
  • Describing e-waste only as 'old electronics' without naming the toxic substances.
  • Recommending throwing old devices in normal household waste.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is public and long-lasting: colleges and employers look at it, and once something is posted it is very hard to remove completely, so it is worth checking what your own footprint says about you.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is the IT Act, 2000? State any three of its key provisions.

Show Answer

Ans:

The Information Technology Act, 2000 is India's principal legislation governing electronic commerce and cybercrime, amended in 2008. Three key provisions are: it gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, so an online document or contract is as valid as a paper one; it defines cyber offences and their punishments, such as tampering with source documents under Section 65 and hacking under Section 66; and it provides a legal framework for electronic governance and secure electronic transactions, including penalties for identity theft under Section 66C and for publishing obscene material under Section 67.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is a digital footprint? Explain its two types.

Show Answer

Ans:

A digital footprint is the trail of data a person leaves behind while using the internet — accounts, posts, comments, photographs, search history and the metadata attached to them. It has two types. An active footprint is created deliberately, when you post on social media, fill in a form or upload a photograph. A passive footprint is collected without your direct action, such as your IP address, approximate location, browsing history and cookies. Both matter because the internet rarely forgets — content can be copied and screenshotted, and colleges and employers increasingly check what is publicly visible.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why is e-waste hazardous and how should it be managed?

Show Answer

Ans:

e-Waste is hazardous because discarded electronics contain toxic substances such as lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants. When dumped in ordinary landfills these chemicals leach into soil and groundwater, and burning the waste releases poisonous fumes that harm both workers and the surrounding population. At the same time e-waste contains recoverable gold, silver and copper, so throwing it away wastes valuable material. It should be managed through the 3R approach — reduce, reuse and recycle — by repairing and passing on working devices where possible and handing the rest to authorised e-waste recyclers or collection centres rather than to regular waste collection.

SHORTS93 marks

9. What does the Information Technology Act 2000 cover, and how should e-waste be handled?

Show Answer

Ans:

The Act gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, so that online documents and contracts are valid.
It defines cyber offences such as hacking, theft of data, identity theft, sending obscene material and spreading viruses, and sets fines and imprisonment for them.
It also created the framework for adjudicating officers and the Cyber Appellate Tribunal.
e-Waste must never be burnt or thrown into household garbage: under the E-Waste (Management) Rules it should be handed to an authorised recycler or returned to the manufacturer, because phones and computers contain lead, mercury and cadmium.
