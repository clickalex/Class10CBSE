Chapter 3 · Network Types, Topologies & Devices

Chapter 3 of 13. Topology questions ask for a diagram plus one advantage and one disadvantage. Prepare both.

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Classify a network by its size, identify a topology from a description, name a device's job.
Diagrams score — practise drawing them.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Networks by size. A LAN (Local Area Network) covers a room or a building — a school lab. A MAN (Metropolitan Area Network) covers a city — a cable TV network. A WAN (Wide Area Network) covers countries or the world; the internet is the largest WAN. A PAN (Personal Area Network) is the smallest, joining devices around one person, usually by Bluetooth.
2. Topologies — the shape of the wiring. Bus: one central cable with all nodes attached; cheap but if the main cable fails everything stops. Star: every node connects to a central hub or switch; a single cable failing only drops one node, but the centre is a single point of failure. Ring: nodes form a closed loop, data travelling one way; easy to add nodes, but a break stops the ring. Mesh: every node links to several others, giving the highest reliability at the highest cost. Tree combines stars in a hierarchy.
3. Transmission media. Wired — twisted-pair cable (RJ-45 connector, common in LANs), coaxial cable, and optical fibre, which carries light and is the fastest and least affected by interference. Wireless — Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), Bluetooth, infrared, and satellite links.
4. Devices. A hub repeats a signal to every port and cannot tell machines apart. A switch learns which device sits on which port and sends data only there — this is why switches replaced hubs. A router joins different networks and chooses the best path, working at the network layer. A modem converts digital signals to analog and back so data can travel over telephone or cable lines. A repeater simply boosts a weakening signal. A gateway connects networks that use different protocols. A NIC (Network Interface Card) is what physically attaches a computer to the network; every NIC has a unique MAC address.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • LAN < MAN < WAN < the Internet — by area covered
  • Topologies: bus (one cable), star (all to a hub/switch), ring (each to the next), mesh (every node to many)
  • Devices: hub repeats to all · switch sends to one · router joins networks · modem converts the signal
  • Transmission media: twisted pair, coaxial, fibre optic (guided); radio, microwave, satellite (unguided)

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Decide the area the network covers — a room, a campus, or a country.
  2. Draw the topology in words: what sits at the centre, and what happens if it fails.
  3. Choose the device for each job — switch inside a network, router between networks.
  4. Pick the medium on cost, distance and interference, and state the reason for the choice.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • PAN < LAN < MAN < WAN. Smallest to largest.
  • Star is the safest single-node failure, mesh the safest overall, bus the cheapest.
  • Hub shouts, switch whispers, router chooses. One line for all three.
  • Optical fibre = light = fastest + no electrical interference.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Confusing hub and switch — a hub broadcasts to all ports, a switch sends only to the intended one.
  • Confusing router and modem — a modem converts signals, a router routes between networks.
  • Answering a topology question without naming one advantage and one disadvantage.
  • Writing MAC address when the question asks for IP address — MAC is fixed to the hardware, IP is assigned to the network.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Which IEEE standard defines Wi-Fi?

Show Answer

Ans:

IEEE 802.11 defines Wi-Fi. The IEEE 802.3 standard, by contrast, defines wired Ethernet.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is optical fibre preferred over copper cable for long distances?

Show Answer

Ans:

Optical fibre carries data as pulses of light through glass, so it suffers almost no signal loss over long distances and is immune to electrical interference from motors, power lines and lightning. It also has a far higher bandwidth than copper. Its drawbacks are that it is more expensive and more fragile to install, which is why copper still dominates the last few metres.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is the difference between a MAC address and an IP address?

Show Answer

Ans:

A MAC address is a 48-bit hardware address burnt into the network card; it never changes and identifies the physical device. An IP address is assigned logically for the network the device is on, so it changes when the device moves to a different network. MAC works within one local network, while IP works across the whole internet.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Distinguish between a LAN and a WAN.

Show Answer

Ans:

A LAN covers a small area such as a room, a building or a campus, is owned by one organisation, uses switches and cables or Wi-Fi, and is fast. A WAN covers a city, a country or the world, links LANs over leased telephone lines, fibre or satellites, and is slower and shared among many organisations.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain star topology with its advantages and disadvantages.

Show Answer

Ans:

In star topology every node is connected by its own cable to a central device — a hub or a switch. All communication passes through that centre. Its advantages are that a cable failure affects only the one node attached to it, nodes can be added or removed without disturbing the rest, and faults are easy to locate. Its disadvantages are that it needs more cable than a bus, and the central device is a single point of failure — if the switch dies, the whole network stops.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Differentiate between a hub, a switch and a router.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hub works at the physical layer and repeats an incoming signal out of every port, so all devices see all traffic and collisions are common. A switch works at the data-link layer, learns the MAC address on each port and forwards a frame only to the intended port, which makes it far faster and more secure. A router works at the network layer and connects different networks, using IP addresses to choose the best path between them — it is what links your LAN to the internet.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Compare the star and bus topologies and name one advantage of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

In a star topology every computer is connected to a central switch or hub; a fault in one cable affects only that computer, and new computers can be added easily.
In a bus topology all computers share one main cable with terminators at each end; it is cheap and needs less cable.
Star costs more because of the switch and the extra cable, while bus is slow when many computers share the cable and stops working if the main cable breaks.
Star is the topology used in almost every modern office network.
