SHORTS11 mark

1. When did India first get internet access, and through which provider?

Show Answer

Ans:

India got internet access on 15 August 1995, through VSNL (Videsh Sanchar Nigam Limited).

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is the internet? Give two of its features.

Show Answer

Ans:

The internet is a worldwide network of networks in which millions of smaller networks are interconnected, allowing any computer to communicate with any other. Two of its features are: it has no single owner or central control, running instead on shared protocols; and it provides many services at once — the World Wide Web, email, file transfer and video calls all travel over the same infrastructure.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What was ARPANET and why is it important?

Show Answer

Ans:

ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network) was started in 1969 by the US Department of Defense's research agency. It is important because it was the first packet-switched network and the direct ancestor of today's internet — its first connection ran between UCLA and Stanford Research Institute.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the role of DNS?

Show Answer

Ans:

DNS (Domain Name System) translates human-readable domain names such as clickalex.github.io into the numeric IP addresses that computers actually use to find each other. Without it, users would have to memorise numbers like 140.82.121.4 to reach any website. DNS works like a distributed phone book for the internet.

SHORTS52 marks

5. What is the internet and how did it begin?

Show Answer

Ans:

The internet is a worldwide network of networks that links millions of computers using the TCP/IP family of protocols. It began in 1969 as ARPANET, a project of the US Department of Defense that linked four university computers, and grew into the public network we use after the World Wide Web was invented in 1989.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Distinguish between IPv4 and IPv6.

Show Answer

Ans:

IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses, giving roughly 4.3 billion possible addresses — written as four numbers separated by dots, such as 192.168.1.5. IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses, written in hexadecimal groups such as 2001:0db8:85a3::8a2e:0370:7334. IPv6 was created because the supply of IPv4 addresses was exhausted by the growth in connected devices; it also has better built-in support for security and automatic configuration.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes: “The internet and the World Wide Web are the same thing, and the internet is owned by Google.” Correct both claims.

Show Answer

Ans:

The internet is the global network of networks (cables, routers and protocols). The World Wide Web is only one service that runs on it: linked web pages fetched with HTTP. E-mail and file transfer use the internet but are not the web.
The internet has no single owner or central controller; thousands of independent networks cooperate by following common protocols.
Organisations such as ICANN and the IETF only coordinate names, addresses and standards.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. Sunita has a 100 Mbps broadband plan, yet her video calls lag and freeze. Her brother says the problem is high latency, not low bandwidth. Explain the difference and how each affects a video call.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bandwidth is how much data the connection can carry per second; latency is how long a packet takes to travel to the other end and back.
A large bandwidth helps with big downloads and HD streaming, but a call is a real-time exchange of many small packets.
If latency is high, the voice and video arrive late even though the connection is “fast”, so the call lags.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Explain the terms World Wide Web, webpage, website and homepage.

Show Answer

Ans:

The World Wide Web is the collection of linked documents and resources reached over the internet, invented by Tim Berners-Lee.
A webpage is a single document, written in HTML, that a browser displays.
A website is a group of related webpages stored under one domain name, such as a school's site with pages for admissions, results and contact.
The homepage is the first page that opens when you visit a website, from which the other pages are reached.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: In a new school lab, 30 computers are connected to a router, which is connected to an Internet Service Provider (ISP). (a) What is the role of the ISP? (b) What does the router do? (c) What gives each computer a unique address on the network?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The ISP gives the lab access to the internet; it is the company or organisation whose network the school’s network joins (in India, VSNL gave the first public access in 1995).
(b) The router connects the lab’s network to the internet and forwards data packets towards the right destination.
(c) Each device is given an IP address.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A student reads: “ARPANET started in 1969, and Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989.” (a) How many years separate the two events? (b) Which came first, and does the web run on the internet? (c) Name the three technologies on which the web is built.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 1989 − 1969 = 20 years.
(b) ARPANET (the ancestor of the internet) came first; the web is a service that runs on top of the internet.
(c) HTML (to write pages), HTTP (to transfer them) and URLs (to address them).

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe, step by step, what happens from the moment you type a web address into a browser until the page appears.

Show Answer

Ans:

Step 1: you type a URL, for example www.example.org, into the browser.
Step 2: the browser asks a DNS server to translate the domain name into the numeric IP address of the web server.
Step 3: using that IP address, the browser sends an HTTP request for the page to the web server.
Step 4: the server sends back the page (HTML, images and other files), split into small data packets that may travel by different routes.
Step 5: the packets are reassembled and the browser displays the page.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Give the full form of FTP and its default port.

Show Answer

Ans:

FTP stands for File Transfer Protocol, and its default port is 21. It is used to upload and download files between a client and a server.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is the difference between HTTP and HTTPS?

Show Answer

Ans:

HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol) is the protocol used to fetch web pages, and it sends everything in plain text on port 80. HTTPS is the same protocol wrapped in encryption using TLS/SSL, on port 443. The practical difference is that with HTTPS a third party watching the network cannot read or alter the data — which is why banking and login pages always use it.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Explain the difference between POP3 and IMAP.

Show Answer

Ans:

Both protocols receive email, but they handle storage differently. POP3 downloads messages to your device and normally deletes them from the server, so the mail is only on that one machine. IMAP leaves messages on the server and synchronises, so the same mailbox looks identical on a phone, a laptop and a web browser. IMAP needs a constant connection and server space; POP3 works offline once downloaded.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between TCP and UDP?

Show Answer

Ans:

TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) is connection-oriented: it checks that every packet arrived and reorders them, so delivery is guaranteed but slightly slower. UDP (User Datagram Protocol) simply sends packets without checking, so it is faster but some data may be lost. TCP is used for web pages and email, where a missing piece breaks the result; UDP is used for live video, VoIP and gaming, where a small loss is better than a delay.

SHORTS52 marks

5. Match each service with the protocol it uses: browsing the web, sending email, reading email, transferring files.

Show Answer

Ans:

Browsing uses HTTP or HTTPS; sending email uses SMTP; reading email uses POP3 or IMAP; transferring files uses FTP. DNS is not a service but the system that converts a domain name into an IP address so that the service can find the right computer.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Break down the parts of this URL: https://clickalex.github.io/IT-402-/index.html.

Show Answer

Ans:

The URL has three parts. https:// is the protocol — it says the page will be fetched securely over HTTPS on port 443. clickalex.github.io is the domain name, which DNS resolves into an IP address. /IT-402-/index.html is the path, pointing to a specific file inside the IT-402- folder on that server.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student writes that SMTP is the protocol that delivers e-mail to your inbox. Correct this and name the protocols used for receiving mail.

Show Answer

Ans:

SMTP only sends mail, from your mail program to the server and from server to server.
For receiving, a mail program uses POP3 or IMAP.
POP3 downloads messages to the device (usually removing them from the server); IMAP keeps them on the server so that every device shows the same mailbox.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. Riya reads her mail on her phone, tablet and laptop and wants all three to show the same inbox and folders. Her brother reads mail on one computer, even offline. Which protocol suits each, and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

Riya needs IMAP: the mail stays on the server, so every device sees the same messages and folders.
Her brother can use POP3: it downloads the messages to his computer, where he can read them offline.
The difference is where the master copy of the mail is kept: on the server (IMAP) or on the device (POP3).

SHORTS93 marks

9. Describe what happens when you send an email.

Show Answer

Ans:

Your mail program hands the message to your mail server using SMTP.
That server asks DNS for the mail server of the recipient's domain, such as gmail.com, and passes the message to it.
The receiving server stores the message in the recipient's mailbox.
The recipient's program collects it with POP3, which usually downloads and deletes it, or with IMAP, which leaves it on the server and synchronises folders.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A design studio must send a 500 MB video to a client in another city. The e-mail service allows attachments of only 25 MB. (a) Which internet service and protocol should the studio use? (b) Why is e-mail unsuitable? (c) What security weakness does plain FTP have, and how can it be reduced?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) File transfer using FTP (File Transfer Protocol), which uses port 21 by default.
(b) E-mail attachments are limited in size and are not designed for very large files.
(c) Plain FTP sends the user name and password unencrypted; using a secure version (SFTP or FTPS) encrypts them.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Priya chats with Rohan using a messaging app and then switches to a video call. (a) What is chat (instant messaging)? (b) What is VoIP? (c) Why does a video call need a better connection than an e-mail?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The exchange of text messages between users in real time over the internet.
(b) VoIP (Voice over IP) turns speech into digital packets and sends them over the internet instead of over a telephone line.
(c) A call is a continuous real-time stream that must arrive with little delay, while an e-mail can wait a few seconds.

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe any four internet services, naming the protocol each uses and giving one use of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Web (WWW): HTTP or HTTPS carries web pages to the browser; used for browsing and online shopping.
E-mail: SMTP sends mail and POP3 or IMAP receives it; used for written communication and documents.
File transfer: FTP moves files between computers; used to upload a website to its server.
Chat and VoIP: real-time protocols carry messages, voice and video; used for online classes and calls.
Each service has its own protocol, and the common base for all of them is TCP/IP.

Ch 3 · Network Types, Topologies & Devices (12)

Save this chapter: Text (.txt) ·

SHORTS11 mark

1. Which IEEE standard defines Wi-Fi?

Show Answer

Ans:

IEEE 802.11 defines Wi-Fi. The IEEE 802.3 standard, by contrast, defines wired Ethernet.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why is optical fibre preferred over copper cable for long distances?

Show Answer

Ans:

Optical fibre carries data as pulses of light through glass, so it suffers almost no signal loss over long distances and is immune to electrical interference from motors, power lines and lightning. It also has a far higher bandwidth than copper. Its drawbacks are that it is more expensive and more fragile to install, which is why copper still dominates the last few metres.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is the difference between a MAC address and an IP address?

Show Answer

Ans:

A MAC address is a 48-bit hardware address burnt into the network card; it never changes and identifies the physical device. An IP address is assigned logically for the network the device is on, so it changes when the device moves to a different network. MAC works within one local network, while IP works across the whole internet.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Distinguish between a LAN and a WAN.

Show Answer

Ans:

A LAN covers a small area such as a room, a building or a campus, is owned by one organisation, uses switches and cables or Wi-Fi, and is fast. A WAN covers a city, a country or the world, links LANs over leased telephone lines, fibre or satellites, and is slower and shared among many organisations.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain star topology with its advantages and disadvantages.

Show Answer

Ans:

In star topology every node is connected by its own cable to a central device — a hub or a switch. All communication passes through that centre. Its advantages are that a cable failure affects only the one node attached to it, nodes can be added or removed without disturbing the rest, and faults are easy to locate. Its disadvantages are that it needs more cable than a bus, and the central device is a single point of failure — if the switch dies, the whole network stops.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Differentiate between a hub, a switch and a router.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hub works at the physical layer and repeats an incoming signal out of every port, so all devices see all traffic and collisions are common. A switch works at the data-link layer, learns the MAC address on each port and forwards a frame only to the intended port, which makes it far faster and more secure. A router works at the network layer and connects different networks, using IP addresses to choose the best path between them — it is what links your LAN to the internet.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student says a hub and a switch are the same because both connect many computers. Correct this and say which is better for a busy school lab.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hub sends every incoming signal to all its ports (it broadcasts), so every computer receives data meant for one.
A switch learns the address of the device on each port and sends data only to the intended port.
So a switch reduces traffic and collisions and improves speed and privacy; it is the better choice for a busy lab.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. A school has two computer labs on different floors and wants every computer to share a printer and reach the internet. List the devices needed and the job of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

A switch in each lab connects its computers and the printer into a local network.
A router joins the lab network to the internet through the Internet Service Provider and directs the traffic.
A modem converts the signal between the ISP’s line and the digital signal the router uses (it is often built into the router); a wireless access point can add Wi-Fi.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Compare the star and bus topologies and name one advantage of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

In a star topology every computer is connected to a central switch or hub; a fault in one cable affects only that computer, and new computers can be added easily.
In a bus topology all computers share one main cable with terminators at each end; it is cheap and needs less cable.
Star costs more because of the switch and the extra cable, while bus is slow when many computers share the cable and stops working if the main cable breaks.
Star is the topology used in almost every modern office network.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: In an old computer lab, all the computers were attached to one long common cable with a terminator at each end. One day the main cable broke and the whole lab went offline. (a) Name the topology. (b) State one advantage of it. (c) Why was a single break so serious?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Bus topology.
(b) It is cheap and simple to install and needs less cable than a star.
(c) Every computer depends on the single backbone cable, so a break stops communication for all of them; performance also falls as more devices share it.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A laptop has the MAC address 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E printed in its hardware. At school it gets the IP address 192.168.1.15 and at home it gets 192.168.0.7. (a) Which address stays the same wherever the laptop goes? (b) Which one changes with the network? (c) Who or what assigns the IP address?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The MAC address: it is fixed in the network hardware.
(b) The IP address, which depends on the network the laptop joins.
(c) The network itself, usually the router, using DHCP.

LONGL125 marks

12. Differentiate between PAN, LAN, MAN and WAN, giving the area covered and an example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

PAN (Personal Area Network): a few metres around one person, for example a phone linked to earphones by Bluetooth.
LAN (Local Area Network): a room, building or campus, for example a school computer lab.
MAN (Metropolitan Area Network): a whole city, for example a cable-TV or city-wide broadband network.
WAN (Wide Area Network): countries or continents, for example the internet or a bank’s network linking branches.
As the area grows, so do the cost and the number of devices, while the speed per user usually falls.

Ch 4 · Mobile & Wireless Technology (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is the difference between Bluetooth and NFC?

Show Answer

Ans:

Bluetooth works over a range of a few metres and is used for continuous connections such as headphones, speakers and keyboards. NFC (Near Field Communication) works only within a few centimetres and is used for a single quick exchange — contactless payments, transit cards, pairing by tapping. Bluetooth needs pairing; NFC works by simply bringing two devices together.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is a cell and what is handover?

Show Answer

Ans:

A cell is the geographic area covered by one mobile tower. A whole city is divided into many cells, which is why mobile networks are called cellular. Handover is the automatic process of moving an ongoing call or data session from one cell's tower to the next as the user travels, so the connection does not drop.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why does infrared need line of sight but Wi-Fi does not?

Show Answer

Ans:

Infrared uses light waves, which cannot pass through solid objects, so the transmitter and receiver must see each other directly — that is why a TV remote only works when pointed at the TV. Wi-Fi uses radio waves, which pass through walls and furniture, so it works throughout a building without a direct line of sight.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What does 4G mean and how is it better than 3G?

Show Answer

Ans:

4G is the fourth generation of mobile network technology, designed for data rather than voice, with download speeds of up to about 100 Mbps, smooth video streaming and low delay. 3G was built mainly to improve voice calls and gave only modest data speeds, so tasks such as video calling were slow or impossible.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Compare 3G, 4G and 5G.

Show Answer

Ans:

3G made mobile internet practical, offering speeds up to about 2 Mbps, which allowed video calling and basic browsing. 4G (LTE) is an all-IP network designed around data rather than voice, reaching around 100 Mbps and making HD video streaming and VoIP everyday features. 5G targets up to 20 Gbps with latency below one millisecond; its real significance is not faster phones but support for massive numbers of connected devices — IoT sensors, autonomous vehicles and remote medical procedures.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student writes: “4G is simply a faster version of 3G.” Correct the statement.

Show Answer

Ans:

4G is faster, but speed is not the main change.
The key difference is that 4G is all-IP: both voice and data travel as packets over an IP network, while earlier generations carried voice separately.
This is what makes HD video streaming, video calls and mobile gaming practical on a phone.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Choose the most suitable wireless technology and justify it: (i) paying at a shop by tapping a phone, (ii) sending photos to a friend sitting next to you, (iii) changing channels with a TV remote, (iv) joining a laptop to home broadband without a cable.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) NFC: it works only at a few centimetres, which is safe for payments.
(ii) Bluetooth: a short-range link of a few metres that needs no router.
(iii) Infrared: a cheap short-range signal that needs a clear line of sight to the TV.
(iv) Wi-Fi: a wireless local network that gives broadband-speed access through a router.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Name four short-range wireless technologies and give one use of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bluetooth connects a phone to wireless earphones, a keyboard or a speaker within about ten metres.
Wi-Fi connects devices to a local network and through it to the internet, at home, in school or in a cafe.
NFC allows contactless payments and data exchange when two devices are touched or held a few centimetres apart.
Infrared needs a direct line of sight and is still used in television remote controls.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Why is 5G described as ‘for machines’ rather than just ‘faster phones’? Explain with reference to IoT and latency.

Show Answer

Ans:

Speed alone would only improve video on phones, which 4G already handles well.
5G's real change is low latency plus massive device density: millions of sensors, vehicles and appliances can talk to each other in milliseconds.
That is what the Internet of Things needs — a driverless car or a factory robot cannot wait for a slow round-trip. Hence: 1 voice, 2 text, 3 web, 4 video, 5 machines.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A phone’s specification sheet lists: 5G, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3, NFC and an infrared blaster. (a) Which of these has the shortest range? (b) Which one needs line of sight? (c) Which one connects the phone to the mobile operator’s towers?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) NFC, which works over only a few centimetres.
(b) Infrared, because it cannot pass around obstacles, unlike Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
(c) 5G, the cellular technology.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A village gets a mobile tower and each family buys a SIM card. (a) What is a SIM card for? (b) What does the tower do? (c) What is a “cell”?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) It identifies the subscriber to the network and stores the phone number and security details.
(b) The tower (base station) sends and receives radio signals to and from the phones around it and connects them to the rest of the network.
(c) The area covered by one tower; a mobile network is divided into many such cells, and when a user moves from one to the next, the call is handed over.

LONGL125 marks

12. Trace the evolution of mobile technology from 1G to 5G, stating the main improvement at each step.

Show Answer

Ans:

1G: analogue voice calls only, with poor quality and no security.
2G: digital voice with SMS and better call quality.
3G: mobile internet and the first practical video calls.
4G: high speed on an all-IP network, which made HD streaming and mobile apps routine.
5G: very high speed, very low delay and the ability to connect huge numbers of devices, which suits the Internet of Things.

Ch 5 · Web Browsers, Search & Web Concepts (12)

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name any three web browsers.

Show Answer

Ans:

Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox and Microsoft Edge. Apple's Safari and Opera are other common examples.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate between a web browser and a search engine.

Show Answer

Ans:

A web browser is software installed on your computer or phone — Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari — that sends requests for pages and renders the HTML it receives into the page you see. A search engine such as Google is itself a website whose job is to index other websites and return a ranked list of results for your query. You need a browser in order to open a search engine, so they work together rather than as alternatives.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What are cookies and why are they used?

Show Answer

Ans:

A cookie is a small piece of data that a website stores in the user's browser. Websites use cookies to remember something about the user between visits — whether they are logged in, which language they chose, or what is in their shopping cart. Without cookies, every page request would be treated as a brand-new visitor. Because cookies can also be used to track browsing behaviour, browsers offer private modes that discard them when the window closes.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is caching and what is its benefit?

Show Answer

Ans:

Caching means keeping a local copy of a resource — a page, an image or a stylesheet — so that it does not have to be downloaded again. The benefits are that pages load faster and use less bandwidth, which matters most on slow or metered connections. The trade-off is that a cached copy can be out of date, which is why a forced refresh sometimes shows a different page.

SHORTS52 marks

5. How does a search engine find and rank pages?

Show Answer

Ans:

A search engine uses a crawler — also called a spider — that automatically visits web pages and follows the links on them to discover more. What it finds is stored in an index, a huge searchable catalogue. When you type a query, a ranking algorithm scores the matching pages by relevance, authority and quality, and returns them in order.

SHORTS62 marks

6. What is the difference between a web browser and a search engine?

Show Answer

Ans:

A browser is the program that requests, receives and displays web pages — Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari. A search engine is a website, such as Google or Bing, that keeps an index of pages and returns a list of results for your query. The browser is the tool you use; the search engine is a site you visit with it.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student says, “I opened Google to browse the internet, so Google is my browser.” Correct the error.

Show Answer

Ans:

Google is a search engine, a website that finds web pages for you.
A browser is the software, such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari, that you use to open any website, including Google.
You need a browser to reach a search engine, not the other way round.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. After shopping on a website, Amit keeps seeing ads for the same product on other sites, and the shop’s pages open faster the second time. Which effect is due to cookies and which to the cache?

Show Answer

Ans:

The ads following him come from cookies: small files that websites store on his device to recognise him and remember what he looked at.
The faster loading comes from the cache: a stored copy of images and pages that the browser reuses instead of downloading them again.
Cookies hold identity data; the cache is a speed copy.

SHORTS93 marks

9. What are cookies and cache, and why should you clear them sometimes?

Show Answer

Ans:

Cookies are small files a website stores on your computer to remember who you are, what is in your basket and what your preferences are.
The cache stores copies of images, scripts and pages so that a site loads faster the next time you visit it.
Clearing them removes old or corrupted files that can make a site load wrongly, frees disk space, logs you out of accounts and limits tracking.
The cost is that you have to sign in again and the first visit to a site is slower.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Neha logs in to her e-mail on a school computer and does not want any trace left on it. (a) Which browser mode should she use? (b) What does that mode not hide? (c) What should she do when she finishes?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) A private (incognito) window.
(b) It does not save history or cookies on that device after the window is closed, but the school network, the internet provider and the websites can still see her activity.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is CSS? State two advantages of using CSS with an HTML page.

Show Answer

Ans:

CSS (Cascading Style Sheets) is the language that controls how a page looks — colours, fonts, borders and spacing — while HTML keeps only the content. Two advantages: (1) one rule styles every matching element, so all headings can be recoloured with a single edit; (2) content and presentation stay separate, so the design can be changed without touching the text.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What do the CSS properties color and background-color control? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

color sets the colour of the text itself, e.g. p { color: green; }. background-color sets the colour of the box behind the text, e.g. body { background-color: #FFFFCC; }, which paints the whole page pale yellow. Values may be colour names or hex codes.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Explain border-style with any two values. How is outline different from a border?

Show Answer

Ans:

border-style draws the edge of an element’s box; values include solid (a continuous line) and dashed (a broken line), besides dotted, double and none. outline draws a second line just outside the border (e.g. outline: solid blue;) and is often used to highlight the field a visitor is typing in.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What do height, width and margin do in CSS? Give one example.

Show Answer

Ans:

height and width fix how big an element’s box is, e.g. img { width: 200px; height: 150px; }. margin is the empty space outside the border that pushes neighbouring elements away, e.g. p { margin: 15px; } leaves a 15-pixel gap around every paragraph.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain the three font properties in the syllabus with one example that uses all three.

Show Answer

Ans:

The three font properties are font-family (the typeface, e.g. Arial), font-size (how big the text is, e.g. 24px) and font-style (usually normal or italic). Together: h2 { font-family: Arial; font-size: 24px; font-style: italic; } — every second-level heading becomes large, Arial and italic.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How is the float property used to place an image beside a paragraph? Write the CSS.

Show Answer

Ans:

float pushes an element to one side (left or right) so that the following text wraps around it. To float a picture left of a paragraph with a small gap: img { float: left; margin: 10px; }. The margin keeps the text from touching the picture.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Explain the <audio> tag with its attributes.

Show Answer

Ans:

The <audio> tag embeds a sound file in a page. src gives the location of the audio file. controls displays the play, pause and volume buttons — without it the player is invisible and the user cannot control playback. autoplay starts the sound as soon as the page loads, loop repeats it endlessly, and muted starts it silently. An example is <audio src='song.mp3' controls loop>.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Write HTML to embed a video with the browser’s own controls. Name the attributes you used.

Show Answer

Ans:

<video src="lesson.mp4" width="320" height="240" controls></video> embeds the video with play, pause and volume controls.
src gives the video file, width and height fix the size of the picture, and controls shows the browser’s own buttons.
<audio src="song.mp3" controls></audio> does the same job for sound, using the same controls idea.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is netiquette? List any four netiquette rules.

Show Answer

Ans:

Netiquette (network + etiquette) is the code of polite, responsible behaviour on the internet. Four rules: (1) write courteous messages and respect others’ privacy; (2) do not type in ALL CAPS — it reads as shouting; (3) do not forward rumours, chain messages or spam; (4) give credit for others’ work instead of copying it.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is e-commerce? Give two examples from the syllabus.

Show Answer

Ans:

E-commerce means carrying out trade — buying, selling, paying and banking — over the internet. Two syllabus examples: e-shopping (ordering goods on a website) and e-banking (transferring money or paying bills through a bank’s site or app). e-reservation and online bill payment are two more.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why is privacy the biggest concern in e-commerce? State two precautions a buyer should take.

Show Answer

Ans:

Every e-commerce transaction moves money plus personal data (name, address, card number, OTP), which thieves can misuse. Two precautions: (1) shop only on known, reputed sites showing https:// and the padlock; (2) never share passwords or OTPs with anyone, and check what data an app collects before installing it.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the digital divide?

Show Answer

Ans:

The digital divide is the gap between people who have access to computers, smartphones and the internet — and those who do not. It is caused by poverty, remote villages with no network, lack of digital skills, age, disability and language barriers, and it locks the excluded out of online schooling, banking and services.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Explain online fraud in e-commerce with two examples. How can a buyer avoid each?

Show Answer

Ans:

Online fraud is cheating a buyer or seller over the internet. Example 1: fake shops that take payment and never deliver — avoid by buying only from known sites and checking reviews. Example 2: phishing messages that pose as a bank and steal passwords — avoid by never clicking such links and never sharing OTPs.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How is data kept secure during transmission? Explain any three methods.

Show Answer

Ans:

Three methods: (1) Encryption — scrambling the data so only the intended receiver can read it. (2) https:// — HTTP over a secure channel (the browser padlock); plain http sends everything as readable text. (3) OTPs — one-time passwords sent to the buyer’s phone that prove the transaction is really theirs.

SHORTS73 marks

7. What is freedom of information? How does e-governance support it?

Show Answer

Ans:

Freedom of information is the principle that citizens have the right to access information held by public authorities. E-governance supports it by putting records, results, forms and services online — certificates, exam results, land records — so people can see, question and use them without middlemen or secrecy.

SHORTS83 marks

8. State three consequences of the digital divide and three ways to bridge it.

Show Answer

Ans:

Consequences: students miss online classes, citizens miss digital banking and certificates, and job-seekers miss online opportunities. Bridging it: (1) cheap smartphones and low-cost data; (2) mobile networks and public Wi-Fi in villages; (3) services and training in regional languages so language is no barrier.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is plagiarism and how can it be avoided?

Show Answer

Ans:

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's words, ideas, data or code as if they were your own, without giving credit. It is an ethical offence even when no money changes hands. It is avoided by always citing the source, using quotation marks around copied text, paraphrasing genuinely rather than swapping a few words, and keeping a record of every source consulted while working.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is software piracy?

Show Answer

Ans:

Software piracy is the unauthorised copying, distribution or use of software without a valid licence. It includes installing one licensed copy on many machines, downloading cracked versions, and selling counterfeit discs. It deprives developers of income and often exposes the user to malware, since pirated copies are frequently bundled with it.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Distinguish between freeware and shareware.

Show Answer

Ans:

Freeware can be used free of charge indefinitely, but its source code is usually closed and it cannot be modified or sold — an example is Skype. Shareware is distributed free only as a trial, either for a limited period or with reduced features, and the user must pay to continue using it after the trial ends.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is plagiarism and how is it different from quoting?

Show Answer

Ans:

Plagiarism is using someone else's words, ideas, images or code and presenting them as your own, even if you change a few words or do not name the source. Quoting keeps the original wording inside quotation marks and names the author and the work, so the reader knows whose words they are; that is allowed and expected in school work.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is IPR? Name and explain its four main types.

Show Answer

Ans:

IPR (Intellectual Property Rights) are the legal rights granted to the creator of an original work, allowing them to control and benefit from its use and stopping others from copying it without permission. The four main types are copyright, which protects original literary, artistic, musical and software works; patent, which protects an invention or process for a fixed period; trademark, which protects a brand name, logo or slogan; and trade secret, which protects confidential business information such as a formula or a method.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Differentiate between free and open source software and proprietary software, with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

FOSS makes the source code available, so anyone may use, study, modify and redistribute the program; examples are Linux, Firefox and LibreOffice. Here 'free' refers to freedom rather than price. Proprietary software keeps its source code secret and grants only a licence to use it, forbidding copying, modification or redistribution; examples are Microsoft Windows and Adobe Photoshop. The practical difference is that with FOSS you can fix or extend the software yourself, while with proprietary software you depend entirely on the vendor.

Show Answer

(i) A poem is a creative work, protected by copyright, which arises automatically when it is created.
(ii) A new invention is protected by a patent, which has to be applied for.
(iii) A brand name or logo is protected by a trademark.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Explain the four main kinds of intellectual property.

Show Answer

Ans:

Copyright protects original literary, musical, artistic and software works, and lasts for the life of the author plus sixty years in India.
A patent protects a new invention for twenty years, and lets the owner stop others from making or selling it.
A trademark protects a name, logo or symbol that identifies a business or product, such as a brand name or a jingle.
A design right protects the shape, pattern or look of a manufactured article, and trade secrets protect information kept confidential by a business.

LONGL125 marks

12. What is cybercrime? Explain any four types with an example each, and give two ways of protecting yourself.

Show Answer

Ans:

Cybercrime is an illegal act carried out using a computer, a network or the internet.
Hacking: unauthorised access to a computer or account, for example guessing a password to read someone’s mail.
