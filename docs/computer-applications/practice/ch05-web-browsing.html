MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Google is an example of a — (a) browser (b) search engine (c) protocol (d) server operating system

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) search engine.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which stores a local copy of a page to speed up loading? (a) cookie (b) bookmark (c) cache (d) portal

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) cache.

MCQM31 mark

3. The entry page of a website is called the — (a) portal (b) home page (c) cache (d) feed

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) home page, usually index.html.

MCQM41 mark

4. The program that automatically visits and indexes web pages is called a — (a) router (b) crawler (c) modem (d) gateway

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) crawler or spider.

MCQM51 mark

5. A bookmark in a web browser is used to: (a) save the address of a page for quick access later (b) download the whole website (c) block pop-up windows (d) speed up the internet connection

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) save the address of a page for quick access later. Bookmarks store web addresses so that you can reopen a page with one click.

MCQM61 mark

6. Cookies are small files that: (a) a browser deletes after every page (b) speed up the router (c) a website stores on your device to remember you (d) protect the computer from viruses

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) a website stores on your device to remember you. Cookies let sites recognise returning visitors and keep information such as a login or a shopping cart.

MCQM71 mark

7. The address bar of a browser shows the: (a) URL of the page you are on (b) IP address of your own computer (c) size of the page (d) number of visitors to the site

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) URL of the page you are on. You can also type an address there to go to a site.

MCQM81 mark

8. A padlock symbol in the address bar means the page: (a) is served over an encrypted HTTPS connection (b) is free (c) is saved offline (d) belongs to the government

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) is served over an encrypted HTTPS connection. It does not prove that the site itself is honest.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Name any three web browsers.

Show Answer

Ans:

Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox and Microsoft Edge. Apple's Safari and Opera are other common examples.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate between a web browser and a search engine.

Show Answer

Ans:

A web browser is software installed on your computer or phone — Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari — that sends requests for pages and renders the HTML it receives into the page you see. A search engine such as Google is itself a website whose job is to index other websites and return a ranked list of results for your query. You need a browser in order to open a search engine, so they work together rather than as alternatives.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What are cookies and why are they used?

Show Answer

Ans:

A cookie is a small piece of data that a website stores in the user's browser. Websites use cookies to remember something about the user between visits — whether they are logged in, which language they chose, or what is in their shopping cart. Without cookies, every page request would be treated as a brand-new visitor. Because cookies can also be used to track browsing behaviour, browsers offer private modes that discard them when the window closes.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is caching and what is its benefit?

Show Answer

Ans:

Caching means keeping a local copy of a resource — a page, an image or a stylesheet — so that it does not have to be downloaded again. The benefits are that pages load faster and use less bandwidth, which matters most on slow or metered connections. The trade-off is that a cached copy can be out of date, which is why a forced refresh sometimes shows a different page.

SHORTS52 marks

5. How does a search engine find and rank pages?

Show Answer

Ans:

A search engine uses a crawler — also called a spider — that automatically visits web pages and follows the links on them to discover more. What it finds is stored in an index, a huge searchable catalogue. When you type a query, a ranking algorithm scores the matching pages by relevance, authority and quality, and returns them in order.

SHORTS62 marks

6. What is the difference between a web browser and a search engine?

Show Answer

Ans:

A browser is the program that requests, receives and displays web pages — Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari. A search engine is a website, such as Google or Bing, that keeps an index of pages and returns a list of results for your query. The browser is the tool you use; the search engine is a site you visit with it.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student says, “I opened Google to browse the internet, so Google is my browser.” Correct the error.

Show Answer

Ans:

Google is a search engine, a website that finds web pages for you.
A browser is the software, such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari, that you use to open any website, including Google.
You need a browser to reach a search engine, not the other way round.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. After shopping on a website, Amit keeps seeing ads for the same product on other sites, and the shop’s pages open faster the second time. Which effect is due to cookies and which to the cache?

Show Answer

Ans:

The ads following him come from cookies: small files that websites store on his device to recognise him and remember what he looked at.
The faster loading comes from the cache: a stored copy of images and pages that the browser reuses instead of downloading them again.
Cookies hold identity data; the cache is a speed copy.

SHORTS93 marks

9. What are cookies and cache, and why should you clear them sometimes?

Show Answer

Ans:

Cookies are small files a website stores on your computer to remember who you are, what is in your basket and what your preferences are.
The cache stores copies of images, scripts and pages so that a site loads faster the next time you visit it.
Clearing them removes old or corrupted files that can make a site load wrongly, frees disk space, logs you out of accounts and limits tracking.
The cost is that you have to sign in again and the first visit to a site is slower.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Neha logs in to her e-mail on a school computer and does not want any trace left on it. (a) Which browser mode should she use? (b) What does that mode not hide? (c) What should she do when she finishes?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) A private (incognito) window.
(b) It does not save history or cookies on that device after the window is closed, but the school network, the internet provider and the websites can still see her activity.
(c) Log out of her account and close the window; on a shared computer she can also clear the cache and cookies.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A page’s address bar shows a padlock and the address begins with https://. (a) What does the padlock tell you? (b) Does it prove that the site is trustworthy? (c) What should you check before typing a password?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The connection is encrypted, so data sent between the browser and the site cannot easily be read on the way.
(b) No. Fake or phishing sites can also use https; the padlock does not prove who owns the site.
(c) Check that the domain name is spelt correctly and is the official one, and do not follow unknown links.

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe five useful features of a web browser.

Show Answer

Ans:

Address bar: you type or paste a URL here, and it shows the address of the current page.
Tabs: several pages can be open in one window.
Bookmarks: save the address of a page for quick access later.
History: a list of pages visited, which helps you return to a page.
Downloads and settings: the browser manages downloaded files and lets you clear cookies and the cache or use private mode.
