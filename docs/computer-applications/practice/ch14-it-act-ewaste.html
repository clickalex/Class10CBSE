Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Which section of the IT Act deals with publishing obscene material electronically?

Show Answer

Ans:

Section 67 of the IT Act deals with publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is meant by digital citizenship?

Show Answer

Ans:

Digital citizenship is the responsible, respectful and safe use of technology. A good digital citizen protects their own and other people's privacy, respects intellectual property by not copying or pirating, communicates politely without abusing or bullying others, checks facts before sharing information, and reports harmful content instead of spreading it.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is a digital footprint and why should you manage it?

Show Answer

Ans:

A digital footprint is the trail of information you leave whenever you browse, search, post, comment, like a picture or fill in a form online. It is public and long-lasting: colleges and employers look at it, and once something is posted it is very hard to remove completely, so it is worth checking what your own footprint says about you.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is the IT Act, 2000? State any three of its key provisions.

Show Answer

Ans:

The Information Technology Act, 2000 is India's principal legislation governing electronic commerce and cybercrime, amended in 2008. Three key provisions are: it gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, so an online document or contract is as valid as a paper one; it defines cyber offences and their punishments, such as tampering with source documents under Section 65 and hacking under Section 66; and it provides a legal framework for electronic governance and secure electronic transactions, including penalties for identity theft under Section 66C and for publishing obscene material under Section 67.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is a digital footprint? Explain its two types.

Show Answer

Ans:

A digital footprint is the trail of data a person leaves behind while using the internet — accounts, posts, comments, photographs, search history and the metadata attached to them. It has two types. An active footprint is created deliberately, when you post on social media, fill in a form or upload a photograph. A passive footprint is collected without your direct action, such as your IP address, approximate location, browsing history and cookies. Both matter because the internet rarely forgets — content can be copied and screenshotted, and colleges and employers increasingly check what is publicly visible.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why is e-waste hazardous and how should it be managed?

Show Answer

Ans:

e-Waste is hazardous because discarded electronics contain toxic substances such as lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants. When dumped in ordinary landfills these chemicals leach into soil and groundwater, and burning the waste releases poisonous fumes that harm both workers and the surrounding population. At the same time e-waste contains recoverable gold, silver and copper, so throwing it away wastes valuable material. It should be managed through the 3R approach — reduce, reuse and recycle — by repairing and passing on working devices where possible and handing the rest to authorised e-waste recyclers or collection centres rather than to regular waste collection.

SHORTS93 marks

9. What does the Information Technology Act 2000 cover, and how should e-waste be handled?

Show Answer

Ans:

The Act gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, so that online documents and contracts are valid.
It defines cyber offences such as hacking, theft of data, identity theft, sending obscene material and spreading viruses, and sets fines and imprisonment for them.
It also created the framework for adjudicating officers and the Cyber Appellate Tribunal.
e-Waste must never be burnt or thrown into household garbage: under the E-Waste (Management) Rules it should be handed to an authorised recycler or returned to the manufacturer, because phones and computers contain lead, mercury and cadmium.
