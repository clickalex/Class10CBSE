Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. State the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

SHORTS22 marks

2. In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.

Show Answer

Ans:

By BPT, AD/DB = AE/EC.
1.5/3 = 1/EC, so EC = 2 cm.

SHORTS33 marks

3. The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 9:16. If one side of the smaller triangle is 4.5 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger.

Show Answer

Ans:

Area ratio = square of side ratio, so 9/16 = (4.5/x)².
Taking square roots: 3/4 = 4.5/x, so x = 6 cm.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that in a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides.

Show Answer

Ans:

In right ΔABC, right-angled at B, draw BD ⊥ AC.
ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA), so AD/AB = AB/AC, giving AB² = AD × AC.
ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA), so DC/BC = BC/AC, giving BC² = DC × AC.
Adding: AB² + BC² = (AD + DC) × AC = AC².

LONGL55 marks

5. Discuss “Triangles” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Similar polygons. Corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio.
Marks lens: Triangles carries most of the 15-mark Geometry unit (Circles is the rest).
Method / memory trick: Area ratio is the square of the side ratio. Sides in ratio 2:3 means areas in ratio 4:9. Forgetting the square is the classic error.
Do not: Writing the area ratio equal to the side ratio, without squaring.
