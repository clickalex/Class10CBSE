MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The volume of a cone of radius r and height h is: (a) πr²h (b) πr²h/3 (c) 2πr²h (d) πrh

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) πr²h/3.

MCQM21 mark

2. When a solid is melted and recast into another shape, which quantity is conserved? (a) surface area (b) volume (c) both (d) neither

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Volume. Surface area almost always changes.

MCQM31 mark

3. The slant height of a frustum with radii R and r and height h is: (a) √[h² + (R + r)²] (b) √[h² + (R − r)²] (c) h + R + r (d) √(R² − r²)

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) √[h² + (R − r)²].

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Surface Areas and Volumes” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Combinations of solids, conversion of solids, and the frustum..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Surface Areas and Volumes” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. The curved surface area of a cylinder of radius r and height h is: (a) 2πrh (b) πr²h (c) 2πr(r + h) (d) πrl

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 2πrh. 2πr(r + h) is the total surface area and πr²h is the volume.

MCQM71 mark

7. The volume of a sphere of radius r is: (a) (4/3)πr³ (b) 4πr² (c) (2/3)πr³ (d) πr³

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) (4/3)πr³. 4πr² is the surface area of the sphere.

MCQM81 mark

8. A cone, a hemisphere and a cylinder have equal bases and equal heights. The ratio of their volumes is: (a) 1 : 2 : 3 (b) 2 : 3 : 1 (c) 3 : 1 : 2 (d) 1 : 3 : 2

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 1 : 2 : 3. With radius = height = r: πr³/3 : 2πr³/3 : πr³.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm (π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

Total surface area of a solid hemisphere = 3πr² = 3 × (22/7) × 49 = 462 cm² (curved 308 + flat circular face 154).

SHORTS23 marks

2. A solid consists of a cylinder with a hemisphere on one end and a cone on the other, all of radius 7 cm. The cylinder is 20 cm high, the cone 12 cm high. Find the total surface area (take π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

Cylinder CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 880 cm².
Hemisphere CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 308 cm².
Cone slant height l = √(7² + 12²) = √193 ≈ 13.89 cm, so cone CSA = (22/7) × 7 × 13.89 ≈ 305.6 cm².
Total ≈ 880 + 308 + 305.6 = 1493.6 cm². The two circular joining faces are not counted.

SHORTS33 marks

3. A solid metallic sphere of radius 4.2 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 6 cm. Find the height of the cylinder.

Show Answer

Ans:

Volume of sphere = volume of cylinder.
(4/3)π(4.2)³ = π(6)²h.
h = (4 × 74.088)/(3 × 36) = 296.352/108 ≈ 2.74 cm.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Riya is revising “Surface Areas and Volumes” (Combinations of solids, conversion of solids, and the frustum.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Including the joining face in the total surface area. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Hemisphere on top of a cuboid or cylinder: the flat circular base of the hemisphere is not part of the outer surface. Use only its curved area, 2πr².
Why the mistake costs marks: Including the joining face in the total surface area.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Surface Areas and Volumes”: “A solid consists of a cylinder with a hemisphere on one end and a cone on the other, all of radius 7 cm. The cylinder is 20 cm high, the cone 12 cm high. Find the total surface area (take π = 22/7).” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Combinations of solids, conversion of solids, and the frustum..
Cylinder CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 880 cm². Hemisphere CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 308 cm². Cone slant height l = √(7² + 12²) = √193 ≈ 13.89 cm, so cone CSA = (22/7) × 7 × 13.89 ≈ 305.6 cm². Total ≈ 880 + 308 + 305.6 = 1493.6 cm². The two circular joining faces are not counted.

SHORTS63 marks

6. A cylindrical bucket 32 cm high with base radius 18 cm is emptied to form a conical heap of sand 24 cm high. Find the radius of the heap.

Show Answer

Ans:

Volumes are equal: π(18)²(32) = (1/3)πr²(24).
⇒ 324 × 32 = 8r² ⇒ r² = 10368/8 = 1296.
So r = 36 cm. (The height of the heap is ¼ of the cylinder’s, but the radius works out larger.)

SHORTS73 marks

7. A cylindrical vessel of radius 6 cm and height 12 cm is full of water. How many conical cups of radius 2 cm and height 6 cm can be filled from it?

Show Answer

Ans:

Volume of the vessel = π × 6² × 12 = 432π cm³.
Volume of one cup = (1/3)π × 2² × 6 = 8π cm³.
Number of cups = 432π ÷ 8π = 54.
So 54 conical cups can be filled.

LONGL84 marks

8. 100 solid iron spheres, each of radius 1 cm, are melted to form one solid sphere. Find its radius.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total volume is conserved: 100 × (4/3)π(1)³ = (4/3)πR³.
Cancelling (4/3)π: R³ = 100, so R = ∛100 ≈ 4.64 cm.

LONGL94 marks

9. A frustum of a cone has radii 14 cm and 7 cm and height 24 cm. Find its slant height and curved surface area (take π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

l = √[24² + (14 − 7)²] = √(576 + 49) = √625 = 25 cm.
CSA = π(R + r)l = (22/7) × (14 + 7) × 25 = (22/7) × 21 × 25 = 1650 cm².

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Two students attempt: “A solid consists of a cylinder with a hemisphere on one end and a cone on the other, all of radius 7 cm. The cylinder is 20 cm high, the cone 12 cm high. Find the total surface area (take π = 22/7).” Student A writes one line. Student B writes points, an example and a check. (a) Which answer frame should you use for full marks? (b) Write the model points. (c) Add one memory trick you would use on exam morning.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Statement → working or reasons in points → example or check. One-line answers lose step marks.
(b) Cylinder CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 880 cm². Hemisphere CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 308 cm². Cone slant height l = √(7² + 12²) = √193 ≈ 13.89 cm, so cone CSA = (22/7) × 7 × 13.89 ≈ 305.6 cm². Total ≈ 880 + 308 + 305.6 = 1493.6 cm². The two circular joining faces are not counted.
(c) Hemisphere on top of a cuboid or cylinder: the flat circular base of the hemisphere is not part of the outer surface. Use only its curved area, 2πr².

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. A drinking glass is shaped like a frustum of a cone with height 14 cm and end diameters 4 cm and 2 cm. Find its capacity.

Show Answer

Ans:

R = 2 cm, r = 1 cm, h = 14 cm.
Volume = (πh/3)(R² + r² + Rr) = (π × 14/3)(4 + 1 + 2) = (14π/3)(7).
= 98π/3 = (98/3)(22/7) = 308/3 ≈ 102.7 cm³.
