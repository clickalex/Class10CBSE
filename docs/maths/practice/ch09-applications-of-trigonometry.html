MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The angle of depression is always measured from: (a) the vertical (b) the horizontal (c) the object (d) the ground below

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) The horizontal at the observer’s end.

MCQM21 mark

2. If the angle of elevation of the sun is 45°, the length of the shadow of a pole equals: (a) half its height (b) its height (c) twice its height (d) √3 times its height

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Its height. tan 45° = 1.

MCQM31 mark

3. From the top of a tower the angle of depression of an object on the ground is 30°. The angle of elevation of the top from that object is: (a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 90°

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 30°. Alternate angles between parallel horizontals.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Some Applications of Trigonometry” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Heights and distances — angle of elevation and depression..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Some Applications of Trigonometry” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. When the shadow of a pole equals its height, the sun’s angle of elevation is: (a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 90°

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 45°. tan θ = height/shadow = 1, so θ = 45°.

MCQM71 mark

7. The angle made by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object is below the observer is the angle of: (a) elevation (b) depression (c) reflection (d) inclination

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) depression. Elevation looks up; depression looks down.

MCQM81 mark

8. A 10 m ladder reaches a window 8 m above the ground. Its foot is from the wall at a distance of: (a) 4 m (b) 6 m (c) 7 m (d) 9 m

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 6 m. By Pythagoras, √(100 − 64) = √36 = 6 m.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Show Answer

Ans:

In the right triangle, tan 60° = height ÷ distance, so √3 = h/30 and h = 30√3 m ≈ 51.96 m. Draw the triangle first — the tower is the perpendicular, the 30 m ground distance is the base, and the angle of elevation is at the foot. Unless asked for a decimal, 30√3 m is the exact answer and is preferred.

SHORTS23 marks

2. From the top of a 75 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships on the same side are 30° and 45°. Find the distance between the ships.

Show Answer

Ans:

For the ship at 45°: tan 45° = 75/x, so x = 75 m.
For the ship at 30°: tan 30° = 75/y, so y = 75√3 m.
Distance = 75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) ≈ 54.9 m.

APPLICATIONA33 marks

3. Riya is revising “Some Applications of Trigonometry” (Heights and distances — angle of elevation and depression.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Angle of depression equals the angle of elevation from the other end (alternate angles). So a depression problem becomes an elevation problem on a mirrored triangle.
Why the mistake costs marks: Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Some Applications of Trigonometry”: “The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height of the tower.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Heights and distances — angle of elevation and depression..
tan 60° = h/30, so √3 = h/30. h = 30√3 m ≈ 51.96 m.

SHORTS53 marks

5. The shadow of a tower is 20 m longer when the sun’s elevation is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the height be h. Shadow at 60° = h/tan 60° = h/√3; shadow at 30° = h/tan 30° = h√3.
h√3 − h/√3 = 20 ⇒ h(3 − 1)/√3 = 20 ⇒ 2h = 20√3.
So h = 10√3 m ≈ 17.3 m.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Two poles of heights 6 m and 11 m stand upright on level ground with their feet 12 m apart. Find the distance between their tops.

Show Answer

Ans:

The difference in height is 11 − 6 = 5 m and the horizontal gap is 12 m.
Distance between the tops = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 m.

SHORTS73 marks

7. A kite flies with 100 m of string out so that its height above the ground is 50 m. If the string is taut, find the angle of elevation of the kite.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the angle of elevation be θ. The string is the hypotenuse (100 m) and the height is the opposite side (50 m).
So sin θ = 50/100 = 1/2.
Therefore θ = 30°. The angle of elevation of the kite is 30°.

LONGL84 marks

8. A person on the bank of a river sees the top of a tree on the opposite bank at an angle of elevation of 60°. Walking 20 m away from the bank, the angle becomes 30°. Find the height of the tree and the width of the river.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the height be h and the width x.
From the nearer point: h = x√3. From the farther point: h = (x + 20)/√3.
Equating: x√3 = (x + 20)/√3, so 3x = x + 20 and x = 10 m.
Therefore h = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m and the river is 10 m wide.

10. Read and answer: Two students attempt: “The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height of the tower.” Student A writes one line. Student B writes points, an example and a check. (a) Which answer frame should you use for full marks? (b) Write the model points. (c) Add one memory trick you would use on exam morning.

Show Answer

(b) tan 60° = h/30, so √3 = h/30. h = 30√3 m ≈ 51.96 m.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower the angle of elevation of its top is 30°. After hoisting a flag on top, the angle of elevation of the top of the flag is 45°. Find the height of the flag.

Show Answer

Ans:

Height of the tower = 40 tan 30° = 40/√3 ≈ 23.1 m.
Height of tower + flag = 40 tan 45° = 40 m.
Height of the flag = 40 − 40/√3 = 40(1 − 1/√3) ≈ 16.9 m.

Marks lens: The remaining 4–5 marks of the 12-mark Trigonometry unit.
Method / memory trick: Angle of depression equals the angle of elevation from the other end (alternate angles). So a depression problem becomes an elevation problem on a mirrored triangle.
Do not: Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical.
