MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The probability of an impossible event is: (a) 1 (b) 0 (c) 1/2 (d) undefined

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 0.

MCQM21 mark

2. Two dice are thrown together. The total number of outcomes is: (a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 36 (d) 24

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 36.

MCQM31 mark

3. The number of face cards in a deck of 52 cards is: (a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 16 (d) 4

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 12. Jacks, queens and kings of four suits.

MCQM41 mark

4. A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. The probability that a ball drawn at random is not red is: (a) 2/5 (b) 3/5 (c) 4/6 (d) 1/6

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 3/5. Blue balls = 6 out of 10, so P(not red) = 6/10 = 3/5.

MCQM51 mark

5. The probability of getting an even prime number in one throw of a die is: (a) 1/2 (b) 1/3 (c) 0 (d) 1/6

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1/6. The only even prime is 2, which is one of the six equally likely outcomes.

MCQM61 mark

6. In one throw of a die, the probability of getting a number less than 7 is: (a) 0 (b) 1/6 (c) 5/6 (d) 1

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1. Every outcome 1–6 is less than 7, so it is a sure event.

MCQM71 mark

7. If P(E) = 0.37, then P(not E) = : (a) 0.37 (b) 0.63 (c) 1 (d) 0.53

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 0.63. P(not E) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.37 = 0.63.

MCQM81 mark

8. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. The probability of a black queen is: (a) 1/52 (b) 1/26 (c) 1/13 (d) 2/13

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 1/26. Two black queens out of 52 cards gives 2/52 = 1/26.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a red king?

Show Answer

Ans:

There are 2 red kings, so P = 2/52 = 1/26.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting (i) a prime number, (ii) a number greater than 4.

Show Answer

Ans:

Primes on a die are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so P = 2/6 = 1/3.

SHORTS32 marks

3. A bag contains 5 red and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is not red.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total outcomes = 5 + 3 = 8. Not red means green, so P(not red) = 3/8. Equivalently, using the complement rule: P(not red) = 1 − P(red) = 1 − 5/8 = 3/8. The complement route is quicker when the event you are asked about has more outcomes than the one you are not.

SHORTS42 marks

4. A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue and 3 green balls. Find the probability of drawing a ball that is not green.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total balls = 12. Balls that are not green = 4 + 5 = 9. So P(not green) = 9/12 = 3/4.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is 9.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total outcomes = 36.
Favourable pairs: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) — 4 outcomes.
P = 4/36 = 1/9.

SHORTS63 marks

6. A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability that it is (i) a face card, (ii) neither a king nor a red card.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) There are 12 face cards, so P = 12/52 = 3/13.
(ii) Red cards are 26, of which 2 are kings. Neither a king nor red means a black card that is not a king: 26 − 2 = 24.
P = 24/52 = 6/13.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student says, “Two dice are thrown, so there are 6 + 6 = 12 outcomes and P(sum = 7) = 6/12 = 1/2.” Find the error and give the correct probability.

Show Answer

Ans:

Error: each die has 6 outcomes, so two dice have 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes, not 12.
Favourable outcomes for a sum of 7: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1) — 6 outcomes.
P(sum = 7) = 6/36 = 1/6.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. A die has the letters A, B, C, D, E, A on its six faces. It is thrown once. Find the probability of getting (i) the letter A and (ii) the letter D.

Show Answer

Ans:

There are 6 equally likely outcomes.
(i) The letter A appears on 2 faces, so P(A) = 2/6 = 1/3.
(ii) The letter D appears on 1 face, so P(D) = 1/6.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Two coins are tossed together. Find the probability of getting (i) two heads, (ii) at least one tail, (iii) at most one head.

Show Answer

Ans:

Sample space = {HH, HT, TH, TT}: 4 equally likely outcomes.
(i) Two heads: only HH, so P = 1/4.
(ii) At least one tail: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.
(iii) At most one head: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A bag contains 5 red, 8 white and 7 black balls. One ball is drawn at random. (a) Find the probability that it is red. (b) Find the probability that it is not white. (c) Find the probability that it is black or white.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Total balls = 20, so P(red) = 5/20 = 1/4.
(b) P(not white) = 1 − 8/20 = 12/20 = 3/5.
(c) P(black or white) = (7 + 8)/20 = 15/20 = 3/4.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: In a school raffle, 200 tickets numbered 1 to 200 are sold and one ticket is drawn at random. (a) What is the probability that the number is a multiple of 10? (b) What is the probability that it is a perfect square? (c) What is the probability that it is not a multiple of 5?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Multiples of 10 up to 200: 20, so P = 20/200 = 1/10.
(b) Perfect squares up to 200: 1², 2², …, 14² (14² = 196), so 14 of them and P = 14/200 = 7/100.
(c) Multiples of 5: 40, so P(not a multiple of 5) = 1 − 40/200 = 4/5.

APPLICATIONA124 marks

12. A box contains 90 discs numbered 1 to 90. One disc is drawn at random. Find the probability that it bears (i) a two-digit number, (ii) a number divisible by 5, (iii) a perfect square number.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) Two-digit numbers are 10 to 90, i.e. 81 discs, so P = 81/90 = 9/10.
(ii) Multiples of 5 from 5 to 90: 18 numbers, so P = 18/90 = 1/5.
(iii) Perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 — nine numbers, so P = 9/90 = 1/10.

LONGL135 marks

13. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that (i) the sum is less than 5, (ii) the product of the numbers is 12, (iii) a doublet is obtained, (iv) the sum is a prime number.

Show Answer

Ans:

The sample space has 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely outcomes.
(i) Sum 2, 3 or 4: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 outcomes, so P = 6/36 = 1/6.
(ii) Product 12: (2,6), (3,4), (4,3), (6,2) — 4 outcomes, so P = 4/36 = 1/9.
(iii) Doublets (1,1) … (6,6): 6 outcomes, so P = 6/36 = 1/6.
(iv) Prime sums 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 occur 1 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 2 = 15 times, so P = 15/36 = 5/12.
