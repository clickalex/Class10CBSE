Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a red king?

Show Answer

Ans:

There are 2 red kings, so P = 2/52 = 1/26.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting (i) a prime number, (ii) a number greater than 4.

Show Answer

Ans:

Primes on a die are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so P = 2/6 = 1/3.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is 9.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total outcomes = 36.
Favourable pairs: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) — 4 outcomes.
P = 4/36 = 1/9.

SHORTS42 marks

4. A bag contains 5 red and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is not red.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total = 8. P(not red) = P(green) = 3/8.
Equivalently, 1 − 5/8 = 3/8.

SHORTS53 marks

5. A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability that it is (i) a face card, (ii) neither a king nor a red card.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) There are 12 face cards, so P = 12/52 = 3/13.
(ii) Red cards are 26, of which 2 are kings. Neither a king nor red means a black card that is not a king: 26 − 2 = 24.
P = 24/52 = 6/13.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Probability” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Classical definition. P(E) = (number of outcomes favourable to E) / (total number of possible outcomes), assuming all outcomes are equally likely.
Marks lens: The remaining 3–4 marks of the 11-mark unit.
Method / memory trick: Always write the total number of outcomes first. Half of all probability errors are wrong totals, not wrong favourable counts.
Do not: Forgetting that a die has 6 outcomes, not 6 faces times something — and that two dice have 36, not 12.
