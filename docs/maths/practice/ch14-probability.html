MCQM61 mark

6. In one throw of a die, the probability of getting a number less than 7 is: (a) 0 (b) 1/6 (c) 5/6 (d) 1

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1. Every outcome 1–6 is less than 7, so it is a sure event.

MCQM71 mark

7. If P(E) = 0.37, then P(not E) = : (a) 0.37 (b) 0.63 (c) 1 (d) 0.53

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 0.63. P(not E) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.37 = 0.63.

MCQM81 mark

8. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. The probability of a black queen is: (a) 1/52 (b) 1/26 (c) 1/13 (d) 2/13

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 1/26. Two black queens out of 52 cards gives 2/52 = 1/26.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a red king?

Show Answer

Ans:

There are 2 red kings, so P = 2/52 = 1/26.

SHORTS22 marks

2. A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting (i) a prime number, (ii) a number greater than 4.

Show Answer

Ans:

Primes on a die are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so P = 2/6 = 1/3.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is 9.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total outcomes = 36.
Favourable pairs: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) — 4 outcomes.
P = 4/36 = 1/9.

SHORTS42 marks

4. A bag contains 5 red and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is not red.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total = 8. P(not red) = P(green) = 3/8.
Equivalently, 1 − 5/8 = 3/8.

SHORTS53 marks

5. A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability that it is (i) a face card, (ii) neither a king nor a red card.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) There are 12 face cards, so P = 12/52 = 3/13.
(ii) Red cards are 26, of which 2 are kings. Neither a king nor red means a black card that is not a king: 26 − 2 = 24.
P = 24/52 = 6/13.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Probability” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Classical definition. P(E) = (number of outcomes favourable to E) / (total number of possible outcomes), assuming all outcomes are equally likely.
Marks lens: The remaining 3–4 marks of the 11-mark unit.
Method / memory trick: Always write the total number of outcomes first. Half of all probability errors are wrong totals, not wrong favourable counts.
Do not: Forgetting that a die has 6 outcomes, not 6 faces times something — and that two dice have 36, not 12.

SHORTS112 marks

11. A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue and 3 green balls. Find the probability of drawing a ball that is not green.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total balls = 12. Balls that are not green = 4 + 5 = 9. So P(not green) = 9/12 = 3/4.

SHORTS123 marks

12. Two coins are tossed together. Find the probability of getting (i) two heads, (ii) at least one tail, (iii) at most one head.

Show Answer

Ans:

Sample space = {HH, HT, TH, TT}: 4 equally likely outcomes.
(i) Two heads: only HH, so P = 1/4.
(ii) At least one tail: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.
(iii) At most one head: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.

APPLICATIONA134 marks

13. A box contains 90 discs numbered 1 to 90. One disc is drawn at random. Find the probability that it bears (i) a two-digit number, (ii) a number divisible by 5, (iii) a perfect square number.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) Two-digit numbers are 10 to 90, i.e. 81 discs, so P = 81/90 = 9/10.
(ii) Multiples of 5 from 5 to 90: 18 numbers, so P = 18/90 = 1/5.
(iii) Perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 — nine numbers, so P = 9/90 = 1/10.
