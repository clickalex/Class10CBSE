MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The number of tangents from a point on the circle is: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) infinite

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 1.

MCQM21 mark

2. The angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact is: (a) 45° (b) 60° (c) 90° (d) 180°

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 90°.

MCQM31 mark

3. Two tangents from an external point to a circle are: (a) unequal (b) equal (c) always perpendicular (d) parallel

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Equal.

MCQM41 mark

4. A tangent to a circle meets the circle in: (a) no point (b) exactly one point (c) exactly two points (d) infinitely many points

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) exactly one point. By definition, a tangent touches the circle at exactly one point, the point of contact.

MCQM51 mark

5. If the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point P to a circle of radius a and centre O is 60°, then OP = (a) 2a (b) a√3 (c) a√2 (d) a/2

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 2a. ∠APB = 60° gives ∠APO = 30° and, in right triangle OAP, OA = OP sin 30°, so OP = a/(1/2) = 2a.

MCQM61 mark

6. The number of tangents that can be drawn from a point outside a circle is: (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) infinite

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 2. Exactly two, and they are equal in length.

MCQM71 mark

7. A line that cuts a circle in two distinct points is called a: (a) tangent (b) secant (c) chord (d) diameter

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) secant. A tangent meets the circle at one point; a chord is the segment joining the two points.

MCQM81 mark

8. A tangent of length 24 cm is drawn from a point 25 cm from the centre. The radius is: (a) 5 cm (b) 7 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 24 cm

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 7 cm. r² + 24² = 25², so r = √49 = 7 cm.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. How many tangents can be drawn from a point inside a circle?

Show Answer

Ans:

None. A line through an interior point must cut the circle at two points.

SHORTS22 marks

2. From a point 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm, a tangent is drawn. Find its length.

Show Answer

Ans:

The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so the three lengths form a right triangle.
Length = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. If ∠POQ = 110°, find ∠PTQ.

Show Answer

Ans:

The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, so ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°. In quadrilateral POQT: ∠PTQ = 360° − (90° + 90° + 110°) = 70°.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let P be the external point and PA, PB the tangents with points of contact A and B.
Join OA, OB and OP.
In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: OA = OB (radii), OP is common, and ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius).
By RHS congruence, ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP, so PA = PB.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student says, “From every point P in the plane, exactly two equal tangents can be drawn to a given circle.” Test the claim for a point outside the circle, on the circle and inside the circle.

Show Answer

Ans:

P outside the circle: exactly two tangents can be drawn, and their lengths are equal.
P on the circle: exactly one tangent can be drawn (at P itself); there is no pair of tangents.
P inside the circle: no tangent exists, since every line through P cuts the circle in two points. So the claim holds only for an external point.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. The tangent at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at Q, where OQ = 12 cm. Find the length PQ.

Show Answer

Ans:

The tangent at P is perpendicular to the radius OP, so ∠OPQ = 90°.
In the right triangle OPQ: PQ² = OQ² − OP² = 144 − 25 = 119.
PQ = √119 cm ≈ 10.9 cm.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Prove that a chord of the larger of two concentric circles which touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at P; O is the common centre.
OP is a radius of the smaller circle and AB is a tangent to it, so OP ⊥ AB.
In the larger circle, the perpendicular drawn from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
Hence AP = PB: the chord is bisected at the point of contact.

LONGL83 marks

8. Prove that the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line joining the points of contact at the centre.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let TP and TQ be tangents from T, touching the circle at P and Q.
∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90° because a radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
In quadrilateral POQT the angles sum to 360°, so ∠PTQ + ∠POQ = 360° − 180° = 180°.
Therefore the two angles are supplementary.

LONGL94 marks

9. A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the tangent segments from A, B, C, D be p, q, r, s respectively.
Then AB = p + q, BC = q + r, CD = r + s and AD = p + s.
AB + CD = p + q + r + s and AD + BC = p + s + q + r.
The two sums are identical, so AB + CD = AD + BC.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: From an external point T, two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O. It is given that ∠PTQ = 70°. (a) State why ∠OPT = 90°. (b) Find ∠POQ. (c) Find ∠OPQ.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The tangent at a point is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
(b) In quadrilateral OPTQ, ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°, so ∠POQ = 360° − 90° − 90° − 70° = 110°.
(c) TP = TQ, so in ΔTPQ, ∠TPQ = (180° − 70°)/2 = 55°. Then ∠OPQ = ∠OPT − ∠TPQ = 90° − 55° = 35°.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A circle is inscribed in ΔABC and touches the sides BC, CA and AB at D, E and F respectively. AB = 12 cm, BC = 8 cm and CA = 10 cm. (a) Which tangent lengths are equal? (b) Find AF, BD and CE. (c) Verify your answers against the three given sides.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Tangents from one point are equal: AF = AE, BF = BD and CD = CE.
(b) Let AF = x, BD = y, CE = z. Then x + y = 12, y + z = 8, z + x = 10, so x + y + z = 15. Hence AF = 7 cm, BD = 5 cm, CE = 3 cm.
(c) AB = AF + FB = 7 + 5 = 12 ✓; BC = BD + DC = 5 + 3 = 8 ✓; CA = CE + EA = 3 + 7 = 10 ✓.

LONGL125 marks

12. Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

Show Answer

Ans:

Given: a circle with centre O and a tangent XY touching it at P. To prove: OP ⊥ XY.
Take any other point Q on XY and join OQ.
The tangent meets the circle only at P, so Q lies outside the circle. Hence OQ is longer than the radius: OQ > OP.
This is true for every point Q other than P on XY, so OP is the shortest distance from O to the line XY.
The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular, so OP ⊥ XY. ∎
