Ch 1 · Real Numbers (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. The HCF of 8 and 12 is: (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 24

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 4. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3; the lowest power of the common prime 2 is 2² = 4.

MCQM21 mark

2. Which has a terminating decimal expansion? (a) 7/24 (b) 5/13 (c) 9/40 (d) 11/21

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 9/40. 40 = 2³ × 5, of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ. The others have denominators with other primes.

MCQM31 mark

3. If the HCF of two numbers is 6 and their LCM is 60, one number being 12, the other is: (a) 20 (b) 30 (c) 40 (d) 60

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 30. Other number = (6 × 60)/12 = 30.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Real Numbers” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is HCF/LCM, irrationality proofs, terminating decimals..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Real Numbers” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. The decimal expansion of 23/(2³ × 5²) terminates after: (a) 2 places (b) 3 places (c) 4 places (d) 5 places

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 3 places. 23/200 = 115/1000 = 0.115. The number of decimal places is the larger exponent, max(3, 2) = 3.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which of these is irrational? (a) √(16/9) (b) √12 (c) 0.444… (d) 22/7

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) √12. √12 = 2√3 and √3 is irrational. √(16/9) = 4/3, 0.444… = 4/9 and 22/7 are all rational.

MCQM81 mark

8. The HCF of the smallest prime number and the smallest composite number is: (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 2. Smallest prime = 2, smallest composite = 4, and HCF(2, 4) = 2.

Ch 2 · Polynomials (8)

MCQM11 mark

MCQM61 mark

6. A polynomial of degree 3 is called: (a) linear (b) quadratic (c) cubic (d) biquadratic

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) cubic. Degree 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic and 4 is biquadratic.

MCQM71 mark

7. If one zero of x² − 4x + 1 is 2 + √3, the other zero is: (a) 2 − √3 (b) −2 − √3 (c) √3 − 2 (d) −√3

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 2 − √3. Sum of zeroes = 4, so the other zero = 4 − (2 + √3) = 2 − √3.

MCQM81 mark

8. A quadratic polynomial can have at most: (a) 0 zeroes (b) 1 zero (c) 2 zeroes (d) 3 zeroes

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.

Ch 3 · Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. The pair x + y = 5 and 2x + 2y = 10 represents: (a) intersecting lines (b) parallel lines (c) coincident lines (d) none

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Coincident lines. All three ratios equal 1/2.

MCQM21 mark

2. For what value of k does kx + 3y = 7 and 2x + 6y = 14 have infinitely many solutions? (a) 1 (b) −1 (c) 2 (d) 1/2

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 1. We need k/2 = 3/6 = 7/14, and 3/6 = 1/2, so k = 1.

MCQM31 mark

3. If a boat’s downstream speed is 12 km/h and upstream speed is 8 km/h, its still-water speed is: (a) 10 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 20 km/h

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 10 km/h. Still-water speed = (downstream + upstream)/2.

MCQM41 mark

MCQM51 mark

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. The pair 6x − 3y + 10 = 0 and 2x − y + 9 = 0 has: (a) one solution (b) two solutions (c) infinitely many solutions (d) no solution

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) no solution. 6/2 = 3 and (−3)/(−1) = 3 but 10/9 ≠ 3, so the lines are parallel and distinct.

MCQM71 mark

7. If x + 2y = 5 and 3x + ky = 15 are coincident lines, then k = : (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) 15

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 6. Coincident lines need 1/3 = 2/k = 5/15, and 2/k = 1/3 gives k = 6.

MCQM81 mark

8. The solution of x + y = 14 and x − y = 4 is: (a) x = 9, y = 5 (b) x = 5, y = 9 (c) x = 10, y = 4 (d) x = 8, y = 6

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) x = 9, y = 5. Adding the equations gives 2x = 18, so x = 9 and then y = 5.

MCQM61 mark

Show Answer

MCQM71 mark

MCQM71 mark
