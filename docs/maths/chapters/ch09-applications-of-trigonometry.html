🎯 Marks lens

The remaining 4–5 marks of the 12-mark Trigonometry unit.
Almost always a 3–4 mark word problem. The CBSE syllabus restricts this to two right triangles — no more than two lines of sight.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Line of sight is the line from the observer’s eye to the object being observed.
2. Angle of elevation is the angle the line of sight makes with the horizontal when the object is above the horizontal.
3. Angle of depression is the angle the line of sight makes with the horizontal when the object is below the horizontal. Measured from the horizontal at the observer’s end, never from the vertical.
4. Method. Draw the right triangle, mark the given angle, choose the ratio that involves the known side and the unknown side, then solve. Only 30°, 45° and 60° are used in the board paper.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • tan θ = opposite / adjacent
  • For θ = 45°: height = horizontal distance
  • For θ = 60°: height = distance × √3
  • For θ = 30°: height = distance / √3

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Draw the diagram before writing anything else. Mark the observer, the horizontal and the object.
  2. If the observer has height, subtract it: the triangle starts at eye level, not ground level.
  3. Label the known side and the unknown side.
  4. Pick the ratio involving both — almost always tan.
  5. Solve, then add back any height you subtracted, and give the answer with units.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Angle of depression equals the angle of elevation from the other end (alternate angles). So a depression problem becomes an elevation problem on a mirrored triangle.
  • √3 ≈ 1.732 is usually given in the question. If the answer is expected as 10√3, leave it as a surd unless told otherwise.
  • If the problem has two angles from two positions, you get two equations in the same height. Equate them.
  • “From the foot” of a tower means the horizontal distance starts at the base.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical.
  • Forgetting to add the observer’s eye height back to the final answer.
  • Using the wrong pair of sides in the ratio — check which side is opposite the marked angle.
  • Drawing the horizontal from the object instead of from the observer.

SHORTS13 marks

1. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Show Answer

Ans:

tan 60° = h/30, so √3 = h/30.
h = 30√3 m ≈ 51.96 m.

SHORTS23 marks

2. From the top of a 75 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships on the same side are 30° and 45°. Find the distance between the ships.

Show Answer

Ans:

For the ship at 45°: tan 45° = 75/x, so x = 75 m.
For the ship at 30°: tan 30° = 75/y, so y = 75√3 m.
Distance = 75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) ≈ 54.9 m.

LONGL34 marks

3. A person on the bank of a river sees the top of a tree on the opposite bank at an angle of elevation of 60°. Walking 20 m away from the bank, the angle becomes 30°. Find the height of the tree and the width of the river.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the height be h and the width x.
From the nearer point: h = x√3. From the farther point: h = (x + 20)/√3.
Equating: x√3 = (x + 20)/√3, so 3x = x + 20 and x = 10 m.
Therefore h = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m and the river is 10 m wide.
