Chapter 12 · Surface Areas and Volumes

Chapter 12 of 14. A table of formulas plus three standard question types. The frustum is worth learning separately.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

The rest of the 10-mark Mensuration unit, roughly 5–6 marks.
Expect one question on a combination of solids (3–4 marks) and one on conversion of solids or a frustum (3–4 marks).
Surface area questions almost always involve removing a surface where two solids join — that is where the marks are.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Combinations of solids. A solid made of two or more basic solids. Total surface area = sum of the curved/lateral surface areas actually exposed. Where two solids join, the joining face is not part of the outer surface.
2. Volume is additive. Unlike surface area, the volume of a combination is simply the sum of the volumes of its parts.
3. Conversion of solids. When one solid is melted and recast into another, or many small solids are combined into one, the volume stays the same. Set volume before = volume after and solve.
4. Frustum of a cone. The part of a cone left after a smaller cone is cut off the top by a plane parallel to the base. Its slant height is l = √[h² + (R − r)²].

5. Cylindrical hole drilled through a solid. Subtract the cylinder’s volume, but its curved surface is now exposed, so add it to the surface area. Then remove the two circular faces where it was drilled.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Cylinder: CSA = 2πrh, V = πr²h
  • Cone: CSA = πrl, V = πr²h/3, l = √(r² + h²)
  • Sphere: SA = 4πr², V = 4πr³/3
  • Hemisphere: CSA = 2πr², TSA = 3πr², V = 2πr³/3
  • Frustum: l = √[h² + (R − r)²], CSA = π(R + r)l
  • Frustum volume = πh(R² + r² + Rr)/3
  • Total surface area of a frustum = π(R + r)l + πR² + πr²

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Sketch the solid and label every dimension, including any that must be inferred (for example the height of a cone placed on a cylinder).
  2. Decide what is exposed. Cross out joining faces on the sketch.
  3. Write the formula for each part separately before substituting anything.
  4. For conversion problems, write volume before = volume after first, then substitute.
  5. Give the final answer with units, and to the number of decimal places asked (usually two, with π = 22/7 or 3.14 as specified).

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Hemisphere on top of a cuboid or cylinder: the flat circular base of the hemisphere is not part of the outer surface. Use only its curved area, 2πr².
  • Cone on top of a cylinder with the same radius: the joining circular face disappears from the surface area, but both heights add in the volume.
  • Conversion problems are one equation. Number of small spheres × volume of one = volume of the big one. Do not compute individual volumes first.
  • Frustum slant height uses (R − r), not (R + r). That single sign decides the whole answer.
  • When a question says “slant height” is given, do not recompute it from r and h.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Including the joining face in the total surface area.
  • Using πr²h for a cone instead of πr²h/3.
  • Forgetting to divide by 3 for cones and pyramids.
  • In conversion problems, equating surface areas instead of volumes. Melting preserves volume, never surface area.
  • Writing the answer without units, or with the wrong unit (cm² for area, cm³ for volume).

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm (π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

Total surface area of a solid hemisphere = 3πr² = 3 × (22/7) × 49 = 462 cm² (curved 308 + flat circular face 154).

SHORTS23 marks

2. A solid consists of a cylinder with a hemisphere on one end and a cone on the other, all of radius 7 cm. The cylinder is 20 cm high, the cone 12 cm high. Find the total surface area (take π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

Cylinder CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 880 cm².
Hemisphere CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 49 = 308 cm².
Cone slant height l = √(7² + 12²) = √193 ≈ 13.89 cm, so cone CSA = (22/7) × 7 × 13.89 ≈ 305.6 cm².
Total ≈ 880 + 308 + 305.6 = 1493.6 cm². The two circular joining faces are not counted.

SHORTS33 marks

3. A solid metallic sphere of radius 4.2 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 6 cm. Find the height of the cylinder.

Show Answer

Ans:

Volume of sphere = volume of cylinder.
(4/3)π(4.2)³ = π(6)²h.
h = (4 × 74.088)/(3 × 36) = 296.352/108 ≈ 2.74 cm.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Surface Areas and Volumes”: “A solid consists of a cylinder with a hemisphere on one end and a cone on the other, all of radius 7 cm. The cylinder is 20 cm high, the cone 12 cm high. Find the total surface area (take π = 22/7).” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Combinations of solids, conversion of solids, and the frustum..

SHORTS63 marks

6. A cylindrical bucket 32 cm high with base radius 18 cm is emptied to form a conical heap of sand 24 cm high. Find the radius of the heap.

Show Answer

Ans:

Volumes are equal: π(18)²(32) = (1/3)πr²(24).
⇒ 324 × 32 = 8r² ⇒ r² = 10368/8 = 1296.
So r = 36 cm. (The height of the heap is ¼ of the cylinder’s, but the radius works out larger.)

LONGL74 marks

7. 100 solid iron spheres, each of radius 1 cm, are melted to form one solid sphere. Find its radius.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total volume is conserved: 100 × (4/3)π(1)³ = (4/3)πR³.
Cancelling (4/3)π: R³ = 100, so R = ∛100 ≈ 4.64 cm.

LONGL84 marks

8. A frustum of a cone has radii 14 cm and 7 cm and height 24 cm. Find its slant height and curved surface area (take π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

l = √[24² + (14 − 7)²] = √(576 + 49) = √625 = 25 cm.
CSA = π(R + r)l = (22/7) × (14 + 7) × 25 = (22/7) × 21 × 25 = 1650 cm².

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. A drinking glass is shaped like a frustum of a cone with height 14 cm and end diameters 4 cm and 2 cm. Find its capacity.

Show Answer

Ans:

R = 2 cm, r = 1 cm, h = 14 cm.
Volume = (πh/3)(R² + r² + Rr) = (π × 14/3)(4 + 1 + 2) = (14π/3)(7).
= 98π/3 = (98/3)(22/7) = 308/3 ≈ 102.7 cm³.

✍️ Hands-on task

Take a cylindrical container and a cone of the same radius and height. Fill the cone with water and pour it into the cylinder three times — it should exactly fill it. That is the 1/3 in the cone formula.
