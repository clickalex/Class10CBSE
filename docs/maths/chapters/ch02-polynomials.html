Chapter 2 · Polynomials

Chapter 2 of 14. A short chapter. The whole of it reduces to one relationship and one division.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →Mock-test this chapter (timed, scored) →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Usually one 2-mark question plus part of an objective question.
Asked as: count zeroes from a graph, find k given a relationship between zeroes, or a division-algorithm problem with a remainder condition.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Zeroes of a polynomial. A zero of p(x) is a value of x for which p(x) = 0. Geometrically it is where the graph cuts or touches the x-axis.

Graph behaviourNumber of zeroes
Cuts the x-axis at two points2 distinct real zeroes
Touches the x-axis at one point2 equal real zeroes (the vertex lies on the axis)
Does not meet the x-axisNo real zeroes

2. Relationship between zeroes and coefficients. For ax² + bx + c with zeroes α and β: sum α + β = −b/a and product αβ = c/a.
3. Forming a polynomial from its zeroes. x² − (sum)x + (product). Multiply through to clear fractions.
4. Division algorithm. Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder, with degree(remainder) < degree(divisor).

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • α + β = −b/a
  • αβ = c/a
  • p(x) = x² − (α + β)x + αβ
  • p(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x), deg r < deg g

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Read off a, b, c from the polynomial, signs included, and note the degree — most mistakes here start with a copied sign.
  2. For zeroes, either factorise (split the middle term or use an identity) or apply the quadratic formula. Verify by substituting each zero back into p(x).
  3. For sum and product questions use the relations directly: sum of zeroes = −b/a, product = c/a. For a cubic, add the third relation: sum of pairwise products = c/a and product = −d/a.
  4. To build a polynomial from given zeroes, use k[x² − (sum)x + (product)]. Finish by finding k from any extra condition, then write the polynomial with all terms expanded.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Sign trap: the sum is minus b/a. Almost every wrong answer here has the wrong sign.
  • Given the graph, count crossings of the x-axis — touching counts as two equal zeroes, not one.
  • “Zeroes are reciprocal” ⇒ product = 1, i.e. c = a. “Equal in magnitude but opposite in sign” ⇒ sum = 0, i.e. b = 0.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing the sum as +b/a. It is −b/a.
  • When asked to form the quadratic, leaving it as x² − (5/2)x + 3. Clear the fraction: 2x² − 5x + 6.
  • Confusing “number of zeroes” with “number of times the graph touches” when the vertex sits on the axis.
  • In division problems, forgetting that the remainder’s degree must be less than the divisor’s.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.

Show Answer

Ans:

x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

SHORTS22 marks

2. If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.

Show Answer

Ans:

A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

SHORTS43 marks

4. If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

SHORTS73 marks

7. If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 6x + k and 3α + 2β = 20, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 6 (sum of zeroes) and 3α + 2β = 20.
Subtract twice the first equation from the second: α = 20 − 12 = 8, so β = −2.
Product αβ = 8(−2) = −16, and product = c/a = k, so k = −16.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Divide 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5 by x² + 2x + 1 and verify the division algorithm.

Show Answer

Ans:

Quotient = 3x − 5, remainder = 9x + 10.
Check: (x² + 2x + 1)(3x − 5) + 9x + 10
= 3x³ + 6x² + 3x − 5x² − 10x − 5 + 9x + 10
= 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5, which is the dividend — so dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. A quadratic polynomial cuts the x-axis at −2 and 3 and its graph passes through (0, 6). Find the polynomial.

Show Answer

Ans:

With zeroes −2 and 3, p(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 3).
It passes through (0, 6), so 6 = a(2)(−3) = −6a, giving a = −1.
Hence p(x) = −(x + 2)(x − 3) = −x² + x + 6.

✍️ Hands-on task

Pick any quadratic with integer zeroes. Compute sum and product from the zeroes, then check them against −b/a and c/a. Do one where a ≠ 1 — that is the case students get wrong.
