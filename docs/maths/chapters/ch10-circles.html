Chapter 10 · Circles

Chapter 10 of 14. Two theorems and a handful of standard figures cover this chapter.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

The rest of the 15-mark Geometry unit, roughly 5–6 marks.
Expect a tangent-length proof (3 marks) and objective questions on the number of tangents from a point.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Tangent. A line that touches the circle at exactly one point. That point is the point of contact.
2. Theorem 10.1. The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
3. Theorem 10.2. The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Position of the pointNumber of tangents
Inside the circleNone
On the circleExactly one
Outside the circleExactly two

4. Standard consequences. A tangent is parallel to the tangent at the opposite end of a diameter; a parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus; and the angle between two tangents from an external point is supplementary to the angle subtended by the chord of contact at the centre.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Tangent ⊥ radius at the point of contact
  • PA = PB for tangents from an external point P
  • Tangent length = √(d² − r²), where d is the distance from the centre

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Note whether the line touches (tangent, one point), cuts (secant, two points) or misses the circle — the number of intersections decides the whole theorem.
  2. Recall the two core facts: the tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, and tangents from an external point are equal in length.
  3. When two tangents are drawn from an external point, join the centre to that point: it bisects the angle between the tangents and the line joining the points of contact is bisected by it.
  4. In the proof, name the theorem you used at each step and finish with the angle or length asked for, with units where relevant.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Always join the centre to the point of contact. That creates the right angle you need, and it is the first line of almost every proof here.
  • For a triangle circumscribing a circle, let the tangent segments from each vertex be x, y, z. The three side lengths give three equations, and the perimeter is 2(x + y + z).
  • “Prove the tangents are equal” is done by joining the centre to both points of contact and to the external point, then using RHS congruence.
  • The angle between two tangents and the angle at the centre add to 180°.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting to state that the radius is perpendicular to the tangent — that is a marked step.
  • Assuming the two tangent lengths are equal when the point is inside or on the circle.
  • In the circumscribed-triangle problem, mixing up which segment belongs to which vertex.
  • Writing “the angle is 90°” without naming the theorem that gives it.

SHORTS11 mark

1. How many tangents can be drawn from a point inside a circle?

Show Answer

Ans:

None. A line through an interior point must cut the circle at two points.

SHORTS22 marks

2. From a point 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm, a tangent is drawn. Find its length.

Show Answer

Ans:

The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so the three lengths form a right triangle.
Length = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. If ∠POQ = 110°, find ∠PTQ.

Show Answer

Ans:

The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, so ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°. In quadrilateral POQT: ∠PTQ = 360° − (90° + 90° + 110°) = 70°.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let P be the external point and PA, PB the tangents with points of contact A and B.
Join OA, OB and OP.
In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: OA = OB (radii), OP is common, and ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius).
By RHS congruence, ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP, so PA = PB.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Prove that a chord of the larger of two concentric circles which touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at P; O is the common centre.
OP is a radius of the smaller circle and AB is a tangent to it, so OP ⊥ AB.
In the larger circle, the perpendicular drawn from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
Hence AP = PB: the chord is bisected at the point of contact.

LONGL83 marks

8. Prove that the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line joining the points of contact at the centre.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let TP and TQ be tangents from T, touching the circle at P and Q.
∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90° because a radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
In quadrilateral POQT the angles sum to 360°, so ∠PTQ + ∠POQ = 360° − 180° = 180°.
Therefore the two angles are supplementary.

LONGL94 marks

9. A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the tangent segments from A, B, C, D be p, q, r, s respectively.
Then AB = p + q, BC = q + r, CD = r + s and AD = p + s.
AB + CD = p + q + r + s and AD + BC = p + s + q + r.
The two sums are identical, so AB + CD = AD + BC.
