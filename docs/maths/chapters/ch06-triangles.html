🎯 Marks lens

Triangles carries most of the 15-mark Geometry unit (Circles is the rest).
Expect a Basic Proportionality Theorem proof (3 marks), a similarity numerical (3 marks) and objective questions on the criteria.
Learn the theorem statements verbatim — the statement itself is often worth a mark.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Similar polygons. Corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio.
2. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales). If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, intersecting the other two sides at distinct points, it divides those two sides in the same ratio.
3. Converse of BPT. If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

CriterionCondition
AAA (or AA)Corresponding angles are equal
SSSCorresponding sides are in the same ratio
SASOne pair of angles equal and the including sides proportional

4. Area theorem. The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles equals the ratio of the squares of their corresponding sides.
5. Pythagoras theorem. In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides. Converse: if this holds, the angle opposite the longest side is a right angle.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (CA/FD)²
  • Right triangle: hypotenuse² = base² + perpendicular²
  • BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Area ratio is the square of the side ratio. Sides in ratio 2:3 means areas in ratio 4:9. Forgetting the square is the classic error.
  • When two triangles share an angle and have a pair of parallel sides, look for AA similarity — the parallel lines supply the equal angles.
  • For “prove that …² = … × …” questions you almost always need to prove two triangles similar first and then write the proportion.
  • In a right triangle, an altitude drawn to the hypotenuse creates three mutually similar triangles. That single fact solves most standard proofs.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing the area ratio equal to the side ratio, without squaring.
  • Corresponding the wrong vertices. Match the equal angles first, then name the triangles in that order.
  • In BPT proofs, omitting the construction lines and the auxiliary join.
  • Using the converse of Pythagoras without stating it.

SHORTS22 marks

2. In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.

Show Answer

Ans:

By BPT, AD/DB = AE/EC.
1.5/3 = 1/EC, so EC = 2 cm.

SHORTS33 marks

3. The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 9:16. If one side of the smaller triangle is 4.5 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger.

Show Answer

Ans:

Area ratio = square of side ratio, so 9/16 = (4.5/x)².
Taking square roots: 3/4 = 4.5/x, so x = 6 cm.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that in a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides.

Show Answer

Ans:

In right ΔABC, right-angled at B, draw BD ⊥ AC.
ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA), so AD/AB = AB/AC, giving AB² = AD × AC.
ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA), so DC/BC = BC/AC, giving BC² = DC × AC.
Adding: AB² + BC² = (AD + DC) × AC = AC².
