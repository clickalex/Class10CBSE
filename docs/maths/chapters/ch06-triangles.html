Chapter 6 · Triangles

Chapter 6 of 14. The theory statements are free marks; the numericals and proofs are where the unit is won.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Triangles carries most of the 15-mark Geometry unit (Circles is the rest).
Expect a Basic Proportionality Theorem proof (3 marks), a similarity numerical (3 marks) and objective questions on the criteria.
Learn the theorem statements verbatim — the statement itself is often worth a mark.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Similar polygons. Corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio.
2. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales). If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, intersecting the other two sides at distinct points, it divides those two sides in the same ratio.
3. Converse of BPT. If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

CriterionCondition
AAA (or AA)Corresponding angles are equal
SSSCorresponding sides are in the same ratio
SASOne pair of angles equal and the including sides proportional

4. Area theorem. The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles equals the ratio of the squares of their corresponding sides.
5. Pythagoras theorem. In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides. Converse: if this holds, the angle opposite the longest side is a right angle.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (CA/FD)²
  • Right triangle: hypotenuse² = base² + perpendicular²
  • BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Fix the similarity criterion before anything else — AA (two angles), SAS (one angle with the two including sides proportional) or SSS (all three sides proportional). Quote the criterion by name.
  2. Write the correspondence explicitly (say 'triangle ABC ~ triangle DEF'), because every ratio that follows depends on the order of the letters.
  3. Use the standard consequences: ratio of corresponding sides is constant, ratio of areas = square of the ratio of sides, and ratio of medians/altitudes/perimeters = ratio of sides.
  4. For a proof, end with the Pythagoras step if right triangles are involved, and state the result in the units asked for.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Area ratio is the square of the side ratio. Sides in ratio 2:3 means areas in ratio 4:9. Forgetting the square is the classic error.
  • When two triangles share an angle and have a pair of parallel sides, look for AA similarity — the parallel lines supply the equal angles.
  • For “prove that …² = … × …” questions you almost always need to prove two triangles similar first and then write the proportion.
  • In a right triangle, an altitude drawn to the hypotenuse creates three mutually similar triangles. That single fact solves most standard proofs.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing the area ratio equal to the side ratio, without squaring.
  • Corresponding the wrong vertices. Match the equal angles first, then name the triangles in that order.
  • In BPT proofs, omitting the construction lines and the auxiliary join.
  • Using the converse of Pythagoras without stating it.

SHORTS11 mark

1. State the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

SHORTS22 marks

2. In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.

Show Answer

Ans:

By the Basic Proportionality Theorem, a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio: AD/DB = AE/EC. So 1.5/3 = 1/EC, giving EC = (3 × 1)/1.5 = 2 cm. The theorem only applies because DE ∥ BC is given — without that, the ratio cannot be used.

SHORTS32 marks

3. State the converse of the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side. In ΔABC, if a line through D on AB and E on AC satisfies AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE ∥ BC.

SHORTS43 marks

4. The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 9:16. If one side of the smaller triangle is 4.5 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger.

Show Answer

Ans:

Area ratio = square of side ratio, so 9/16 = (4.5/x)².
Taking square roots: 3/4 = 4.5/x, so x = 6 cm.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Prove that in a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides.

Show Answer

Ans:

In right ΔABC, right-angled at B, draw BD ⊥ AC.
ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA), so AD/AB = AB/AC, giving AB² = AD × AC.
ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA), so DC/BC = BC/AC, giving BC² = DC × AC.
Adding: AB² + BC² = (AD + DC) × AC = AC².

SHORTS83 marks

8. In ΔABC, D and E lie on AB and AC with AD = 5.7 cm, DB = 9.5 cm, AE = 4.8 cm and EC = 8 cm. Is DE parallel to BC?

Show Answer

Ans:

AD/DB = 5.7/9.5 = 0.6 and AE/EC = 4.8/8 = 0.6.
The two ratios are equal, so by the converse of the Basic Proportionality Theorem DE ∥ BC.

LONGL114 marks

11. Prove that the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles equals the square of the ratio of their corresponding medians.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let ΔABC ~ ΔPQR with medians AD and PS to the sides BC and QR.
Since the triangles are similar, AB/PQ = BC/QR and ∠B = ∠Q.
D and S are midpoints, so BD = BC/2 and QS = QR/2, giving BD/QS = BC/QR = AB/PQ.
With the included angles equal, ΔABD ~ ΔPQS (SAS), so AD/PS = AB/PQ.
Therefore ar(ABC)/ar(PQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (AD/PS)².
