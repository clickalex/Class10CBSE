Chapter 1 · Real Numbers

Chapter 1 of 14. The smallest unit but the most predictable one. Three question types cover all 6 marks.

🎯 Marks lens

6 marks, always procedural. Expect one HCF/LCM word problem (2–3 marks), one irrationality proof (3 marks), and one terminating-decimal question (1 mark).
Euclid’s division lemma itself is asked as a 1-mark statement question.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Euclid’s Division Lemma. Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r satisfying a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b. It is the basis of the HCF algorithm: divide, then repeat with (b, r) until the remainder is 0. The last non-zero divisor is the HCF.
2. HCF and LCM by prime factorisation. Write each number as a product of primes. HCF = product of the lowest powers of the common primes. LCM = product of the highest powers of all primes present.

RelationUse
HCF × LCM = product of the two numbersValid for two numbers only — never for three
HCF always divides both numbersQuick check on your answer
LCM is always a multiple of bothQuick check on your answer

3. Irrationality proofs. The standard method is proof by contradiction: assume the number is rational p/q in lowest terms (q ≠ 0, p and q coprime), square both sides, then show a prime divides both p and q — contradicting coprimality.
4. Terminating decimal expansion. A rational number p/q in lowest terms has a terminating decimal if and only if q is of the form 2^m × 5^n, where m and n are non-negative integers. Otherwise it is non-terminating repeating.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b
  • HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b
  • p/q terminates ⇔ q = 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Decide which tool the question needs: Euclid's division lemma for a general proof, the prime factorisation route for HCF and LCM, or a contradiction for proving irrationality.
  2. For HCF and LCM, write both numbers as products of primes in index form. HCF = smallest power of each common prime; LCM = greatest power of every prime present. Check with HCF × LCM = product of the numbers.
  3. To prove a number irrational, assume it is rational (write it as p/q in lowest terms), square or rearrange until a prime divides both p and q, and point out the contradiction.
  4. For a terminating decimal, factorise the denominator: if it is of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ the decimal terminates (after max(n, m) places); otherwise it repeats without terminating.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Terminating test: look only at the denominator. If, after reducing, the denominator has any prime other than 2 or 5, it repeats. Ignore the numerator entirely.
  • Irrationality answer shape: assumption → square → prime divides p → prime divides q → contradiction. Five lines, every time.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers. It fails. Factorise instead.
  • In the irrationality proof, writing “p/q” without stating coprime and q ≠ 0. That is a marked step.
  • Forgetting to reduce the fraction before applying the terminating-decimal test. 12/60 looks non-terminating; it is 1/5.
  • Stopping the Euclid algorithm one step early. Stop when the remainder is 0, then take the previous divisor.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Without performing long division, state whether 13/3125 will have a terminating decimal expansion.

Show Answer

Ans:

3125 = 5⁵, which is of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ with m = 0, n = 5. The fraction is already in lowest terms, so yes — it terminates.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume 5 − √3 is rational and equals r. Then √3 = 5 − r, which is rational because it is the difference of two rationals. That contradicts the fact that √3 is irrational, so 5 − √3 must be irrational.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
Squaring: 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Hence 3 divides p², and since 3 is prime, 3 divides p.
Write p = 3k. Then 9k² = 3q², so q² = 3k². Hence 3 divides q², so 3 divides q.
So 3 divides both p and q, contradicting that they are coprime.
Therefore √3 is irrational.

SHORTS53 marks

5. The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find their LCM.

Show Answer

Ans:

For any two positive integers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. So LCM = (96 × 404) ÷ 4 = 38784 ÷ 4 = 9696. It is worth checking the result: 9696 ÷ 96 = 101 and 9696 ÷ 404 = 24, and 101 and 24 share no factor, so 9696 really is the least common multiple.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Use Euclid’s division algorithm to find the HCF of 867 and 255.

Show Answer

Ans:

867 = 255 × 3 + 102
255 = 102 × 2 + 51
102 = 51 × 2 + 0
The remainder is now 0, so the divisor at that step, 51, is the HCF. Always stop at a zero remainder and take the previous divisor.

LONGL94 marks

9. An army contingent of 616 members marches behind an army band of 32 members. Both groups must march in the same number of columns. What is the maximum number of columns?

Show Answer

Ans:

Maximum columns = HCF(616, 32).
616 = 2³ × 7 × 11 and 32 = 2⁵.
HCF = 2³ = 8, so the maximum is 8 columns.

LONGL124 marks

12. Can 6ⁿ end with the digit 0 for any natural number n? Give a reason.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. If 6ⁿ ended in 0 it would be divisible by 10 = 2 × 5, so 5 would have to be a prime factor of 6ⁿ. But 6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ and, by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, its only prime factors are 2 and 3. Since 5 never appears, 6ⁿ cannot end in 0 for any n.
