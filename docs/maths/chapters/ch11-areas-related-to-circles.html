Chapter 11 · Areas Related to Circles

Chapter 11 of 14. Perimeter of a sector and area of a segment — two formulas and a lot of care with the radius.

🎯 Marks lens

Part of the 10-mark Mensuration unit, roughly 4–5 marks.
Typically one question on the perimeter or area of a sector, and one on a segment or a combined figure.
Only sectors and segments of a circle remain in the rationalised syllabus.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Sector. The region between two radii and the arc joining their endpoints. The angle at the centre is θ.
2. Segment. The region between a chord and the arc it cuts off. The minor segment uses the minor arc.

QuantityFormula (θ in degrees)
Length of arc(θ/360) × 2πr
Area of sector(θ/360) × πr²
Area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord½ r² sin θ
Area of the segmentArea of sector − area of that triangle

3. Combined figures. Most questions are a square or rectangle with quadrant-shaped corners removed, or a circle inscribed in a square. Subtract the parts you do not want.
4. Circle inscribed in a square: the diameter equals the side. Circle circumscribing a square: the diameter equals the diagonal, a√2.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr
  • Sector area = (θ/360) × πr²
  • Segment area = sector area − ½ r² sin θ
  • Perimeter of a sector = 2r + arc length
  • Quadrant = sector with θ = 90°

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Perimeter of a sector is 2r + arc, not just the arc. Missing the two radii is the most common error here.
  • For θ = 90° the sector area is exactly one quarter of πr²; for θ = 60°, one sixth.
  • Circle inscribed in a square of side a: wasted area = a² − πa²/4. Circle circumscribing it: wasted area = πa²/2 − a².
  • Take π as 22/7 when the radius is a multiple of 7 — the arithmetic collapses. Otherwise use 3.14.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting to add the two radii to the perimeter of a sector.
  • Using the diameter where the formula needs the radius.
  • Computing the segment as sector minus chord length instead of sector minus triangle area.
  • Mixing degrees and radians. The CBSE formulas use degrees only.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the length of an arc of a sector with radius 21 cm and central angle 120°.

Show Answer

Ans:

Arc = (120/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = (1/3) × 132 = 44 cm.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Find the area of a sector of radius 6 cm with a central angle of 60° (take π = 3.14).

Show Answer

Ans:

Area = (60/360) × 3.14 × 36 = (1/6) × 113.04 = 18.84 cm².

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find the perimeter of a sector with radius 14 cm and central angle 90°.

Show Answer

Ans:

Arc = (90/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 14 = (1/4) × 88 = 22 cm.
Perimeter = 2(14) + 22 = 50 cm.

LONGL44 marks

4. A square of side 14 cm has a circle inscribed in it. Find the area between the square and the circle.

Show Answer

Ans:

The inscribed circle has diameter 14 cm, so radius 7 cm.
Area of square = 196 cm². Area of circle = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm².
Required area = 196 − 154 = 42 cm².
