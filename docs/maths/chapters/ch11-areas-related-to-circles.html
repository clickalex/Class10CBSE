Chapter 11 · Areas Related to Circles

Chapter 11 of 14. Perimeter of a sector and area of a segment — two formulas and a lot of care with the radius.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →Mock-test this chapter (timed, scored) →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Part of the 10-mark Mensuration unit, roughly 4–5 marks.
Typically one question on the perimeter or area of a sector, and one on a segment or a combined figure.
Only sectors and segments of a circle remain in the rationalised syllabus.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Sector. The region between two radii and the arc joining their endpoints. The angle at the centre is θ.
2. Segment. The region between a chord and the arc it cuts off. The minor segment uses the minor arc.

QuantityFormula (θ in degrees)
Length of arc(θ/360) × 2πr
Area of sector(θ/360) × πr²
Area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord½ r² sin θ
Area of the segmentArea of sector − area of that triangle

3. Combined figures. Most questions are a square or rectangle with quadrant-shaped corners removed, or a circle inscribed in a square. Subtract the parts you do not want.
4. Circle inscribed in a square: the diameter equals the side. Circle circumscribing a square: the diameter equals the diagonal, a√2.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr
  • Sector area = (θ/360) × πr²
  • Segment area = sector area − ½ r² sin θ
  • Perimeter of a sector = 2r + arc length
  • Quadrant = sector with θ = 90°

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. List the radius or diameter first, then decide what the question is asking for — circumference, area of a circle, length of an arc, area of a sector, or area of a segment.
  2. Use the angle at the centre: arc length = (θ/360°) × 2πr and area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr².
  3. For a segment, subtract the triangle from the sector: minor segment = sector − triangle, and for θ = 60°, 90° or 120° the triangle part is usually found by ½r² sin θ or by the standard values.
  4. For combined figures, add or subtract whole shapes and never add a part twice. Keep π as 22/7 only when the radius is a multiple of 7, otherwise use 3.14, and state the unit as cm or cm².

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Perimeter of a sector is 2r + arc, not just the arc. Missing the two radii is the most common error here.
  • For θ = 90° the sector area is exactly one quarter of πr²; for θ = 60°, one sixth.
  • Circle inscribed in a square of side a: wasted area = a² − πa²/4. Circle circumscribing it: wasted area = πa²/2 − a².
  • Take π as 22/7 when the radius is a multiple of 7 — the arithmetic collapses. Otherwise use 3.14.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting to add the two radii to the perimeter of a sector.
  • Using the diameter where the formula needs the radius.
  • Computing the segment as sector minus chord length instead of sector minus triangle area.
  • Mixing degrees and radians. The CBSE formulas use degrees only.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the length of an arc of a sector with radius 21 cm and central angle 120°.

Show Answer

Ans:

Arc length = (θ/360°) × 2πr = (120/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = (1/3) × 132 = 44 cm. Use 22/7 for π whenever the radius is a multiple of 7, as it is here — the sevens cancel and the arithmetic stays exact.

SHORTS22 marks

2. The circumference of a circle exceeds its diameter by 30 cm. Find the radius (take π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

2πr − 2r = 30 ⇒ 2r(22/7 − 1) = 30 ⇒ 2r(15/7) = 30 ⇒ r = 7 cm. Check: circumference 44 cm, diameter 14 cm, difference 30 cm.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find the area of a sector of radius 6 cm with a central angle of 60° (take π = 3.14).

Show Answer

Ans:

Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr² = (60/360) × 3.14 × 36 = (1/6) × 113.04 = 18.84 cm². Here the radius is not a multiple of 7, so π = 3.14 as the question directs, and the unit is cm² because this is an area, not a length.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Find the perimeter of a sector with radius 14 cm and central angle 90°.

Show Answer

Ans:

Arc = (90/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 14 = (1/4) × 88 = 22 cm.
Perimeter = 2(14) + 22 = 50 cm.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

SHORTS73 marks

7. Find the area of the minor segment of a circle of radius 14 cm whose central angle is 90° (π = 22/7).

Show Answer

Ans:

Area of sector = (90/360) × (22/7) × 14² = ¼ × 616 = 154 cm².
Area of the right triangle formed by the two radii = ½ × 14 × 14 = 98 cm².
Area of the minor segment = 154 − 98 = 56 cm².

LONGL84 marks

8. A square of side 14 cm has a circle inscribed in it. Find the area between the square and the circle.

Show Answer

Ans:

The inscribed circle has diameter 14 cm, so radius 7 cm.
Area of square = 196 cm². Area of circle = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm².
Required area = 196 − 154 = 42 cm².

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. Two circles touch externally. The sum of their areas is 130π cm² and the distance between their centres is 14 cm. Find their radii.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the radii be r₁ and r₂. Since they touch externally, r₁ + r₂ = 14.
πr₁² + πr₂² = 130π ⇒ r₁² + r₂² = 130.
(r₁ + r₂)² = r₁² + r₂² + 2r₁r₂ ⇒ 196 = 130 + 2r₁r₂ ⇒ r₁r₂ = 33.
So r₁ and r₂ are roots of t² − 14t + 33 = 0, giving 11 cm and 3 cm.

✍️ Hands-on task
