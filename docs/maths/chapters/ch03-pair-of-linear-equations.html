Chapter 3 · Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

Chapter 3 of 14. Two halves: a one-line consistency test, and word problems that are worth more marks.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →Mock-test this chapter (timed, scored) →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

5–6 marks. One consistency/nature question (2 marks) and one word problem (4–5 marks).
Word problems on upstream/downstream speed, ages, fractions and fixed-charge-plus-rate are the recurring ones.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Nature of the pair. Compare a₁/a₂, b₁/b₂ and c₁/c₂ for the pair a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0.

ConditionNatureLines
a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂Unique solution — consistentIntersect at one point
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂Infinitely many solutions — dependentCoincident
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂No solution — inconsistentParallel

2. Substitution. Express one variable from the simpler equation and substitute into the other.
3. Elimination. Multiply each equation so one variable’s coefficients match, then add or subtract.
4. Cross-multiplication. For a unique solution: x/(b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) = y/(c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) = 1/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁).
5. Equations reducible to linear pairs. Terms like 1/x and 1/y are handled by substituting u = 1/x, v = 1/y, solving, then substituting back.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • x/(b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) = y/(c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) = 1/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)
  • Upstream speed = still-water speed − stream speed
  • Downstream speed = still-water speed + stream speed

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Read the problem and name the two unknowns explicitly — “let the speed of the boat in still water be x km/h”.
  2. Translate each sentence into one equation. Two independent facts → two equations.
  3. Solve by elimination (usually fastest) and write both values.
  4. Check both values in the original word statements, not just in the equations. Then answer in a full sentence with units.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Ratio order matters. Compare a with a, b with b, c with c — never a with b.
  • Downstream is always the faster one. If your downstream speed comes out smaller, you swapped the signs.
  • Age problems: “after n years” means add n to both ages; “n years ago” means subtract from both.
  • A fixed charge plus a per-unit charge means the fixed charge is the constant term and the rate is the coefficient.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Declaring a pair inconsistent when only two of the three ratios are equal. You must check c₁/c₂ as well.
  • Losing a negative sign while multiplying an equation before elimination.
  • Solving correctly but answering “x = 5, y = 3” instead of the actual quantities asked for.
  • In substitution problems with 1/x, forgetting to convert back from u and v at the end.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.

Show Answer

Ans:

a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2.
All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 10 and 2x − 3y = 1.

Show Answer

Ans:

Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 4: 9x + 12y = 30 and 8x − 12y = 4.
Adding: 17x = 34, so x = 2.
Substituting in the first: 6 + 4y = 10, so y = 1.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables”: “Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Consistency conditions, three solving methods, word problems..
a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2. All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

SHORTS53 marks

5. For what value of k does the pair 3x + y = 1 and (2k − 1)x + (k − 1)y = 2k + 1 have no solution?

Show Answer

Ans:

For no solution the lines must be parallel: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
3/(2k − 1) = 1/(k − 1) ⇒ 3k − 3 = 2k − 1 ⇒ k = 2.
Check: at k = 2 the equations become 3x + y = 1 and 3x + y = 5, which are parallel and distinct — so no solution.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Solve by cross-multiplication: 2x + 3y = 46 and 3x + 5y = 74.

Show Answer

Ans:

Write as 2x + 3y − 46 = 0 and 3x + 5y − 74 = 0.
x/(3(−74) − 5(−46)) = y/(−46(3) − (−74)(2)) = 1/(2(5) − 3(3))
x/(−222 + 230) = y/(−138 + 148) = 1/1, so x = 8 and y = 10.
Check: 2(8) + 3(10) = 46 and 3(8) + 5(10) = 74.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Solve by substitution: x + 3y = 16 and 2x − y = 4.

Show Answer

Ans:

From the second equation, y = 2x − 4.
Substituting in the first: x + 3(2x − 4) = 16, so x + 6x − 12 = 16.
Hence 7x = 28, giving x = 4 and y = 2(4) − 4 = 4.
Check: 4 + 12 = 16 and 8 − 4 = 4. The solution is x = 4, y = 4.

LONGL84 marks

8. Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are they?

Show Answer

Ans:

Let Nuri be x and Sonu be y now.
x − 5 = 3(y − 5) ⇒ x − 3y = −10.
x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10.
Subtracting: −y = −20, so y = 20 and x = 50.
Nuri is 50 and Sonu is 20.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. A fraction becomes 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from its numerator, and becomes 1/4 when 8 is added to its denominator. Find the fraction.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the fraction be x/y.
(x − 1)/y = 1/3 ⇒ y = 3x − 3. And x/(y + 8) = 1/4 ⇒ 4x = y + 8.
Substituting: 4x = 3x − 3 + 8 ⇒ x = 5, so y = 12.
The fraction is 5/12. Check: (5 − 1)/12 = 1/3 and 5/20 = 1/4.

LONGL125 marks

12. A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours. In 13 hours it can go 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream. Find the speed of the stream and of the boat in still water.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the still-water speed be x km/h and the stream’s speed be y km/h.
Put u = 1/(x − y) and v = 1/(x + y).
30u + 44v = 10 and 40u + 55v = 13.
Solving gives u = 1/8 and v = 1/11, so x − y = 8 and x + y = 11.
Therefore x = 9.5 km/h and y = 1.5 km/h.

✍️ Hands-on task

Write one word problem of your own that needs two equations — for example a fixed charge plus a daily rate — solve it, then hand it to someone else. Writing the problem is what teaches the translation step.
