SHORTS11 mark

1. State Euclid’s division lemma.

Show Answer

Ans:

Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Without performing long division, state whether 13/3125 will have a terminating decimal expansion.

Show Answer

Ans:

3125 = 5⁵, which is of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ with m = 0, n = 5. The fraction is already in lowest terms, so yes — it terminates.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume 5 − √3 is rational and equals r. Then √3 = 5 − r, which is rational because it is the difference of two rationals. That contradicts the fact that √3 is irrational, so 5 − √3 must be irrational.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
Squaring: 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Hence 3 divides p², and since 3 is prime, 3 divides p.
Write p = 3k. Then 9k² = 3q², so q² = 3k². Hence 3 divides q², so 3 divides q.
So 3 divides both p and q, contradicting that they are coprime.
Therefore √3 is irrational.

SHORTS53 marks

5. The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find their LCM.

Show Answer

Ans:

For any two positive integers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. So LCM = (96 × 404) ÷ 4 = 38784 ÷ 4 = 9696. It is worth checking the result: 9696 ÷ 96 = 101 and 9696 ÷ 404 = 24, and 101 and 24 share no factor, so 9696 really is the least common multiple.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student finds the LCM of 12, 18 and 30 by writing LCM = (12 × 18 × 30) ÷ HCF = 6480 ÷ 6 = 1080, using “HCF × LCM = product of the numbers”. Find the error and give the correct LCM.

Show Answer

Ans:

The relation HCF × LCM = product holds only for two numbers. For three numbers it fails, so 1080 is wrong.
Use prime factorisation: 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3², 30 = 2 × 3 × 5.
LCM = product of the highest powers of every prime = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Find the largest number that divides 1251, 9377 and 15628 leaving remainders 1, 2 and 3 respectively.

Show Answer

Ans:

If the number leaves remainders 1, 2, 3, it divides 1251 − 1 = 1250, 9377 − 2 = 9375 and 15628 − 3 = 15625 exactly.
1250 = 2 × 5⁴, 9375 = 3 × 5⁵, 15625 = 5⁶.
The largest such number is the HCF = 5⁴ = 625.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Use Euclid’s division algorithm to find the HCF of 867 and 255.

Show Answer

Ans:

867 = 255 × 3 + 102
255 = 102 × 2 + 51
102 = 51 × 2 + 0
The remainder is now 0, so the divisor at that step, 51, is the HCF. Always stop at a zero remainder and take the previous divisor.

LONGL94 marks

9. An army contingent of 616 members marches behind an army band of 32 members. Both groups must march in the same number of columns. What is the maximum number of columns?

Show Answer

Ans:

Maximum columns = HCF(616, 32).
616 = 2³ × 7 × 11 and 32 = 2⁵.
HCF = 2³ = 8, so the maximum is 8 columns.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A calculator displays these decimal forms: 7/80 = 0.0875, 11/30 = 0.3666…, 13/125 = 0.104 and 17/60 = 0.28333… (a) Which fractions have terminating decimals and which do not? (b) Using the prime factors of the denominator, explain why 7/80 terminates but 11/30 does not. (c) After how many decimal places does 13/125 terminate, and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 7/80 and 13/125 terminate; 11/30 and 17/60 do not.
(b) 80 = 2⁴ × 5 has only the prime factors 2 and 5, so 7/80 terminates. 30 = 2 × 3 × 5 contains the prime 3 (and 11/30 is already in lowest terms), so 11/30 never terminates.
(c) 125 = 5³, so 13/125 = 13 × 2³/(2³ × 5³) = 104/1000 = 0.104. It terminates after 3 places, the larger of the powers of 2 and 5 in the denominator.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Three traffic lights change after every 48 s, 72 s and 108 s. They all change together at 8:00:00 a.m. (a) After how many seconds will they change together again? (b) At what time will that be? (c) Which concept from this chapter did you use?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The interval is the LCM of 48, 72 and 108: 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 72 = 2³ × 3², 108 = 2² × 3³, so LCM = 2⁴ × 3³ = 432 s.
(b) 432 s = 7 min 12 s, so they change together at 8:07:12 a.m.
(c) Prime factorisation and the LCM (the smallest common multiple of all three times).

LONGL124 marks

12. Can 6ⁿ end with the digit 0 for any natural number n? Give a reason.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. If 6ⁿ ended in 0 it would be divisible by 10 = 2 × 5, so 5 would have to be a prime factor of 6ⁿ. But 6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ and, by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, its only prime factors are 2 and 3. Since 5 never appears, 6ⁿ cannot end in 0 for any n.

LONGL135 marks

13. Prove that √5 is irrational. Hence show that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume √5 is rational, so √5 = p/q with p and q coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
Squaring: 5q² = p², so 5 divides p². Since 5 is prime, 5 divides p. Write p = 5m.
Then 5q² = 25m², so q² = 5m², hence 5 divides q² and so 5 divides q.
Now 5 is a common factor of p and q, which contradicts that they are coprime. So √5 is irrational.
Next, suppose 3 + 2√5 = r is rational. Then √5 = (r − 3)/2 would be rational, contradicting the result above. Hence 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.

Show Answer

Ans:

x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

SHORTS22 marks

2. If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.

Show Answer

Ans:

A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

SHORTS43 marks

4. If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student writes: “For 2x² − 5x + 3, the sum of the zeroes is b/a = −5/2 and the product is c/a = 3/2.” Find the error, then give the correct sum and verify it with the actual zeroes.

Show Answer

Ans:

Error: the sum of the zeroes is −b/a, not b/a. Here −b/a = −(−5)/2 = 5/2. (The product c/a = 3/2 is correct.)
Factorise: 2x² − 5x + 3 = (2x − 3)(x − 1), so the zeroes are 3/2 and 1.
Check: 3/2 + 1 = 5/2 ✓ and (3/2) × 1 = 3/2 ✓.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. The area of a rectangular plot is (x² + 7x + 12) square metres and its length is (x + 4) metres. Find its breadth and the zeroes of the area polynomial.

Show Answer

Ans:

Breadth = area ÷ length = (x² + 7x + 12) ÷ (x + 4).
x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4), so the breadth is (x + 3) metres.
The zeroes come from (x + 3)(x + 4) = 0, so x = −3 and x = −4.

SHORTS73 marks

7. If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 6x + k and 3α + 2β = 20, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 6 (sum of zeroes) and 3α + 2β = 20.
Subtract twice the first equation from the second: α = 20 − 12 = 8, so β = −2.
Product αβ = 8(−2) = −16, and product = c/a = k, so k = −16.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Divide 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5 by x² + 2x + 1 and verify the division algorithm.

Show Answer

Ans:

Quotient = 3x − 5, remainder = 9x + 10.
Check: (x² + 2x + 1)(3x − 5) + 9x + 10
= 3x³ + 6x² + 3x − 5x² − 10x − 5 + 9x + 10
= 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5, which is the dividend — so dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: The graph of y = p(x), where p(x) is a quadratic polynomial with leading coefficient 1, is a parabola opening upward that cuts the x-axis at (−1, 0) and (4, 0). (a) How many zeroes does p(x) have, and what are they? (b) Find the sum and the product of the zeroes. (c) Write p(x).

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Two zeroes, −1 and 4 — the x-coordinates of the points where the graph meets the x-axis.
(b) Sum = −1 + 4 = 3 and product = (−1)(4) = −4.
(c) p(x) = x² − (sum)x + product = x² − 3x − 4.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A toy company models its profit (in thousands of rupees) from selling x thousand toys as P(x) = x² − 7x + 10. (a) For which numbers of toys does the profit become zero? (b) Verify the relation between the zeroes and the coefficients. (c) Give an example of a quadratic polynomial whose graph touches the x-axis at only one point.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) x² − 7x + 10 = (x − 2)(x − 5) = 0, so the profit is zero at x = 2 and x = 5 thousand toys.
(b) Sum = 2 + 5 = 7 = −(−7)/1 ✓; product = 2 × 5 = 10 = 10/1 ✓.
(c) x² − 4x + 4 = (x − 2)²: its two zeroes coincide, so the parabola just touches the x-axis at (2, 0).

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. A quadratic polynomial cuts the x-axis at −2 and 3 and its graph passes through (0, 6). Find the polynomial.

Show Answer

Ans:

With zeroes −2 and 3, p(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 3).
It passes through (0, 6), so 6 = a(2)(−3) = −6a, giving a = −1.
Hence p(x) = −(x + 2)(x − 3) = −x² + x + 6.

LONGL125 marks

12. If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 4x + 1, find (i) α² + β², (ii) 1/α + 1/β, and (iii) a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are α² and β².

Show Answer

Ans:

From the polynomial: α + β = 4 and αβ = 1.
(i) α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 16 − 2 = 14.
(ii) 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 4/1 = 4.
(iii) Sum of the new zeroes = α² + β² = 14; product = (αβ)² = 1.
Required polynomial: x² − 14x + 1.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.

Show Answer

Ans:

a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2.
All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 10 and 2x − 3y = 1.

Show Answer

Ans:

Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 4: 9x + 12y = 30 and 8x − 12y = 4.
Adding: 17x = 34, so x = 2.
Substituting in the first: 6 + 4y = 10, so y = 1.

APPLICATIONA33 marks

3. To solve 2/x + 3/y = 13 and 5/x − 4/y = −2, a student puts u = 1/x and v = 1/y, finds u = 2 and v = 3 and writes “x = 2, y = 3”. What is wrong? Give the correct solution.

Show Answer

Ans:

The student stopped at the substituted variables. Since u = 1/x and v = 1/y, the answer must be converted back: x = 1/u = 1/2 and y = 1/v = 1/3.
(Solving 2u + 3v = 13 and 5u − 4v = −2 does give u = 2, v = 3.)
Check: 2 ÷ (1/2) + 3 ÷ (1/3) = 4 + 9 = 13 ✓ and 5 ÷ (1/2) − 4 ÷ (1/3) = 10 − 12 = −2 ✓.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Two notebooks and three pens cost Rs 90, while three notebooks and two pens cost Rs 85. Find the price of one notebook and one pen.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let a notebook cost Rs x and a pen Rs y: 2x + 3y = 90 and 3x + 2y = 85.
Adding: 5x + 5y = 175, so x + y = 35. Subtracting the first from the second: x − y = −5.
Solving: x = 15 and y = 20. A notebook costs Rs 15 and a pen Rs 20. Check: 2(15) + 3(20) = 90 ✓.

SHORTS53 marks

5. For what value of k does the pair 3x + y = 1 and (2k − 1)x + (k − 1)y = 2k + 1 have no solution?

Show Answer

Ans:

For no solution the lines must be parallel: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
3/(2k − 1) = 1/(k − 1) ⇒ 3k − 3 = 2k − 1 ⇒ k = 2.
Check: at k = 2 the equations become 3x + y = 1 and 3x + y = 5, which are parallel and distinct — so no solution.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Solve by cross-multiplication: 2x + 3y = 46 and 3x + 5y = 74.

Show Answer

Ans:

Write as 2x + 3y − 46 = 0 and 3x + 5y − 74 = 0.
x/(3(−74) − 5(−46)) = y/(−46(3) − (−74)(2)) = 1/(2(5) − 3(3))
x/(−222 + 230) = y/(−138 + 148) = 1/1, so x = 8 and y = 10.
Check: 2(8) + 3(10) = 46 and 3(8) + 5(10) = 74.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Solve by substitution: x + 3y = 16 and 2x − y = 4.

Show Answer

Ans:

From the second equation, y = 2x − 4.
Substituting in the first: x + 3(2x − 4) = 16, so x + 6x − 12 = 16.
Hence 7x = 28, giving x = 4 and y = 2(4) − 4 = 4.
Check: 4 + 12 = 16 and 8 − 4 = 4. The solution is x = 4, y = 4.

LONGL84 marks

8. Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are they?

Show Answer

Ans:

Let Nuri be x and Sonu be y now.
x − 5 = 3(y − 5) ⇒ x − 3y = −10.
x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10.
Subtracting: −y = −20, so y = 20 and x = 50.
Nuri is 50 and Sonu is 20.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Two taxi companies charge a fixed fee plus a rate per km. Company A charges Rs 40 plus Rs 10 per km; Company B charges Rs 100 plus Rs 5 per km. A student draws the lines y = 10x + 40 and y = 5x + 100, where x is the distance in km and y is the fare in rupees. (a) Write the pair in the form ax + by + c = 0. (b) At what distance do both companies charge the same fare, and what is it? (c) Which company is cheaper for a 20 km trip?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 10x − y + 40 = 0 and 5x − y + 100 = 0.
(b) 10x + 40 = 5x + 100 gives x = 12. The fares are equal at 12 km, where each charges Rs 160 — the point (12, 160) where the lines intersect.
(c) For 20 km: A = Rs 240, B = Rs 200, so Company B is cheaper.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A student plots the lines 3x + 2y = 12 and 6x + 4y = k on graph paper for different values of k. (a) For which value of k do the lines coincide? (b) For which values of k are they parallel? (c) Can this pair ever have exactly one solution? Explain.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) a₁/a₂ = 1/2 and b₁/b₂ = 1/2, and c₁/c₂ = 12/k. The lines coincide when 12/k = 1/2, i.e. k = 24.
(b) They are parallel (no solution) when k ≠ 24.
(c) No. Because a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ for every k, the lines are never intersecting: the pair has either no solution or infinitely many.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. A fraction becomes 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from its numerator, and becomes 1/4 when 8 is added to its denominator. Find the fraction.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the fraction be x/y.
(x − 1)/y = 1/3 ⇒ y = 3x − 3. And x/(y + 8) = 1/4 ⇒ 4x = y + 8.
Substituting: 4x = 3x − 3 + 8 ⇒ x = 5, so y = 12.
The fraction is 5/12. Check: (5 − 1)/12 = 1/3 and 5/20 = 1/4.

LONGL125 marks

12. A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours. In 13 hours it can go 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream. Find the speed of the stream and of the boat in still water.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the still-water speed be x km/h and the stream’s speed be y km/h.
Put u = 1/(x − y) and v = 1/(x + y).
30u + 44v = 10 and 40u + 55v = 13.
Solving gives u = 1/8 and v = 1/11, so x − y = 8 and x + y = 11.
Therefore x = 9.5 km/h and y = 1.5 km/h.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the nature of the roots of 2x² − 6x + 3 = 0.

Show Answer

Ans:

D = (−6)² − 4(2)(3) = 36 − 24 = 12.
D > 0 but 12 is not a perfect square, so the roots are real, distinct and irrational.

SHORTS22 marks

2. For what values of k does x² + kx + 16 = 0 have equal roots?

Show Answer

Ans:

Equal roots means the discriminant is zero: k² − 4(1)(16) = 0, so k² = 64 and k = ±8. For k = 8 the root is −4; for k = −8 the root is 4.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find the value of k for which 2x² + kx + 3 = 0 has two equal roots.

Show Answer

Ans:

Equal roots means the discriminant is zero: D = b² − 4ac = 0. Here k² − 4(2)(3) = 0, so k² = 24 and k = ±2√6. Both signs are needed — (+2√6)² and (−2√6)² are both 24, so each value gives a perfect square with a double root.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Solve 6x² − x − 2 = 0 by factorisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Split −x using numbers with product 6 × (−2) = −12 and sum −1: these are −4 and 3.
6x² − 4x + 3x − 2 = 2x(3x − 2) + 1(3x − 2) = (3x − 2)(2x + 1).
So x = 2/3 or x = −1/2.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student solves 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 with the formula and writes x = (−7 ± √(49 − 24))/4 = (−7 ± 5)/4, so x = −1/2 or x = −3. Find the mistake and give the correct roots.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mistake: here b = −7, so −b = +7. The student used −7.
Correct: x = (7 ± √(49 − 24))/4 = (7 ± 5)/4, so x = 12/4 = 3 or x = 2/4 = 1/2.
Check by factorising: (2x − 1)(x − 3) = 2x² − 7x + 3 ✓.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. The length of a rectangular field is 3 m more than twice its breadth and its area is 65 m². Find its dimensions.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the breadth be x m; the length is (2x + 3) m. Area: x(2x + 3) = 65, so 2x² + 3x − 65 = 0.
Factorise: (2x + 13)(x − 5) = 0, so x = 5 or x = −13/2. A length cannot be negative, so x = 5.
The field is 5 m × 13 m (area 5 × 13 = 65 m² ✓).

SHORTS73 marks

7. Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0 by completing the square.

Show Answer

Ans:

x² + 5x = −6.
Add (5/2)² to both sides: x² + 5x + 25/4 = −6 + 25/4 = 1/4.
(x + 5/2)² = 1/4, so x + 5/2 = ±1/2.
Hence x = −2 or x = −3.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: Aman studies the equation x² − 6x + k = 0 for different values of k. (a) For which values of k are the roots real and distinct? (b) Find k for equal roots, and the root. (c) Show that there is no real root when k = 10.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) D = 36 − 4k. Real and distinct roots need D > 0, so k < 9.
(b) Equal roots need D = 0, so k = 9 and the equation becomes (x − 3)² = 0 with the repeated root x = 3.
(c) For k = 10, D = 36 − 40 = −4 < 0, so the equation has no real roots.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Two water taps together fill a tank in 9⅜ hours (75/8 h). The tap of larger diameter alone takes 10 hours less than the smaller one. (a) If the smaller tap alone takes x hours, form the quadratic equation. (b) Solve it. (c) Why is one root rejected?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 1/x + 1/(x − 10) = 8/75, which simplifies to 4x² − 115x + 375 = 0.
(b) (4x − 15)(x − 25) = 0, so x = 25 or x = 15/4.
(c) For x = 15/4 = 3.75 the larger tap would need 3.75 − 10 < 0 hours, which is impossible. So the smaller tap takes 25 hours and the larger 15 hours.

APPLICATIONA104 marks

10. A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed were 5 km/h more, it would take 1 hour less. Find the speed of the train.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the speed be v km/h. Time difference: 360/v − 360/(v + 5) = 1.
⇒ 360[5/(v(v + 5))] = 1 ⇒ v² + 5v − 1800 = 0.
⇒ (v + 45)(v − 40) = 0, so v = 40 (speed cannot be negative).
The train travels at 40 km/h; check: 360/40 = 9 h and 360/45 = 8 h.

LONGL115 marks

11. The sum of the areas of two squares is 468 m². If the difference of their perimeters is 24 m, find the sides of the two squares.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the sides be x and y with x > y. Then x² + y² = 468 and 4x − 4y = 24, so x − y = 6.
Substituting y = x − 6: x² + (x − 6)² = 468 ⇒ 2x² − 12x + 36 = 468.

The cumulative frequency first crosses 17.5 in the class 20–30, so that is the median class.
Median = l + [(N/2 − cf)/f] × h = 20 + [(17.5 − 13)/12] × 10 = 20 + 3.75 = 23.75.
