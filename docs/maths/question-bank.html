SHORTS11 mark

1. State Euclid’s division lemma.

Show Answer

Ans:

Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Without performing long division, state whether 13/3125 will have a terminating decimal expansion.

Show Answer

Ans:

3125 = 5⁵, which is of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ with m = 0, n = 5. The fraction is already in lowest terms, so yes — it terminates.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Prove that √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
Squaring: 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Hence 3 divides p², and since 3 is prime, 3 divides p.
Write p = 3k. Then 9k² = 3q², so q² = 3k². Hence 3 divides q², so 3 divides q.
So 3 divides both p and q, contradicting that they are coprime.
Therefore √3 is irrational.

SHORTS43 marks

4. The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find their LCM.

Show Answer

Ans:

HCF × LCM = product, so LCM = (96 × 404)/4 = 38784/4 = 9696.

LONGL54 marks

5. An army contingent of 616 members marches behind an army band of 32 members. Both groups must march in the same number of columns. What is the maximum number of columns?

Show Answer

Ans:

Maximum columns = HCF(616, 32).
616 = 2³ × 7 × 11 and 32 = 2⁵.
HCF = 2³ = 8, so the maximum is 8 columns.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Real Numbers” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Euclid’s Division Lemma. Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r satisfying a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b. It is the basis of the HCF algorithm: divide, then repeat with (b, r) until the remainder is 0. The last non-zero divisor is the HCF.
Marks lens: marks, always procedural. Expect one HCF/LCM word problem (2–3 marks), one irrationality proof (3 marks), and one terminating-decimal question (1 mark).
Method / memory trick: Terminating test: look only at the denominator. If, after reducing, the denominator has any prime other than 2 or 5, it repeats. Ignore the numerator entirely.
Do not: Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers. It fails. Factorise instead.

Ch 2 · Polynomials (9)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.

Show Answer

Ans:

x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

SHORTS22 marks

2. If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.

Show Answer

Ans:

p(x) = x² − (1/4)x − 1.
Clearing the fraction: 4x² − x − 4.

SHORTS43 marks

4. If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

Ch 3 · Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (8)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.

Show Answer

Ans:

a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2.
All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 10 and 2x − 3y = 1.

Show Answer

Ans:

Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 4: 9x + 12y = 30 and 8x − 12y = 4.
Adding: 17x = 34, so x = 2.
Substituting in the first: 6 + 4y = 10, so y = 1.

LONGL35 marks

3. A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours. In 13 hours it can go 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream. Find the speed of the stream and of the boat in still water.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the still-water speed be x km/h and the stream’s speed be y km/h.
Put u = 1/(x − y) and v = 1/(x + y).
30u + 44v = 10 and 40u + 55v = 13.
Solving gives u = 1/8 and v = 1/11, so x − y = 8 and x + y = 11.
Therefore x = 9.5 km/h and y = 1.5 km/h.

LONGL44 marks

4. Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are they?

Show Answer

Ans:

Let Nuri be x and Sonu be y now.
x − 5 = 3(y − 5) ⇒ x − 3y = −10.
x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10.
Subtracting: −y = −20, so y = 20 and x = 50.
Nuri is 50 and Sonu is 20.

Ch 4 · Quadratic Equations (9)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the nature of the roots of 2x² − 6x + 3 = 0.

Show Answer

Ans:

D = (−6)² − 4(2)(3) = 36 − 24 = 12.
D > 0 but 12 is not a perfect square, so the roots are real, distinct and irrational.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Find the value of k for which 2x² + kx + 3 = 0 has two equal roots.

Show Answer

Ans:

For equal roots, D = 0, so k² − 4(2)(3) = 0.
k² = 24, so k = ±2√6.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Solve 6x² − x − 2 = 0 by factorisation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Split −x using numbers with product 6 × (−2) = −12 and sum −1: these are −4 and 3.
6x² − 4x + 3x − 2 = 2x(3x − 2) + 1(3x − 2) = (3x − 2)(2x + 1).
So x = 2/3 or x = −1/2.

LONGL45 marks

4. The sum of the areas of two squares is 468 m². If the difference of their perimeters is 24 m, find the sides of the two squares.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the sides be x and y with x > y. Then x² + y² = 468 and 4x − 4y = 24, so x − y = 6.
Substituting y = x − 6: x² + (x − 6)² = 468 ⇒ 2x² − 12x + 36 = 468.
2x² − 12x − 432 = 0, so x² − 6x − 216 = 0.
(x − 18)(x + 12) = 0, so x = 18 (rejecting −12) and y = 12.
The sides are 18 m and 12 m.

Ch 6 · Triangles (9)

SHORTS11 mark

1. State the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

Show Answer

Ans:

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

SHORTS22 marks

2. In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.

Show Answer

Ans:

By BPT, AD/DB = AE/EC.
1.5/3 = 1/EC, so EC = 2 cm.

SHORTS33 marks

3. The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 9:16. If one side of the smaller triangle is 4.5 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger.

Show Answer

Ans:

Area ratio = square of side ratio, so 9/16 = (4.5/x)².
Taking square roots: 3/4 = 4.5/x, so x = 6 cm.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that in a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides.

Show Answer

Ans:

In right ΔABC, right-angled at B, draw BD ⊥ AC.
ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA), so AD/AB = AB/AC, giving AB² = AD × AC.
ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA), so DC/BC = BC/AC, giving BC² = DC × AC.
Adding: AB² + BC² = (AD + DC) × AC = AC².

Marks lens: Triangles carries most of the 15-mark Geometry unit (Circles is the rest).

3. Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let P be the external point and PA, PB the tangents with points of contact A and B.
Join OA, OB and OP.
In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: OA = OB (radii), OP is common, and ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius).
By RHS congruence, ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP, so PA = PB.

LONGL44 marks

4. A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC.

Show Answer

Ans:

Let the tangent segments from A, B, C, D be p, q, r, s respectively.
Then AB = p + q, BC = q + r, CD = r + s and AD = p + s.
AB + CD = p + q + r + s and AD + BC = p + s + q + r.
The two sums are identical, so AB + CD = AD + BC.

Show Answer

Ans:

Primes on a die are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so P = 2/6 = 1/3.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is 9.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total outcomes = 36.
Favourable pairs: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) — 4 outcomes.
P = 4/36 = 1/9.

SHORTS42 marks

4. A bag contains 5 red and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is not red.

Show Answer

Ans:

Total = 8. P(not red) = P(green) = 3/8.
Equivalently, 1 − 5/8 = 3/8.

SHORTS53 marks

5. A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability that it is (i) a face card, (ii) neither a king nor a red card.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) There are 12 face cards, so P = 12/52 = 3/13.
(ii) Red cards are 26, of which 2 are kings. Neither a king nor red means a black card that is not a king: 26 − 2 = 24.
P = 24/52 = 6/13.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Probability” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Classical definition. P(E) = (number of outcomes favourable to E) / (total number of possible outcomes), assuming all outcomes are equally likely.
Marks lens: The remaining 3–4 marks of the 11-mark unit.
Method / memory trick: Always write the total number of outcomes first. Half of all probability errors are wrong totals, not wrong favourable counts.
Do not: Forgetting that a die has 6 outcomes, not 6 faces times something — and that two dice have 36, not 12.
