Show Answer

SHORTS22 marks

2. Without performing long division, state whether 13/3125 will have a terminating decimal expansion.

Show Answer

Ans:

3125 = 5⁵, which is of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ with m = 0, n = 5. The fraction is already in lowest terms, so yes — it terminates.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume 5 − √3 is rational and equals r. Then √3 = 5 − r, which is rational because it is the difference of two rationals. That contradicts the fact that √3 is irrational, so 5 − √3 must be irrational.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Prove that √3 is irrational.

Show Answer

Ans:

Assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
Squaring: 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Hence 3 divides p², and since 3 is prime, 3 divides p.
Write p = 3k. Then 9k² = 3q², so q² = 3k². Hence 3 divides q², so 3 divides q.
So 3 divides both p and q, contradicting that they are coprime.
Therefore √3 is irrational.

SHORTS53 marks

5. The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find their LCM.

Show Answer

Ans:

For any two positive integers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. So LCM = (96 × 404) ÷ 4 = 38784 ÷ 4 = 9696. It is worth checking the result: 9696 ÷ 96 = 101 and 9696 ÷ 404 = 24, and 101 and 24 share no factor, so 9696 really is the least common multiple.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Use Euclid’s division algorithm to find the HCF of 867 and 255.

Show Answer

Ans:

867 = 255 × 3 + 102
255 = 102 × 2 + 51
102 = 51 × 2 + 0
The remainder is now 0, so the divisor at that step, 51, is the HCF. Always stop at a zero remainder and take the previous divisor.

LONGL124 marks

12. Can 6ⁿ end with the digit 0 for any natural number n? Give a reason.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. If 6ⁿ ended in 0 it would be divisible by 10 = 2 × 5, so 5 would have to be a prime factor of 6ⁿ. But 6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ and, by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, its only prime factors are 2 and 3. Since 5 never appears, 6ⁿ cannot end in 0 for any n.

Ch 2 · Polynomials (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.

Show Answer

Ans:

x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

SHORTS22 marks

2. If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.

Show Answer

Ans:

α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.

Show Answer

Ans:

A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

SHORTS43 marks

4. If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.

Show Answer

Ans:

Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.

Show Answer

Ans:

a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2.
All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

SHORTS23 marks

Show Answer

Adding: 17x = 34, so x = 2.
Substituting in the first: 6 + 4y = 10, so y = 1.

Show Answer

SHORTS22 marks

Show Answer

Ans:

SHORTS33 marks

Show Answer
