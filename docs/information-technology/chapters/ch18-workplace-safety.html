Chapter 18 · Health, Safety and Security at the Workplace

Chapter 18 of 20. Unit 4 is definition-plus-example. Hazards and electrical safety are the core.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧮 Formulas 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

10 marks with the rest of Unit 4. Hazard types + two precautions each.
Data security is part of this unit in the current curriculum — passwords, backups, authorised access.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Hazard = anything that can cause harm. Risk = chance that the harm will occur.
2. Types of hazard: physical (cables, noise, fire), electrical, chemical (cleaner fumes), biological, ergonomic (bad chair), psychosocial (stress, bullying).
3. Occupational health: physical and mental wellbeing at work. Fatigue, eye strain and RSI are IT-lab issues.
4. Electrical safety: no overloaded sockets, no frayed wires, dry hands, don’t open the SMPS, use earthed plugs, UPS/surge protector.
5. Fire: know exits, don’t block them, switch off power, use the correct extinguisher (CO2 / dry powder on electrical — not water).
6. Security: lock the lab, visitor register, don’t share passwords, lock the screen, authorised software only, backup.
7. Policy / responsibility: follow school/lab rules, report hazards, use equipment as trained. Employers provide a safe workplace; employees cooperate.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Hazard = something that can hurt; Risk = how likely and how badly
  • PPE: Personal Protective Equipment — gloves, goggles, masks, ear plugs, safety shoes
  • Safety sign colours: red prohibition/fire, yellow warning, blue mandatory, green emergency
  • Report every near-miss — a near-miss today is an accident tomorrow

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Look around the workplace and list what could hurt someone — cables, wet floors, hot surfaces.
  2. Judge each hazard: how likely, and how bad. Fix the high risk ones first.
  3. Remove or guard the hazard, and wear the PPE that the task requires.
  4. Report anything you cannot fix yourself, and write it in the logbook.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Water on an electrical fire is the classic wrong answer.
  • Hazard vs accident: hazard is the potential; accident is the event.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing “be careful” with no concrete precaution.
  • Mixing this unit with the old “Web Applications and Security” syllabus.

SHORTS12 marks

1. Differentiate a hazard and an accident with an IT-lab example.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hazard is a potential source of harm (a loose cable across the aisle). An accident is the event (a student trips and the monitor falls).

SHORTS22 marks

2. List four electrical safety rules for a computer lab.

Show Answer

Ans:

Dry hands; earthed plugs; no overloaded boards; don’t open the power supply; report frayed wires; use a surge protector.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is a hazard? Give two examples from a computer laboratory.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hazard is anything in the workplace that can cause injury or ill health. In a computer laboratory the hazards include a trailing cable that can trip someone, a socket or adapter that is overloaded and can start a fire, a wet floor, and a chair with a broken back that can cause a fall.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between a hazard and a risk? Give one IT-lab example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

A hazard is anything that can cause harm — for example, a trailing cable across the lab floor.
Risk is the chance that the harm actually happens — for example, the high risk that someone hurrying between PCs trips over that cable.
Safety work removes hazards first, then reduces the remaining risk with guards, rules and training.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student pours water on a sparking socket. Two mistakes and the correct actions.

Show Answer

Ans:

Water on electrical fire; staying next to a live spark. Instead: don’t touch, switch off mains if safe, alert staff, use CO2/powder extinguisher, evacuate.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Riya is revising “Health, Safety and Security at the Workplace” (Hazards, occupational health, electrical and fire safety, security of data.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing “be careful” with no concrete precaution. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Water on an electrical fire is the classic wrong answer.
Why the mistake costs marks: Writing “be careful” with no concrete precaution.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

SHORTS73 marks

7. List four fire safety rules for a workplace.

Show Answer

Ans:

Keep every fire exit and aisle clear of boxes, cables and furniture, and make sure the exit signs are lit.
Check that fire extinguishers are in place, visible and within their expiry date, and know which type to use for an electrical fire.
Switch off and unplug equipment at the end of the day, and never overload a socket with adapters.
If a fire starts, raise the alarm, cut off the power if it is safe to do so, leave by the marked route without using the lift, and go to the assembly point.

SHORTS83 marks

8. List four data-security measures for a school computer lab and explain the purpose of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Unique passwords per user — so every action can be traced to its author.
Authorised access only — outsiders cannot reach student records or exam files.
Regular backups — a failed disk or ransomware cannot destroy the only copy of practical files.
Locked server and storeroom — physical theft of drives is blocked at the door.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain four types of workplace hazard with one IT-lab example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Physical — trailing cables; electrical — open socket; ergonomic — low screen causing neck pain; psychosocial — long hours without a break. Control each: cable covers, close sockets, raise the monitor, timetable breaks.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Lab PCs face a window, chairs don’t adjust, passwords are on sticky notes. (a) Name three hazard/security issues. (b) One control for each. (c) Who should you report this to?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Glare (ergonomic), poor posture, password leak (security).
(b) Blinds/position screens, adjustable chairs, unique passwords stored in a password manager / teacher vault — never sticky notes.
(c) Lab teacher / system admin / safety in-charge.
