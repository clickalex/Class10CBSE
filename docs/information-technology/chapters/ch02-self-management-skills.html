Chapter 2 · Self-Management Skills–II

Chapter 2 of 20. The highest-scoring employability unit. Lists plus one workplace example cover almost every question.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

3 marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
Always add a workplace example — a data-entry operator under a deadline is enough.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision.
2. Stress is the body's response to pressure. Eustress is positive (a deadline that focuses you); distress is harmful. Symptoms: headache, fast heartbeat, irritability, poor sleep, missed errors.
3. Stress management: deep breathing, short breaks, exercise, talking to someone, breaking work into chunks, saying no to extra load, sleep and a hobby.
4. Self-awareness is knowing your strengths, weaknesses, values and emotions. Tools: SWOT, feedback from others, a journal.
5. Self-motivation is working without being pushed. Intrinsic (interest, pride) lasts longer than extrinsic (marks, money).
6. Self-regulation is managing impulses — staying calm, delaying gratification, following rules.
7. Time management: list tasks, set priorities (urgent/important), use a timetable, avoid multitasking, finish one chunk, review at the end of the day.
8. SMART goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic/Relevant, Time-bound. “Type 30 invoices without error by 5 pm Friday” is SMART; “do better” is not.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
  • Urgent vs important: exam tomorrow is both; scrolling is neither.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
  • Treating all stress as bad — eustress is useful.
  • Giving no example in a 3-marker.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.

Show Answer

Ans:

Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

LONGL34 marks

3. Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Meera has three tasks: a 5 pm file, a messy desk, and a social-media reel. Using urgent/important, what should she do first and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

COMPETENCYC54 marks

5. Read and answer: Ravi’s hands shake before every practical, he skips breakfast and then makes typing errors. (a) Name the type of stress. (b) List four management steps. (c) Write one SMART goal for his next practical.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Distress — it is harming performance.
(b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.
(c) “Complete the Base table of 10 records with primary key set, in 25 minutes, with zero errors, on Friday.”

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Self-Management Skills–II” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision.
Marks lens: marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
Method / memory trick: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
Do not: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Riya is revising “Self-Management Skills–II” (Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
Why the mistake costs marks: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: A Class 10 student is preparing “Self-Management Skills–II”. Their note says: Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision. The chapter is described as: Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.. (a) Name the key idea. (b) How is this usually asked in the paper? (c) State one mistake that would cost a mark.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.
(b) marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
(c) Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

SHORTS92 marks

9. Distinguish between stress and a stressor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

SHORTS103 marks

10. Suggest three ways of managing stress and building self-motivation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Plan the day, break big tasks into small ones and do the hardest first, so that the work never feels endless.
Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.
