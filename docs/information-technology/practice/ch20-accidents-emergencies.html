MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. During a fire, do not: (a) use stairs (b) go to the assembly point (c) use the lift (d) follow the teacher

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) use the lift.

MCQM21 mark

2. For electric shock, first: (a) grab the person (b) switch off power (c) pour water (d) apply toothpaste

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) switch off power.

MCQM31 mark

3. A near-miss should be: (a) ignored (b) reported (c) punished always (d) posted on social media

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) reported.

MCQM41 mark

4. A minor burn is cooled with: (a) ice cubes rubbed hard (b) running water (c) toothpaste (d) cotton oil

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) running water.

MCQM51 mark

5. The most exam-ready way to revise “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Causes of accidents, evacuation, first aid, emergency numbers and incident reporting..

MCQM61 mark

6. A 3-mark answer on “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM71 mark

7. The single all-India emergency number for police, fire and ambulance is: (a) 112 (b) 100 (c) 101 (d) 108

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 112. 100 is police, 101 is fire and 108 is the ambulance service in most states.

MCQM81 mark

8. During an evacuation you should: (a) walk quickly to the assembly point without using the lift (b) run and push past others (c) use the lift to leave faster (d) go back for your bag

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) walk quickly to the assembly point without using the lift. Report to the assembly point so that everyone can be counted.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. List four steps of evacuation in the correct order.

Show Answer

Ans:

Hear the alarm → leave via stairs (not lift) → go to the assembly point → answer the headcount and wait for the all-clear.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why report a near-miss?

Show Answer

Ans:

A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is an emergency, and what should a workplace emergency plan contain?

Show Answer

Ans:

An emergency is an unexpected event that threatens life, health or property, such as a fire, an earthquake, a chemical spill or a sudden illness. A plan should name the escape routes and the assembly point, the person responsible for each task, the emergency telephone numbers, the location of first-aid kits and extinguishers, and how often evacuation drills will be held.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between an accident and a near-miss? Why must both be reported?

Show Answer

Ans:

An accident is an event that causes harm — for example, a shock from a damaged cable. A near-miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not — for example, spotting the same damaged cable before anyone touched it. Both must be reported because the near-miss reveals the hazard while there is still time to fix it.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. The fire alarm rings. A student runs back for a USB and takes the lift. Two wrong actions and the correct ones.

Show Answer

Ans:

Going back for belongings and using the lift. Correct: leave immediately by stairs, USB can be replaced, assemble for headcount.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Riya is revising “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies” (Causes of accidents, evacuation, first aid, emergency numbers and incident reporting.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Power off before touch is the electric-shock line examiners want.
Why the mistake costs marks: Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe the first aid for a minor burn and for a person who has fainted.

Show Answer

Ans:

For a minor burn: hold the area under cool running water for about ten minutes, remove rings or tight clothing near it, cover it with a clean, non-sticky cloth, and do not apply ice, oil or ointment and do not break blisters.
For fainting: lay the person flat on their back, raise the legs above the level of the heart, loosen tight clothing and keep the airway clear.
Do not crowd around, do not throw water on the face and do not give anything to drink until the person is fully conscious.
Call for medical help if the person does not recover within a minute or if breathing is not normal.

SHORTS83 marks

8. What should a workplace emergency plan contain? List its essential parts with one line on each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Alarm and exits — how the alarm sounds and which stairs and exits to use (never the lift).
Assembly and headcount — where to gather outside and who checks that everyone is out.
First aid and numbers — where the kit is and the emergency number 112 for fire, police and ambulance.
Roles and reporting — who leads the evacuation and how every incident and near-miss is written up afterwards.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain first aid for (i) a small cut (ii) a minor burn (iii) suspected electric shock.

Show Answer

Ans:

Cut: wash, pressure, clean dressing. Burn: cool with running water 10 minutes, loose sterile cover, no toothpaste/ice. Shock: do not touch until power is off; then separate with a dry non-conductor, call help, monitor breathing.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: During a practical a student gets a shock from a damaged mouse cable, laughs it off, and no one writes it down. (a) Immediate first actions. (b) Why a report still matters. (c) Two prevention steps so it does not recur.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Switch off power, check breathing, first aid, inform the teacher.
(b) Near-miss/incident record → cable replaced, others warned, pattern spotted.
(c) Inspect peripherals, remove damaged devices, remind: report all shocks.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A Class 10 student is preparing “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies”. Their note says: Causes of accidents: unsafe conditions (wet floor, bad wiring) and unsafe acts (running, bypassing rules). Most lab accidents are preventable. The chapter is described as: Causes of accidents, evacuation, first aid, emergency numbers and incident reporting.. (a) Name the key idea. (b) How is this usually asked in the paper? (c) State one mistake that would cost a mark.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Causes of accidents, evacuation, first aid, emergency numbers and incident reporting.
(b) Evacuation order and first aid don’ts are the 2–3 markers.
(c) Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn.

LONGL125 marks

12. Discuss “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Causes of accidents: unsafe conditions (wet floor, bad wiring) and unsafe acts (running, bypassing rules). Most lab accidents are preventable.
Marks lens: Evacuation order and first aid don’ts are the 2–3 markers.
Method / memory trick: Power off before touch is the electric-shock line examiners want.
Do not: Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn.
