Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.

Show Answer

Ans:

Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Distinguish between stress and a stressor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is self-regulation, and how does it differ from self-motivation? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-regulation is controlling your emotions and behaviour to stay on track — for example, closing social media during study hours despite the urge to scroll.
Self-motivation is generating your own drive to start and continue — for example, setting a personal target of finishing ten Calc rows before lunch.
In short: motivation starts the engine, regulation keeps it on the road.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Meera has three tasks: a 5 pm file, a messy desk, and a social-media reel. Using urgent/important, what should she do first and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Riya is revising “Self-Management Skills–II” (Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
Why the mistake costs marks: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Suggest three ways of managing stress and building self-motivation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Plan the day, break big tasks into small ones and do the hardest first, so that the work never feels endless.
Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.

LONGL83 marks

8. Explain the four steps of time management (list → prioritise → one chunk → review) with a study-evening example.

Show Answer

Ans:

List — write down everything for the evening: finish the Writer practical, revise ten formulas, pack the bag.
Prioritise — rank by urgent/important: the practical due tomorrow comes first, packing the bag last.
One chunk — work on the top task in a single unbroken block instead of switching every ten minutes.
Review — at the end, tick off what is done and carry only the remainder forward.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Ravi’s hands shake before every practical, he skips breakfast and then makes typing errors. (a) Name the type of stress. (b) List four management steps. (c) Write one SMART goal for his next practical.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Distress — it is harming performance.
(b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.
(c) “Complete the Base table of 10 records with primary key set, in 25 minutes, with zero errors, on Friday.”

COMPETENCYC114 marks

Their note says: Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision. The chapter is described as: Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.. (a) Name the key idea. (b) How is this usually asked in the paper? (c) State one mistake that would cost a mark.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.
(b) marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
(c) Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

LONGL125 marks

12. Discuss “Self-Management Skills–II” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision.
Marks lens: marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
Method / memory trick: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
Do not: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
