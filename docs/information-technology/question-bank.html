Ch 1 · Communication Skills–II (10)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Enlist the different methods of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Verbal — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report). (2) Non-verbal — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone. (3) Visual — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Which is the easiest method of communication and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

Non-verbal is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally and work across languages.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?

Show Answer

Ans:

Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Clear — simple, exact message (“Submit the file by Friday 5 pm”). (2) Courteous — polite words (“please”, “thank you”, “sorry for the delay”).

LONGL54 marks

5. Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

Verbal = communication using words, in two forms.
Oral (speech, phone, meeting): fast, instant feedback; no permanent record.
Written (letter, email, report): permanent proof, re-readable, formal; slower, needs literacy.
Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written when a record is needed.

LONGL64 marks

6. Explain any four barriers to communication with one example and one measure each.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic.
(2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals.
(3) Cultural — a thumbs-up is rude in some places → learn customs.
(4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, choose the right time.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Riya messages her team “do it soon”. Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.

Show Answer

Ans:

Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: “Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email.” — now clear, complete and courteous.

APPLICATIONA83 marks

8. A team leader’s juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Replace jargon with simple words and show a chart (linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask “any doubts?”, and paraphrase replies (feedback + active listening).

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says “no questions, I am getting late”. The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify four communication mistakes. (b) Suggest four fixes.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback).
(b) Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, one idea each • slow, clear speech • invite questions and summarise.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: For the school fest, Class X puts up posters at shops, announces on a loudspeaker, and the principal sends a written notice to parents. (a) Name the three methods used. (b) Why is combining them better than one?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral/verbal; notice = written.
(b) Visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together they cover more audiences with both speed and a record.

Ch 2 · Self-Management Skills–II (8)

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.

Show Answer

Ans:

Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

LONGL34 marks

Show Answer

Ans:

Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

(b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.

Ch 4 · Entrepreneurial Skills–II (8)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.

Show Answer

Ans:

A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

SHORTS22 marks

2. State two myths about entrepreneurship and correct them.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) “Born, not made” — skills can be learned. (2) “Needs huge capital” — many start with a small idea and grow.

LONGL34 marks

3. Explain four functions of an entrepreneur with a bakery example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Identify demand for fresh bread; arrange shop, oven and staff; take the risk of unsold stock; market via samples and a board; keep accounts. Each function maps to one quality: opportunity-spotting, organising, risk-taking, selling.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

Opportunity-spotting, initiative, risk-taking (she invests in a printer), problem-solving and customer focus.

(a) Myth of huge capital / only the highly educated.
(b) A tiffin service from a home kitchen; a tuition class in a garage.
(c) Jobs, new products, local resources used, local problems solved.

Show Answer

Show Answer

Ch 9 · Scenarios and Goal Seek (Calc) (8)

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is Goal Seek in Calc?

Show Answer

Ans:

A what-if tool: you set the result you want in a formula cell and Calc finds the input value that produces it.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is a scenario?

Show Answer

Ans:

A named set of values for specified cells so you can switch between cases (best/worst) without retyping.

LONGL34 marks

3. Differentiate Consolidate and Subtotals.

Show Answer

Ans:

Consolidate combines several ranges/sheets into one summary (often another sheet). Subtotals insert summary rows inside one sorted list (sum of each group). Consolidate is across blocks; Subtotals is inside a table.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. A shop wants profit of ₹8,000. Profit is =B2*B3-B4 (price × qty − cost). Which tool, and which cell is “By changing” if qty and cost are fixed?

Show Answer

Ans:

Goal Seek. Set cell = profit, To value = 8000, By changing = price (B2).

COMPETENCYC54 marks

5. Read and answer: Four quarterly sheets have the same layout of expenses. The owner wants one yearly total that updates if a quarter changes. (a) Which tool? (b) Two settings to tick/choose. (c) Why not just type the four totals by hand?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Data → Consolidate.
(b) Function Sum; add each range; Link to source data.
(c) Manual totals go stale; a link updates and reduces typing errors.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Scenarios and Goal Seek (Calc)” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Consolidate: Data → Consolidate. Combines the same layout from several sheets (e.g. four term marks) using Sum/Average. Tick “Link to source data” to auto-update.
Marks lens: Goal Seek and Scenarios are the theory + practical pair.
Method / memory trick: Goal Seek needs a formula in the set cell. Typing a number there will fail.
Do not: Mixing up Goal Seek (one input) with Solver (many inputs + constraints).

Show Answer

Ans:

Hear the alarm → leave via stairs (not lift) → go to the assembly point → answer the headcount and wait for the all-clear.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why report a near-miss?

Show Answer

Ans:

A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

LONGL34 marks

3. Explain first aid for (i) a small cut (ii) a minor burn (iii) suspected electric shock.

Show Answer

Ans:

Cut: wash, pressure, clean dressing. Burn: cool with running water 10 minutes, loose sterile cover, no toothpaste/ice. Shock: do not touch until power is off; then separate with a dry non-conductor, call help, monitor breathing.
