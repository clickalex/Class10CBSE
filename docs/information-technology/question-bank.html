Ch 1 · Communication Skills–II (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Enlist the different methods of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Verbal — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report). (2) Non-verbal — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone. (3) Visual — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Which is the easiest method of communication and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

Non-verbal is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally and work across languages.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?

Show Answer

Ans:

Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Clear — simple, exact message (“Submit the file by Friday 5 pm”). (2) Courteous — polite words (“please”, “thank you”, “sorry for the delay”).

SHORTS52 marks

5. Name any four barriers to effective communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

Physical barriers such as noise or distance; language barriers such as jargon or an unfamiliar accent; psychological barriers such as anger, fear or prejudice; and cultural barriers, where the same word or gesture means different things. Poor listening and the absence of feedback make all of them worse.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Riya messages her team “do it soon”. Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.

Show Answer

Ans:

Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: “Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email.” — now clear, complete and courteous.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A team leader’s juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Replace jargon with simple words and show a chart (linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask “any doubts?”, and paraphrase replies (feedback + active listening).

SHORTS83 marks

8. What are the 7 Cs of effective communication? Explain any three.

Show Answer

Ans:

The seven Cs are clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous.
Clear: say what you mean in words the listener understands, and avoid jargon.
Concise: use only as many words as the message needs, so the point is not lost.
Complete: give all the facts the receiver needs, including who, what, when and where, so that no further questions are needed.

LONGL94 marks

9. Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

Verbal = communication using words, in two forms.
Oral (speech, phone, meeting): fast, instant feedback; no permanent record.
Written (letter, email, report): permanent proof, re-readable, formal; slower, needs literacy.
Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written when a record is needed.

LONGL104 marks

10. Explain any four barriers to communication with one example and one measure each.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic.
(2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals.
(3) Cultural — a thumbs-up is rude in some places → learn customs.
(4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, choose the right time.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says “no questions, I am getting late”. The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify four communication mistakes. (b) Suggest four fixes.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback).
(b) Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, one idea each • slow, clear speech • invite questions and summarise.

COMPETENCYC124 marks

12. Read and answer: For the school fest, Class X puts up posters at shops, announces on a loudspeaker, and the principal sends a written notice to parents. (a) Name the three methods used. (b) Why is combining them better than one?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral/verbal; notice = written.
(b) Visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together they cover more audiences with both speed and a record.

Ch 2 · Self-Management Skills–II (12)

Save this chapter: Text (.txt) ·

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.

Show Answer

Ans:

Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Distinguish between stress and a stressor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is self-regulation, and how does it differ from self-motivation? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-regulation is controlling your emotions and behaviour to stay on track — for example, closing social media during study hours despite the urge to scroll.
Self-motivation is generating your own drive to start and continue — for example, setting a personal target of finishing ten Calc rows before lunch.
In short: motivation starts the engine, regulation keeps it on the road.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Meera has three tasks: a 5 pm file, a messy desk, and a social-media reel. Using urgent/important, what should she do first and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student writes SMART as “Specific, Achievable, Measurable, Time-bound, Realistic”. Is the order correct? Give the correct expansion and turn “I want to type faster” into a SMART goal.

Show Answer

Ans:

The letters must follow the order of the word: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic (or Relevant), Time-bound. The student swapped A and M and put T before R.
“Type faster” is vague: it is not specific, measurable or time-bound.
SMART version: “Type 35 words per minute with at most 2 errors in a 5-minute test by the end of this month.”

SHORTS73 marks

7. Suggest three ways of managing stress and building self-motivation.

Show Answer

Ans:

Plan the day, break big tasks into small ones and do the hardest first, so that the work never feels endless.
Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.

LONGL83 marks

8. Explain the four steps of time management (list → prioritise → one chunk → review) with a study-evening example.

Show Answer

Ans:

List — write down everything for the evening: finish the Writer practical, revise ten formulas, pack the bag.
Prioritise — rank by urgent/important: the practical due tomorrow comes first, packing the bag last.
One chunk — work on the top task in a single unbroken block instead of switching every ten minutes.
Review — at the end, tick off what is done and carry only the remainder forward.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Ravi’s hands shake before every practical, he skips breakfast and then makes typing errors. (a) Name the type of stress. (b) List four management steps. (c) Write one SMART goal for his next practical.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Distress — it is harming performance.
(b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.
(c) “Complete the Base table of 10 records with primary key set, in 25 minutes, with zero errors, on Friday.”

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Asha knows she is good at organising events but weak at public speaking, so she joins the debate club. On her first day she feels nervous, but she takes deep breaths and goes on stage anyway. (a) Which skill does knowing her strengths and weaknesses show? (b) How does joining the debate club show self-motivation? (c) Which skill is shown when she calms herself and goes on stage?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Self-awareness: understanding one’s own strengths, weaknesses, feelings and values.
(b) She sets herself a goal to improve and acts on it without being told; the drive comes from inside her.
(c) Self-regulation: controlling her emotions and behaviour in a situation.

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe five ways in which a student can manage stress, giving an example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Plan and prioritise: make a to-do list and do the important task first, so exam work does not pile up.
Exercise and breathing: a 20-minute walk, yoga or deep breathing calms the body.
Sleep and food: sleeping 7–8 hours and eating on time keeps the mind fresh before a test.
Talk it out: sharing a worry with a friend, parent or counsellor stops it from growing.
Hobbies and breaks: music or sport after study gives the mind a rest; positive self-talk also helps.

SHORTS11 mark

1. What is an operating system? Give two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

System software that manages hardware and runs programs. Examples: Windows, Linux, Android.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate a file and a folder with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

A file stores data (marks.ods). A folder stores files and other folders (Class10/IT/).

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is an operating system? Name two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

An operating system is the system software that manages the computer's memory, processor, disks and devices and lets the user run application programs. Examples are Windows, Linux, macOS and Android.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the Recycle Bin, and how is restoring a file different from deleting it permanently?

Show Answer

Ans:

The Recycle Bin is a holding area where deleted files wait until it is emptied. Restoring returns the file to its original folder, so nothing is lost. Deleting permanently (emptying the Bin, or Shift + Delete) removes the file beyond easy recovery — which is why important files should be backed up before the Bin is emptied.

SHORTS52 marks

5. List four basic care rules that protect a computer from physical damage and data loss.

Show Answer

Ans:

Keep the machine dust-free and away from food and drink, which damage keyboards and circuits.
Use a surge protector so voltage spikes do not harm the hardware.
Shut down through the OS command instead of pulling the plug, which can corrupt files.
Run updated antivirus software and keep a backup of important work.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Amit eats chips over the keyboard and never shuts the PC down. List three risks and three care steps.

Show Answer

Ans:

Risks: crumbs jam keys, oil damage, overheating, data loss. Care: no food/drink, proper shutdown, dust-free vents, surge protector, backups.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student says, “Windows, Linux and LibreOffice Writer are all operating systems.” Correct the statement.

Show Answer

Ans:

Windows and Linux are operating systems: system software that controls the hardware and lets other programs run.
LibreOffice Writer is application software, a word processor used to create documents; it runs on top of an operating system.
Other operating systems are macOS and Android; other applications are Calc and a web browser.

SHORTS83 marks

8. How would you organise files and folders so that your work is always easy to find?

Show Answer

Ans:

Make one folder for each subject or project, and inside it one folder per term or per type of work.
Give every file a short, descriptive name with the date, such as maths_notes_2026-08-12.
Keep the folder tree shallow — no more than two or three levels — and delete or archive old versions.
Keep a backup copy on an external drive or in the cloud, and run an antivirus scan on anything you download.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Why should files be given clear names and kept in subject-wise folders? Illustrate with a school-project example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Clear names and folders make work findable: a file named aaa.odt on a crowded Desktop is easily lost or overwritten.
Example — a project split as Project/notes.odt, Project/images/, Project/final-report.odt lets any part be found in seconds.
Good organisation also protects against loss: one folder is easy to back up, scattered files are not.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: Meera’s project file vanished after a power cut. She had saved only on the Desktop as aaa.odt. (a) Two file-management mistakes. (b) Two care/maintenance steps that would have helped.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) No backup; a meaningless file name; Desktop-only copy.
(b) Surge protector / UPS; Save As into a named folder; weekly backup on a pen drive or cloud.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Rohan creates a letter in Writer, a budget in Calc and a database in Base. He also wants to give his teacher a copy of the letter that opens in Microsoft Word. (a) Give the default file extension for each of the three files. (b) What is the difference between Save and Save As? (c) How can he make a Word-compatible copy?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Writer: .odt; Calc: .ods; Base: .odb.
(b) Save overwrites the current file; Save As lets you choose a new name, folder or file type and keeps the original.
(c) Use File → Save As and choose the Word file type (.docx) in the “Save as type” box.

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe five basic file-management operations in an operating system and give the shortcut or method for each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Create a folder: right-click an empty space and choose New → Folder, then type its name.
Rename: select the file and press F2 (or right-click → Rename).
Copy: select the file, press Ctrl + C, open the destination and press Ctrl + V.
Move: select the file, press Ctrl + X, open the destination and press Ctrl + V (or drag it there).
Delete: press Delete to send it to the Recycle Bin, from where it can be restored; Shift + Delete removes it permanently.

Ch 4 · Entrepreneurial Skills–II (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.

Show Answer

Ans:

A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

SHORTS22 marks

2. State two myths about entrepreneurship and correct them.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) “Born, not made” — skills can be learned. (2) “Needs huge capital” — many start with a small idea and grow.

SHORTS32 marks

3. List any four qualities an entrepreneur needs.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-confidence, the willingness to take calculated risks, initiative and perseverance, the ability to plan and organise, and the readiness to learn from failure and try again.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is a social entrepreneur? How does the aim differ from that of a business entrepreneur?

Show Answer

Ans:

A social entrepreneur starts an enterprise mainly to solve a social problem — for example, cheap solar lamps for villages — rather than to maximise profit. A business entrepreneur also creates value but measures success primarily in profit and growth. Both take risks and organise resources; they differ in what counts as the return.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Neha notices students waiting 40 minutes for a shared printer. She starts a paid print-and-bind desk. Which entrepreneurial qualities is she using?

Show Answer

Ans:

Opportunity-spotting, initiative, risk-taking (she invests in a printer), problem-solving and customer focus.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student says, “Every shopkeeper is an entrepreneur.” Correct this using the ideas of risk and innovation.

Show Answer

Ans:

An entrepreneur starts a new venture, brings something new (a product, service or way of working), organises the resources and bears the risk of loss.
A shopkeeper who only resells goods in the usual way, with no new idea, is a trader or a small business owner.
A shopkeeper who starts a new kind of service, such as home delivery that no one offered before, takes a risk and innovates, and so acts as an entrepreneur.

SHORTS73 marks

7. What functions does an entrepreneur perform in running an enterprise?

Show Answer

Ans:

Spotting an opportunity and turning it into a product or service people will pay for.
Arranging the money, buying raw materials, machines and premises, and hiring and training workers.
Organising production, keeping accounts and marketing the product.
Bearing the risk of loss, and taking the decisions that keep the enterprise alive.

LONGL83 marks

8. Explain the role of entrepreneurs in society with reference to jobs, products and local resources.

Show Answer

Ans:

Jobs — every new enterprise hires people, from a bakery's helpers to a startup's designers.
Products — entrepreneurs turn needs into goods and services, such as affordable school stationery or repair shops.
Local resources — they use nearby material and talent, for example fruit from local orchards in a jam unit, keeping wealth in the area.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain four functions of an entrepreneur with a bakery example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Identify demand for fresh bread; arrange shop, oven and staff; take the risk of unsold stock; market via samples and a board; keep accounts. Each function maps to one quality: opportunity-spotting, organising, risk-taking, selling.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A poster says “Only rich, highly educated people can start a business.” (a) Name the myth. (b) Give two counter-examples from daily life. (c) State two roles of entrepreneurship in society.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Myth of huge capital / only the highly educated.
(b) A tiffin service from a home kitchen; a tuition class in a garage.
(c) Jobs, new products, local resources used, local problems solved.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Amit notices that farmers near his village lose money because vegetables spoil on the way to the city. He buys a van with a cooling unit, collects vegetables directly from farmers, sells them to city shops and hires two local youths. (a) Which entrepreneurial qualities does Amit show? (b) Name two functions he performs as an entrepreneur. (c) How does his enterprise help society?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) He spots a need (opportunity recognition), is innovative in finding a solution and is willing to take a financial risk.
(b) He organises resources (the van, workers, suppliers) and bears the risk of the business.
(c) It creates jobs, reduces food wastage and increases the farmers’ income.

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe five steps in setting up a small enterprise.

Show Answer

Ans:

Step 1: generate and evaluate a business idea that meets a real need.
Step 2: study the market: who the customers are, what they will pay and who the competitors are.
Step 3: prepare a business plan covering the product, costs, expected sales and profit.
Step 4: arrange the money (capital) from savings, family or a loan, and choose a suitable location.
Step 5: complete legal formalities such as registration and licences, then start and review the business regularly.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Define sustainable development.

Show Answer

Ans:

Development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What are green skills? Give two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

Skills that help a person live and work in an environmentally friendly way. Examples: waste segregation, saving electricity, water conservation.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is sustainable development?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is development that meets the needs of the present without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs — using resources carefully, keeping pollution low and protecting ecosystems while the economy grows.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is a green economy? State its three features.

Show Answer

Ans:

A green economy is one that improves human well-being while reducing environmental risks. Its three features are: low carbon emissions, efficient use of resources, and inclusive growth that does not leave the poor behind.

SHORTS52 marks

5. Arrange Reduce, Reuse, Recycle in order of preference and justify the order with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

First Reduce — preventing waste is best: carry a cloth bag instead of taking a plastic one each time.
Then Reuse — using again costs nothing extra: refill the same water bottle for months.
Last Recycle — reprocessing still needs energy: send worn-out notebooks for paper recycling only when reuse is over.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A computer lab leaves 20 PCs on overnight. Suggest three green practices and one green skill the lab assistant needs.

Show Answer

Ans:

Shut down PCs, use sleep mode, switch off AC/lights, enable duplex printing. Skill: energy-conservation habit / e-waste handling.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A student defines sustainable development as “using no natural resources so that nothing is wasted”. Correct the definition.

Show Answer

Ans:

Sustainable development means meeting the needs of the present without harming the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
It is about wise use of resources, not zero use: we still use water, energy and materials, but carefully.
Examples are using renewable energy, saving water and recycling.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Give three examples of green jobs and explain what makes a job green.

Show Answer

Ans:

Installing and maintaining solar panels, managing recycling and composting, organic farming, water harvesting and energy auditing are all green jobs.
A job is green when it reduces the use of energy or raw materials, cuts greenhouse-gas emissions and waste, and protects or restores ecosystems.
It should also provide decent work and income, because a green economy is meant to improve human well-being as well as the environment.

SHORTS93 marks

9. What makes a job a ‘green job’? Name two green jobs and the green skill each needs.

Show Answer

Ans:

A job is green when it directly protects or restores the environment — through clean energy, waste handling, conservation or green construction.
Solar-panel technician — needs the skill of installing and maintaining photovoltaic systems safely.
Waste-segregation supervisor — needs the skill of sorting waste at source into wet, dry and hazardous streams.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: A factory dumps dye in a river but pays workers well. (a) Is this sustainable development? Why? (b) Name two green-economy changes. (c) One green job this town could create.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) No — present wages at the cost of future water and health.
(b) Treat effluent, switch to safer dye, reuse water.
(c) Water-treatment operator or waste-segregation worker.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: A repair shop collects old computers, refurbishes some for sale at low cost and sends the rest to an authorised recycler. (a) Which of the 3Rs does each activity show? (b) Why is it dangerous to throw such equipment into the dustbin? (c) Name a green job linked to this work.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Refurbishing and reselling is reuse; sending the rest to a recycler is recycle.
(b) E-waste contains metals such as lead and mercury, which pollute soil and water and harm health.
(c) An e-waste recycling technician (or a refurbishing specialist).

LONGL125 marks

12. Describe five green practices that a school computer lab can follow.

Show Answer

Ans:

Switch off computers, monitors and lights when not in use, and use energy-saving settings.
Print only when necessary and print on both sides, and share files digitally.
Repair and reuse equipment, and give old devices to an authorised e-waste recycler.
Use rechargeable batteries and energy-efficient (LED) lighting.
Make students aware of these habits, for example by appointing an “eco-monitor”.

Ch 6 · Introduction to Styles (Writer) (12)

Save this chapter: Text (.txt) ·

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is a style in Writer? Name any four categories.

Show Answer

Ans:

A named set of formatting that can be reused. Categories: Paragraph, Character, Page, Frame, List, Table.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is Fill Format mode?

Show Answer

Ans:

A Styles-window tool that copies the selected style onto each paragraph (or object) you click, like a format paintbrush.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why are styles better than formatting each heading by hand?

Show Answer

Ans:

A style stores the whole set of formatting — font, size, spacing, alignment — in one named item. Change the style once and every paragraph using it changes, the document stays consistent, and headings styled with Heading 1, 2 and 3 can be collected automatically into a table of contents.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between a paragraph style and a character style? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

A paragraph style formats a whole paragraph at once — for example, Heading 1 sets the font, size and spacing of every chapter title.
A character style formats only selected words inside a paragraph — for example, an Emphasis style that italicises a single term without touching the rest of the line.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A 20-page report uses Bold 16 pt for titles. The examiner wants Heading 1 in navy. What should the student do instead of selecting each title?

Show Answer

Ans:

Create or modify Heading 1 (navy, size, spacing), then Fill Format / select each title and apply Heading 1. Later, one modify updates all.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student wants one heading in a 30-page report to be red, so she changes the colour in the style “Heading 1”. Every heading in the report turns red. Why did this happen, and how could she have changed only one?

Show Answer

Ans:

A style is a stored set of formatting applied to all paragraphs that use it; changing the style changes every paragraph in that style.
To change only one heading, select it and apply the colour as direct formatting (or give that heading its own new style).
Use “Update Style” or Modify only when you want a change to apply across the whole document.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe how to create and then apply a paragraph style in LibreOffice Writer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Format one paragraph exactly as you want it, then open the Styles window with F11 and click the New Style from Selection button.
Give the style a name such as ‘MyBodyText' and click OK.
To apply it, click anywhere in the paragraph you want to change and double-click the style name under Paragraph Styles.
To change the whole document, right-click the style, choose Modify and set the font, size and spacing once.

SHORTS83 marks

Switch off and unplug equipment at the end of the day, and never overload a socket with adapters.
If a fire starts, raise the alarm, cut off the power if it is safe to do so, leave by the marked route without using the lift, and go to the assembly point.

12. Name four types of fire extinguisher and say which one should be used on an electrical fire in a computer lab. State the PASS steps for using an extinguisher.

Show Answer

Ans:

Types: water, foam, dry powder and carbon dioxide (CO₂).
Water and foam must not be used on an electrical fire, because they conduct electricity and can cause a shock or damage the equipment.
For computers, a CO₂ extinguisher is best because it leaves no residue; dry powder also works but can harm the electronics.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Why report a near-miss?

Show Answer

Ans:

A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is an emergency, and what should a workplace emergency plan contain?

Show Answer

Ans:

An emergency is an unexpected event that threatens life, health or property, such as a fire, an earthquake, a chemical spill or a sudden illness. A plan should name the escape routes and the assembly point, the person responsible for each task, the emergency telephone numbers, the location of first-aid kits and extinguishers, and how often evacuation drills will be held.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between an accident and a near-miss? Why must both be reported?

Show Answer

Ans:

An accident is an event that causes harm — for example, a shock from a damaged cable. A near-miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not — for example, spotting the same damaged cable before anyone touched it. Both must be reported because the near-miss reveals the hazard while there is still time to fix it.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student says that toothpaste or ice should be put on a minor burn. Correct this and give the right first aid.

Show Answer

Ans:

Toothpaste, butter and ice must not be put on a burn; they can damage the skin or cause infection.
Hold the burn under cool running water for about 10–20 minutes.
Then cover it loosely with a clean, non-sticky dressing, do not burst any blisters, and get medical help if the burn is large or deep.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe the first aid for a minor burn and for a person who has fainted.

Show Answer

Ans:

For a minor burn: hold the area under cool running water for about ten minutes, remove rings or tight clothing near it, cover it with a clean, non-sticky cloth, and do not apply ice, oil or ointment and do not break blisters.
For fainting: lay the person flat on their back, raise the legs above the level of the heart, loosen tight clothing and keep the airway clear.
Do not crowd around, do not throw water on the face and do not give anything to drink until the person is fully conscious.
Call for medical help if the person does not recover within a minute or if breathing is not normal.
