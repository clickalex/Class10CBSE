Ch 1 · Communication Skills–II (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Enlist the different methods of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Verbal — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report). (2) Non-verbal — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone. (3) Visual — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Which is the easiest method of communication and why?

Show Answer

Ans:

Non-verbal is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally and work across languages.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?

Show Answer

Ans:

Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.

SHORTS42 marks

4. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Clear — simple, exact message (“Submit the file by Friday 5 pm”). (2) Courteous — polite words (“please”, “thank you”, “sorry for the delay”).

SHORTS52 marks

5. Name any four barriers to effective communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

Physical barriers such as noise or distance; language barriers such as jargon or an unfamiliar accent; psychological barriers such as anger, fear or prejudice; and cultural barriers, where the same word or gesture means different things. Poor listening and the absence of feedback make all of them worse.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Riya messages her team “do it soon”. Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.

Show Answer

Ans:

Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: “Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email.” — now clear, complete and courteous.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. A team leader’s juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Replace jargon with simple words and show a chart (linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask “any doubts?”, and paraphrase replies (feedback + active listening).

SHORTS83 marks

8. What are the 7 Cs of effective communication? Explain any three.

Show Answer

Ans:

The seven Cs are clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous.
Clear: say what you mean in words the listener understands, and avoid jargon.
Concise: use only as many words as the message needs, so the point is not lost.
Complete: give all the facts the receiver needs, including who, what, when and where, so that no further questions are needed.

LONGL94 marks

9. Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.

Show Answer

Ans:

Verbal = communication using words, in two forms.
Oral (speech, phone, meeting): fast, instant feedback; no permanent record.
Written (letter, email, report): permanent proof, re-readable, formal; slower, needs literacy.
Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written when a record is needed.

LONGL104 marks

10. Explain any four barriers to communication with one example and one measure each.

Show Answer

Ans:

(1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic.
(2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals.
(3) Cultural — a thumbs-up is rude in some places → learn customs.
(4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, choose the right time.

COMPETENCYC114 marks

11. Read and answer: Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says “no questions, I am getting late”. The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify four communication mistakes. (b) Suggest four fixes.

Show Answer

(a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback).

COMPETENCYC124 marks

12. Read and answer: For the school fest, Class X puts up posters at shops, announces on a loudspeaker, and the principal sends a written notice to parents. (a) Name the three methods used. (b) Why is combining them better than one?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral/verbal; notice = written.
(b) Visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together they cover more audiences with both speed and a record.

Ch 2 · Self-Management Skills–II (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.

Show Answer

Ans:

Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Distinguish between stress and a stressor.

Show Answer

Ans:

Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is self-regulation, and how does it differ from self-motivation? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Self-regulation is controlling your emotions and behaviour to stay on track — for example, closing social media during study hours despite the urge to scroll.
Self-motivation is generating your own drive to start and continue — for example, setting a personal target of finishing ten Calc rows before lunch.
In short: motivation starts the engine, regulation keeps it on the road.

SHORTS73 marks

Show Answer

Ans:

Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.

LONGL83 marks

8. Explain the four steps of time management (list → prioritise → one chunk → review) with a study-evening example.

Show Answer

Ans:

List — write down everything for the evening: finish the Writer practical, revise ten formulas, pack the bag.
Prioritise — rank by urgent/important: the practical due tomorrow comes first, packing the bag last.
One chunk — work on the top task in a single unbroken block instead of switching every ten minutes.
Review — at the end, tick off what is done and carry only the remainder forward.

LONGL94 marks

9. Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.

Show Answer

Ans:

Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

Ch 3 · ICT Skills–II (12)

SHORTS11 mark

1. What is an operating system? Give two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

System software that manages hardware and runs programs. Examples: Windows, Linux, Android.

SHORTS22 marks

Show Answer

Ans:

A file stores data (marks.ods). A folder stores files and other folders (Class10/IT/).

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is an operating system? Name two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

An operating system is the system software that manages the computer's memory, processor, disks and devices and lets the user run application programs. Examples are Windows, Linux, macOS and Android.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the Recycle Bin, and how is restoring a file different from deleting it permanently?

Show Answer

Ans:

The Recycle Bin is a holding area where deleted files wait until it is emptied. Restoring returns the file to its original folder, so nothing is lost. Deleting permanently (emptying the Bin, or Shift + Delete) removes the file beyond easy recovery — which is why important files should be backed up before the Bin is emptied.

SHORTS52 marks

5. List four basic care rules that protect a computer from physical damage and data loss.

Show Answer

Ans:

Keep the machine dust-free and away from food and drink, which damage keyboards and circuits.
Use a surge protector so voltage spikes do not harm the hardware.
Shut down through the OS command instead of pulling the plug, which can corrupt files.
Run updated antivirus software and keep a backup of important work.

Keep a backup copy on an external drive or in the cloud, and run an antivirus scan on anything you download.

Good organisation also protects against loss: one folder is easy to back up, scattered files are not.

Ch 4 · Entrepreneurial Skills–II (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.

Show Answer

Ans:

A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

SHORTS22 marks

Show Answer

SHORTS32 marks

Self-confidence, the willingness to take calculated risks, initiative and perseverance, the ability to plan and organise, and the readiness to learn from failure and try again.

SHORTS42 marks

Show Answer

Ans:

A social entrepreneur starts an enterprise mainly to solve a social problem — for example, cheap solar lamps for villages — rather than to maximise profit. A business entrepreneur also creates value but measures success primarily in profit and growth. Both take risks and organise resources; they differ in what counts as the return.

Ch 6 · Introduction to Styles (Writer) (12)

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is a style in Writer? Name any four categories.

Show Answer

Ans:

A named set of formatting that can be reused. Categories: Paragraph, Character, Page, Frame, List, Table.

SHORTS22 marks

2. What is Fill Format mode?

Show Answer

Ans:

A Styles-window tool that copies the selected style onto each paragraph (or object) you click, like a format paintbrush.

SHORTS32 marks

3. Why are styles better than formatting each heading by hand?

Show Answer

Ans:

A style stores the whole set of formatting — font, size, spacing, alignment — in one named item. Change the style once and every paragraph using it changes, the document stays consistent, and headings styled with Heading 1, 2 and 3 can be collected automatically into a table of contents.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between a paragraph style and a character style? Give one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

A paragraph style formats a whole paragraph at once — for example, Heading 1 sets the font, size and spacing of every chapter title.
A character style formats only selected words inside a paragraph — for example, an Emphasis style that italicises a single term without touching the rest of the line.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A 20-page report uses Bold 16 pt for titles. The examiner wants Heading 1 in navy. What should the student do instead of selecting each title?

Show Answer

Ans:

Create or modify Heading 1 (navy, size, spacing), then Fill Format / select each title and apply Heading 1. Later, one modify updates all.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe how to create and then apply a paragraph style in LibreOffice Writer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Give the style a name such as ‘MyBodyText' and click OK.

Situation — a report has forty Heading 2 titles in black; the teacher wants them navy. Format one title navy, click Update Style, and all forty change in one step instead of forty.

Show Answer

Show Answer

Ans:

A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

SHORTS32 marks

3. What is an emergency, and what should a workplace emergency plan contain?

Show Answer

Ans:

An emergency is an unexpected event that threatens life, health or property, such as a fire, an earthquake, a chemical spill or a sudden illness. A plan should name the escape routes and the assembly point, the person responsible for each task, the emergency telephone numbers, the location of first-aid kits and extinguishers, and how often evacuation drills will be held.

SHORTS42 marks

4. What is the difference between an accident and a near-miss? Why must both be reported?

Show Answer

Ans:

An accident is an event that causes harm — for example, a shock from a damaged cable. A near-miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not — for example, spotting the same damaged cable before anyone touched it. Both must be reported because the near-miss reveals the hazard while there is still time to fix it.

7. Describe the first aid for a minor burn and for a person who has fainted.

Show Answer

Ans:

For a minor burn: hold the area under cool running water for about ten minutes, remove rings or tight clothing near it, cover it with a clean, non-sticky cloth, and do not apply ice, oil or ointment and do not break blisters.

Call for medical help if the person does not recover within a minute or if breathing is not normal.
