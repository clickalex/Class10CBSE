A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.
    Ans:
      x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
      Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
      Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.
    Ans:
      α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
      1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.
    Ans: A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.
    Ans:
      Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
      Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
      k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Polynomials” (Zeroes of a quadratic and their relation to coefficients). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing the sum as +b/a. It is −b/a. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:

      Why the mistake costs marks: Writing the sum as +b/a. It is −b/a.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Polynomials”: “Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Zeroes of a quadratic and their relation to coefficients.
      x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2. Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓ Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 6x + k and 3α + 2β = 20, find k.
    Ans:
      α + β = 6 (sum of zeroes) and 3α + 2β = 20.
      Subtract twice the first equation from the second: α = 20 − 12 = 8, so β = −2.
      Product αβ = 8(−2) = −16, and product = c/a = k, so k = −16.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Divide 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5 by x² + 2x + 1 and verify the division algorithm.
    Ans:
      Quotient = 3x − 5, remainder = 9x + 10.
      Check: (x² + 2x + 1)(3x − 5) + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + 6x² + 3x − 5x² − 10x − 5 + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5, which is the dividend — so dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] A quadratic polynomial cuts the x-axis at −2 and 3 and its graph passes through (0, 6). Find the polynomial.
    Ans:
      With zeroes −2 and 3, p(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 3).
      It passes through (0, 6), so 6 = a(2)(−3) = −6a, giving a = −1.
      Hence p(x) = −(x + 2)(x − 3) = −x² + x + 6.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The zeroes of x² + 7x + 10 are: (a) 2, 5  (b) −2, −5  (c) 2, −5  (d) −2, 5
    Ans: (b) −2, −5. Sum = −7, product = 10, so both zeroes are negative.

M2. If α, β are zeroes of x² − 5x + 6, then α + β = ? (a) 6  (b) 5  (c) −5  (d) 1
    Ans: (b) 5. Sum = −b/a = 5.

M3. A quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 3 and −3 is: (a) x² − 9  (b) x² + 9  (c) x² − 6x + 9  (d) x² − 3
    Ans: (a) x² − 9. Sum = 0 and product = −9.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Polynomials” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Zeroes of a quadratic and their relation to coefficients.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Polynomials” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. A polynomial of degree 3 is called: (a) linear  (b) quadratic  (c) cubic  (d) biquadratic
    Ans: (c) cubic. Degree 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic and 4 is biquadratic.

M7. If one zero of x² − 4x + 1 is 2 + √3, the other zero is: (a) 2 − √3  (b) −2 − √3  (c) √3 − 2  (d) −√3
    Ans: (a) 2 − √3. Sum of zeroes = 4, so the other zero = 4 − (2 + √3) = 2 − √3.

M8. A quadratic polynomial can have at most: (a) 0 zeroes  (b) 1 zero  (c) 2 zeroes  (d) 3 zeroes
    Ans: (c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.
