A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.
    Ans:
      x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
      Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
      Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.
    Ans:
      α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
      1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.
    Ans: A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.
    Ans:
      Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
      Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
      k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A student writes: “For 2x² − 5x + 3, the sum of the zeroes is b/a = −5/2 and the product is c/a = 3/2.” Find the error, then give the correct sum and verify it with the actual zeroes.
    Ans:
      Error: the sum of the zeroes is −b/a, not b/a. Here −b/a = −(−5)/2 = 5/2. (The product c/a = 3/2 is correct.)
      Factorise: 2x² − 5x + 3 = (2x − 3)(x − 1), so the zeroes are 3/2 and 1.
      Check: 3/2 + 1 = 5/2 ✓ and (3/2) × 1 = 3/2 ✓.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] The area of a rectangular plot is (x² + 7x + 12) square metres and its length is (x + 4) metres. Find its breadth and the zeroes of the area polynomial.
    Ans:
      Breadth = area ÷ length = (x² + 7x + 12) ÷ (x + 4).
      x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4), so the breadth is (x + 3) metres.
      The zeroes come from (x + 3)(x + 4) = 0, so x = −3 and x = −4.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 6x + k and 3α + 2β = 20, find k.
    Ans:
      α + β = 6 (sum of zeroes) and 3α + 2β = 20.
      Subtract twice the first equation from the second: α = 20 − 12 = 8, so β = −2.
      Product αβ = 8(−2) = −16, and product = c/a = k, so k = −16.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Divide 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5 by x² + 2x + 1 and verify the division algorithm.
    Ans:
      Quotient = 3x − 5, remainder = 9x + 10.
      Check: (x² + 2x + 1)(3x − 5) + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + 6x² + 3x − 5x² − 10x − 5 + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5, which is the dividend — so dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    The graph of y = p(x), where p(x) is a quadratic polynomial with leading coefficient 1, is a parabola opening upward that cuts the x-axis at (−1, 0) and (4, 0).

    (a) How many zeroes does p(x) have, and what are they?
    (b) Find the sum and the product of the zeroes.
    (c) Write p(x).
    Ans:
      (a) Two zeroes, −1 and 4 — the x-coordinates of the points where the graph meets the x-axis.
      (b) Sum = −1 + 4 = 3 and product = (−1)(4) = −4.
      (c) p(x) = x² − (sum)x + product = x² − 3x − 4.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A toy company models its profit (in thousands of rupees) from selling x thousand toys as P(x) = x² − 7x + 10.

    (a) For which numbers of toys does the profit become zero?
    (b) Verify the relation between the zeroes and the coefficients.
    (c) Give an example of a quadratic polynomial whose graph touches the x-axis at only one point.
    Ans:
      (a) x² − 7x + 10 = (x − 2)(x − 5) = 0, so the profit is zero at x = 2 and x = 5 thousand toys.
      (b) Sum = 2 + 5 = 7 = −(−7)/1 ✓; product = 2 × 5 = 10 = 10/1 ✓.
      (c) x² − 4x + 4 = (x − 2)²: its two zeroes coincide, so the parabola just touches the x-axis at (2, 0).

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] A quadratic polynomial cuts the x-axis at −2 and 3 and its graph passes through (0, 6). Find the polynomial.
    Ans:
      With zeroes −2 and 3, p(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 3).
      It passes through (0, 6), so 6 = a(2)(−3) = −6a, giving a = −1.
      Hence p(x) = −(x + 2)(x − 3) = −x² + x + 6.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 4x + 1, find (i) α² + β², (ii) 1/α + 1/β, and (iii) a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are α² and β².
    Ans:
      From the polynomial: α + β = 4 and αβ = 1.
      (i) α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 16 − 2 = 14.
      (ii) 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 4/1 = 4.
      (iii) Sum of the new zeroes = α² + β² = 14; product = (αβ)² = 1.
      Required polynomial: x² − 14x + 1.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The zeroes of x² + 7x + 10 are: (a) 2, 5  (b) 2, −5  (c) −2, −5  (d) −2, 5
    Ans: (c) −2, −5. Sum = −7, product = 10, so both zeroes are negative.

M2. If α, β are zeroes of x² − 5x + 6, then α + β = ? (a) 6  (b) 5  (c) −5  (d) 1
    Ans: (b) 5. Sum = −b/a = 5.

M3. A quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 3 and −3 is: (a) x² − 9  (b) x² + 9  (c) x² − 6x + 9  (d) x² − 3
    Ans: (a) x² − 9. Sum = 0 and product = −9.

M4. If the zeroes of a quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, then: (a) a = 0  (b) b = 0  (c) c = 0  (d) a + c = 0
    Ans: (b) b = 0. Zeroes α and −α have sum 0, and the sum is −b/a, so b = 0.

M5. The graph of a quadratic polynomial lies entirely above the x-axis. The number of real zeroes it has is: (a) one  (b) two  (c) infinitely many  (d) none
    Ans: (d) none. Zeroes are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph meets the x-axis; a graph that never meets it has no real zero.

M6. A polynomial of degree 3 is called: (a) linear  (b) quadratic  (c) biquadratic  (d) cubic
    Ans: (d) cubic. Degree 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic and 4 is biquadratic.

M7. If one zero of x² − 4x + 1 is 2 + √3, the other zero is: (a) 2 − √3  (b) −2 − √3  (c) √3 − 2  (d) −√3
    Ans: (a) 2 − √3. Sum of zeroes = 4, so the other zero = 4 − (2 + √3) = 2 − √3.

M8. A quadratic polynomial can have at most: (a) 0 zeroes  (b) 1 zero  (c) 2 zeroes  (d) 3 zeroes
    Ans: (c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.
