A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the length of an arc of a sector with radius 21 cm and central angle 120°.
    Ans: Arc length = (θ/360°) × 2πr = (120/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = (1/3) × 132 = 44 cm. Use 22/7 for π whenever the radius is a multiple of 7, as it is here — the sevens cancel and the arithmetic stays exact.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] The circumference of a circle exceeds its diameter by 30 cm. Find the radius (take π = 22/7).
    Ans: 2πr − 2r = 30 ⇒ 2r(22/7 − 1) = 30 ⇒ 2r(15/7) = 30 ⇒ r = 7 cm. Check: circumference 44 cm, diameter 14 cm, difference 30 cm.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find the area of a sector of radius 6 cm with a central angle of 60° (take π = 3.14).
    Ans: Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr² = (60/360) × 3.14 × 36 = (1/6) × 113.04 = 18.84 cm². Here the radius is not a multiple of 7, so π = 3.14 as the question directs, and the unit is cm² because this is an area, not a length.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Find the perimeter of a sector with radius 14 cm and central angle 90°.
    Ans:
      Arc = (90/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 14 = (1/4) × 88 = 22 cm.
      Perimeter = 2(14) + 22 = 50 cm.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A student finds the area of a circular pond of diameter 28 m as (22/7) × 28² = 2464 m². Identify the error and give the correct area.
    Ans:
      Error: the formula πr² needs the radius, but the student substituted the diameter 28 m.
      Radius = 28 ÷ 2 = 14 m.
      Area = (22/7) × 14² = (22/7) × 196 = 616 m² (one-fourth of the wrong value).

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] A horse is tied to a peg at one corner of a square field of side 15 m by a rope 5 m long. Find the area of the field over which the horse can graze. (Take π = 3.14.)
    Ans:
      The horse can reach points within 5 m of the corner, but only inside the field, which spans a 90° angle at that corner.
      So the grazing region is a quadrant (sector of 90°) of radius 5 m.
      Area = (90/360) × 3.14 × 5² = ¼ × 78.5 = 19.625 m².

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Find the area of the minor segment of a circle of radius 14 cm whose central angle is 90° (π = 22/7).
    Ans:
      Area of sector = (90/360) × (22/7) × 14² = ¼ × 616 = 154 cm².
      Area of the right triangle formed by the two radii = ½ × 14 × 14 = 98 cm².
      Area of the minor segment = 154 − 98 = 56 cm².

Q8. [Long · 4 marks] A square of side 14 cm has a circle inscribed in it. Find the area between the square and the circle.
    Ans:
      The inscribed circle has diameter 14 cm, so radius 7 cm.
      Area of square = 196 cm². Area of circle = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm².
      Required area = 196 − 154 = 42 cm².

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A pizza of radius 14 cm is cut into 8 equal slices. (Take π = 22/7.)
    
    (a) What is the central angle of each slice?
    (b) What is the area of one slice?
    (c) What is the perimeter of one slice, counting both straight edges?
    Ans:
      (a) 360° ÷ 8 = 45°.
      (b) Area = (45/360) × (22/7) × 14² = ⅛ × 616 = 77 cm².
      (c) Arc length = (45/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 14 = ⅛ × 88 = 11 cm. Perimeter = 11 + 14 + 14 = 39 cm.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A circular park of radius 14 m is surrounded by a path 7 m wide. (Take π = 22/7.)
    
    (a) What is the outer radius of the path?
    (b) What is the area of the path?
    (c) What is the cost of paving it at Rs 40 per m²?
    Ans:
      (a) Outer radius = 14 + 7 = 21 m.
      (b) Area of path = π(21² − 14²) = (22/7)(441 − 196) = (22/7) × 245 = 770 m².
      (c) Cost = 770 × 40 = Rs 30,800.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Two circles touch externally. The sum of their areas is 130π cm² and the distance between their centres is 14 cm. Find their radii.
    Ans:
      Let the radii be r₁ and r₂. Since they touch externally, r₁ + r₂ = 14.
      πr₁² + πr₂² = 130π ⇒ r₁² + r₂² = 130.
      (r₁ + r₂)² = r₁² + r₂² + 2r₁r₂ ⇒ 196 = 130 + 2r₁r₂ ⇒ r₁r₂ = 33.
      So r₁ and r₂ are roots of t² − 14t + 33 = 0, giving 11 cm and 3 cm.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] The minute hand of a clock is 14 cm long. Find (i) the angle it turns in 10 minutes, (ii) the distance moved by its tip in that time and (iii) the area swept by the hand. (Take π = 22/7.)
    Ans:
      (i) The minute hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, so in 10 minutes it turns 60°.
      (ii) The tip moves along an arc of a circle of radius 14 cm: arc = (60/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 14 = ⅙ × 88 = 44/3 cm ≈ 14.67 cm.
      (iii) The hand sweeps a sector of angle 60°: area = (60/360) × (22/7) × 14² = ⅙ × 616 = 308/3 cm² ≈ 102.67 cm².

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The area of a quadrant of a circle of radius r is: (a) πr²/2  (b) πr²/4  (c) 2πr  (d) πr²
    Ans: (b) πr²/4.

M2. The perimeter of a sector includes: (a) only the arc  (b) the arc and one radius  (c) the arc and two radii  (d) the chord and the arc
    Ans: (c) The arc and two radii.

M3. The area of a segment is found by: (a) sector − triangle  (b) sector + triangle  (c) circle − sector  (d) triangle − sector
    Ans: (a) Sector − triangle.

M4. The length of the arc of a sector of angle θ in a circle of radius r is: (a) (θ/360°) × πr²  (b) (θ/180°) × πr²  (c) (θ/360°) × 2πr  (d) (θ/360°) × 2r
    Ans: (c) (θ/360°) × 2πr. The arc is the fraction θ/360° of the whole circumference 2πr.

M5. The area of a sector of a circle of radius 7 cm with central angle 90° is (π = 22/7): (a) 77 cm²  (b) 154 cm²  (c) 19.25 cm²  (d) 38.5 cm²
    Ans: (d) 38.5 cm². Area = (90/360) × (22/7) × 7² = ¼ × 154 = 38.5 cm².

M6. The area of a circle whose circumference is 44 cm is: (a) 77 cm²  (b) 154 cm²  (c) 308 cm²  (d) 616 cm²
    Ans: (b) 154 cm². 2πr = 44 gives r = 7 cm, so the area is πr² = 154 cm².

M7. If the radius of a circle is doubled, its area becomes: (a) 2 times  (b) 4 times  (c) 8 times  (d) unchanged
    Ans: (b) 4 times. Area ∝ r², so doubling r multiplies the area by 2² = 4.

M8. The area of a semicircle of radius r is: (a) πr²  (b) πr²/2  (c) 2πr  (d) πr²/4
    Ans: (b) πr²/2. A semicircle is half of a full circle.
