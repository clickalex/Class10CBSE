A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (13)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] State Euclid’s division lemma.
    Ans: Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Without performing long division, state whether 13/3125 will have a terminating decimal expansion.
    Ans: 3125 = 5⁵, which is of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ with m = 0, n = 5. The fraction is already in lowest terms, so yes — it terminates.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.
    Ans: Assume 5 − √3 is rational and equals r. Then √3 = 5 − r, which is rational because it is the difference of two rationals. That contradicts the fact that √3 is irrational, so 5 − √3 must be irrational.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Prove that √3 is irrational.
    Ans:
      Assume √3 is rational. Then √3 = p/q where p, q are coprime integers and q ≠ 0.
      Squaring: 3 = p²/q², so p² = 3q². Hence 3 divides p², and since 3 is prime, 3 divides p.
      Write p = 3k. Then 9k² = 3q², so q² = 3k². Hence 3 divides q², so 3 divides q.
      So 3 divides both p and q, contradicting that they are coprime.
      Therefore √3 is irrational.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find their LCM.
    Ans: For any two positive integers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. So LCM = (96 × 404) ÷ 4 = 38784 ÷ 4 = 9696. It is worth checking the result: 9696 ÷ 96 = 101 and 9696 ÷ 404 = 24, and 101 and 24 share no factor, so 9696 really is the least common multiple.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Real Numbers” (HCF/LCM, irrationality proofs, terminating decimals). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers. It fails. Factorise instead. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Terminating test: look only at the denominator. If, after reducing, the denominator has any prime other than 2 or 5, it repeats. Ignore the numerator entirely.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers. It fails. Factorise instead.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Real Numbers”: “State Euclid’s division lemma.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: HCF/LCM, irrationality proofs, terminating decimals.
      Given positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Use Euclid’s division algorithm to find the HCF of 867 and 255.
    Ans:
      867 = 255 × 3 + 102
      255 = 102 × 2 + 51
      102 = 51 × 2 + 0
      The remainder is now 0, so the divisor at that step, 51, is the HCF. Always stop at a zero remainder and take the previous divisor.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] An army contingent of 616 members marches behind an army band of 32 members. Both groups must march in the same number of columns. What is the maximum number of columns?
    Ans:
      Maximum columns = HCF(616, 32).
      616 = 2³ × 7 × 11 and 32 = 2⁵.
      HCF = 2³ = 8, so the maximum is 8 columns.

Q12. [Long · 4 marks] Can 6ⁿ end with the digit 0 for any natural number n? Give a reason.
    Ans: No. If 6ⁿ ended in 0 it would be divisible by 10 = 2 × 5, so 5 would have to be a prime factor of 6ⁿ. But 6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ and, by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, its only prime factors are 2 and 3. Since 5 never appears, 6ⁿ cannot end in 0 for any n.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The HCF of 8 and 12 is: (a) 2  (b) 4  (c) 8  (d) 24
    Ans: (b) 4. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3; the lowest power of the common prime 2 is 2² = 4.

M2. Which has a terminating decimal expansion? (a) 7/24  (b) 5/13  (c) 9/40  (d) 11/21
    Ans: (c) 9/40. 40 = 2³ × 5, of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ. The others have denominators with other primes.

M3. If the HCF of two numbers is 6 and their LCM is 60, one number being 12, the other is: (a) 20  (b) 30  (c) 40  (d) 60
    Ans: (b) 30. Other number = (6 × 60)/12 = 30.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Real Numbers” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is HCF/LCM, irrationality proofs, terminating decimals.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Real Numbers” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The decimal expansion of 23/(2³ × 5²) terminates after: (a) 2 places  (b) 3 places  (c) 4 places  (d) 5 places
    Ans: (b) 3 places. 23/200 = 115/1000 = 0.115. The number of decimal places is the larger exponent, max(3, 2) = 3.

M7. Which of these is irrational? (a) √(16/9)  (b) √12  (c) 0.444…  (d) 22/7
    Ans: (b) √12. √12 = 2√3 and √3 is irrational. √(16/9) = 4/3, 0.444… = 4/9 and 22/7 are all rational.

M8. The HCF of the smallest prime number and the smallest composite number is: (a) 1  (b) 2  (c) 4  (d) 6
    Ans: (b) 2. Smallest prime = 2, smallest composite = 4, and HCF(2, 4) = 2.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.
    Ans:
      x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2.
      Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓
      Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of 6x² − 3x − 7, find 1/α + 1/β.
    Ans:
      α + β = 3/6 = 1/2 and αβ = −7/6.
      1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (1/2)/(−7/6) = −3/7.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum 1/4 and product −1.
    Ans: A quadratic with zeroes whose sum is S and product is P is k[x² − Sx + P]. Here S = 1/4 and P = −1, so p(x) = k[x² − (1/4)x − 1]. Taking k = 4 to clear the fraction gives 4x² − x − 4. Any non-zero value of k is accepted, since multiplying a polynomial by a constant does not change its zeroes.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] If one zero of (k² − 1)x² − 3x + 2 is the reciprocal of the other, find k.
    Ans:
      Product of zeroes = c/a = 2/(k² − 1).
      Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1, so 2/(k² − 1) = 1.
      k² − 1 = 2, k² = 3, so k = ±√3.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Polynomials” (Zeroes of a quadratic and their relation to coefficients). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing the sum as +b/a. It is −b/a. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Sign trap: the sum is minus b/a. Almost every wrong answer here has the wrong sign.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Writing the sum as +b/a. It is −b/a.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Polynomials”: “Find the zeroes of x² − 2x − 8 and verify the relationship with the coefficients.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Zeroes of a quadratic and their relation to coefficients.
      x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2), so the zeroes are 4 and −2. Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 = −(−2)/1 = −b/a ✓ Product = 4 × (−2) = −8 = c/a ✓

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] If α and β are the zeroes of x² − 6x + k and 3α + 2β = 20, find k.
    Ans:
      α + β = 6 (sum of zeroes) and 3α + 2β = 20.
      Subtract twice the first equation from the second: α = 20 − 12 = 8, so β = −2.
      Product αβ = 8(−2) = −16, and product = c/a = k, so k = −16.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Divide 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5 by x² + 2x + 1 and verify the division algorithm.
    Ans:
      Quotient = 3x − 5, remainder = 9x + 10.
      Check: (x² + 2x + 1)(3x − 5) + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + 6x² + 3x − 5x² − 10x − 5 + 9x + 10
      = 3x³ + x² + 2x + 5, which is the dividend — so dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

M6. A polynomial of degree 3 is called: (a) linear  (b) quadratic  (c) cubic  (d) biquadratic
    Ans: (c) cubic. Degree 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic and 4 is biquadratic.

M7. If one zero of x² − 4x + 1 is 2 + √3, the other zero is: (a) 2 − √3  (b) −2 − √3  (c) √3 − 2  (d) −√3
    Ans: (a) 2 − √3. Sum of zeroes = 4, so the other zero = 4 − (2 + √3) = 2 − √3.

M8. A quadratic polynomial can have at most: (a) 0 zeroes  (b) 1 zero  (c) 2 zeroes  (d) 3 zeroes
    Ans: (c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.
    Ans:
      a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2.
      All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 10 and 2x − 3y = 1.
    Ans:
      Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 4: 9x + 12y = 30 and 8x − 12y = 4.
      Adding: 17x = 34, so x = 2.
      Substituting in the first: 6 + 4y = 10, so y = 1.

Q3. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables” (Consistency conditions, three solving methods, word problems). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Declaring a pair inconsistent when only two of the three ratios are equal. You must check c₁/c₂ as well. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Ratio order matters. Compare a with a, b with b, c with c — never a with b.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Declaring a pair inconsistent when only two of the three ratios are equal. You must check c₁/c₂ as well.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables”: “Without solving, tell whether 2x − 3y = 7 and 4x − 6y = 14 have a unique solution.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Consistency conditions, three solving methods, word problems.
      a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2, b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2, c₁/c₂ = (−7)/(−14) = 1/2. All three ratios are equal, so the lines are coincident — infinitely many solutions, not a unique one.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] For what value of k does the pair 3x + y = 1 and (2k − 1)x + (k − 1)y = 2k + 1 have no solution?
    Ans:
      For no solution the lines must be parallel: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
      3/(2k − 1) = 1/(k − 1) ⇒ 3k − 3 = 2k − 1 ⇒ k = 2.
      Check: at k = 2 the equations become 3x + y = 1 and 3x + y = 5, which are parallel and distinct — so no solution.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Solve by cross-multiplication: 2x + 3y = 46 and 3x + 5y = 74.
    Ans:
      Write as 2x + 3y − 46 = 0 and 3x + 5y − 74 = 0.
      x/(3(−74) − 5(−46)) = y/(−46(3) − (−74)(2)) = 1/(2(5) − 3(3))
      x/(−222 + 230) = y/(−138 + 148) = 1/1, so x = 8 and y = 10.
      Check: 2(8) + 3(10) = 46 and 3(8) + 5(10) = 74.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Solve by substitution: x + 3y = 16 and 2x − y = 4.
    Ans:
      From the second equation, y = 2x − 4.
      Substituting in the first: x + 3(2x − 4) = 16, so x + 6x − 12 = 16.
      Hence 7x = 28, giving x = 4 and y = 2(4) − 4 = 4.
      Check: 4 + 12 = 16 and 8 − 4 = 4. The solution is x = 4, y = 4.

Q8. [Long · 4 marks] Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are they?
    Ans:
      Let Nuri be x and Sonu be y now.
      x − 5 = 3(y − 5) ⇒ x − 3y = −10.
      x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10.
      Subtracting: −y = −20, so y = 20 and x = 50.
      Nuri is 50 and Sonu is 20.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] A fraction becomes 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from its numerator, and becomes 1/4 when 8 is added to its denominator. Find the fraction.
    Ans:
      Let the fraction be x/y.
      (x − 1)/y = 1/3 ⇒ y = 3x − 3. And x/(y + 8) = 1/4 ⇒ 4x = y + 8.
      Substituting: 4x = 3x − 3 + 8 ⇒ x = 5, so y = 12.
      The fraction is 5/12. Check: (5 − 1)/12 = 1/3 and 5/20 = 1/4.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours. In 13 hours it can go 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream. Find the speed of the stream and of the boat in still water.
    Ans:
      Let the still-water speed be x km/h and the stream’s speed be y km/h.
      Put u = 1/(x − y) and v = 1/(x + y).
      30u + 44v = 10 and 40u + 55v = 13.
      Solving gives u = 1/8 and v = 1/11, so x − y = 8 and x + y = 11.
      Therefore x = 9.5 km/h and y = 1.5 km/h.

    Ans: (a) x = 9, y = 5. Adding the equations gives 2x = 18, so x = 9 and then y = 5.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the nature of the roots of 2x² − 6x + 3 = 0.
    Ans:
      D = (−6)² − 4(2)(3) = 36 − 24 = 12.
      D > 0 but 12 is not a perfect square, so the roots are real, distinct and irrational.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] For what values of k does x² + kx + 16 = 0 have equal roots?
    Ans: Equal roots means the discriminant is zero: k² − 4(1)(16) = 0, so k² = 64 and k = ±8. For k = 8 the root is −4; for k = −8 the root is 4.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find the value of k for which 2x² + kx + 3 = 0 has two equal roots.

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] State the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
    Ans: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.
    Ans: By the Basic Proportionality Theorem, a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio: AD/DB = AE/EC. So 1.5/3 = 1/EC, giving EC = (3 × 1)/1.5 = 2 cm. The theorem only applies because DE ∥ BC is given — without that, the ratio cannot be used.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] State the converse of the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
    Ans: If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side. In ΔABC, if a line through D on AB and E on AC satisfies AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE ∥ BC.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 9:16. If one side of the smaller triangle is 4.5 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger.
    Ans:
      Area ratio = square of side ratio, so 9/16 = (4.5/x)².
      Taking square roots: 3/4 = 4.5/x, so x = 6 cm.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] Prove that in a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides.
    Ans:
      In right ΔABC, right-angled at B, draw BD ⊥ AC.
      ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA), so AD/AB = AB/AC, giving AB² = AD × AC.
      ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA), so DC/BC = BC/AC, giving BC² = DC × AC.
      Adding: AB² + BC² = (AD + DC) × AC = AC².

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the distance between (−2, 3) and (4, −5).
    Ans: Using the distance formula d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]: d = √[(4 − (−2))² + (−5 − 3)²] = √[36 + 64] = √100 = 10 units. Note the signs: subtracting a negative coordinate becomes an addition, and both differences are squared, so it does not matter which point is taken first.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Find the point that divides the segment joining (−1, 7) and (4, −3) in the ratio 2:3.
    Ans:

      y = (2(−3) + 3(7))/5 = (−6 + 21)/5 = 3.
      The point is (1, 3).

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find a relation between x and y if the point (x, y) is equidistant from (3, 6) and (−3, 4).
    Ans:
      Set the squared distances equal: (x−3)² + (y−6)² = (x+3)² + (y−4)².
      Expanding and cancelling x² and y²: −6x − 12y + 45 = 6x − 8y + 25.
      So 12x + 4y = 20, that is 3x + y = 5.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Find the area of the triangle with vertices (1, −1), (−4, 2k) and (−k, 5) if the area is 24/2 square units.
    Ans:

      = ½|2k − 5 − 24 + k + 2k²| = ½|2k² + 3k − 29| = 12.
      So 2k² + 3k − 29 = ±24, giving 2k² + 3k − 53 = 0 or 2k² + 3k − 5 = 0.
      The second factorises as (2k + 5)(k − 1) = 0, so k = 1 or k = −5/2. Always keep both the + and − cases because of the modulus.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the class mark of the class 25–35.
    Ans: The class mark is the midpoint of the class: (lower limit + upper limit) ÷ 2 = (25 + 35)/2 = 30. It stands in for every value in that interval when a mean is calculated from grouped data, which is why it is also called the mid-value.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Write the formula for the mode of grouped data and explain each symbol.
    Ans: Mode = l + [(f₁ − f₀)/(2f₁ − f₀ − f₂)] × h, where l is the lower limit of the modal class, f₁ its frequency, f₀ the frequency of the class before it, f₂ the frequency of the class after it, and h the class size. The modal class is the class with the highest frequency.

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a red king?

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting (i) a prime number, (ii) a number greater than 4.
    Ans:
      Primes on a die are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
      Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so P = 2/6 = 1/3.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] A bag contains 5 red and 3 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is not red.
    Ans: Total outcomes = 5 + 3 = 8. Not red means green, so P(not red) = 3/8. Equivalently, using the complement rule: P(not red) = 1 − P(red) = 1 − 5/8 = 3/8. The complement route is quicker when the event you are asked about has more outcomes than the one you are not.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue and 3 green balls. Find the probability of drawing a ball that is not green.
    Ans: Total balls = 12. Balls that are not green = 4 + 5 = 9. So P(not green) = 9/12 = 3/4.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is 9.
    Ans:
      Total outcomes = 36.
      Favourable pairs: (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3) — 4 outcomes.
      P = 4/36 = 1/9.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] A card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability that it is (i) a face card, (ii) neither a king nor a red card.
    Ans:
      (i) There are 12 face cards, so P = 12/52 = 3/13.
      (ii) Red cards are 26, of which 2 are kings. Neither a king nor red means a black card that is not a king: 26 − 2 = 24.
      P = 24/52 = 6/13.

Q8. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Probability”: “A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. What is the probability that it is a red king?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:

Q9. [Short · 3 marks] Two coins are tossed together. Find the probability of getting (i) two heads, (ii) at least one tail, (iii) at most one head.
    Ans:
      Sample space = {HH, HT, TH, TT}: 4 equally likely outcomes.
      (i) Two heads: only HH, so P = 1/4.
      (ii) At least one tail: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.
      (iii) At most one head: HT, TH, TT, so P = 3/4.

Q12. [Application · 4 marks] A box contains 90 discs numbered 1 to 90. One disc is drawn at random. Find the probability that it bears (i) a two-digit number, (ii) a number divisible by 5, (iii) a perfect square number.
    Ans:

      (ii) Multiples of 5 from 5 to 90: 18 numbers, so P = 18/90 = 1/5.

B. MCQs (8)

M1. The probability of an impossible event is: (a) 1  (b) 0  (c) 1/2  (d) undefined
    Ans: (b) 0.

M2. Two dice are thrown together. The total number of outcomes is: (a) 6  (b) 12  (c) 36  (d) 24
    Ans: (c) 36.

M3. The number of face cards in a deck of 52 cards is: (a) 8  (b) 12  (c) 16  (d) 4
    Ans: (b) 12. Jacks, queens and kings of four suits.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Probability” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Classical probability, complementary events, dice, cards and coins.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Probability” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. In one throw of a die, the probability of getting a number less than 7 is: (a) 0  (b) 1/6  (c) 5/6  (d) 1
    Ans: (d) 1. Every outcome 1–6 is less than 7, so it is a sure event.

M7. If P(E) = 0.37, then P(not E) = : (a) 0.37  (b) 0.63  (c) 1  (d) 0.53
    Ans: (b) 0.63. P(not E) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.37 = 0.63.

M8. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. The probability of a black queen is: (a) 1/52  (b) 1/26  (c) 1/13  (d) 2/13
    Ans: (b) 1/26. Two black queens out of 52 cards gives 2/52 = 1/26.
