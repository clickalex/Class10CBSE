A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Find the nature of the roots of 2x² − 6x + 3 = 0.
    Ans:
      D = (−6)² − 4(2)(3) = 36 − 24 = 12.
      D > 0 but 12 is not a perfect square, so the roots are real, distinct and irrational.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] For what values of k does x² + kx + 16 = 0 have equal roots?
    Ans: Equal roots means the discriminant is zero: k² − 4(1)(16) = 0, so k² = 64 and k = ±8. For k = 8 the root is −4; for k = −8 the root is 4.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Find the value of k for which 2x² + kx + 3 = 0 has two equal roots.
    Ans: Equal roots means the discriminant is zero: D = b² − 4ac = 0. Here k² − 4(2)(3) = 0, so k² = 24 and k = ±2√6. Both signs are needed — (+2√6)² and (−2√6)² are both 24, so each value gives a perfect square with a double root.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Solve 6x² − x − 2 = 0 by factorisation.
    Ans:
      Split −x using numbers with product 6 × (−2) = −12 and sum −1: these are −4 and 3.
      6x² − 4x + 3x − 2 = 2x(3x − 2) + 1(3x − 2) = (3x − 2)(2x + 1).
      So x = 2/3 or x = −1/2.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Quadratic Equations” (Factorisation, the quadratic formula, discriminant and nature of roots). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Dropping the sign of b when writing −b in the formula. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Splitting the middle term: you need two numbers with product a × c (not just c) and sum b. Forgetting the a is the most common error when a ≠ 1.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Dropping the sign of b when writing −b in the formula.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Quadratic Equations”: “Find the nature of the roots of 2x² − 6x + 3 = 0.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Factorisation, the quadratic formula, discriminant and nature of roots.
      D = (−6)² − 4(2)(3) = 36 − 24 = 12. D > 0 but 12 is not a perfect square, so the roots are real, distinct and irrational.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0 by completing the square.
    Ans:
      x² + 5x = −6.
      Add (5/2)² to both sides: x² + 5x + 25/4 = −6 + 25/4 = 1/4.
      (x + 5/2)² = 1/4, so x + 5/2 = ±1/2.
      Hence x = −2 or x = −3.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed were 5 km/h more, it would take 1 hour less. Find the speed of the train.
    Ans:
      Let the speed be v km/h. Time difference: 360/v − 360/(v + 5) = 1.
      ⇒ 360[5/(v(v + 5))] = 1 ⇒ v² + 5v − 1800 = 0.
      ⇒ (v + 45)(v − 40) = 0, so v = 40 (speed cannot be negative).
      The train travels at 40 km/h; check: 360/40 = 9 h and 360/45 = 8 h.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] The sum of the areas of two squares is 468 m². If the difference of their perimeters is 24 m, find the sides of the two squares.
    Ans:
      Let the sides be x and y with x > y. Then x² + y² = 468 and 4x − 4y = 24, so x − y = 6.
      Substituting y = x − 6: x² + (x − 6)² = 468 ⇒ 2x² − 12x + 36 = 468.
      2x² − 12x − 432 = 0, so x² − 6x − 216 = 0.
      (x − 18)(x + 12) = 0, so x = 18 (rejecting −12) and y = 12.
      The sides are 18 m and 12 m.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Quadratic Equations” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:
      Standard form. ax² + bx + c = 0, with a ≠ 0.
      Marks lens: –7 marks. A nature-of-roots question (2–3 marks) and a word problem (4–5 marks).
      Method / memory trick: Splitting the middle term: you need two numbers with product a × c (not just c) and sum b. Forgetting the a is the most common error when a ≠ 1.
      Do not: Dropping the sign of b when writing −b in the formula.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0 are: (a) 2, 3  (b) −2, −3  (c) 1, 6  (d) −1, −6
    Ans: (a) 2, 3. Product 6 and sum 5, both positive.

M2. If the discriminant of a quadratic is zero, the roots are: (a) real and distinct  (b) real and equal  (c) not real  (d) always integers
    Ans: (b) Real and equal.

M3. For x² + 4x + k = 0 to have real roots, k must satisfy: (a) k ≤ 4  (b) k ≥ 4  (c) k ≤ −4  (d) k = 4
    Ans: (a) k ≤ 4. D = 16 − 4k ≥ 0 gives k ≤ 4.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Quadratic Equations” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Factorisation, the quadratic formula, discriminant and nature of roots.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Quadratic Equations” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The sum and product of the roots of 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 are: (a) 7/2 and 3/2  (b) 7/2 and 3  (c) −7/2 and 3/2  (d) 7/2 and −3/2
    Ans: (a) 7/2 and 3/2. Sum = −b/a = 7/2 and product = c/a = 3/2.

M7. Which one is NOT a quadratic equation? (a) x² + 3x + 2 = 0  (b) (x + 1)² = 2(x − 3)  (c) x³ − x² + 1 = 0  (d) 2x² = 8
    Ans: (c) x³ − x² + 1 = 0. Its degree is 3, so it is a cubic; the others reduce to degree 2.

M8. If the roots of x² − 8x + k = 0 are equal, then k = : (a) 8  (b) 16  (c) −16  (d) 64
    Ans: (b) 16. D = 0 ⇒ 64 − 4k = 0 ⇒ k = 16.
