A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] How many tangents can be drawn from a point inside a circle?
    Ans: None. A line through an interior point must cut the circle at two points.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] From a point 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm, a tangent is drawn. Find its length.
    Ans:
      The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so the three lengths form a right triangle.
      Length = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. If ∠POQ = 110°, find ∠PTQ.
    Ans: The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, so ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°. In quadrilateral POQT: ∠PTQ = 360° − (90° + 90° + 110°) = 70°.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
    Ans:
      Let P be the external point and PA, PB the tangents with points of contact A and B.
      Join OA, OB and OP.
      In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: OA = OB (radii), OP is common, and ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius).
      By RHS congruence, ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP, so PA = PB.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A student says, “From every point P in the plane, exactly two equal tangents can be drawn to a given circle.” Test the claim for a point outside the circle, on the circle and inside the circle.
    Ans:
      P outside the circle: exactly two tangents can be drawn, and their lengths are equal.
      P on the circle: exactly one tangent can be drawn (at P itself); there is no pair of tangents.
      P inside the circle: no tangent exists, since every line through P cuts the circle in two points. So the claim holds only for an external point.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] The tangent at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at Q, where OQ = 12 cm. Find the length PQ.
    Ans:
      The tangent at P is perpendicular to the radius OP, so ∠OPQ = 90°.
      In the right triangle OPQ: PQ² = OQ² − OP² = 144 − 25 = 119.
      PQ = √119 cm ≈ 10.9 cm.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Prove that a chord of the larger of two concentric circles which touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.
    Ans:
      Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at P; O is the common centre.
      OP is a radius of the smaller circle and AB is a tangent to it, so OP ⊥ AB.
      In the larger circle, the perpendicular drawn from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
      Hence AP = PB: the chord is bisected at the point of contact.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Prove that the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line joining the points of contact at the centre.
    Ans:
      Let TP and TQ be tangents from T, touching the circle at P and Q.
      ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90° because a radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
      In quadrilateral POQT the angles sum to 360°, so ∠PTQ + ∠POQ = 360° − 180° = 180°.
      Therefore the two angles are supplementary.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC.
    Ans:
      Let the tangent segments from A, B, C, D be p, q, r, s respectively.
      Then AB = p + q, BC = q + r, CD = r + s and AD = p + s.
      AB + CD = p + q + r + s and AD + BC = p + s + q + r.
      The two sums are identical, so AB + CD = AD + BC.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    From an external point T, two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O. It is given that ∠PTQ = 70°.

    (a) State why ∠OPT = 90°.
    (b) Find ∠POQ.
    (c) Find ∠OPQ.
    Ans:
      (a) The tangent at a point is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
      (b) In quadrilateral OPTQ, ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°, so ∠POQ = 360° − 90° − 90° − 70° = 110°.
      (c) TP = TQ, so in ΔTPQ, ∠TPQ = (180° − 70°)/2 = 55°. Then ∠OPQ = ∠OPT − ∠TPQ = 90° − 55° = 35°.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A circle is inscribed in ΔABC and touches the sides BC, CA and AB at D, E and F respectively. AB = 12 cm, BC = 8 cm and CA = 10 cm.

    (a) Which tangent lengths are equal?
    (b) Find AF, BD and CE.
    (c) Verify your answers against the three given sides.
    Ans:
      (a) Tangents from one point are equal: AF = AE, BF = BD and CD = CE.
      (b) Let AF = x, BD = y, CE = z. Then x + y = 12, y + z = 8, z + x = 10, so x + y + z = 15. Hence AF = 7 cm, BD = 5 cm, CE = 3 cm.
      (c) AB = AF + FB = 7 + 5 = 12 ✓; BC = BD + DC = 5 + 3 = 8 ✓; CA = CE + EA = 3 + 7 = 10 ✓.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
    Ans:
      Given: a circle with centre O and a tangent XY touching it at P. To prove: OP ⊥ XY.
      Take any other point Q on XY and join OQ.
      The tangent meets the circle only at P, so Q lies outside the circle. Hence OQ is longer than the radius: OQ > OP.
      This is true for every point Q other than P on XY, so OP is the shortest distance from O to the line XY.
      The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular, so OP ⊥ XY. ∎

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The number of tangents from a point on the circle is: (a) 0  (b) 1  (c) 2  (d) infinite
    Ans: (b) 1.

M2. The angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact is: (a) 45°  (b) 60°  (c) 90°  (d) 180°
    Ans: (c) 90°.

M3. Two tangents from an external point to a circle are: (a) unequal  (b) always perpendicular  (c) parallel  (d) equal
    Ans: (d) Equal.

M4. A tangent to a circle meets the circle in: (a) exactly one point  (b) no point  (c) exactly two points  (d) infinitely many points
    Ans: (a) exactly one point. By definition, a tangent touches the circle at exactly one point, the point of contact.

M5. If the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point P to a circle of radius a and centre O is 60°, then OP = (a) a√3  (b) a√2  (c) a/2  (d) 2a
    Ans: (d) 2a. ∠APB = 60° gives ∠APO = 30° and, in right triangle OAP, OA = OP sin 30°, so OP = a/(1/2) = 2a.

M6. The number of tangents that can be drawn from a point outside a circle is: (a) 1  (b) 2  (c) 3  (d) infinite
    Ans: (b) 2. Exactly two, and they are equal in length.

M7. A line that cuts a circle in two distinct points is called a: (a) secant  (b) tangent  (c) chord  (d) diameter
    Ans: (a) secant. A tangent meets the circle at one point; a chord is the segment joining the two points.

M8. A tangent of length 24 cm is drawn from a point 25 cm from the centre. The radius is: (a) 24 cm  (b) 12 cm  (c) 7 cm  (d) 5 cm
    Ans: (c) 7 cm. r² + 24² = 25², so r = √49 = 7 cm.
