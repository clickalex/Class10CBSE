A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Anne Frank feel the need for a diary?
    Ans: Because although she had a loving family, friends and admirers, she had no true friend — nobody to whom she could confide her innermost thoughts. She wrote that paper has more patience than people, so she began the diary as a substitute for a real friend, and named it Kitty.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What argument did Anne make in her first essay, 'A Chatterbox'?
    Ans: She argued that talking is a student's trait and that she would try to keep it under control, but that she would never be able to cure herself completely of the habit, because her mother talks as much as she does. She added that there is little one can do about inherited traits — an argument which Mr Keesing took in the right spirit.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What does Anne write about her grandmother?
    Ans: Anne writes that nobody knows how often she thinks of her grandmother and still loves her. Her grandmother was born in 1888 and died in January 1942, and Anne says that she wrote the diary partly so that this love would not be lost. She adds that this whole diary was written in the thought of her grandmother.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Anne Frank decide to keep a diary?
    Ans:
      She had no real friend in whom she could confide; although she had many acquaintances, she could not discuss her innermost thoughts with them.
      A diary cannot talk back, and she felt that paper has more patience than people.
      She wanted to write out her deepest thoughts and feelings, and the diary became the friend she called Kitty.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What was Anne's argument in her third essay, and why did it win over Mr Keesing?
    Ans:
      The third essay was a poem — ‘Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox’ — about a mother duck and a father swan whose three ducklings are bitten to death by the father for quacking too much.
      The joke was aimed at Mr Keesing himself: too much punishment for too much talking. Luckily he took it the right way, laughed, read it to the class, and from then on let Anne talk — becoming, in her words, the nicest teacher.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] Describe Mr Keesing's punishment and how Anne used it to her advantage.
    Ans:
      Anne was caught talking in class, so the maths teacher Mr Keesing gave her extra homework: first an essay on ‘A Chatterbox', then ‘An Incorrigible Chatterbox', and then ‘Quack Quack Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox'.
      Anne wrote the first essay arguing that talking is a student's trait which she inherited from her mother and could never cure.
      For the second she argued that she could not stop talking in a maths class because it was impossible to keep quiet.
      For the third she wrote a poem about a swan with three ducklings that bit their father to death, and illustrated it; Mr Keesing was amused, read the poem to the class and stopped punishing her.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Describe Mr Keesing and how his attitude towards Anne changed.
    Ans:
      Mr Keesing was Anne's mathematics teacher, an old-fashioned teacher who was annoyed by her constant talking in class. He punished her by assigning an essay titled 'A Chatterbox'.
      Anne's response: she wrote three pages, admitting the habit but arguing that talking is a student's trait and that her mother talks as much as she does, so it is an inherited trait and there is little one can do about it. Mr Keesing took the joke in the right spirit.
      The second and third essays: when she talked again he assigned 'An Incorrigible Chatterbox', and the third time he set 'Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox' — this time in verse. With the help of her friend Sanne, Anne wrote a comic poem about a mother duck and a father swan whose three ducklings are bitten to death by the swan for quacking too much.
      The change: Mr Keesing read the poem to his own class and then to another class, adding his own comments, and laughed at it. From then on he allowed Anne to talk in class and never gave her extra homework again. Anne calls him the nicest teacher she ever met.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Anne named her diary: (a) Margot (b) Kitty (c) Sanne (d) Anne
    Ans: (b) Kitty — she treated the diary as the true friend she did not have.

M2. The third essay Mr Keesing assigned was: (a) A Chatterbox (b) An Incorrigible Chatterbox (c) Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox (d) A Talkative Girl
    Ans: (c) Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox, and this time it had to be written in verse.

M3. The line 'paper has more patience than people' means that: (a) paper lasts longer than people (b) a diary will listen without judging or tiring (c) people are impatient with writing (d) diaries are more valuable than friends
    Ans: (b) — Anne could write things to paper that she could never say aloud to a person.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “From the Diary of Anne Frank” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is A thirteen-year-old's first diary entries and her punishment essay.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “From the Diary of Anne Frank” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Anne Frank named her diary: (a) Kitty  (b) Margot  (c) Lies  (d) Sanne
    Ans: (a) Kitty. She addressed all her entries to this imaginary friend.

M7. Anne and her family went into hiding in: (a) Amsterdam  (b) Berlin  (c) Paris  (d) Frankfurt
    Ans: (a) Amsterdam. They hid in the secret annexe behind her father's office on the Prinsengracht.

M8. Anne's grandmother died in: (a) January 1942 (b) July 1942 (c) June 1941 (d) December 1942
    Ans: (a) January 1942. Anne writes that no one could understand her serious illness, and her birthday candles were lit in her memory.
