A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] How did Ausable get rid of Max?
    Ans: Ausable invented a balcony outside his window and told Max that he had complained twice to the hotel management about people entering his room through it. When the knock came at the door, he said it was the police, whom he had called. Max, panicking, said he would wait on the balcony and jumped backwards through the window — onto a balcony that did not exist — and fell six storeys to his death.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] How is Ausable different from the usual picture of a secret agent?
    Ans: He is very fat and sloppy, and lives in a small, musty room on the top floor of a gloomy French hotel, which does not fit the image of a dashing spy. He speaks French and German only passably, and has never fired a shot or been involved in dramatic action. Yet he is a highly effective agent, because he uses his wits rather than weapons.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What was the knock at the door?
    Ans: It was a waiter bringing the drink that Ausable had ordered before Max arrived. Ausable had already mentioned expecting a drink, so when the knock came he was able to pretend it was the police he had called. The reveal that it was only a waiter is the story's comic ending.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Fowler expects a secret agent to be dramatic, but Ausable looks ordinary. Explain how the story uses this mismatch to change Fowler's idea of a spy.
    Ans:
      Ausable is fat and unglamorous, unlike the romantic spy Fowler had imagined.
      His quick thinking under pressure proves more useful than a dramatic appearance or weapon.
      Fowler sees that intelligence and control of a situation, not looks, make Ausable an effective agent.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Max has a pistol, while Ausable has no weapon ready. Name the two invented details Ausable uses to make Max act against his own interests.
    Ans:
      Ausable invents a balcony outside the window and says that the knock at the door is the police.
      Max believes both claims, tries to escape by the nonexistent balcony, and falls when he jumps out of the window.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How does Ausable outwit Max?
    Ans:
      Ausable tells Max and Fowler that his room has a balcony, and that someone has already got into it; this makes Max anxious.
      When the waiter knocks, Ausable says it must be the police, whom he has asked to check on him.
      Max backs towards the window, steps out onto what he believes is the balcony, and falls to his death — there is no balcony at all.
      Ausable had invented the whole story, and his quick thinking removes a dangerous rival without a shot being fired.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Fowler feel disappointed when he first met Ausable? How had his opinion changed by the end?
    Ans: Fowler, a young writer, expected a romantic, mysterious secret agent and found instead a very fat, sloppy man in a cheap French hotel — nothing like the films. By the end his disappointment turns to admiration: Ausable defeats an armed rival with nothing but two invented stories, proving that real spycraft is presence of mind, not glamour.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Max waits in Ausable's room and demands the secret report. Ausable calmly claims that there is a balcony outside the window and that the police have come. The knock is actually made by a waiter carrying a drink.

    (a) What is Max trying to obtain?
    (b) Which part of Ausable's story about the room is invented?
    (c) Why does the waiter’s knock help Ausable?
    Ans:
      Max wants the important report about the missiles.
      There is no balcony outside the window; Ausable invents it to make Max think he has an escape route.
      Max believes Ausable's claim that the police are at the door and panics, so the ordinary knock gives the bluff urgency.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Fowler comes to meet an agent but finds a quiet hotel room and an armed rival. Ausable remains calm, invents a detail about the building, and gives Max a reason to hurry toward the window.

    (a) What quality does Ausable show?
    (b) Why is Max's weapon not enough to give him control?
    (c) What does the scene suggest about presence of mind?
    Ans:
      Ausable shows composure and quick improvisation under threat.
      Max has a pistol, but he accepts Ausable's false story and lets fear dictate his next move.
      A clear mind can turn an opponent's assumptions against him; a weapon alone does not guarantee control.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What is ironic about Ausable as a secret agent?
    Ans:
      Fowler, a writer, expects a secret agent to be slim, romantic and mysterious — a figure out of a thriller.
      Instead he meets a fat, sloppy man with a thick accent, in a shabby room on the sixth floor of a French hotel.
      This plain, unimpressive man is the one who outwits an armed spy by using only his wits and a fabricated story.
      The irony is that the real professional is dull and clever, while the romantic picture exists only in books — which is the point of the story.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Ausable invents the balcony and the police visit within seconds. What does this presence of mind tell us about real spycraft versus film spycraft?
    Ans:
      Film spycraft is weapons, chases and gadgets; Ausable has none of these — only his wits.
      In seconds he reads Max's fear (exposure) and builds a trap from it: a balcony that does not exist and policemen who are really a waiter.
      The story's point is that intelligence beats intimidation — Max holds the gun, but Ausable holds the situation. That is why appearances mislead.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] How does Ausable defeat Max without using a weapon, and what does Fowler learn from the encounter?
    Ans:
      Max enters with a pistol and expects Ausable to hand over the missile report, putting the agent in immediate danger.
      Ausable stays calm and invents a balcony outside the window, then claims the police are at the door. The actual visitor is a waiter with a drink.
      Max believes the fabricated escape route and jumps out of the window when he hears the knock; Ausable has outwitted him with words rather than force.
      Fowler's romantic expectations give way to respect for Ausable's intelligence. The story shows that presence of mind and knowledge of an opponent's assumptions can matter more than appearance or weapons.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Ausable is described as: (a) very fat and sloppy (b) tall and handsome (c) thin and sinister (d) old and blind
    Ans: (a) very fat and sloppy — deliberately unlike the usual picture of a secret agent.

M2. The knock at the door was: (a) the police (b) a waiter with a drink (c) another agent (d) the hotel manager
    Ans: (b) a waiter with a drink that Ausable had ordered.

M3. Max died because: (a) Ausable shot him (b) the police caught him (c) he jumped onto a balcony that did not exist (d) he fell in the corridor
    Ans: (c) he jumped backwards through the window onto a balcony that did not exist, and fell six storeys.

M4. What is Max holding when Fowler and Ausable meet him? (a) A camera (b) A suitcase of books (c) A hotel key (d) A pistol
    Ans: (d) A pistol. Max uses it to threaten Ausable and demand the report.

M5. What is Ausable's invented escape route for Max? (a) A balcony beyond the window (b) A staircase behind the desk (c) A tunnel under the room (d) A lift beside the bed
    Ans: (a) A balcony beyond the window. There is no balcony; the false detail is part of Ausable's bluff.

M6. Ausable told Max that the balcony he saw belonged to: (a) Ausable's own room  (b) the next apartment  (c) the hotel manager  (d) nobody
    Ans: (b) the next apartment. The lie made Max step out of the window and fall.

M7. Fowler, the writer, expected a secret agent to be: (a) fat and foolish  (b) elderly and rich  (c) romantic and mysterious  (d) an Indian
    Ans: (c) romantic and mysterious. Ausable, fat and unglamorous, is a deliberate contrast.

M8. Max entered Ausable's room to steal: (a) money (b) a passport (c) a gun (d) a report about new missiles
    Ans: (d) a report about new missiles. The secret papers were the reason for the midnight visit.
