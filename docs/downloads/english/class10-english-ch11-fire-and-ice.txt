A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] What do 'fire' and 'ice' stand for in the poem?
    Ans: Fire stands for desire or passion, and ice stands for hatred. The poet says that from what he has tasted of desire he agrees with those who say the world will end in fire, but he knows enough of hate to say that for destruction ice is also great and would suffice. Both emotions are destructive enough to end the world.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What is the central idea of the poem?
    Ans: The central idea is that the world may be destroyed by two opposite but equally powerful human emotions — desire, which is like fire, and hatred, which is like ice. The poet does not say which is worse; he says that both would suffice. Desire consumes quickly and passionately, while hatred works slowly and coldly, but the result is the same destruction.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Comment on the rhyme scheme of the poem.
    Ans: The rhyme scheme is aba abc bcb, an interlocking pattern rather than a simple alternating one. It links the two ideas of fire and ice across the stanzas, and it allows the poet to repeat the sound of 'fire' and 'desire' and of 'ice', 'suffice' and 'twice', so the two symbols keep echoing each other.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What do fire and ice symbolise in Frost's poem?
    Ans:
      Fire stands for desire — greed, passion, ambition and the appetite that consumes.
      Ice stands for hatred — coldness, cruelty and indifference towards others.
      Both are presented as powerful enough to destroy the world, which is the poet's way of saying that human feeling, not natural disaster, is the real danger.
      The poem is brief but packs a moral warning into everyday words.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] The poet says ‘from what I've tasted of desire I hold with those who favour fire’. What personal experience is he hinting at?
    Ans: He hints that he has himself felt desire — greed, longing, passion — and knows from the inside how consuming it is. That is why he leans towards fire as the world's end: he has ‘tasted’ it. Yet he is fair enough to add that hatred, the cold force he has also seen, would serve equally well.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Explain the last four lines of ‘Fire and Ice'.
    Ans:
      The poet says that if the world had to be destroyed twice, he knows enough of hate to say that ice is also great and would suffice.
      This means he has seen enough cruelty and coldness among people to believe it could destroy the world as surely as desire.
      By saying ‘I think I know enough of hate', he accepts hatred as an ordinary human experience, not an exotic evil.
      The ending is chilling precisely because it is spoken so calmly.

Q11. [Long · 4 marks] Why does Frost say ice ‘would suffice’ for destruction? Explain with one present-day example each of desire and hatred destroying something.
    Ans:
      Ice ‘would suffice’ because hatred needs no flame — cold indifference, contempt and cruelty can destroy slowly but completely.
      Desire destroys — for example, greed for quick profit driving deforestation that wipes out forests and wildlife.
      Hatred destroys — for example, communal hatred on social media turning neighbours into enemies. Both end worlds, one hot and one cold.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. In the poem, 'ice' symbolises: (a) death (b) sorrow (c) hatred (d) winter
    Ans: (c) hatred — fire symbolises desire.

M2. The rhyme scheme of 'Fire and Ice' is: (a) aba abc bcb (b) abab cdcd (c) aabb ccdd (d) abcb abcb
    Ans: (a) aba abc bcb, an unusual interlocking scheme.

M3. The poet says that for destruction ice is: (a) not sufficient (b) less dangerous than fire (c) also great and would suffice (d) impossible
    Ans: (c) also great and would suffice — hatred alone could destroy the world.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Fire and Ice” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) skip written practice  (c) memorise a random extra topic  (d) learn the key idea plus one example
    Ans: (d) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Robert Frost on desire and hatred, and how the world could end.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Fire and Ice” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. In the poem, ‘fire' symbolises: (a) hatred  (b) water  (c) snow  (d) desire
    Ans: (d) desire. ‘From what I've tasted of desire, I hold with those who favour fire.'

M7. ‘Ice' in the poem stands for: (a) hatred and coldness  (b) winter  (c) the sea  (d) innocence
    Ans: (a) hatred and coldness. It is described as ‘also great' and would suffice for destruction.

M8. ‘Fire and Ice’ is a poem of: (a) eight lines (b) nine lines (c) twelve lines (d) fourteen lines
    Ans: (b) nine lines. Frost compresses the end of the world into nine short lines.
