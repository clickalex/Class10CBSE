A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why was the young seagull afraid to fly? What compelled him to finally make the flight?
    Ans: He was afraid because he felt certain that his wings would never support him, and because his siblings had already flown while he was left behind on the ledge. What compelled him to fly was hunger: when his mother tore a piece of fish and stood on the ledge, the sight of the food maddened him, and he dived at it. In that dive he fell outward into space and, flapping his wings, found that he could fly.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the help the pilot received during the storm in 'The Black Aeroplane'.
    Ans: When his instruments failed and he ran low on fuel, he saw a black aeroplane with no lights on its wings. Its pilot waved to him and led him through the storm until the narrator came out of the clouds and could see the runway. When he landed and asked the woman in the control centre about the other pilot, she told him there had been no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on her radar.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] How did the seagull's family react when he flew for the first time?
    Ans: They screamed around him, praising him and offering him scraps of fish. They landed ahead of him on the water, beckoning him to join them, and when he finally sank tired onto the sea and floated, they praised him again — his first flight was complete.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] A student writes that the young seagull flew because his father threatened him. Correct this with evidence from the story.
    Ans:
      It was hunger, not a threat, that made him fly.
      His parents and brothers had coaxed and taunted him, but he stayed frozen on the ledge.
      When his mother flew by with a piece of fish and stopped just out of reach, he was so hungry that he dived at it, and in the dive his wings opened and he found himself flying.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A friend refuses to try swimming after one bad experience. Using the young seagull’s experience, give her three pieces of advice.
    Ans:
      Fear often comes from imagining that we cannot do something; the seagull was sure his wings would not hold him, yet they did.
      A small push or a good reason can help us take the first step; in the story it was hunger.
      Once we begin, the fear passes quickly, as it did for the seagull, so she should try again with someone supportive.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How did the young seagull finally overcome his fear of flying?
    Ans:
      His family taunted and cajoled him, but he was too afraid to leave the ledge.
      When his mother flew past him with a piece of fish in her beak and stopped just out of reach, the pang of hunger was stronger than his fear.
      He dived at the fish, lost his balance, and his wings spread and lifted him automatically.
      His fear lasted only a moment; once in the air he was flying, and his parents and brothers praised him.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Compare the mother's role in ‘His First Flight’ with the black aeroplane pilot's role. How is each a guide who appears at the right moment?
    Ans:
      The mother tempts the seagull with fish and then stops short, forcing him to cross the gap between fear and flight — she guides by testing.
      The unknown pilot appears when the narrator's instruments fail and his fuel runs low, leading him through the storm to safety — he guides by rescuing.
      Both appear at the exact moment when the frightened one must move forward, and both vanish once courage takes over.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    The seagull’s parents and brothers coaxed and scolded him, and his mother finally held a piece of fish out of his reach.

    (a) Why did the mother hold the food out of reach?
    (b) What effect did hunger have on the seagull?
    (c) Do you approve of the family’s method? Why?
    Ans:
      (a) She wanted to tempt him to leave the ledge; he could get the fish only by flying.
      (b) It was stronger than his fear, so he dived at the fish and began to fly.
      (c) An answer with a reason: for example, it worked because gentle persuasion had failed, though it was a harsh push for a frightened young bird.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Over the storm, the pilot’s instruments failed and his fuel ran low. Then a black aeroplane appeared in front of him.

    (a) How would you describe the pilot’s state of mind?
    (b) Which two details increase the tension?
    (c) What finally brought him to safety?
    Ans:
      (a) Anxious and frightened, but hopeful once he saw the other plane.
      (b) The failure of his instruments in the storm and the shortage of fuel.
      (c) He followed the black aeroplane, which led him through the clouds until he saw the runway.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] Who do you think was in the black aeroplane? Give reasons for your answer.
    Ans:
      The pilot saw no lights, no other plane and no runway below, and he had lost his radio, compass and fuel gauge in the storm.
      The black aeroplane appeared beside him, guided him through the clouds and then vanished before he landed.
      The Paris control room reported that the radar showed an unidentified plane flying beside him the whole time.
      These details suggest the helper was not an ordinary pilot — it reads like the pilot's own courage, or a guardian spirit, and the story leaves the question deliberately open.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] 'Both the stories are about overcoming fear.' Justify with reference to the two stories.
    Ans:
      In His First Flight, the young seagull is paralysed by fear while his brothers and sister fly. His parents scold him and his siblings taunt him, but fear keeps him on the ledge for twenty-four hours. Only extreme hunger, and the sight of his mother with a piece of fish, drive him to dive — and in that desperate moment he discovers that his wings do support him. His fear is overcome by necessity and by the example of his family.
      In The Black Aeroplane, the narrator is an experienced pilot, but the storm terrifies him: the compass and radio fail, he cannot see anything, and he has only a few minutes of fuel left. His fear is overcome by following the mysterious black aeroplane, whose pilot leads him out of the storm and vanishes. He never finds out who helped him, which suggests that the courage came from within.
      In both stories, fear is conquered only by taking the leap — the seagull by diving, the pilot by following into the dark — and in both the reward is a safe arrival.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Draw a character sketch of the young seagull and show how he changes from the beginning of the story to the end.
    Ans:
      At first he is timid and afraid: he stays on the ledge while his brothers and sisters fly.
      He is stubborn in his fear: he ignores the coaxing and scolding of his family.
      He is also very hungry, and this makes him forget his fear for a moment and dive for the fish.
      Once in the air he finds that his wings carry him; he becomes joyful and confident.
      The change shows that fear is often only in the mind, and that an experience can end it.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. 'His First Flight' is written by: (a) Frederick Forsyth (b) Liam O'Flaherty (c) G. L. Fuentes (d) Gavin Maxwell
    Ans: (b) Liam O'Flaherty.

M2. How long was the young seagull left alone on his ledge before he flew? (a) twelve hours (b) two days (c) twenty-four hours (d) three days
    Ans: (c) twenty-four hours, during which he was hungry and afraid while his siblings flew.

M3. In 'The Black Aeroplane', the woman in the control centre told the narrator that: (a) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night (b) the other pilot had landed elsewhere (c) the black plane was a military aircraft (d) the radar had failed
    Ans: (a) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on the radar.

M4. “The Black Aeroplane” was written by: (a) Liam O’Flaherty (b) Anton Chekhov (c) Frederick Forsyth (d) Gavin Maxwell
    Ans: (c) Frederick Forsyth. Frederick Forsyth wrote The Black Aeroplane; Liam O’Flaherty wrote His First Flight.

M5. The young seagull finally flew when he: (a) was pushed off the ledge by his father (b) saw a storm approaching (c) followed another bird over the sea (d) dived at the piece of fish his mother held
    Ans: (d) dived at the piece of fish his mother held. Hunger overcame his fear, and in diving for the fish he found that he could fly.

M6. In ‘His First Flight', the young seagull's mother tempted him with: (a) a piece of fish  (b) a worm  (c) a leaf  (d) her call alone
    Ans: (a) a piece of fish. Hunger overcame his fear and he dived for it.

M7. The narrator of ‘The Black Aeroplane' was flying his Dakota from: (a) London to Paris  (b) Paris to London  (c) New York to Paris  (d) Dakar to Paris
    Ans: (b) Paris to London. He wanted to reach England for breakfast with his family.

M8. The young seagull was afraid to fly because: (a) he hated his family (b) he was ill (c) the weather was bad (d) he felt his wings would not support him over the great depth below
    Ans: (d) he felt his wings would not support him over the great depth below. Fear of falling kept him on the ledge for twenty-four hours.
