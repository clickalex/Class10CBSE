A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] How did Richard Ebright's mother help him in his work?
    Ans: She was his only companion after his father's death, and she encouraged his learning in every way. She took him on trips, bought him telescopes, microscopes, cameras and other scientific equipment, and always found work for him to do. Most importantly she gave him the children's book The Travels of Monarch X, which opened the world of science to him and started him on his work with butterflies.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What did Ebright discover about the twelve gold spots on a monarch pupa?
    Ans: He found that the twelve tiny gold spots on a monarch pupa were not merely ornamental, as was thought, but produced a hormone necessary for the full development of the butterfly. He demonstrated this by removing the spots and showing the effect, and the discovery won him the county science fair and a place at the National Insect Collection.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What qualities go into the making of a scientist?
    Ans: According to the chapter, three things are needed: a first-rate mind, curiosity, and the will to win for the right reasons — not merely to win, but to do the best job one can. Richard Ebright had all three, and he also had the advantage of a mother who encouraged him and a teacher who opened his mind to ideas and taught him to compete in a healthy way.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What did Richard Ebright learn from his monarch butterfly research?
    Ans:
      He began by tagging monarch butterflies to track their migration to Mexico and beyond.
      He discovered what caused a viral disease that kills nearly all monarchs, and later, with a friend, he found that the gold spots on the pupa are not decorative.
      The spots produce a hormone needed for the full development of the butterfly.
      This work won him the Searle scholarship, the Schering Plough award and a place in the Intel Science Talent Search.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] How did Ebright's failure at the county science fair in seventh grade help him later?
    Ans: Young Ebright exhibited slides of frog tissues — neat work, but only display, not experiment — and won nothing. The failure taught him what real science needs: a question, an experiment and an answer. Thereafter he did genuine investigations, starting with the viral disease of monarch caterpillars, and began winning every fair he entered.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What qualities made Richard Ebright a scientist?
    Ans:
      He had first-rate curiosity and a mind that never stopped: he collected butterflies, rocks, fossils and coins from childhood.
      He was competitive in the best sense — when he lost a county science fair, he resolved to do a real experiment the next year.
      He was a hard worker and a careful observer, and he had the drive to write to a top scientist, which led to work at a laboratory.
      Most of all, he had a mother who encouraged him, teachers and mentors who took him seriously, and the imagination to ask why.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] ‘A scientist needs a first-rate mind, curiosity and the will to win for the right reasons.’ Show how Ebright's life illustrates each of these three.
    Ans:
      A first-rate mind — he designed real experiments, like proving the gold spots produce a hormone, and published in a scientific journal while still at school.
      Curiosity — from collecting butterflies and rocks as a child to chasing the question of cell life, he never stopped asking why.
      The will to win for the right reasons — his teacher Weiherer taught him to compete to do the best job he could, not merely to beat others.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The book that opened the world of science to Richard Ebright was: (a) The Travels of Monarch X (b) The Making of a Scientist (c) The Diary of a Young Girl (d) The Butterfly Effect
    Ans: (a) The Travels of Monarch X, a children's book about the migration of monarch butterflies.

M2. Ebright's teacher Richard A. Weiherer taught him to: (a) win at any cost (b) compete in order to do the best job he could (c) avoid competition (d) study only butterflies
    Ans: (b) compete in order to do the best job he could, not for the sake of winning.

M3. The twelve tiny gold spots on a monarch pupa: (a) are ornamental (b) produce a hormone needed for the butterfly's development (c) attract mates (d) protect the pupa from heat
    Ans: (b) they produce a hormone necessary for the full development of the butterfly.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “The Making of a Scientist” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Richard Ebright's projects, his curiosity and his mother's encouragement.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “The Making of a Scientist” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Ebright's first serious scientific work was on: (a) monarch butterflies and a viral disease  (b) ants and their nests  (c) frog anatomy  (d) the stars
    Ans: (a) monarch butterflies and a viral disease. He had collected butterflies since he was in the second grade.

M7. Ebright and his friend discovered that the gold spots on a monarch pupa: (a) produce a hormone needed for the butterfly's development  (b) are only decoration  (c) warn away birds  (d) store food
    Ans: (a) produce a hormone needed for the butterfly's development. This was the finding that made his name.

M8. Richard Ebright grew up in: (a) Reading, Pennsylvania (b) New York City (c) London (d) Chicago
    Ans: (a) Reading, Pennsylvania. There his mother encouraged his collecting and gave him The Travels of Monarch X.
