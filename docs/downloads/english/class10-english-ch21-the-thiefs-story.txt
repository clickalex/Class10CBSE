A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did the thief return to Anil?
    Ans: Because while walking in the rain he realised that Anil would not be grieved by the loss of money — Anil was careless about money — but he would be grieved by the loss of trust. The thief understood that he had destroyed something more valuable than the six hundred rupees, and that realisation, along with the thought of what he was learning from Anil, brought him back.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What did the thief learn from Anil, and why was it important to him?
    Ans: Anil taught him to write his name, and then to write whole sentences. The thief realised that once he could write like an educated man there would be no limit to what he could achieve — he could earn respectably instead of stealing small amounts. Education offered him a future that crime could not.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Did Anil know that the money had been stolen? How can you tell?
    Ans: Yes, he knew. The fifty-rupee note he gave the thief in the morning was still damp from the rain, and Anil said nothing about it. His silence, and his decision to pay the boy regularly from that day, show that he understood what had happened and chose to give the boy a second chance rather than to accuse him.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “The Thief's Story” (Ruskin Bond: a small thief, a kind writer, and a changed mind). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Saying Anil punished him. He says nothing and pays him regularly instead. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: The thief returns because of loss of trust, not loss of money. That sentence is the standard 3-mark answer.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Saying Anil punished him. He says nothing and pays him regularly instead.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “The Thief's Story”: “Why did the thief return to Anil?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Ruskin Bond: a small thief, a kind writer, and a changed mind.
      Because while walking in the rain he realised that Anil would not be grieved by the loss of money — Anil was careless about money — but he would be grieved by the loss of trust. Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Why does Hari Singh come back to Anil after stealing the money?
    Ans:
      He gets on the train at Lucknow but does not board it; he cannot bring himself to break away from Anil.
      He knows that stealing is easy but education would change his life, and Anil was the only person teaching him to read and write.
      He also fears that if he leaves, the wet notes will be all he has, and the chance to become ‘a big man' will be gone.
      So he returns, puts the notes back under the mattress, and accepts Anil's quiet forgiveness, which we never see refused.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why could the thief not board the train to Lucknow after stealing the money?
    Ans: He reached the station just as the Lucknow Express was moving out and could not board it — he had no ticket and no idea where to spend the night. Left alone on the platform in the rain, with the notes getting damp, he began to think about Anil's trust instead of the money. The missed train gave his conscience the time it needed.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Describe Anil's character and how he changes Hari Singh.
    Ans:
      Anil is a young, easy-going writer who earns irregularly, lives simply and is generous, patient and trusting without being foolish.
      He feeds Hari Singh and teaches him to cook, to write his name and to add numbers, never asking where the boy came from.
      He knows the money was stolen but says nothing about it; instead he gives the boy a fifty-rupee note and says he will pay him regularly.
      That trust shames Hari Singh into honesty: for the first time someone believes in him, and he chooses education and work over theft.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Anil's ‘easy’ attitude to money is called his most appealing quality. How does this quality both invite the theft and reform the thief?
    Ans:
      It invites the theft because Anil leaves money carelessly — under the mattress, in plain sight — making stealing easy for a boy who lives by it.
      It reforms the thief because this same carelessness shows complete trust: Anil would grieve the loss of trust, not of money.
      The thief realises that stealing from a man who trusts him costs more than it gains — Anil's ease with money teaches him that some things matter more than money.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “The Thief's Story” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:
      Summary. The narrator is a fairly successful thief, still only fifteen but already experienced. He meets Anil, a tall, lean, easy-going young writer at a wrestling match, and decides to rob him. He gives his name as Hari Singh and changes it every month to escape the police. Anil takes him in as a servant, but he pays him irregularly and mostly in food, so there is little to steal. The narrator learns to write his na.
      Marks lens: Short answers and a value-based question on why the thief came back.
      Method / memory trick: The thief returns because of loss of trust, not loss of money. That sentence is the standard 3-mark answer.
      Do not: Saying Anil punished him. He says nothing and pays him regularly instead.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The thief gave his name as: (a) Anil (b) Hari Singh (c) Ruskin (d) Arun
    Ans: (b) Hari Singh, and he changed his name every month to escape the police.

M2. Anil was a: (a) wrestler (b) struggling writer (c) moneylender (d) shopkeeper
    Ans: (b) a struggling writer — tall, lean and easy-going.

M3. The thief returned the money because: (a) he was caught (b) he feared the police (c) he realised he had lost Anil's trust (d) the notes were ruined
    Ans: (c) he realised he had lost Anil's trust, which mattered more than the money.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “The Thief's Story” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Ruskin Bond: a small thief, a kind writer, and a changed mind.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “The Thief's Story” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Hari Singh says that he changes his name: (a) every month to hide from the police and old employers  (b) every year  (c) never  (d) every week
    Ans: (a) every month. It is the habit of a thief who never stays long in one place or with one name.

M7. Anil earned his living by: (a) writing  (b) working in a shop  (c) teaching  (d) wrestling
    Ans: (a) writing. He wrote for magazines, which is why his income was irregular.

M8. The amount the thief stole from Anil was: (a) 600 rupees (b) 60 rupees (c) 6,000 rupees (d) 100 rupees
    Ans: (a) 600 rupees. Anil had earned it by selling a book to a publisher the previous day.
