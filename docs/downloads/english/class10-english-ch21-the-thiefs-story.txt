A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did the thief return to Anil?
    Ans: Because while walking in the rain he realised that Anil would not be grieved by the loss of money — Anil was careless about money — but he would be grieved by the loss of trust. The thief understood that he had destroyed something more valuable than the six hundred rupees, and that realisation, along with the thought of what he was learning from Anil, brought him back.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What did the thief learn from Anil, and why was it important to him?
    Ans: Anil taught him to write his name, and then to write whole sentences. The thief realised that once he could write like an educated man there would be no limit to what he could achieve — he could earn respectably instead of stealing small amounts. Education offered him a future that crime could not.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Did Anil know that the money had been stolen? How can you tell?
    Ans: Yes, he knew. The fifty-rupee note he gave the thief in the morning was still damp from the rain, and Anil said nothing about it. His silence, and his decision to pay the boy regularly from that day, show that he understood what had happened and chose to give the boy a second chance rather than to accuse him.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Hari can keep the stolen money and disappear, yet he returns to Anil before morning. Explain how the prospect of education influences that decision.
    Ans:
      Hari has begun to see writing as a route to a different life, beyond the quick money available through theft.
      Returning risks losing the notes, but stealing from Anil would also betray the trust that made learning possible.
      He chooses the chance of education and a new start over the immediate gain; the decision shows his conscience beginning to change.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Anil gives Hari food, shelter and lessons rather than treating him as a criminal. What does Hari's response show about the effect of trust?
    Ans:
      Anil's trust gives Hari a reason to imagine a future that does not depend on stealing.
      Hari still gives in to temptation, but the kindness makes him feel shame and return the money; trust helps awaken his conscience.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Why does Hari Singh come back to Anil after stealing the money?
    Ans:
      He gets on the train at Lucknow but does not board it; he cannot bring himself to break away from Anil.
      He knows that stealing is easy but education would change his life, and Anil was the only person teaching him to read and write.
      He also fears that if he leaves, the wet notes will be all he has, and the chance to become ‘a big man' will be gone.
      So he returns, puts the notes back under the mattress, and accepts Anil's quiet forgiveness, which we never see refused.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why could the thief not board the train to Lucknow after stealing the money?
    Ans: He reached the station just as the Lucknow Express was moving out and could not board it — he had no ticket and no idea where to spend the night. Left alone on the platform in the rain, with the notes getting damp, he began to think about Anil's trust instead of the money. The missed train gave his conscience the time it needed.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Hari takes Anil's money and reaches the railway station, intending to leave for Lucknow. In the rain he does not board the train. Before morning he returns, puts the damp notes back, and waits for Anil to wake.

    (a) What opportunity does Hari abandon at the station?
    (b) What do his actions after returning show?
    (c) Why is Anil's trust important to this change?
    Ans:
      He gives up the immediate escape to Lucknow with the stolen money.
      Returning the damp notes shows remorse and a choice to repair the betrayal rather than run away.
      Anil's earlier kindness and offer to teach him have made the relationship worth preserving; Hari now values that trust and the education it brings.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Anil does not confront Hari about the missing money. The next morning he gives him a note and says they will begin writing sentences. Hari notices that the note is still damp from the rain.

    (a) What does the damp note allow Hari to realise?
    (b) How does Anil respond to the theft?
    (c) What does the offer of writing lessons suggest about Anil's purpose?
    Ans:
      Hari understands that Anil has likely noticed the theft and knows the money has been returned wet.
      Anil does not shame or punish him; he quietly restores the money and continues to treat Hari with trust.
      He is offering Hari a constructive future through education, not merely a job as a servant.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Describe Anil's character and how he changes Hari Singh.
    Ans:
      Anil is a young, easy-going writer who earns irregularly, lives simply and is generous, patient and trusting without being foolish.
      He feeds Hari Singh and teaches him to cook, to write his name and to add numbers, never asking where the boy came from.
      He knows the money was stolen but says nothing about it; instead he gives the boy a fifty-rupee note and says he will pay him regularly.
      That trust shames Hari Singh into honesty: for the first time someone believes in him, and he chooses education and work over theft.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Anil's ‘easy’ attitude to money is called his most appealing quality. How does this quality both invite the theft and reform the thief?
    Ans:
      It invites the theft because Anil leaves money carelessly — under the mattress, in plain sight — making stealing easy for a boy who lives by it.
      It reforms the thief because this same carelessness shows complete trust: Anil would grieve the loss of trust, not of money.
      The thief realises that stealing from a man who trusts him costs more than it gains — Anil's ease with money teaches him that some things matter more than money.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] How does Anil's trust change Hari Singh, and why is the decision to return more important than the money itself?
    Ans:
      Hari begins as an experienced young thief who changes his name regularly and intends to rob Anil.
      Anil offers him food and shelter, pays him, and begins teaching him to read and write; the lessons reveal a lawful path beyond petty theft.
      Hari steals the notes, but cannot bring himself to board the train. He returns them even though he could have escaped with the money.
      The return marks a moral choice: he values Anil's trust and his chance to learn more than a short-lived gain. Anil's silence gives that change room to grow.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The thief gave his name as: (a) Anil (b) Ruskin (c) Arun (d) Hari Singh
    Ans: (d) Hari Singh, and he changed his name every month to escape the police.

M2. Anil was a: (a) wrestler (b) struggling writer (c) moneylender (d) shopkeeper
    Ans: (b) a struggling writer — tall, lean and easy-going.

M3. The thief returned the money because: (a) he realised he had lost Anil's trust (b) he was caught (c) he feared the police (d) the notes were ruined
    Ans: (a) he realised he had lost Anil's trust, which mattered more than the money.

M4. What new skill does Anil begin teaching Hari? (a) Repairing locks (b) Driving a train (c) Writing sentences (d) Keeping accounts for a shop
    Ans: (c) Writing sentences. Anil's lessons offer Hari a path toward education and a different future.

M5. What does Hari do with the stolen money before Anil wakes? (a) He returns it to its place (b) He gives it to the police (c) He hides it at the station (d) He spends it on food
    Ans: (a) He returns it to its place. He puts the damp notes back after deciding not to leave.

M6. Hari Singh says that he changes his name: (a) every year  (b) never  (c) every month to hide from the police and old employers  (d) every week
    Ans: (c) every month. It is the habit of a thief who never stays long in one place or with one name.

M7. Anil earned his living by: (a) working in a shop  (b) writing  (c) teaching  (d) wrestling
    Ans: (b) writing. He wrote for magazines, which is why his income was irregular.

M8. The amount the thief stole from Anil was: (a) 60 rupees (b) 6,000 rupees (c) 100 rupees (d) 600 rupees
    Ans: (d) 600 rupees. Anil had earned it by selling a book to a publisher the previous day.
