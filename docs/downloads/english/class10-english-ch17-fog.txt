A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] What does the poet compare the fog to? Does he actually say that the fog is a cat?
    Ans: The poet compares the fog to a cat. He says it comes on little cat feet, sits on silent haunches looking over the harbour and city, and then moves on — all the quiet, soft, unhurried movements of a cat. He never says directly that the fog is a cat, and he never uses the word 'like' or 'as', so the comparison is a metaphor rather than a simile.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What is the central idea of the poem?
    Ans: The central idea is that fog, like a cat, arrives silently and without warning, covers the city and the harbour, looks down on them, and then moves away. The comparison brings out the mysterious, gentle and unobtrusive nature of fog, and shows how a simple natural event can be described vividly in a few lines.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Identify the figure of speech used in 'The fog comes on little cat feet'.
    Ans: It is a metaphor, since the fog is directly compared to a cat without the use of 'like' or 'as'. There is also personification, because the fog is given the cat's action of walking on little feet and sitting on silent haunches.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How does Carl Sandburg describe the fog?
    Ans:
      The fog comes ‘on little cat feet': silently, slowly and almost without anyone noticing.
      It sits looking over harbour and city on silent haunches, like a cat perched and watching.
      Then it moves on; it does not announce itself, and it does not stay.
      The six-line poem turns an ordinary weather event into a living creature.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] The fog ‘sits looking over harbour and city’ and then ‘moves on’. What does this arrival and departure suggest about fog?
    Ans: It suggests that fog comes quietly, stays a while like a watchful visitor, and leaves on its own — no one can summon or dismiss it. The sitting shows its stillness and patience; the moving on shows its mystery and freedom. Fog, like a cat, arrives and departs exactly as it pleases.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What does the comparison with a cat achieve in the poem?
    Ans:
      A cat is quiet, self-possessed and indifferent to human plans, which is exactly how fog behaves in a city.
      The comparison explains why fog is noticed only when it has already arrived, and why it leaves without ceremony.
      The image also softens what is usually an inconvenience: the fog becomes playful rather than threatening.
      By using a domestic animal, Sandburg makes the natural phenomenon familiar, which is typical of his plain, imagist style.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Why is the fog compared to a cat rather than a dog? Give two qualities the fog shares with a cat.
    Ans:
      First, silence — a cat walks on soft feet and a dog barks; fog arrives noiselessly ‘on little cat feet’.
      Second, independence — a cat sits where it likes and moves on when it likes; fog settles over the harbour and city and leaves without explanation.
      A dog would suggest noise and obedience, the opposite of fog's quiet mystery.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. 'Fog' is written by: (a) Robert Frost (b) Carl Sandburg (c) Adrienne Rich (d) John Berryman
    Ans: (b) Carl Sandburg.

M2. The fog comes: (a) with thunder (b) over the mountains (c) after the rain (d) on little cat feet
    Ans: (d) on little cat feet — silently and softly.

M3. The comparison of fog to a cat is an example of: (a) metaphor (b) simile (c) hyperbole (d) onomatopoeia
    Ans: (a) metaphor — the word 'like' or 'as' is not used.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Fog” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) skip written practice  (c) memorise a random extra topic  (d) learn the key idea plus one example
    Ans: (d) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Carl Sandburg: six lines comparing fog to a cat.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Fog” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) copy the whole chapter  (c) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (c) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The fog in the poem is compared to a: (a) cat  (b) dog  (c) cloud  (d) ghost
    Ans: (a) cat. It arrives on little cat feet and sits on haunches.

M7. The fog sits looking over: (a) the sea only  (b) harbour and city  (c) the hills  (d) the garden
    Ans: (b) harbour and city. Then, as a cat does, it moves on.

M8. ‘Fog’ is a poem of: (a) eight lines (b) ten lines (c) six lines (d) four lines
    Ans: (c) six lines. Sandburg builds the whole cat-metaphor in just six short lines.
