A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] What things does Mijbil do which tell you that he is an intelligent, friendly and fun-loving animal who needs love?
    Ans: He invented games with marbles, ping-pong balls and rubber fruit, and could keep himself amused for hours. He learned to walk on a lead and to respond to his name. He escaped from his box on the aeroplane, but instead of running away he came back and sat on Maxwell's knee and nuzzled his face — showing affection rather than fear. He developed compulsive rituals of his own, like the wall-jumping routine he repeated every day on the way to school, which shows both intelligence and a need for company.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Maxwell think the air-hostess was 'the very queen of her kind'?
    Ans: Because when he told her about Mijbil and the trouble he expected on the flight, she was extremely friendly and helpful. She suggested that he keep the box on his knee, and her kindness made a difficult journey possible — so he called her the very queen of her kind.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What guesses did the Londoners make about what Mijbil was?
    Ans: A Londoner who had never seen an otter made wild guesses: that it was a baby seal, a squirrel, a walrus, a hippo, a beaver, a bear cub, a leopard — one even said it was a brontosaurus. None of them recognised the animal, because otters were unknown in London.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How did Mijbil behave during the flight to London?
    Ans:
      At first he was upset and kept trying to escape, rolling and twisting in his box.
      Once Maxwell let him out, he disappeared onto the aircraft and scrambled over luggage, seats and passengers, creating chaos for a quarter of an hour.
      Maxwell caught him by the tail and returned him to the box, and thereafter the otter slept.
      On the drive home Mijbil again escaped and was finally found on the floor of the car under the driver's seat.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] How did Maxwell get Mijbil, and why was the otter's species named after him?
    Ans:
      In 1956, at Basra in Iraq, two Arabs brought Maxwell an otter from the Tigris marshes; he named it Mijbil and decided to keep it.
      When zoologists examined it, they found it belonged to a previously unknown subspecies, which they named Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli — after Maxwell himself.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What games did Mijbil invent, and what do they show about him?
    Ans:
      He invented a game with a terrapin: he put the terrapin in his box and shut the lid, then pushed the box around the floor and used it as a sledge down slopes and into the water.
      He also played with a rubber ball, which he kept dipping into water and rolling, and with marbles rattling along the corrugated surface of a suitcase.
      The games show an animal of unusual intelligence and curiosity, who needs company and occupation and who learns by playing.
      They also show how closely Maxwell observed him, and why the otter stayed devoted to him.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Describe the journey from Basra to London and the trouble Mijbil caused on the aeroplane.
    Ans:
      Maxwell had to travel to London with Mijbil, and the airline insisted that the otter be packed in a box not more than eighteen inches square, to be carried on the floor near his feet.
      Thirty minutes before the flight, the box was lined with metal and Mijbil was put inside. When the plane took off, Mijbil tore the lining to shreds and escaped. He ran at high speed down the aircraft, and passengers cried out; a girl screamed 'A rat! A rat!'
      Maxwell dived across the legs of other passengers to catch him, but Mijbil disappeared. When he returned to his seat he found the air-hostess with the otter on her knee. She had caught him and held him.
      Mijbil then came back to Maxwell, sat on his knee and began to nuzzle his face and his neck, so the journey ended with the otter calm again, though the box was in tatters.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Mijbil was scientifically classified as: (a) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli (b) Lutra lutra (c) Aonyx cinereus (d) Enhydra lutris
    Ans: (a) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli — a previously unrecognised species, named Maxwell's otter after Gavin Maxwell.

M2. Where did the author collect Mijbil from? (a) Kerala (b) the Tigris marshes near Basra (c) the Nile delta (d) Assam
    Ans: (b) the Tigris marshes near Basra in Iraq, where two Arabs brought him the otter in a sack.

M3. On the aeroplane Mijbil: (a) slept throughout (b) escaped from his box and tore the lining to shreds (c) bit the air-hostess (d) refused to eat
    Ans: (b) — he escaped, ran down the aircraft and tore the box lining; later he returned to sit on Maxwell's knee.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Mijbil the Otter” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Gavin Maxwell, an otter named Mijbil, and a journey to London.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Mijbil the Otter” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Mijbil was an otter of the species found in: (a) the marshes of southern Iraq  (b) the Amazon  (c) the Himalayas  (d) Africa
    Ans: (a) the marshes of southern Iraq. Maxwell named him Mijbil and trained him to obey a lead.

M7. The author of ‘Mijbil the Otter' is: (a) Gavin Maxwell  (b) Gerald Durrell  (c) Ruskin Bond  (d) James Herriot
    Ans: (a) Gavin Maxwell. The extract is from his book Ring of Bright Water.

M8. Mijbil loved to play with: (a) marbles and ping-pong balls (b) sticks and stones (c) paper boats (d) balloons
    Ans: (a) marbles and ping-pong balls. He juggled them lying on his back and invented compulsive games with them.
