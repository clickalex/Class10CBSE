A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why was the bus journey such a fascination for Valli?
    Ans: Because she had never been to the town and had no companions of her own age, so the bus became her window on the world. Every day it passed her house twice, carrying new people each time, and she longed to travel on it. The sight of the gleaming white bus, with its silver overhead bars and the people inside, filled her with an unbelievable desire, and the thought of travelling to the town and back grew stronger and stronger until she saved enough money to do it.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What did Valli see on her way that made her laugh until tears filled her eyes?
    Ans: She saw a young cow whose tail was high in the air, running very fast right in the middle of the road in front of the bus. The driver sounded his horn again and again, but the more he honked the more frightened the cow became and the faster it ran. Valli found it so funny that she laughed and laughed until tears filled her eyes. On the return journey the same cow lay dead by the roadside, which filled her with horror and took away her joy.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Valli refuse the cold drink offered by the conductor?
    Ans: Because she had only sixty paise with her and wanted to keep enough for the return journey. Even though she was thirsty, she refused the drink and asked only to be taken back on the same bus. It shows her discipline and her determination to complete the plan she had made.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How did Valli plan her first bus journey?
    Ans:
      Valli was eight and longed to ride the bus that ran between her village and the town six miles away.
      She listened carefully to the passengers' talk and to her neighbour to learn the fare, the timings and what the town was like.
      She saved every coin she could, resisting the temptation of peppermints and fair-ground toys, and collected sixty paise for the round trip.
      She then waited for her mother's afternoon nap, slipped out of the house and boarded the bus as an ordinary passenger, calling herself ‘madam' to the conductor.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] The cow is alive on the way out and dead on the way back. What does this episode teach Valli, and why does she tell no one about it?
    Ans:
      The dead cow is Valli's first meeting with death — she learns that life can vanish suddenly, and her joyful mood turns thoughtful for the rest of the ride.
      She tells no one because the journey was her secret and the experience too private to share; like all growing up, this lesson is hers alone.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Describe what Valli saw on her ride and how her mood changed.
    Ans:
      From her window she watched the canal, the palm trees, the green fields, the distant hills and the blue sky, which filled her with joy.
      She saw a young cow running in front of the bus, frisking and refusing to move, and laughed aloud at it.
      On the return journey her mood changed: the same cow lay dead and mangled by the roadside, struck by a fast vehicle.
      The sight silenced her happiness; she understood that the world held ugliness and death as well as beauty, which is the knowledge she brings home.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] How did Valli plan her bus journey? What does this tell you about her?
    Ans:
      Planning: she listened carefully to the conversations of her neighbours and of people who regularly used the bus, and she asked a few discreet questions here and there. From this she learnt that the town was six miles from her village, that the fare was thirty paise one way, and that the trip took forty-five minutes.
      Saving: she resisted every temptation to buy peppermints, toys and balloons, and finally she even overcame her urge to ride the merry-go-round at the village annual fair, so that she could save sixty paise in all.
      Timing: she chose to travel on the one o'clock afternoon bus, when her mother took her daily nap, so that she could be back before her mother woke.
      What it shows: Valli was observant, determined and capable of self-control far beyond her years. She was also proud of being grown up — she resented being treated as a child, and insisted that the conductor call her madam.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Valli's age at the time of the story was: (a) six years (b) eight years and some months (c) ten years (d) twelve years
    Ans: (b) eight years and some months — a detail she is very conscious of.

M2. The fare from Valli's village to the town was: (a) twenty paise (b) thirty paise (c) fifty paise (d) one rupee
    Ans: (b) thirty paise one way, and the journey took forty-five minutes over six miles.

M3. The conductor called Valli: (a) child (b) madam (c) sister (d) little girl
    Ans: (b) madam — hence the title of the story.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Madam Rides the Bus” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Valliammai's eight-year-old journey, and what she learns on the way back.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Madam Rides the Bus” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Valli was: (a) eight years old  (b) ten  (c) twelve  (d) six
    Ans: (a) eight years old. For all her smallness she was a determined and careful planner.

M7. Valli saved what amount for her bus journey? (a) 30 paise  (b) 60 paise  (c) one rupee  (d) two rupees
    Ans: (b) 60 paise. Thirty paise each way, for the round trip from her village to the town and back.

M8. Valli travelled to the town on the: (a) morning bus (b) one o'clock bus (c) evening bus (d) night bus
    Ans: (b) one o'clock bus. She timed it so that she would be back by about a quarter to four, unseen.
