A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Think-Tank mistake the book for an earth sandwich?
    Ans: Because the Martians had never seen a book. Think-Tank examined it from the control room, saw that it was flat and had pages, and concluded that the earthlings ate it as a sandwich. He ordered the crew to eat it, and when they could not, he changed his mind and decided that the books were communication devices used for listening.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] How did the book save the Earth?
    Ans: Because the Martians misunderstood what they read. When Sergeant Oop read 'Mistress Mary, quite contrary', with its silver bells and cockle shells, Think-Tank concluded that the earthlings grew metals and explosives in their gardens. When he heard about the cow jumping over the moon, he believed that the earthlings had taught their animals a high culture of music and space techniques, and that they planned to attack Mars Central Control. Terrified, he ordered the crew to leave Earth at once and later fled to Alpha Centauri — so the invasion was called off, and the Earth was saved by a nursery rhyme.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Describe Think-Tank's character.
    Ans: He is the Commander-in-Chief of the Space Probe fleet of Mars, and he believes himself to be the most powerful and intelligent creature in the universe. In fact he is vain, boastful and stupid: he mistakes a book for a sandwich, cannot understand a nursery rhyme, and panics at the idea of a cow jumping over the moon. He is also a coward, since he flees to Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away, at the first sign of danger.

    Ans:

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How do the Martians misunderstand the nursery rhymes?
    Ans:
      Think-Tank asks to be shown books, and the crew bring him nursery rhymes from a library.
      From ‘Mistress Mary, quite contrary' he decides that humans grow money, and from ‘Mother Goose' he concludes that she is the supreme ruler of Earth.
      He takes the rhyme about the cow that jumped over the moon as proof that Earth's creatures can jump between planets.
      Finally he hears ‘Humpty Dumpty' and ‘Hey diddle diddle' and decides that Earth is planning to launch an attack on Mars.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] How does Noodle's character contrast with Think-Tank's? Why is this contrast important to the play?
    Ans:
      Think-Tank is vain, boastful and stupid — he mistakes a book for a sandwich and nursery rhymes for threats. Noodle, his apprentice, is clever and modest — he quietly suggests the right ideas (that the book is for reading) and lets his master take the credit.
      The contrast drives both the humour and the ending: folly commands, wisdom obeys, until folly flees to Alpha Centauri and Noodle's line — Mars and Earth as friends — wins.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What is the humour in the way the Martians abandon their invasion?
    Ans:
      Their invasion is decided by a commander who is certain he is the greatest intellect in the universe but cannot read a simple rhyme.
      The more evidence he gets, the more absurd his conclusions become: library shelves become ‘sandwiches' and books are ‘communication sandwiches'.
      He panics, orders the fleet to retreat and abandons the invasion of a planet whose only weapons were a cow, a dish and a spoon.
      The joke is that the greatest danger to Earth was never an attack from Mars, but a Martian commander too proud to ask what the words mean.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] ‘Silver bells and cockle shells’ become metals and explosives in Martian eyes. Explain two such misreadings and the panic each causes.
    Ans:
      First, ‘mistress Mary, quite contrary’ with her silver bells and cockle shells is read as an earthling growing metals and explosives — proof that earthlings farm weapons.
      Second, ‘Humpty Dumpty had a great fall’ is read as Earth's plan for Think-Tank himself: ‘he will have a great fall’ sends the commander into panic.
      Each rhyme the Martians decode raises the alarm, until Think-Tank orders the invasion abandoned — a book of rhymes defeats an army.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. 'The Book That Saved the Earth' is written by: (a) Claire Boiko (b) Guy de Maupassant (c) Victor Canning (d) H. G. Wells
    Ans: (a) Claire Boiko, and it is written as a play.

M2. Think-Tank first mistakes the book for: (a) a mirror (b) an earth sandwich (c) a weapon (d) a map
    Ans: (b) an earth sandwich, and he orders the crew to eat it.

M3. The book that frightened Think-Tank was: (a) the Bible (b) Mother Goose (c) an atlas (d) a Martian history
    Ans: (b) Mother Goose, a book of nursery rhymes.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “The Book That Saved the Earth” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Claire Boiko: Martians misread a nursery rhyme and abandon an invasion.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “The Book That Saved the Earth” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The commander of the Martian invasion force was: (a) Think-Tank  (b) Noodle  (c) Omega  (d) Iota
    Ans: (a) Think-Tank. His assistant Noodle is the one who actually understands the books.

M7. The Martians decided that Earth was dangerous after reading: (a) nursery rhymes  (b) a newspaper  (c) a scientific journal  (d) a history book
    Ans: (a) nursery rhymes. ‘Humpty Dumpty' and ‘Hey diddle diddle' convinced them that Earth was attacking Mars.

M8. The play is set in: (a) the twenty-fifth century, the Era of the Book (b) the twentieth century (c) the twenty-first century (d) ancient times
    Ans: (a) the twenty-fifth century, the Era of the Book. A historian of that age stages the story as a history play.
