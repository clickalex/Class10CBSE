A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Give the format of a formal letter.
    Ans: Sender's address at the top left, without the name; the date in full; the receiver's designation and address; a one-line subject; the salutation Sir or Madam; the body in three short paragraphs; the complimentary close Yours faithfully or Yours sincerely; and the signature, name and designation at the bottom left.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What should a letter of complaint contain?
    Ans: It should give the invoice or bill number and the date of purchase, describe precisely what is wrong with the goods or the service, state the remedy required — replacement, repair or refund — and give a reasonable deadline. The tone must be firm but polite, and the letter must end with a request for an early reply.

Q3. [Application · 3 marks] A formal letter has the sender's name above the address and ends “Yours lovingly”. Identify both problems and state what should replace each.
    Ans:
      The sender's address belongs at the top; the sender's name is written with the signature at the end, not in the address block.
      “Yours lovingly” is informal. A formal letter should close with “Yours faithfully” or “Yours sincerely”, as appropriate.
      The correction gives the letter a conventional layout and maintains a respectful formal tone.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] A student is writing to the editor about a blocked drain. Explain what the first, middle and final paragraphs should each do.
    Ans:
      The opening should state the issue and the purpose of writing.
      The middle should give specific evidence, such as the location, duration and effects on residents, without unsupported accusations.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] What is the format of a letter to the editor?
    Ans:
      Sender's address on the left, then the date, then ‘The Editor' with the name of the newspaper and its city.
      Then the subject line in brief, then the salutation ‘Sir' or ‘Madam'.
      The body: introduce the issue in one sentence, explain the problem and its effect, suggest two or three solutions and end with a hope that the authorities will act.
      End with ‘Thanking you' or ‘Yours truly', and then the sender's name and designation or address.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What are the three paragraphs of the body of a formal letter expected to do? Illustrate with a complaint about damaged goods.
    Ans:
      Paragraph 1 states the purpose — the order number, date and the fact that the goods arrived damaged.
      Paragraph 2 gives the details — which items are broken, how the packing failed, and what the invoice says.
      Paragraph 3 states what you want done — replacement, refund or repair — with a clear, polite deadline.

Q7. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A resident writes to the municipal commissioner about a broken footpath near a school. The draft includes a clear subject, describes children stepping into traffic, asks for a repair, and ends with “Send workers soon.” It has no date and begins, “Hey there”.

    (a) What two format or tone changes should be made?
    (b) Which detail in the body supports the request for repair?
    (c) Rewrite the final request in a formal, specific tone.
    Ans:
      Add the date after the sender's address, and replace “Hey there” with the formal salutation “Sir/Madam”.
      The detail that children are forced to step into traffic explains the safety risk and supports the request.
      For example: “I request that the damaged footpath be inspected and repaired promptly to ensure the safety of schoolchildren.”

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A school wants to order 40 laboratory stools. Its letter gives only the item name and ends with “Please send them.” The supplier needs enough information to fulfil the order correctly.

    (a) Name two specifications the school should add.
    (b) What delivery information should be included?
    (c) Why should the subject line be brief and specific?
    Ans:
      It should state the required model or material and any size or quality specification, as well as the quantity of 40.
      It should provide the delivery address and a requested delivery date or schedule; payment or price terms should also be stated where applicable.
      A concise subject such as “Order for 40 laboratory stools” immediately tells the recipient what the letter concerns.

Q9. [Application · 4 marks] Write a letter to the editor of a national daily on the nuisance of loudspeakers in your locality.
    Ans:
      Start with the address, date and ‘The Editor, The Times of India, New Delhi', and give the subject: ‘Nuisance of loudspeakers in residential areas'.
      Body: say that loudspeakers are played at high volume from early morning till late at night, disturbing students, the elderly and night-shift workers.
      Point out that this flouts the Noise Pollution Rules and the Supreme Court's directions, and that repeated complaints to the local police have had no effect.
      Suggest strict enforcement of the 10 pm limit, seizure of equipment on repetition, and a helpline for complaints; end with ‘Yours truly' and your name and address.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] A student writes the sender's name at the top of a formal letter and signs off ‘Yours lovingly’. Correct both errors and explain why each loses marks.
    Ans:
      First error — the sender's name at the top: only the address goes at the top (no name); the name comes at the bottom, under the signature. It breaks the format the examiner checks first.
      Second error — ‘Yours lovingly’: a formal letter opening with Sir/Madam must close with ‘Yours faithfully’ (or ‘Yours sincerely’ for a named officer). An informal close contradicts the letter's tone and loses the format mark.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Write a letter to the editor about the irregular water supply in your locality.
    Ans:
      Examination Hall (or the sender's address) / 12, Civil Lines / Jaipur
      15 March 2027
      The Editor / The Rajasthan Patrika / Jaipur
      Subject: Irregular water supply in Civil Lines
      Sir,
      Para 1: Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I wish to draw the attention of the concerned authorities to the irregular water supply in our locality, which has caused great hardship to the residents for the past three weeks.
      Para 2: Water is supplied for barely half an hour, and that too at odd hours. On many days there is no supply at all. The overhead tanks run dry, and the residents, including schoolchildren and the elderly, are forced to depend on private tankers at high rates. Two written complaints to the Jal Board have received no reply.
      Para 3: I hope this letter will prompt the authorities to restore a regular supply and to inform residents of the timings in advance.
      Yours faithfully, / Amit Sharma / Resident, Civil Lines

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Explain how a formal letter can be both concise and persuasive. Use the format and paragraph structure of a letter to the editor as an example.
    Ans:
      Begin with the sender's address and full date, then the receiver's designation and address, a specific subject, and a formal salutation.
      In the opening paragraph, state the civic issue and purpose. In the middle, give two or three concrete details that show its effect without naming or accusing individuals.
      The final paragraph should request a specific action politely and close formally with the writer's name and signature.
      Concision comes from selecting relevant evidence and keeping each paragraph to its job; persuasion comes from clear facts, a respectful tone and a practical request.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. In a formal letter, the sender's name is written: (a) at the bottom, under the signature (b) at the top with the address (c) in the subject line (d) after the salutation
    Ans: (a) at the bottom, under the signature — the address at the top does not include the name.

M2. The word limit for a formal letter in the board examination is: (a) 50–60 words (b) 100–120 words (c) 150–200 words (d) no limit
    Ans: (b) 100–120 words.

M3. 'Yours faithfully' is used when the letter begins with: (a) Dear Mr Sharma (b) Respected Principal (c) My dear friend (d) Sir or Madam
    Ans: (d) Sir or Madam — 'Yours sincerely' is used when the recipient is addressed by name.

M4. Which detail is most useful in the body of a formal complaint about damaged goods? (a) The invoice number and a precise description of the damage (b) A long story about the writer's childhood (c) A guess about the seller's intentions (d) A poem about shopping
    Ans: (a) The invoice number and a precise description of the damage. These details let the recipient identify the purchase and understand the problem.

M5. Which subject line is the clearest for a letter placing an order? (a) Hello and thank you (b) A very long account of our school (c) Some things we need (d) Order for 40 laboratory stools
    Ans: (d) Order for 40 laboratory stools. A short, specific subject tells the recipient exactly what the letter concerns.

M6. A letter to the editor begins with: (a) the subject line  (b) the sender's address, date, and then ‘The Editor'  (c) ‘Dear Sir' alone  (d) a question
    Ans: (b) the sender's address, date, and then ‘The Editor'. The format marks depend on this order.

M7. In a letter of complaint or order, the subject line should be: (a) long and detailed  (b) left out  (c) short and specific  (d) written as a question
    Ans: (c) short and specific. It tells the reader at once what the letter is about.

M8. A letter of enquiry should always ask for: (a) personal favours (b) a job (c) specific details such as price, terms and delivery (d) an autograph
    Ans: (c) specific details such as price, terms and delivery. Vague enquiries get vague replies — and fewer marks.
