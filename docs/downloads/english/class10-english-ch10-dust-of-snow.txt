A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] What change of mood does the poet describe and what caused it?
    Ans: The poet was in a depressed or sorrowful mood and had regretted the day. The change came when a crow shook down a dust of snow on him from a hemlock tree. This small, unexpected event lifted his spirits and saved some part of the day that he had rued.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why is it significant that the bird is a crow and the tree a hemlock?
    Ans: Because both are traditionally associated with sorrow and death — the crow is considered an ill omen, and the hemlock is a poisonous plant. The poet deliberately chooses them to show that joy can come from the most unlikely and unfavourable sources, and that our associations should not blind us to the good in things.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Explain the central idea of the poem.
    Ans: The central idea is that a small, natural event can transform a person's outlook. The poet is gloomy and regretting his day, but the dust of snow shaken down by a crow gives him a sudden change of mood and saves part of the day. Frost suggests that we should stay open to such small joys, because they have the power to alter our whole perspective.

    Ans:

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How does ‘Dust of Snow' bring about a change of mood?
    Ans:
      The poet is in a bitter, unhappy mood, suggested by the hemlock tree — a poisonous plant — under which he sits.
      A crow, another conventionally unpleasant bird, shakes down a shower of dust of snow onto him.
      Instead of annoying him, the small event is taken as a good omen: it gives his heart a change of mood.
      He says he has rued many a day that day; the rest of the day is saved, so the poem is about how a tiny natural incident can lift depression.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What does the phrase ‘saved some part of a day I had rued’ tell us about the poet's day before the incident?
    Ans: ‘Rued’ means regretted, so the poet had been having a gloomy, regret-filled day — part of it already wasted in a sad mood. The falling dust of snow changes his mood, and the part of the day that remains is rescued from regret. The smallness of the incident and the size of its effect is the poem's whole point.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Why does Frost choose a crow and a hemlock tree rather than a nightingale and a rose?
    Ans:
      Romantic poets used beautiful or musical images; Frost deliberately chooses the ugly and the poisonous — the crow and the hemlock.
      This makes the change of mood more surprising and honest: consolation comes from ordinary, even unpleasant, nature.
      It also suits Frost's view that nature is indifferent and that meaning is made by the mind that receives it.
      The poem thus becomes modern rather than romantic: nature does not flatter us, and yet it can still save a ruined day.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Give an example from your own life of a small incident that changed your mood the way the dust of snow changed the poet's. What does this tell us about the poem's message?
    Ans:
      Example — reaching school upset after an argument at home, then a friend sharing a joke in the corridor, after which the whole day felt lighter.
      Like the crow and the hemlock, the trigger was ordinary and even gloomy, yet it broke the chain of regret.
      The message is that healing does not need grand events; nature and small kindnesses can save a day we had rued.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. 'Dust of Snow' is written by: (a) W. W. E. Ross (b) Leslie Norris (c) Robert Frost (d) Carolyn Wells
    Ans: (c) Robert Frost.

M2. The bird in the poem is a: (a) sparrow (b) robin (c) cuckoo (d) crow
    Ans: (d) crow, perched on a hemlock tree.

M3. The word 'rued' in the poem means: (a) regretted (b) enjoyed (c) remembered (d) forgot
    Ans: (a) regretted — the poet had rued the day before the snow fell.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Dust of Snow” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Robert Frost: a crow, a hemlock tree and a change of mood.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Dust of Snow” should: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (b) be one sentence  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The dust of snow fell on the poet from a: (a) oak tree  (b) pine tree  (c) maple tree  (d) hemlock tree
    Ans: (d) hemlock tree. The tree is poisonous, which matches the poet's dark mood.

M7. The poem says the day was saved because the incident: (a) warmed him  (b) made him laugh  (c) gave his heart a change of mood  (d) brought him money
    Ans: (c) gave his heart a change of mood. The rest of the day was rued by the change.

M8. The rhyme scheme of ‘Dust of Snow’ is: (a) aabb ccdd (b) abab cdcd (c) abcb abcb (d) aba abc
    Ans: (b) abab cdcd. Two neat quatrains carry the eight lines of the poem.
