A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] How did the young woman manage to get the jewels from Horace?
    Ans: She appeared while he was working on the safe and spoke to him as if she were the lady of the house, saying she had arrived home just in time. She told him that the jewels were needed that evening for a party, that she had forgotten the number of the safe, and that if he did not open it for her she would have to call the police. Afraid of being sent to prison, Horace opened the safe and handed her the jewels, and she let him go. She was herself a burglar, who had been waiting for her chance.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why was Horace arrested?
    Ans: Because he had left his fingerprints all over the room. When the young woman told him her story and asked him to open the safe, he was so relieved and so eager to escape that he forgot to put on his gloves. Two days later the police arrested him, using the fingerprints as evidence.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Do you think the young lady was right in tricking Horace? Give reasons.
    Ans: No. She used another person's crime to cover her own, and she let an innocent-seeming man take the blame. Although Horace was himself a thief, she could have simply left the house instead of framing him. Her action shows that 'honour among thieves' does not exist — she was willing to send him to prison to protect herself and keep the jewels.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Horace Danby is usually careful and wears gloves, but he is arrested after the robbery at Shotover Grange. Explain how trusting the woman changes his precautions.
    Ans:
      Horace believes the woman is the house's owner and accepts her claim that she has forgotten the safe combination.
      He follows her request to open the safe and removes his gloves, leaving fingerprints behind.
      His usual care fails because he trusts a stranger and lets the apparent emergency override his plan.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] The woman in red seems confident and familiar with the house. What clues should have made Horace question her story, and what does he do instead?
    Ans:
      Horace has no independent proof that she owns Shotover Grange; her calm manner and claim of authority are not evidence.
      He accepts her story and opens the safe for her, allowing another thief to use his skills.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How is Horace Danby trapped by the young woman at Shotover Grange?
    Ans:
      She finds him unlocking the door, and calmly claims to be the lady of the house who has forgotten her jewels upstairs.
      Horace helps her open the door, and she watches him take the jewels from the safe and lets him go with them.
      Because she saw him handle the safe, her fingerprints are not needed: he has left a clean set of prints all over the house.
      The police find his fingerprints and arrest him, while the real thief, who had read the family's letters in the hall, escapes.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Horace Danby rob only once a year, and what did he do with the money?
    Ans: Horace was otherwise a good, respectable locksmith who loved rare and expensive books but could not afford them honestly. So he robbed one safe a year — carefully planned, always enough to last twelve months — and spent the money secretly through an agent on first editions for his library. Crime, for him, was a yearly subscription to his hobby.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    Horace has studied the house and taken precautions for his annual robbery. A woman appears, says she is the lady of the house, and asks him to open the safe because she has forgotten the combination. Horace removes his gloves while helping her.
    
    (a) Why does Horace agree to open the safe?
    (b) What practical mistake does he make?
    (c) How does the encounter lead to his arrest?
    Ans:
      He believes the woman is the owner and accepts her request as genuine.
      He removes his gloves, abandoning a precaution that would have protected him from leaving fingerprints.
      The police later identify his fingerprints at the safe; the woman has taken the jewels and left Horace to answer for the robbery.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    Horace regards himself as respectable and robs only rich households once a year. At Shotover Grange, a woman takes the jewels and leaves him behind. He is arrested, while the woman is not found.
    
    (a) Why does Horace think of himself as respectable?
    (b) What irony lies in the outcome?
    (c) What warning does the episode give about trust?
    Ans:
      He maintains an ordinary life as a successful locksmith and limits his thefts to one planned robbery each year, though he is still a criminal.
      He considers himself a careful thief but is deceived by another thief and becomes the only person arrested.
      A confident appearance is not proof of honesty; Horace should have questioned the stranger's story rather than trusting it.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] What is the irony in ‘A Question of Trust'?
    Ans:
      Horace Danby is a respectable locksmith and a successful crook who plans his robberies carefully for a year.
      The young woman who tricks him is not a lady but a thief who does no planning at all: she simply reads the letters in the hall, walks in and walks out with the jewels.
      Horace is caught by his own fingerprints while she leaves none, because she wore gloves and was never seen as a thief.
      The title's irony is double: Horace trusted the wrong person, and the police trust only the evidence, so the guiltiest person goes free.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] ‘Honour among thieves does not exist.’ How does the ending prove this? Do you feel any sympathy for Horace?
    Ans:
      The young woman in red is herself a thief; she uses Horace to open the safe, takes the jewels, and leaves him to be caught by his own fingerprints. One thief betrays another without a second thought.
      Some sympathy is natural — Horace is polite, careful and robbed by a cleverer criminal — but the story withholds full pity: he chose crime freely, and a thief complaining of being robbed is the joke the title promises.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] How does the woman in red turn Horace Danby's careful plan against him, and what does the ending say about his idea of honour?
    Ans:
      Horace plans the Shotover Grange robbery carefully, believing his experience as a locksmith and his precautions will keep him safe.
      The woman calmly presents herself as the owner and claims she needs the safe opened. Horace trusts her and removes his gloves while helping.
      She takes the jewels, while his fingerprints connect him to the safe and lead to his arrest. His care is defeated by misplaced trust.
      Horace's belief that he is an honourable thief does not protect him; the other criminal has no loyalty to him. The ending makes his moral code look self-serving and naïve.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Horace Danby robbed a safe: (a) every month (b) once a year (c) whenever he needed money (d) only from the rich and the poor alike
    Ans: (b) once a year, planning each robbery carefully so that the money lasted twelve months.

M2. The safe that Horace planned to rob was at: (a) Shotover Grange (b) Shotover House (c) a London bank (d) a jeweller's shop
    Ans: (a) Shotover Grange, which contained about fifteen thousand pounds' worth of jewels.

M3. Horace was arrested because: (a) the woman informed the police (b) he dropped his tools (c) he was seen by a servant (d) he left his fingerprints
    Ans: (d) he left his fingerprints all over the room — he had forgotten to wear gloves.

M4. What precaution does Horace abandon when he opens the safe for the woman? (a) He switches off the lights (b) He leaves his tools outside (c) He removes his gloves (d) He locks the front door
    Ans: (c) He removes his gloves. His fingerprints are left at the safe and help lead to his arrest.

M5. What is Horace Danby's trade? (a) He sells flowers (b) He trains horses (c) He makes locks (d) He repairs watches
    Ans: (c) He makes locks. His work makes him knowledgeable about safes and security.

M6. Horace Danby's hobby was: (a) painting  (b) collecting rare and expensive books  (c) gardening  (d) singing
    Ans: (b) collecting rare and expensive books. It was the motive for his yearly robbery.

M7. Horace was arrested for robbing: (a) Shotover Grange  (b) a bank  (c) a bookshop  (d) a museum
    Ans: (a) Shotover Grange. His fingerprints all over the house were the evidence.

M8. The jewels at Shotover Grange were said to be worth about: (a) fifteen hundred pounds (b) one hundred and fifty thousand pounds (c) five hundred pounds (d) fifteen thousand pounds
    Ans: (d) fifteen thousand pounds. A year's worth of rare books for Horace — and for the lady in red.
