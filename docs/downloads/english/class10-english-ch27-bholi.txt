A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] How did the teacher transform Bholi's life?
    Ans: The teacher was the first person to treat Bholi with kindness and respect. She spoke to her in a soft, soothing voice, told her not to be afraid, and promised that if she came to school every day she would learn more than anyone else in the village and would soon speak without a stammer. She encouraged Bholi to read, praised her progress, and gave her confidence. Education gave Bholi a voice, self-respect and the courage to refuse a humiliating marriage.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Bholi refuse to marry Bishamber Nath?
    Ans: Because at the wedding, when her veil slipped and he saw her pock-marked face, he demanded five thousand rupees as compensation and refused to marry her without it. He had already agreed to the match when he thought she was fair, so his demand showed that he was mean, greedy and contemptible. Bholi, who had been silent until then, spoke clearly and without a stammer and refused to marry such a coward.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What does the story say about the value of girls' education?
    Ans: It shows that education can transform a neglected, mocked and silent girl into a confident person with self-respect. Before school, Bholi was slow to speak, was laughed at and had no self-worth. After school, she could read, spoke without a stammer, and was able to stand up against a greedy bridegroom and his dowry demand. She finally decides to serve her parents and teach in the school where she learnt so much — so education gives her both independence and a purpose.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Why is Bholi called a simpleton, and how does she change?
    Ans:
      As a child she fell and damaged part of her brain, so she was slow, stammered and was mocked as ‘Bholi, the simpleton'.
      She was neglected, given old clothes and little food, and kept out of school for years.
      The village primary school and then the city school gave her a teacher's kindness, confidence and the ability to speak without stammering.
      By the end she is able to refuse an unequal marriage and to speak up for her father, her teacher and herself.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why was Bholi sent to school when her parents did not value girls' education?
    Ans: The tehsildar had ordered Ramlal, the numberdar, to set an example by sending his daughters to school, and Ramlal dared not disobey. Since Bholi — pock-marked, stammering, considered a simpleton — was the least valued child and unlikely to marry, her parents sacrificed her to the school while keeping her sisters at home. Neglect, ironically, gave her the chance that changed her life.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] How does education give Bholi a voice?
    Ans:
      Her teacher's encouragement makes her believe she can learn; she is given books, a clean dress and, above all, attention.
      Education gives her self-respect: she learns to read, write and do sums, and she stops stammering in front of others.
      When Bishamber Nath demands five thousand rupees as dowry, she understands that she is being bought and tears up the wedding garland.
      She finally declares that she will serve her parents and teach at the same school, showing that education has turned a frightened child into a person with a will of her own.

Q11. [Long · 4 marks] Bholi throws the wedding garland into the fire. Why is this gesture the climax of the story?
    Ans:
      The garland stands for the marriage her parents arranged at any price — even a 5,000-rupee dowry to a middle-aged widower.
      By throwing it into the fire, Bholi burns not just one wedding but the whole bargain: dowry, obedience and the idea that a girl is a burden.
      It is the climax because the stammering simpleton now speaks clearly and chooses for herself — education has given her a voice, and she uses it.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Bholi's real name was: (a) Sunita (b) Kamla (c) Sulekha (d) Rukmini
    Ans: (c) Sulekha — Bholi was the name she was given because she was considered simple.

M2. Bholi's face was pock-marked because of: (a) an attack of smallpox (b) a fall from the cot (c) a burn (d) a skin disease
    Ans: (a) an attack of smallpox, which left black pock marks all over her body.

M3. Bishamber Nath demanded five thousand rupees because: (a) he was poor (b) he saw Bholi's pock-marked face (c) her father refused (d) the priest demanded it
    Ans: (b) he saw Bholi's pock-marked face when her veil slipped at the wedding.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Bholi” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is K. A. Abbas: a neglected girl finds her voice through education.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Bholi” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) copy the whole chapter  (c) leave the example out  (d) start with the idea, then a reason or method
    Ans: (d) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Bholi's real name is: (a) Sulekha  (b) Bholi  (c) Mangla  (d) Radha
    Ans: (a) Sulekha. She was called Bholi, meaning the simpleton, because of her looks and her slowness.

M7. Bholi refuses to marry Bishamber Nath because he: (a) is poor  (b) is uneducated  (c) demands dowry from her father after the garlands are exchanged  (d) is a widower
    Ans: (c) demands dowry from her father after the garlands are exchanged. She throws the garland into the fire.

M8. Bishamber Nath, the bridegroom Ramlal finds for Bholi, was: (a) a young schoolteacher (b) a rich landlord (c) a doctor (d) a middle-aged grocer and widower from another village
    Ans: (d) a middle-aged grocer and widower from another village. He limped, had grown-up children — and demanded five thousand rupees on seeing Bholi's face.
