A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the structure of an analytical paragraph.
    Ans: The paragraph opens with a sentence introducing the data — what it shows, the period it covers and the units used. The middle sentences describe the main features, selecting the significant figures and comparing them. The final sentence gives a conclusion or a trend inferred from the data. The whole paragraph is within the word limit and contains no opinion.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What should an analytical paragraph avoid?
    Ans: It should avoid giving personal opinions or recommendations unless the question asks for them, inventing data that is not in the chart, listing every figure instead of selecting the significant ones, and exceeding the word limit. It should describe and compare the data, not comment on it.

Q3. [Application · 3 marks] A chart shows that a club's membership rose from 20 to 50 over five years. A student writes, “The club is the best in the city and should receive more funding.” Explain why this is not a suitable analytical conclusion.
    Ans:
      The figures show a rise in membership, but they do not compare the club with others or give evidence about quality.
      The recommendation for extra funding is an opinion not supported by the chart.
      A suitable conclusion would state only the supported trend, such as “Membership increased over the five-year period.”

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] A table gives sales of three products as 120, 90 and 60 units. Explain how to compare the figures accurately without merely listing all three.
    Ans:
      Identify the highest and lowest figures and compare them directly: sales of the first product are twice those of the third.
      Mention the middle figure only if it helps explain the pattern, and use the same unit for each value.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] What is the structure of an analytical paragraph?
    Ans:
      Begin by naming the data: what the chart or table shows, over what period and for whom.
      Then give the main trend or the largest and smallest categories, using the figures in the data.
      Next explain the reasons or the pattern behind the figures, comparing categories where useful.
      End with a conclusion or a suggestion, and keep the whole paragraph to about 100 to 120 words.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Which phrases of comparison and trend should an analytical paragraph use? Give four examples with the situation for each.
    Ans:
      ‘Rose steadily’ — for a gradual increase over time, as in literacy rates climbing year by year.
      ‘Fell sharply’ — for a sudden drop, as in accidents after speed cameras were installed.
      ‘Peaked in’ — for the highest point, as in sales peaking in December.
      ‘Twice as much as’ — for direct comparison, as in bus users being twice as many as car users.

Q7. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A survey records the percentage of students who travel to school by four methods:
    
    Bus — 45%    Cycle — 25%    Walk — 20%    Private car — 10%
    
    (a) Which method is most common?
    (b) How many percentage points separate bus and walking?
    (c) What is one conclusion the figures support without adding an opinion?
    Ans:
      Bus is the most common method, at 45%.
      The difference is 45 − 20 = 25 percentage points.
      The data show that more students travel by bus than by any other listed method; they do not by themselves explain why.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A small table shows a town's park visitors: Monday 120, Tuesday 150, Wednesday 150, Thursday 90. The writer wants to describe the pattern in an analytical paragraph.
    
    (a) On which days is the figure highest?
    (b) What happens between Wednesday and Thursday?
    (c) Which sentence accurately describes the trend?
    Ans:
      Tuesday and Wednesday tie for the highest number, with 150 visitors each.
      The figure falls from 150 on Wednesday to 90 on Thursday, a decrease of 60.
      “Visitors peaked at 150 on Tuesday and Wednesday, then fell sharply on Thursday” accurately describes the supplied data.

Q9. [Application · 4 marks] A survey of 200 students of a school showed these preferences for co-curricular activities: Sports 80, Music 50, Art 40, Debate 30. Write an analytical paragraph.
    Ans:
      A survey of 200 students shows clear preferences among the four co-curricular activities offered by the school.
      Sports is the most popular choice, preferred by 80 students, or 40 per cent of those surveyed, while music is second with 50 students, or 25 per cent.
      Art is chosen by 40 students (20 per cent) and debate by only 30 (15 per cent), which together account for just over a third of the students.
      The pattern suggests that physical activity attracts the largest number of students, while activities that require performance in front of an audience, such as debate, attract the fewest; the school could encourage the less popular activities by giving them more time, trained coaches and recognition.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] A student writes: ‘I think the chart is wrong and the school should buy more buses.’ Identify what is wrong with this sentence for an analytical paragraph.
    Ans:
      First, ‘I think the chart is wrong’ disputes the given data — the data must be accepted and analysed, never challenged.
      Second, ‘the school should buy more buses’ is a personal opinion and a suggestion — analytical paragraphs report trends and inferences, not opinions.
      The correct close would state what the data shows, for example that bus use dominates and is still rising.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Write an analytical paragraph on a bar chart showing the mode of transport used by students: bus 45%, cycle 25%, walk 20%, private car 10%.
    Ans:
      The bar chart shows how students travel to school, expressed as a percentage of the total number of students.
      The most common mode of transport is the bus, used by 45 per cent of students — almost half. Cycling is the second most popular, at 25 per cent, while 20 per cent of students walk to school. Only 10 per cent travel by private car, the least common mode.
      Taken together, the figures show that the great majority of students — 90 per cent — use public or non-motorised transport, and that reliance on private cars is minimal. This suggests that improving bus services and cycle lanes would benefit far more students than any measure aimed at car users.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Using the park-visitor figures in the case above, describe how you would build an analytical paragraph that reports the pattern without inventing a cause.
    Ans:
      Introduce the data as a record of visitors from Monday to Thursday, naming the unit if the chart provides one.
      Select the significant figures: 120 on Monday, a peak of 150 on Tuesday and Wednesday, and a fall to 90 on Thursday.
      Use comparative language such as “peaked at”, “remained steady” and “fell sharply”; do not list every number without explaining the pattern.
      Conclude with the overall observed trend, but do not claim why attendance changed unless the source gives a reason.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. An analytical paragraph is based on: (a) a story (b) a poem (c) a chart, graph, table or set of clues (d) a personal experience
    Ans: (c) a chart, graph, table, data set, outline or set of clues provided in the question.

M2. The word limit for an analytical paragraph is: (a) 50–60 words (b) 100–120 words (c) 150–200 words (d) no limit
    Ans: (b) 100–120 words.

M3. The concluding sentence of an analytical paragraph should: (a) state a trend or inference from the data (b) give a personal opinion (c) list more figures (d) repeat the introduction
    Ans: (a) state a trend or inference from the data — not a personal opinion, unless the question asks for one.

M4. A chart records attendance as 20, 25, 25 and 15 over four days. Which sentence reports the data accurately? (a) Attendance rose every day (b) Attendance peaked at 25 on the second and third days, then fell (c) The fourth day was the busiest (d) Attendance stayed at 20 throughout
    Ans: (b) Attendance peaked at 25 on the second and third days, then fell. It reports the tie and the later decline without adding an unsupported cause.

M5. Which sentence avoids adding an unsupported cause to a set of figures? (a) “The total fell because the manager was careless” (b) “The change proves that the service is poor” (c) “The total fell from 80 to 60” (d) “The decline will continue next year”
    Ans: (c) “The total fell from 80 to 60.” It describes the figures without inventing a reason or predicting the future.

M6. An analytical paragraph must be based on: (a) personal opinion only  (b) imaginary facts  (c) a news report  (d) the data given in the chart or table
    Ans: (d) the data given in the chart or table. Your own views are added only as a conclusion.

M7. The usual length of an analytical paragraph in the board paper is: (a) 300 words  (b) 20 words  (c) 500 words  (d) 100 to 120 words
    Ans: (d) 100 to 120 words. It should cover the trend, the data and one conclusion.

M8. Figures in an analytical paragraph should be: (a) compared with each other to show a trend (b) listed one by one without comment (c) invented to fill space (d) rounded off to zero
    Ans: (a) compared with each other to show a trend. Data without comparison is a list, not an analysis.
