A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Custard cry for a nice safe cage?
    Ans: Because he was a coward, or at least thought of himself as one. While Belinda and the other pets boasted of their bravery, Custard admitted his fear and asked for a nice safe cage. His cry is honest, and it is what makes his later action so surprising — he is the only one who did not pretend to be brave.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the pirate and how Custard dealt with him.
    Ans: The pirate climbed in through the window with a pistol in his hand and two cutlasses shining, a black beard and one wooden leg, and he was clearly bad. Mustard fled with a terrified cry, Ink trickled down to the bottom of the house, and Blink disappeared into his mouse hole. Custard jumped up, snorting like an engine, clashing his tail like iron in a dungeon, and went at the pirate like a robin at a worm — and he ate him, every bit.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] How does Custard prove himself when the pirate appears?
    Ans:
      While Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all run away in terror, Custard alone stands his ground.
      He snorts, claps his sharp teeth, gulps some mustard at the pirate and charges at him like a robin at a worm.
      The pirate fires two bullets that miss, and Custard swallows him in one bite, clashing his tail like irons in a dungeon.
      The ‘cowardly' dragon turns out to be the only one with real courage, which is the joke of the poem.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How do Belinda and the other pets behave after Custard kills the pirate? What does their behaviour show?
    Ans:
      Belinda embraces Custard and Mustard licks him — but within a page they go back to boasting: Mustard says he would have been twice as brave, Ink and Blink say they would have been three times as brave, and Custard agrees with all of them.
      Their behaviour shows that cowards mock true courage and then claim the credit afterwards — while the truly brave Custard keeps quiet and asks again for his nice safe cage.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] What makes ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon' a ballad of humour?
    Ans:
      It is written in the form of a ballad, with regular quatrains, a strong rhythm and a repeating refrain about the little pet dragon.
      The names are comic — Belinda, Ink, Blink, Mustard and Custard — and the rhyme is deliberately playful, as in ‘realio, trulio, little pet dragon'.
      The situation is upside down: the fierce-looking pets flee while the teased dragon fights, and the pirate is described as a dastardly coward.
      The ending is comic too: once the danger is over, everyone goes back to boasting, and Custard meekly wishes for his nice safe cage again.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon’ is called a ballad. Which features of a ballad do you find in it?
    Ans:
      A story in verse — the ballad narrates a complete tale with a beginning (the household), a crisis (the pirate) and an ending (the kill).
      Music and repetition — the aabb rhyme, the rolling rhythm and repeated refrains like ‘coward, and she called him Custard’ make it singable.
      A dramatic climax — the pirate's attack and Custard's sudden bravery give it the ballad's single moment of high action.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] What is the central idea of the poem? Is Custard a coward?
    Ans:
      The poem is a humorous story about bravery and boasting. Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all boast about how brave they are, while Custard openly admits his fear and asks for a nice safe cage.
      When the pirate actually appears, the boasters run away: Mustard flees with a terrified cry, Ink trickles to the bottom of the house, and Blink disappears into his mouse hole. Only Custard faces the pirate and eats him up, every bit.
      The moral is that those who boast are not necessarily brave, and the one who admits his fear may be the bravest of all when action is needed. Bravery is shown in deeds, not in words.
      Is Custard a coward? The poet leaves this open. Custard himself says he is a coward and would still prefer a nice safe cage, and the others all agree they are braver than he is. The humour lies in the fact that nobody changes their opinion, even after Custard has saved them. So the poem suggests that a person can be genuinely brave in action and still be timid by nature — the two are not contradictory.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. 'The Tale of Custard the Dragon' is written by: (a) Carl Sandburg (b) Ogden Nash (c) Robin Klein (d) Adrienne Rich
    Ans: (b) Ogden Nash.

M2. Blink in the poem is: (a) a little black kitten (b) a little yellow dog (c) a little grey mouse (d) a little pet dragon
    Ans: (c) a little grey mouse. Ink is the black kitten and Mustard is the yellow dog.

M3. When the pirate appeared, Mustard: (a) attacked him (b) hid behind Belinda (c) barked at him (d) fled with a terrified cry
    Ans: (d) fled with a terrified cry — despite having boasted that he was as brave as a tiger in a rage.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “The Tale of Custard the Dragon” is: (a) learn the key idea plus one example  (b) read the heading only  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (a) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Ogden Nash's ballad: a cowardly dragon and a real pirate.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “The Tale of Custard the Dragon” should: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (b) be one sentence  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Custard is teased by the others because he: (a) cannot fly  (b) has no teeth  (c) is too small  (d) is cowardly and cries for a nice safe cage
    Ans: (d) is cowardly and cries for a nice safe cage. His cry for safety is the refrain of the poem.

M7. Belinda's other pets are: (a) a cow and a goat  (b) a kitten, a mouse and a dog  (c) a parrot and a crow  (d) a horse and a hen
    Ans: (b) a kitten, a mouse and a dog. Ink is the kitten, Blink the mouse and Mustard the dog.

M8. The rhyme scheme of the poem is: (a) abab (b) abcb (c) aabb (d) free verse
    Ans: (c) aabb. The paired rhymes give the ballad its rolling, song-like rhythm.
