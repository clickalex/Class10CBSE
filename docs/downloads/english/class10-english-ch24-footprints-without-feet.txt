A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] How did Griffin become invisible?
    Ans: He was a brilliant scientist who carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could be made invisible, and at last he swallowed certain rare drugs. His body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though still as hard to see. To avoid being seen he also had to remove his clothes, which left him naked, homeless and penniless in the middle of winter.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Describe what happened at the inn in Iping.
    Ans: Griffin booked two rooms and paid Mr Hall in advance, but behaved strangely, refusing company and shutting himself in. When Mrs Hall went to his room she found the door open and the room empty, with the clothes he had been wearing scattered about. Suddenly a hat hopped off the bedpost and struck her face, and then a chair became alive, pushed both her and her husband out of the room and slammed and locked the door behind them. Mrs Hall was convinced the room was haunted by spirits.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] Why does Griffin fail as an invisible man?
    Ans:
      He discovers how to make the human body transparent, but he does not discover how to make it visible again, and he cannot reverse his experiment.
      Invisibility leaves him without clothes, food and shelter; he is hungry, cold and hunted everywhere.
      His behavior turns violent: he sets fire to his landlord's house, assaults people who get in his way and steals to survive.
      Finally he is attacked by the crowd in the inn, and his cloak is torn off, revealing an empty space where a man should be.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Griffin burn his landlord's house? What does this first crime tell us about him?
    Ans: Griffin burnt the house in revenge because his landlord tried to eject him. This first crime tells us that Griffin answers every obstacle with destruction — he has brilliance without conscience. The pattern never changes: robbery, assault and finally beating Constable Jaffers follow from the same lawless temper.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Describe Griffin's character and what the story says about the misuse of science.
    Ans:
      Griffin is a brilliant scientist, but he is selfish, violent and dishonest: he uses his discovery to rob, to revenge himself and to terrify ordinary people.
      He is disowned by his father, who had given him the money, and he is prepared to hurt anyone who obstructs him.
      The story suggests that science without moral responsibility destroys the scientist himself: Griffin ends up hunted, hungry and alone.
      Wells thus warns that a discovery used for private gain instead of public good turns its maker into a criminal and a victim.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] Invisibility should have made Griffin powerful, but it made him helpless. Explain this paradox with two examples from the story.
    Ans:
      First, at the inn in Iping, invisibility strands him: without clothes he is naked and freezing, with clothes he is visible and suspicious — the ‘headless’ reveal terrifies everyone and brings the police.
      Second, he cannot eat, sleep or move unseen in a watched village; every theft leaves footprints, literally and figuratively.
      The paradox is that invisibility removes his disguise but not his needs — science without planning is a trap, not a power.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Was Griffin a brilliant scientist or a lawless person? Justify your answer.
    Ans:
      He was certainly a brilliant scientist. He carried out experiment after experiment until he proved that the human body could be made invisible, and succeeded in swallowing drugs that made his body as transparent as glass. It is a discovery of extraordinary difficulty, and it shows real scientific ability and persistence.
      But he was also lawless. His first use of the discovery was revenge: he set fire to his landlord's house because the man disliked him and was trying to eject him. He then robbed a shop in London, stole clothes from a big store, robbed a shopkeeper at Drury Lane, and attacked the village constable Mr Jaffers at Iping.
      Conclusion: scientific brilliance without moral responsibility is dangerous. Griffin used a remarkable discovery for theft, revenge and violence, and ended up a hunted, homeless wanderer with no friends. So while his ability as a scientist cannot be denied, he was, in his behaviour, a lawless person.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Griffin became invisible by: (a) an accident (b) swallowing certain rare drugs (c) a magic spell (d) a laboratory explosion
    Ans: (b) swallowing certain rare drugs after many experiments.

M2. The inn where Griffin stayed was at: (a) Drury Lane (b) London Bridge (c) Iping (d) Shotover
    Ans: (c) Iping, where Mr and Mrs Hall kept the inn.

M3. The village constable who tried to arrest Griffin was: (a) Mr Jaffers (b) Mr Hall (c) Mr Griffin (d) Mr Kemp
    Ans: (a) Mr Jaffers, who was knocked unconscious by a man he could not see.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Footprints without Feet” is: (a) learn the key idea plus one example  (b) read the heading only  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (a) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is H. G. Wells: Griffin the invisible scientist becomes a hunted lawless man.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Footprints without Feet” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) copy the whole chapter  (c) leave the example out  (d) start with the idea, then a reason or method
    Ans: (d) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Griffin was a scientist who discovered: (a) how to fly  (b) a new gas  (c) how to make the human body transparent  (d) a cure for blindness
    Ans: (c) how to make the human body transparent. He could not reverse the process, which ruined him.

M7. Mrs Hall, the innkeeper, first grew suspicious when she saw: (a) footprints in the snow  (b) a broken window  (c) money missing from the till  (d) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes moving
    Ans: (d) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes. The bandages hid the fact that he was invisible.

M8. Griffin first betrayed his presence as: (a) a shadow on the wall (b) footprints appearing in the snow (c) a voice in the dark (d) smoke in the air
    Ans: (b) footprints appearing in the snow. Two boys saw fresh footprints of bare feet where no man was visible.
