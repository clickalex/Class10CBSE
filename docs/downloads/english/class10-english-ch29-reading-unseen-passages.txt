A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] What is the difference between a discursive and a case-based passage?
    Ans: A discursive passage is 400–450 words and argues or discusses an idea, so the questions test inference, interpretation and evaluation. A case-based factual passage is 200–250 words and is supported by visual input such as statistical data or a chart, so the questions test your ability to read the data and connect it to the text.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Describe a method for attempting the reading section in the time available.
    Ans: Read the questions first, so that you know what to look for. Then read the passage once quickly for the main idea. Go question by question, locate the relevant line and underline it. Eliminate the two options that are clearly wrong, then choose between the remaining two by returning to the text. Keep to about ninety seconds per question and mark any you cannot decide, returning to them at the end.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] How should you answer a vocabulary-in-context question?
    Ans: Find the sentence containing the word, cover the word, and decide for yourself what word would fit in its place. Then look at the options and choose the one closest to your own word. Do not choose a meaning you have met elsewhere, because the meaning must fit the sentence in front of you.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] An option in a reading MCQ is factually true in real life but is not supported by the passage. What should a student do, and why?
    Ans:
      Reject it unless the passage provides evidence for it; an unseen-passage answer must be based on the supplied text.
      Locate the relevant line or detail and check whether it actually answers the question, rather than relying on general knowledge.
      This prevents choosing a true but irrelevant distractor and keeps the response evidence-based.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A student reads a chart-based passage but gets a comparison question wrong. Give two checks the student should make before choosing an answer.
    Ans:
      Read the chart's title and identify the categories and period being compared.
      Check both axes, the units and the scale; then compare the actual values named in the question rather than estimating from the paragraph alone.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What is the right way to attempt an unseen passage in the board paper?
    Ans:
      Read the questions first, so that you know what to look for while reading.
      Then read the passage once quickly for the sense and a second time to locate the answers, underlining key words.
      Answer in complete sentences and, wherever asked, in your own words; do not copy long phrases from the passage.
      Stick to the word limit — usually 30 to 40 words for a two-mark question — and number the answers exactly as the questions are numbered.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What is the ‘eliminate two options’ rule, and why does it work in the reading section?
    Ans:
      In every reading MCQ, two options can usually be ruled out at once — one contradicts the passage and one is not mentioned in it at all.
      That leaves a fifty-fifty choice between two close options, which you settle by re-reading the exact lines the question points to.
      It works because boards design one distractor as opposite and one as out-of-scope; eliminating both is faster and safer than hunting directly for the answer.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A library records the number of books borrowed by three classes during one month: Class A, 48; Class B, 62; Class C, 40. The librarian says Class B borrowed the most, but does not claim that its students read more books at home or preferred every kind of book.
    
    (a) Which class borrowed the greatest number?
    (b) What is the difference between the highest and lowest figures?
    (c) Which conclusion would go beyond the data?
    Ans:
      Class B borrowed the greatest number, with 62 books.
      The difference is 62 − 40 = 22 books.
      It would go beyond the data to claim that every student in Class B reads more; the table records class totals only, not individual habits.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A short passage states that a school garden attracted more butterflies after native flowering plants were added. It reports observations from one garden over four weeks, but gives no figures for other schools or seasons.
    
    (a) What change does the passage report?
    (b) Which evidence supports that statement?
    (c) Which broad claim is not justified by this evidence?
    Ans:
      The passage reports an increase in butterfly visits to the school garden after native flowers were planted.
      The support is the observed increase during the four-week period described in the passage.
      The evidence does not prove that all gardens or every season will show the same increase; the report covers only one place and period.

Q10. [Application · 4 marks] How should you answer a case-based factual passage with MCQ and short items?
    Ans:
      Read the whole case once, including any table, chart or data given with it, before looking at the questions.
      For the MCQ, choose the option that is supported by the passage and cannot be contradicted by any line in it; do not bring in outside knowledge.
      For short items, write one clear sentence with the fact taken from the passage and the number or unit it gives.
      Keep every answer brief and grammatical, and do not repeat the question in the answer.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] You have ninety seconds per question and a 450-word passage. Plan your time across reading, locating and answering.
    Ans:
      First minute — read the questions, not the passage, so you know what to hunt for.
      Next three to four minutes — one fast read of the passage, underlining names, figures and opinion words.
      Then about a minute per question — locate the exact line, eliminate two options, mark the answer, and move on without re-reading the whole passage.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] A student spends too long rereading an unseen passage and then guesses at its vocabulary questions. Set out a more effective method that uses the passage as evidence and keeps time under control.
    Ans:
      Read the questions first so you know what details, comparisons or words to look for, then read the passage once for its main idea.
      For each question, return to the relevant sentence or data point. For vocabulary, test a possible meaning in that sentence rather than selecting the most familiar dictionary meaning.
      Eliminate options that contradict the text or do not answer the question, and verify the remaining choice against the evidence.
      Set a time limit for each item. Mark a difficult question, move on, and return if time remains instead of sacrificing the rest of the paper.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The length of the discursive passage in the reading section is: (a) 200–250 words (b) 300–350 words (c) 400–450 words (d) 500–550 words
    Ans: (c) 400–450 words. The case-based factual passage is 200–250 words.

M2. Objective type questions in the reading section assess: (a) memorisation (b) inference, analysis, interpretation, evaluation and vocabulary (c) handwriting (d) grammar rules
    Ans: (b) — they test higher-order reading skills, not recall.

M3. The best first step in attempting an unseen passage is to: (a) read the passage three times (b) read the questions first (c) answer from general knowledge (d) begin with the hardest question
    Ans: (b) read the questions first, so that you know what information to look for.

M4. In “After three dry weeks, the reservoir had fallen to a meagre level,” “meagre” most nearly means: (a) Very small (b) Unusually noisy (c) Deep and full (d) Recently repaired
    Ans: (a) Very small. The dry weeks have left the reservoir with little water, which is the meaning supported by the sentence.

M5. A bar chart shows 30, 45 and 60 books borrowed in three successive months. Which conclusion is supported? (a) Every student borrowed a book (b) The books were all fiction (c) The rise will continue next year (d) The total rose by 30 books from the first to the third month
    Ans: (d) The total rose by 30 books from the first to the third month. The chart supports the observed figures, not claims about individual students, genres or the future.

M6. Before writing the answers to an unseen passage you should: (a) start writing immediately  (b) copy lines from the passage  (c) read the questions, then read the passage  (d) skip the passage
    Ans: (c) read the questions, then read the passage. It saves time and keeps your reading focused.

M7. Answers to an unseen passage should be: (a) in your own words and within the word limit  (b) as long as possible  (c) copied from the passage  (d) written as bullet points only
    Ans: (a) in your own words and within the word limit. Copying whole sentences loses marks.

M8. In a case-based factual passage, the visual input is usually: (a) a photograph (b) a map only (c) a cartoon (d) a chart, table or graph
    Ans: (d) a chart, table or graph. The questions test whether you can read data, not just prose.
