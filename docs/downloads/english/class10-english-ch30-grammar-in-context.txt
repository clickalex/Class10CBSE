A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] Fill in the blank: Neither of the two candidates ____ (has/have) replied yet.
    Ans: has. 'Neither of' takes a singular verb, because the subject is 'neither', not 'candidates'.

Q2. [Short · 1 mark] Fill in the blank with a suitable modal: You ____ consult a doctor if the cough persists. (can / should / may)
    Ans: should — the sentence gives advice, which is the use of 'should'. 'Can' expresses ability and 'may' expresses permission or possibility, neither of which fits.

Q3. [Short · 1 mark] Choose the correct determiner: There isn't ____ milk left in the jug. (some / any / much)
    Ans: any — 'any' is used with uncountable nouns in negative sentences; 'some' is used in affirmative sentences.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] Change into reported speech: 'Where have you kept my files?' she asked the clerk.
    Ans: She asked the clerk where he had kept her files. Note the three changes: the present perfect becomes the past perfect, the question word order becomes statement order, and 'my' becomes 'her'.

Q5. [Application · 2 marks] Fill in the blanks with a reason for each: (i) Slow and steady ___ (win/wins) the race. (ii) No news ___ (is/are) good news.
    Ans:
      (i) wins — ‘slow and steady’ is a single combined idea (one subject), so the verb is singular.
      (ii) is — ‘news’ looks plural but is an uncountable singular noun, so it takes a singular verb.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Grammar in Context” (Tenses, modals, subject-verb agreement, reported speech, determiners). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Using the simple past with 'since'. Since requires a perfect tense. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: In a gap-filling passage, look at the other verbs first — the tense of the passage is usually consistent.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Using the simple past with 'since'. Since requires a perfect tense.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Grammar in Context”: “Fill in the blank: Neither of the two candidates ____ (has/have) replied yet.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Tenses, modals, subject-verb agreement, reported speech, determiners.
      has. 'Neither of' takes a singular verb, because the subject is 'neither', not 'candidates'.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] How do you change a sentence from direct into indirect speech?
    Ans:
      Change the reporting verb into the past tense; since the reporting verb is already in the past, the verb in the reported clause moves one step back — present to past, past to past perfect.
      Change the pronouns according to sense, and change words of nearness into words of distance: now becomes then, today that day, tomorrow the next day, here there.
      Remove the inverted commas and the comma after the reporting verb, and use ‘if' or ‘whether' for yes–no questions.
      Do not change a universal truth: ‘The teacher said that the earth moves round the sun' keeps the present tense.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] Correct these sentences and give a reason for each: (i) She don't like mangoes. (ii) He is working since morning. (iii) The teacher said that the earth moved round the sun. (iv) Neither of the boys were present.
    Ans:
      (i) She doesn't like mangoes — a third-person singular subject takes does not, not do not.
      (ii) He has been working since morning — an action continuing up to the present uses the present perfect continuous, not the present continuous.
      (iii) The teacher said that the earth moves round the sun — a universal truth keeps the present tense even in indirect speech.
      (iv) Neither of the boys was present — ‘neither' is singular, so the verb is was, not were.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Grammar in Context” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:
      Tenses. The tense must agree with the time marker in the sentence. Since and for take the perfect tenses; yesterday, last year take the simple past; now, at present take the present continuous; tomorrow, next week take the future. In a narrative passage, the tense often shifts only when the time shifts — so keep the tense until a new time marker appears.
      Marks lens: About 10 marks, usually as a gap-filling or editing task set within a short passage or dialogue.
      Method / memory trick: In a gap-filling passage, look at the other verbs first — the tense of the passage is usually consistent.
      Do not: Using the simple past with 'since'. Since requires a perfect tense.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Choose the correct sentence: (a) The committee have taken their seats. (b) The committee have taken its seat. (c) The committee has taken their seats. (d) The committee has taken its seat.
    Ans: (d) — when the collective noun acts as a single unit, the verb and the pronoun are both singular.

M2. Report the sentence: He said, 'I am leaving tomorrow.' (a) He said that he is leaving tomorrow. (b) He said that he was leaving the next day. (c) He said that he left tomorrow. (d) He said that he will leave the next day.
    Ans: (b) — the tense moves back one step, and 'tomorrow' becomes 'the next day'.

M3. Fill in the blank: She has been waiting ____ two hours. (a) since (b) from (c) for (d) during
    Ans: (c) for — 'for' is used with a period of time; 'since' is used with a point in time.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Grammar in Context” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Tenses, modals, subject-verb agreement, reported speech, determiners.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Grammar in Context” should: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (b) be one sentence  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (a) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Choose the correct form: Neither of the boys ___ present. (a) were  (b) are  (c) was  (d) have been
    Ans: (c) was. ‘Neither' and ‘either' are treated as singular in formal English.

M7. The correct reported form of ‘He said, “I am busy”' is: (a) He said that he was busy  (b) He said that he is busy  (c) He said that I was busy  (d) He says he was busy
    Ans: (a) He said that he was busy. The pronoun changes to he and the tense moves one step back.

M8. Fill in the blank: He asked me ____ I could help him. (a) that (b) what (c) to (d) if
    Ans: (d) if. A reported yes/no question joins the main clause with if (or whether), never that.
