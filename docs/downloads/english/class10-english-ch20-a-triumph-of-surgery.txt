Unit: Footprints Without Feet

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Tricki become so ill?
    Ans: Because Mrs Pumphrey overfed him. She gave him cream cakes, chocolates, sweets and cod-liver oil, and never allowed him any exercise. The result was that he became hugely fat, with bloodshot, rheumy eyes and a lolling tongue, and could hardly walk. His illness was caused by indulgence, not by disease.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What treatment did the vet give Tricki, and why was it successful?
    Ans: The vet gave no medicine at all. He kept Tricki in his surgery, gave him only water for the first two days, and then plenty of exercise and normal food. The treatment worked because the cause of the illness was overfeeding and lack of exercise, so removing the cause was enough to restore the dog's health.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Do you think Mrs Pumphrey was a wise owner? Give reasons.
    Ans: No. She confused love with indulgence. She fed Tricki cream cakes, chocolates and sweets, and never let him exercise, which is why he became dangerously fat and ill. She also ignored the vet's advice about diet and exercise, and continued to send luxury food even after the dog had recovered. A wise owner would have shown affection through discipline and regular activity rather than through food.

Q4. [Application · 3 marks] Mrs Pumphrey calls Tricki's weakness malnutrition and gives him rich food to build him up. As the vet, correct her diagnosis and identify the change needed for recovery.
    Ans:
      Tricki's illness comes from overfeeding and too little exercise, not a shortage of rich food.
      Cakes, sweets and extra supplements have made him overweight and unwell.
      The vet removes the cause: a short fast, water, and then simple food and exercise rather than an operation or medicine.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A pet owner says that refusing a pet treats is unkind. Use Tricki's recovery to explain why affection also requires limits.
    Ans:
      Giving Tricki constant treats seemed loving, but it damaged his health.
      The vet's restraint and exercise restored him; responsible care means meeting an animal's needs rather than every appetite.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What was wrong with Tricki and how had Mrs Pumphrey treated him?
    Ans: Tricki was hugely overfed and listless: Mrs Pumphrey gave him malt, cod-liver oil, Horlicks, cream cakes, chocolates, and even a whole box of eggs with wine and brandy between meals. He had become a bloated, panting little creature with no exercise, so at the surgery James Herriot diagnosed nothing more serious than overeating and lack of discipline.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Why did Mrs Pumphrey think Tricki's recovery was ‘a triumph of surgery’? What is ironical about this phrase?
    Ans: Mrs Pumphrey believed the vet had performed some difficult medical feat, so she called the recovery a triumph of surgery. The irony is that there was no surgery and no medicine at all — only water, fasting, exercise and normal food. The real cure was removing the cause (overfeeding), not prescribing anything.

Q8. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    Mrs Pumphrey is alarmed by Tricki's heavy body and dull eyes. The vet realises that she has been giving him cakes, chocolates and supplements, while he gets almost no exercise. At the surgery he first gives the dog water and a period without food.
    
    (a) What is the real cause of Tricki's poor health?
    (b) Why does the vet begin with restraint rather than rich food?
    (c) What does this treatment suggest about effective care?
    Ans:
      The dog is overfed and under-exercised; Mrs Pumphrey mistakes the effects of excess for malnutrition.
      A short fast removes the immediate cause instead of adding to it, and water keeps the treatment simple.
      Care is not always giving more; it may mean removing a harmful habit and allowing gradual, appropriate exercise.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    At the surgery, Tricki spends time with the other dogs and becomes active again. Mrs Pumphrey later sees him running and calls his recovery “a triumph of surgery”, though the vet has not operated on him.
    
    (a) What helps Tricki become active at the surgery?
    (b) Why is Mrs Pumphrey's description ironic?
    (c) What is the story gently criticising?
    Ans:
      Simple food, water, exercise and the company of the other dogs help him recover.
      She attributes the result to surgery, but there has been no operation; the cure is ordinary care and a change in routine.
      The story criticises overindulgence disguised as affection and the assumption that a medical-looking solution is always needed.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] How does James Herriot cure Tricki, and what does the story say about love and discipline?
    Ans:
      He takes Tricki away from Mrs Pumphrey, keeps him in the surgery with the other dogs and, for the first two days, gives him no food at all — only water.
      On the third day the dog begins to take interest in his bowl, and he is gradually given small amounts of food.
      He recovers quickly, learns to play and run with the other dogs, and soon ‘the cure was complete'.
      The story shows that love without discipline harms its object: Mrs Pumphrey's affection nearly killed Tricki, while Herriot's firmness restored his health.

Q11. [Application · 4 marks] The vet shares Mrs Pumphrey's food hampers with his partner while Tricki fasts. Does this make him dishonest, or is it part of the humour? Give reasons.
    Ans:
      It is part of the humour, not real dishonesty — the hampers of eggs, wine and brandy were meant to tempt Tricki, and giving them to a fasting dog would have undone the cure.
      The vet and his partner enjoying the food while Tricki slimmed down on water is the story's running joke: the rich food fattens everyone except the patient.
      What matters is that Tricki recovered fully; the vet's method, if not his manners, was exactly right.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Why is the title “A Triumph of Surgery” ironic, and what does Tricki's treatment reveal about the difference between indulgence and care?
    Ans:
      Mrs Pumphrey uses the title to celebrate Tricki's recovery, but James Herriot performs no surgery and gives him no medicine.
      Tricki's illness was caused by excess food and lack of exercise, so the vet's first step is to stop the treats and let the dog rest and drink water.
      As Tricki gains strength, simple food, movement and the company of other dogs restore his health.
      The humour exposes Mrs Pumphrey's mistake: affection without judgement can harm. Good care sometimes means setting limits instead of giving more.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Tricki was a: (a) small Pomeranian (b) large Alsatian (c) greyhound (d) spaniel
    Ans: (a) a small Pomeranian, spoilt and overfed by Mrs Pumphrey.

M2. The vet treated Tricki with: (a) an operation (b) antibiotics (c) cod-liver oil (d) no medicine, only water and exercise
    Ans: (d) no medicine at all — water, fasting at first, then exercise and a normal diet.

M3. The phrase 'a triumph of surgery' is spoken by: (a) Mrs Pumphrey (b) the vet (c) Mrs Pumphrey's friends (d) Tricki
    Ans: (a) Mrs Pumphrey, when she came to collect the recovered dog.

M4. What is the main cause of Tricki's illness? (a) A broken leg (b) A shortage of toys (c) Overfeeding and too little exercise (d) An attack by another dog
    Ans: (c) Overfeeding and too little exercise. Mrs Pumphrey's constant treats and lack of exercise have made him unwell.

M5. Who narrates “A Triumph of Surgery”? (a) Mrs Pumphrey's neighbour (b) The veterinary surgeon (c) Tricki (d) The kennel attendant
    Ans: (b) The veterinary surgeon. James Herriot narrates the story of treating Tricki.

M6. Tricki was: (a) a bulldog  (b) a cat  (c) a poodle  (d) a Pomeranian
    Ans: (d) a Pomeranian. A small dog pampered into illness by his wealthy owner.

M7. Mrs Pumphrey sent Tricki to the surgery with: (a) only water  (b) eggs, wine and brandy  (c) medicines  (d) toys
    Ans: (b) eggs, wine and brandy. She sent two dozen eggs, two bottles of wine and a bottle of brandy.

M8. Tricki recovered fully in: (a) two days (b) a month (c) a fortnight (d) six months
    Ans: (c) a fortnight. Two weeks of water, exercise and plain food cured what rich food had caused.
