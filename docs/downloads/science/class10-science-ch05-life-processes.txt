A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (14)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] Name the functional unit of the kidney and the pigment that absorbs light energy in plants.
    Ans: Nephron; chlorophyll.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like human beings?
    Ans: Because their cells are not all in direct contact with the environment, and the volume of the body is large, so the distance oxygen would have to diffuse is too great. Diffusion is too slow to supply every cell, which is why special respiratory and transport systems are needed.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Name the raw materials and the products of photosynthesis, and write the overall equation.
    Ans: Raw materials: carbon dioxide from the air and water absorbed from the soil, using chlorophyll and sunlight. Products: glucose (stored as starch) and oxygen. Overall: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Why is the rate of breathing in aquatic organisms much faster than in terrestrial organisms?
    Ans: Because the amount of dissolved oxygen in water is far lower than the oxygen in air. Aquatic animals must therefore pass a larger volume of water over their gills per unit time to obtain enough oxygen, which makes the breathing rate faster.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? Name two organisms that use anaerobic respiration.
    Ans:
      Aerobic occurs in the presence of oxygen, in the mitochondria, giving CO2 and H2O and a large amount of energy.
      Anaerobic occurs without oxygen in the cytoplasm, giving ethanol and CO2 (yeast) or lactic acid (muscles), and much less energy.
      Organisms: yeast, some bacteria and parasitic worms such as Taenia; human muscle cells respire anaerobically temporarily.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Draw a labelled diagram of the human excretory system, or describe the working of a nephron.
    Ans:
      Kidneys → ureters → urinary bladder → urethra.
      Nephron: blood enters the glomerulus under pressure inside Bowman's capsule, where water, glucose, amino acids, salts and urea are filtered into the tubule (ultrafiltration).
      As the filtrate moves down the tubule, useful substances and most water are selectively reabsorbed; the remaining fluid, now urine, collects in the collecting duct and passes into the ureter.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] A student writes: “Bile juice digests fats because it contains the enzyme lipase.” What is wrong with this? State the real role of bile and name the enzyme that digests fats.
    Ans:
      Bile, made in the liver and stored in the gall bladder, contains no digestive enzyme.
      Bile salts break large fat globules into tiny droplets (emulsification), giving more surface for enzymes, and make the medium alkaline for the pancreatic enzymes.
      Fats are then digested by the enzyme lipase from the pancreatic and intestinal juices.

Q8. [Application · 3 marks] A potted plant is kept in the dark for three days. One of its leaves is then partly covered with black paper and kept in sunlight for six hours. The leaf is boiled in alcohol and tested with iodine solution. Which part turns blue-black, and what does this prove?
    Ans:
      The leaf is first destarched by keeping the plant in the dark.
      The uncovered part, which received light, turns blue-black with iodine because starch has been made there. The covered part stays brown and contains no starch.
      This shows that light is necessary for photosynthesis.

Q9. [Short · 3 marks] Describe how water and minerals are transported in plants.
    Ans:
      Root hair cells absorb water and dissolved minerals from the soil by osmosis and active transport.
      They pass into the xylem vessels, which form a continuous tube from the roots to the leaves.
      Loss of water vapour through the stomata (transpiration) creates a suction pull that lifts the water column upward; no energy is spent by the plant.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A runner feels a sharp cramp in her leg muscles at the end of a sprint, when her muscles could not get enough oxygen.

    (a) Name the process that supplied energy in her muscle cells and the substance that caused the cramp.
    (b) Write the pathway that begins with glucose.
    (c) How can the cramp be relieved?
    Ans:
      (a) Anaerobic respiration; the build-up of lactic acid caused the cramp.
      (b) Glucose (6-carbon) → pyruvate (3-carbon, in the cytoplasm) → lactic acid (3-carbon) + a small amount of energy.
      (c) Rest, or a hot-water massage, improves blood flow so that more oxygen reaches the muscles and the lactic acid is broken down.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A student examines a model of the human heart and notes that the wall of one lower chamber is much thicker than the others.

    (a) Which chamber has the thickest wall, and why?
    (b) Name the vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the heart to the body and the vessel that brings oxygenated blood from the lungs.
    (c) What prevents blood from flowing backwards?
    Ans:
      (a) The left ventricle, because it must pump blood at high pressure to the whole body.
      (b) The aorta carries blood to the body; the pulmonary vein brings blood from the lungs to the left atrium.
      (c) Valves between the chambers and in the vessels keep the blood flowing in one direction.

Q12. [Application · 4 marks] Explain double circulation in human beings and state why it is advantageous.
    Ans:
      Blood passes through the heart twice during one complete round of the body.
      Pulmonary circuit: right side of the heart → lungs (oxygenation) → left side of the heart.
      Systemic circuit: left side → body organs → right side of the heart.
      Because oxygenated and deoxygenated blood stay separate, tissues receive blood with the maximum possible oxygen, which supports the high energy needs of a warm-blooded body.

Q13. [Long · 5 marks] Explain the process of nutrition in human beings, from ingestion to egestion.
    Ans:
      Mouth: teeth chew the food; salivary amylase begins the breakdown of starch into simpler sugars.
      Oesophagus: peristaltic movements push the food to the stomach.
      Stomach: HCl makes the medium acidic for pepsin to digest proteins, kills germs, and mucus protects the stomach wall.
      Small intestine: the site of complete digestion — bile from the liver emulsifies fat, pancreatic trypsin acts on proteins, lipase on fats, and intestinal enzymes convert carbohydrates to glucose and proteins to amino acids; the finger-like villi absorb the digested food into the blood.
      Large intestine and anus: water is absorbed from the undigested food and the waste is egested.

Q14. [Long · 5 marks] Describe the three main events of photosynthesis and explain how stomata help the process.
    Ans:
      Photosynthesis takes place in the chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll.
      Event 1: chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
      Event 2: light energy is converted into chemical energy and water is split into hydrogen and oxygen.
      Event 3: carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrates: 6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O (in sunlight, with chlorophyll).
      Stomata are tiny pores on the leaf surface through which CO₂ enters and O₂ leaves. Guard cells open and close them, and water vapour is also lost through them.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The kidneys in human beings are a part of the system for: (a) excretion (b) nutrition (c) respiration (d) transportation
    Ans: (a) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.

M2. Which of the following is not a function of the stomach? (a) killing bacteria (b) digestion of protein (c) absorption of digested food (d) protection of the stomach wall by mucus
    Ans: (c). Absorption of digested food occurs mainly in the small intestine through the villi.

M3. The breakdown of pyruvate to give CO2, water and energy takes place in the: (a) mitochondria (b) cytoplasm (c) chloroplast (d) nucleus
    Ans: (a) mitochondria. Glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate) occurs in the cytoplasm; the further oxidation of pyruvate is mitochondrial.

M4. Which of the following gives the correct path of food in the human alimentary canal? (a) mouth → stomach → oesophagus → small intestine → large intestine (b) mouth → oesophagus → small intestine → stomach → large intestine (c) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → large intestine → small intestine (d) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine
    Ans: (d) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. Food is swallowed into the oesophagus, digested in the stomach, absorbed in the small intestine, and the undigested part passes to the large intestine.

M5. The opening and closing of stomata is controlled by: (a) epidermal cells (b) mesophyll cells (c) xylem vessels (d) guard cells
    Ans: (d) guard cells. Guard cells swell or shrink with water, changing the size of the stomatal pore.

M6. Photosynthesis in a leaf takes place inside the: (a) mitochondria  (b) chloroplast  (c) ribosome  (d) vacuole
    Ans: (b) chloroplast. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy.

M7. Which of these is an autotroph? (a) mushroom  (b) Cuscuta  (c) green plant  (d) human being
    Ans: (c) green plant. It prepares its own food by photosynthesis; Cuscuta is a parasite and fungi are saprotrophs.

M8. The main nitrogenous waste filtered out by the kidneys is: (a) carbon dioxide  (b) urea  (c) glucose  (d) glycogen
    Ans: (b) urea. It is filtered out of blood in the nephrons along with water and salts to form urine.
