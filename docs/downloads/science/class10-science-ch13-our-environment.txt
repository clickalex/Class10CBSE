A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (13)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] Name the organisms that always occupy the first trophic level of a food chain, and define an ecosystem.
    Ans: Green plants and blue-green algae (producers). An ecosystem is a self-sustaining system in which all the living organisms of an area interact with each other and with the non-living (abiotic) components such as soil, water and air.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Why are some substances biodegradable and some non-biodegradable?
    Ans: Because micro-organisms such as bacteria and fungi produce enzymes that can break down only certain substances like paper, wood and vegetable waste. Substances such as plastic, glass and DDT cannot be acted upon by these enzymes, so they remain in the environment for a very long time and are called non-biodegradable.

Q3. [Application · 2 marks] Suggest two ways to reduce the problem of waste disposal.
    Ans: Segregate waste at the source and send biodegradable waste for composting or vermicomposting; and practise the 5 R's — refuse what is unnecessary, reduce consumption, reuse containers, repurpose items, and recycle paper, plastic, glass and metal.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between a food chain and a food web.
    Ans: A food chain is a single linear sequence showing who eats whom, for example grass → grasshopper → frog → snake. A food web is a network of many interconnected food chains in an ecosystem; because each organism can feed at more than one level, the web is far more stable than a single chain.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] In a food chain, if 10,000 joules of solar energy is available to the producers, how much energy will be available to the secondary consumer?
    Ans:
      Producers convert about 1% of the solar energy into food energy, but taking 10,000 J as the energy in the producers: primary consumer receives 10% = 1,000 J.
      Secondary consumer receives 10% of 1,000 J = 100 J.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] What is ozone? How is it formed, and why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern?
    Ans:
      Ozone is a molecule of oxygen with three atoms, O3, found in the upper atmosphere.
      Formation: ultraviolet radiation splits an oxygen molecule into free oxygen atoms, O2 →(UV) 2O; each free atom combines with an oxygen molecule to form ozone, O + O2 → O3.
      Concern: the ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun. Its depletion, caused mainly by CFCs from refrigerators, air conditioners and aerosols, allows more UV to reach the earth, causing skin cancer, damage to the eyes, reduced crop yields and harm to marine organisms. The Montreal Protocol of 1987 froze CFC production to protect it.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] A student draws the food chain as Hawk → Snake → Frog → Grasshopper → Grass. What is wrong? Redraw it correctly and name the trophic levels.
    Ans:
      An arrow shows the direction of energy flow, from the food to the organism that eats it. The student’s arrows point from predator to prey.
      Correct chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk.
      Trophic levels: grass is the producer (1st); grasshopper the primary consumer (2nd); frog the secondary consumer (3rd); snake the tertiary consumer (4th); hawk the top consumer (5th).

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Explain the role of decomposers in an ecosystem.
    Ans:
      Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down the dead bodies of plants and animals and their wastes into simple inorganic substances.
      These substances — mineral salts, carbon dioxide and water — go back into the soil and air and are reused by the producers.
      They also clean up the environment. Without decomposers nutrients would stay locked in dead matter and the cycling of matter would stop.

Q9. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    In a grassland food chain, the producers capture 20,000 J of energy.

    (a) How much energy reaches the herbivores?
    (b) How much energy reaches the carnivores that eat the herbivores?
    (c) Why do food chains rarely have more than four or five trophic levels?
    Ans:
      (a) By the 10% law, herbivores get 10% of 20,000 J = 2,000 J.
      (b) The carnivores get 10% of 2,000 J = 200 J.
      (c) About 90% of the energy at each level is lost as heat in life processes, so very little is left to support more levels.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A sealed glass jar contains water, a few water plants, small fish and snails, and some soil at the bottom. It is kept in sunlight.

    (a) Name the producers, consumers and decomposers in the jar.
    (b) What is the role of sunlight?
    (c) What would happen if the jar were kept in the dark for a long time?
    Ans:
      (a) Producers: the water plants (and algae); consumers: the fish and snails; decomposers: the bacteria and fungi in the water and soil.
      (b) Sunlight is the source of energy: the plants use it for photosynthesis, which provides food and oxygen for the other organisms.
      (c) Without light, the plants could not photosynthesise, so food and oxygen would run out and the organisms would die.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] A village panchayat plans to burn its collected plastic waste in the open. Advise them, giving reasons from what you have studied about ecosystems.
    Ans:
      Open burning of plastic releases poisonous gases such as hydrogen chloride and dioxins into the air, harming people, animals and crops.
      Plastics are non-biodegradable, so if they are buried or dumped they persist in the soil for decades and choke drains and the guts of animals.
      Better: segregate waste at source, send recyclable plastic for recycling, compost the biodegradable fraction, and replace single-use plastic with cloth or paper bags.
      This keeps harmful chemicals out of the soil and water, so they cannot enter food chains and build up by biological magnification.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] What is biological magnification? Will the level of magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?
    Ans:
      Biological magnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of non-biodegradable harmful chemicals at successive trophic levels of a food chain.
      Yes, the level differs. Because these chemicals are not metabolised or excreted, they pass on to the next level, and the amount of food consumed at each level is large, so the concentration keeps increasing.
      Example: DDT sprayed on crops is absorbed by plants; grasshoppers eating many plants accumulate more; frogs eating many grasshoppers more still; and hawks at the top accumulate the highest concentration. Human beings, being at the top of many food chains, are among the most affected.

Q13. [Long · 5 marks] What is a trophic level? Explain the flow of energy in an ecosystem and why it is unidirectional.
    Ans:
      A trophic level is a step in a food chain, for example producers, herbivores and carnivores.
      The ultimate source of energy is the Sun; producers capture it through photosynthesis and pass it on when eaten.
      At each step only about 10% of the energy is passed on to the next level (the 10% law); the rest is lost as heat in respiration and other life processes.
      The energy lost as heat cannot be used again by the producers, so energy flows in one direction only, from the Sun to producers to consumers.
      Because so little energy remains, food chains are short, and plants always occupy the first level.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. In the food chain Grass → Deer → Lion, if 10,000 J of energy is available to the grass, the energy available to the lion is: (a) 1000 J (b) 100 J (c) 10 J (d) 1 J
    Ans: (b) 100 J. Grass 10,000 J → deer 1,000 J → lion 100 J (10% at each transfer).

M2. The depletion of the ozone layer is mainly caused by: (a) CFCs (b) CO2 (c) methane (d) SO2
    Ans: (a) CFCs — chlorofluorocarbons from refrigerators, air conditioners and fire extinguishers destroy ozone molecules.

M3. Which of the following groups contains only biodegradable substances? (a) paper, plastic, wood (b) glass, wood, plastic (c) lime juice, grass, aluminium foil (d) vegetable peels, paper, lime juice
    Ans: (d) — all three are broken down by the action of micro-organisms.

M4. Which of the following is a non-biodegradable waste? (a) vegetable peelings (b) a plastic bag (c) a paper bag (d) a cotton cloth
    Ans: (b) a plastic bag. Microorganisms cannot break down plastics, so they persist in the environment for a very long time.

M5. In the food chain Plant → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake, the secondary consumer is the: (a) grasshopper (b) snake (c) frog (d) plant
    Ans: (c) frog. The grasshopper is the primary consumer and the frog, which eats it, is the secondary consumer.

M6. The ‘10% law’ in an ecosystem describes the transfer of: (a) energy  (b) water  (c) minerals  (d) oxygen
    Ans: (a) energy. Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is stored in the next; the rest is lost as heat and in life processes.

M7. Which of these is a renewable resource? (a) coal  (b) petroleum  (c) sunlight  (d) natural gas
    Ans: (c) sunlight. Coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels that take millions of years to form.

M8. The ozone layer is being depleted mainly by: (a) methane  (b) nitrogen fertilisers  (c) carbon monoxide  (d) CFCs
    Ans: (d) CFCs. Chlorofluorocarbons from refrigerants and aerosol sprays release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone molecules.
