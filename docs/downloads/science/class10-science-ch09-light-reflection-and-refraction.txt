A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (14)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] State the mirror formula and the SI unit of the power of a lens.
    Ans: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. The SI unit of power is the dioptre (D), equal to 1 m⁻¹.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Why is a convex mirror preferred as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?
    Ans: Because it always gives an erect, virtual and diminished image, and it has a wider field of view than a plane mirror, so the driver can see a much larger area of the traffic behind.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] A lens of power −4.0 D is placed in the path of light. Find its focal length and identify it.
    Ans: P = 1/f, so f = 1/(−4.0) = −0.25 m = −25 cm. Negative focal length means it is a concave (diverging) lens.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] Define the principal focus of a concave mirror and relate it to the radius of curvature.
    Ans: The principal focus is the point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the axis meet (or appear to meet) after reflection. For a spherical mirror the focus lies midway between the pole and the centre of curvature, so f = R/2.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] An object 4 cm high is placed 25 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the position, nature and size of the image.
    Ans:
      u = −25 cm, f = −15 cm. Using 1/v + 1/u = 1/f: 1/v = 1/(−15) − 1/(−25) = (−5 + 3)/75 = −2/75.
      So v = −37.5 cm — the image forms 37.5 cm in front of the mirror, hence real and inverted.
      m = −v/u = −(−37.5)/(−25) = −1.5, so h' = −1.5 × 4 = −6 cm: the image is 6 cm tall, inverted, and magnified.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] Draw a ray diagram for an object placed between C and F of a concave mirror and state the nature of the image.
    Ans:
      Draw the principal axis with P, F and C marked. Place the object (an arrow) between C and F.
      Ray 1: parallel to the axis, reflected through F. Ray 2: through F, reflected parallel to the axis. (A third ray through C retraces its path.)
      The rays meet beyond C, so the image is real, inverted and magnified. This is the arrangement used in a shaving or makeup mirror when the object is close.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Light — Reflection and Refraction” (Ray diagrams, mirror and lens formulas, magnification and power). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Using the mirror formula for a lens. Lens formula has −v and +u in the same form: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Sign convention: distances measured against the direction of the incident light (to the left of the pole) are negative. Object distance u is always negative. For a concave mirror f is negative; for a convex mirror and a convex lens f is positive; a concave lens has negative f.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Using the mirror formula for a lens. Lens formula has −v and +u in the same form: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q8. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Light — Reflection and Refraction”: “State the mirror formula and the SI unit of the power of a lens.” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:
      Idea: Ray diagrams, mirror and lens formulas, magnification and power.
      /v + 1/u = 1/f. The SI unit of power is the dioptre (D), equal to 1 m⁻¹.

Q9. [Short · 3 marks] An object 5 cm tall is placed 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.
    Ans:
      Using the lens formula 1/f = 1/v − 1/u with f = +20 cm and u = −30 cm:
      1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = (3 − 2)/60 = 1/60, so v = 60 cm.
      Magnification m = v/u = 60/(−30) = −2, so h₂ = m × h₁ = −2 × 5 = −10 cm.
      The image is real, inverted and twice the size, formed 60 cm on the other side of the lens.

Q12. [Long · 4 marks] State the sign convention used for spherical mirrors and lenses, and apply it to a concave mirror.
    Ans:
      All distances are measured from the pole (mirror) or the optical centre (lens).
      Distances measured in the direction of the incident light are positive and those against it are negative; heights above the principal axis are positive and those below are negative.
      In practice the object distance u is always negative, the focal length of a concave mirror and of a concave lens is negative, and that of a convex mirror and a convex lens is positive.

Q13. [Long · 5 marks] State the laws of refraction and solve: light enters from air into glass (n = 1.5) with an angle of incidence of 30°. Find the angle of refraction and the speed of light in glass.
    Ans:
      Laws: (i) the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane; (ii) for a given pair of media and a given colour, sin i / sin r is constant (Snell's law).
      Angle of refraction: sin r = sin i / n = 0.5/1.5 = 0.3333, so r ≈ 19.5° — the ray bends toward the normal.
      Speed in glass: v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.5 = 2 × 10⁸ m/s.

Q14. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Light — Reflection and Refraction” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:
      Reflection. Light travelling in a straight line bounces back on striking a polished surface. Laws of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane. A plane mirror always forms a virtual, erect image, the same size as the object, at the same distance behind the mirror, and laterally inverted.
      Marks lens: –4 marks. One ray diagram, one numerical on the mirror or lens formula, and one question on magnification or power.
      Method / memory trick: Sign convention: distances measured against the direction of the incident light (to the left of the pole) are negative. Object distance u is always negative. For a concave mirror f is negative; for a convex mirror and a convex lens f is positive; a concave lens has negative f.
      Do not: Using the mirror formula for a lens. Lens formula has −v and +u in the same form: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.

B. MCQs (11)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The magnification produced by a rear-view mirror fitted in vehicles is: (a) less than one (b) more than one (c) equal to one (d) more than one or less than one depending on the position of the object
    Ans: (a) less than one. A convex mirror always forms a diminished, virtual and erect image.

M2. Where should an object be placed in front of a convex lens to get a real image of the same size? (a) at F (b) at 2F (c) at infinity (d) between F and 2F
    Ans: (b) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.

M3. The refractive index of water is 1.33. The speed of light in water is: (a) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (b) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 1.33 × 10⁸ m/s (d) 4 × 10⁸ m/s
    Ans: (b) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.33.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Light — Reflection and Refraction” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Ray diagrams, mirror and lens formulas, magnification and power.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Light — Reflection and Refraction” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The power of a convex lens of focal length 50 cm is: (a) +2 D  (b) −2 D  (c) +0.5 D  (d) −0.5 D
    Ans: (a) +2 D. P = 1/f in metres = 1/0.5 = +2 D; a converging lens always has positive power.

M7. Which mirror always forms an erect and diminished image? (a) plane  (b) concave  (c) convex  (d) parabolic
    Ans: (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.

M8. When a ray of light goes from glass into air, it: (a) bends towards the normal  (b) bends away from the normal  (c) does not bend  (d) is totally absorbed
    Ans: (b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.

M9. An object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. The image is formed: (a) 10 cm in front of the mirror  (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror  (c) 20 cm behind the mirror  (d) at infinity
    Ans: (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.

M10. A ray of light enters a denser medium along the normal (angle of incidence 0°). It: (a) bends towards the normal  (b) bends away from the normal  (c) passes undeviated  (d) is totally reflected
    Ans: (c) passes undeviated. Refraction changes the speed but there is no bending when the ray is along the normal, because sin 0° = 0 on both sides of Snell's law.

M11. The image formed by a concave lens of a real object is always: (a) real and inverted  (b) virtual, erect and diminished  (c) virtual, erect and magnified  (d) real and magnified
    Ans: (b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.
