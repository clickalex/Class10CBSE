A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (14)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] What is the sex chromosome combination in a human male, and what is the functional unit of inheritance?
    Ans: XY; the gene (a segment of DNA on a chromosome).

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] A man with blood group A marries a woman with blood group O and their daughter has blood group O. Is this enough to tell which trait is dominant? Why?
    Ans: No. From one family we cannot tell. If A were dominant, the father could be heterozygous (IAi) and the daughter could inherit i from both parents; but a single observation does not establish dominance, which requires studying many such crosses.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between acquired and inherited traits, with one example of each.
    Ans: An acquired trait develops during an organism's lifetime due to experience or environment and is not passed on — for example, a wrestler's well-developed muscles or a pierced ear. An inherited trait is present from birth and is passed on through the DNA of germ cells — for example, the colour of eyes or free ear lobes.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between genotype and phenotype with an example.
    Ans: Genotype is the genetic constitution of an organism (for example Tt), while phenotype is the observable character it produces (for example a tall plant). Because the tall allele is dominant, the genotypes TT and Tt both give the tall phenotype.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] Explain Mendel's monohybrid cross with a Punnett square.
    Ans:
      Pure tall (TT) × pure dwarf (tt). Gametes: T and t. F1: all Tt — tall, because tall is dominant and dwarf is recessive.
      Selfing F1 (Tt × Tt) gives gametes T and t from each parent. Punnett square → TT, Tt, Tt, tt.
      Phenotype: 3 tall : 1 dwarf. Genotype: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. The dwarf character was only masked in F1, not lost.

Q6. [Short · 3 marks] How is the sex of a child determined in human beings? Show diagrammatically.
    Ans:
      Mother (XX) produces only X-bearing eggs; father (XY) produces X-bearing and Y-bearing sperms in equal numbers.
      X egg + X sperm → XX = girl; X egg + Y sperm → XY = boy.
      The probability of either is 50%, so the sex of the child is decided by chance and not by either parent's wish or action.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Heredity” (Mendel's monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, and sex determination).     Ans:

Q8. [Application · 3 marks] Amit is given this exam-style task from “Heredity”: “What is the sex chromosome combination in a human male, and what is the functional unit of inheritance?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.
    Ans:

      XY; the gene (a segment of DNA on a chromosome).

Q9. [Short · 3 marks] A tall pea plant (Tt) is crossed with a dwarf plant (tt). What proportion of the progeny will be tall?
    Ans:
      The tall parent produces two kinds of gametes, T and t; the dwarf parent produces only t.
      Combining them gives Tt, Tt, tt and tt.
      So half the progeny are Tt (tall) and half are tt (dwarf) — a 1 : 1 ratio. This cross with a recessive parent is called a test cross.

Q12. [Application · 4 marks] A family blames the mother whenever a daughter is born. Using what you know about sex determination, write the reply you would give them.
    Ans:
      Human females have two X chromosomes, so every egg carries an X chromosome.
      Human males have one X and one Y, so half their sperm carry X and half carry Y.
      An X-bearing sperm fertilising the egg gives XX, a girl; a Y-bearing sperm gives XY, a boy. The egg always contributes an X, so it is the sperm — that is, the father — that decides the sex of the child.
      Repeated daughters in a family are a matter of chance, exactly like repeated tails when a coin is tossed; there is no biological basis for blaming the mother.

Q13. [Long · 5 marks] Describe Mendel's dihybrid cross and state the conclusion drawn from it.
    Ans:
      He crossed pea plants with round-yellow seeds (RRYY) with plants having wrinkled-green seeds (rryy).
      F1: all plants had round-yellow seeds (RrYy) — round and yellow are dominant.
      On self-pollinating F1, F2 gave four phenotypes: 9 round-yellow : 3 round-green : 3 wrinkled-yellow : 1 wrinkled-green (16 combinations in the 4×4 Punnett square).
      Conclusion: the two pairs of characters are inherited independently of each other, which is why new combinations (round-green and wrinkled-green) appear in F2 that were absent in the parents. This is the law of independent assortment.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. In a monohybrid cross between a tall (TT) and a dwarf (tt) pea plant, the F2 phenotypic ratio is: (a) 1:1 (b) 3:1 (c) 9:3:3:1 (d) 1:2:1
    Ans: (b) 3 tall : 1 dwarf. 1:2:1 is the genotypic ratio of the same cross.

M2. The sex of a human child is determined by: (a) the mother's egg (b) the father's sperm (c) both parents equally (d) the diet of the mother
    Ans: (b) the father's sperm. Eggs always carry X; a sperm carrying Y produces a boy and one carrying X produces a girl.

M3. A cross between F1 hybrid tall plants (Tt × Tt) gives dwarf plants in F2. The expected ratio of tall to dwarf is: (a) 1:3 (b) 3:1 (c) 1:1 (d) 2:2
    Ans: (b) 3:1 — 25% of F2 are homozygous dwarf (tt).

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Heredity” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Mendel's monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, and sex determination.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Heredity” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The phenotypic ratio obtained in a dihybrid cross between two F₁ hybrids is: (a) 3 : 1  (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1  (c) 1 : 1  (d) 1 : 2 : 1
    Ans: (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Mendel obtained this for two independently assorting traits, such as seed shape and seed colour.

M7. Which of these traits can be inherited? (a) the cut tail of a mouse  (b) large muscles built by exercise  (c) eye colour  (d) a scar
    Ans: (c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.

M8. The number of chromosomes in a human zygote is: (a) 23  (b) 46  (c) 92  (d) 22
    Ans: (b) 46. Each gamete carries 23 chromosomes and their fusion restores the 23 pairs.
