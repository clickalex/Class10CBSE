A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.
    Ans: Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.
    Ans: Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between stress and a stressor.
    Ans: Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is self-regulation, and how does it differ from self-motivation? Give one example of each.
    Ans:
      Self-regulation is controlling your emotions and behaviour to stay on track — for example, closing social media during study hours despite the urge to scroll.
      Self-motivation is generating your own drive to start and continue — for example, setting a personal target of finishing ten Calc rows before lunch.
      In short: motivation starts the engine, regulation keeps it on the road.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Meera has three tasks: a 5 pm file, a messy desk, and a social-media reel. Using urgent/important, what should she do first and why?
    Ans: The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Self-Management Skills–II” (Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Suggest three ways of managing stress and building self-motivation.
    Ans:
      Plan the day, break big tasks into small ones and do the hardest first, so that the work never feels endless.
      Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
      Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Explain the four steps of time management (list → prioritise → one chunk → review) with a study-evening example.
    Ans:
      List — write down everything for the evening: finish the Writer practical, revise ten formulas, pack the bag.
      Prioritise — rank by urgent/important: the practical due tomorrow comes first, packing the bag last.
      One chunk — work on the top task in a single unbroken block instead of switching every ten minutes.
      Review — at the end, tick off what is done and carry only the remainder forward.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.
    Ans: Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    Ravi’s hands shake before every practical, he skips breakfast and then makes typing errors.
    
    (a) Name the type of stress.
    (b) List four management steps.
    (c) Write one SMART goal for his next practical.
    Ans:
      (a) Distress — it is harming performance.
      (b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.
      (c) “Complete the Base table of 10 records with primary key set, in 25 minutes, with zero errors, on Friday.”

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A Class 10 student is preparing “Self-Management Skills–II”. Their note says: Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision.
    
    The chapter is described as: Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.
    
    (a) Name the key idea.
    (b) How is this usually asked in the paper?
    (c) State one mistake that would cost a mark.
    Ans:
      (a) Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.
      (b) marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
      (c) Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Self-Management Skills–II” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:
      Self-management is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour and to work towards goals without constant supervision.
      Marks lens: marks. Stress symptoms, SMART goals and time-management steps are the usual asks.
      Method / memory trick: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
      Do not: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. SMART: the M stands for: (a) Magical (b) Measurable (c) Maximum (d) Monthly
    Ans: (b) Measurable.

M2. A hobby after study is mainly: (a) a waste (b) a stress-management method (c) self-employment (d) a barrier
    Ans: (b) a stress-management method.

M3. Knowing your own strengths is: (a) self-awareness (b) encoding (c) a physical barrier (d) green skill
    Ans: (a) self-awareness.

M4. Which goal is SMART? (a) I will study more (b) I will type 40 correct rows in Calc by 3 pm (c) I will become famous (d) I will try
    Ans: (b).

M5. The most exam-ready way to revise “Self-Management Skills–II” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals.

M6. A 3-mark answer on “Self-Management Skills–II” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M7. Self-awareness means: (a) knowing your own strengths, weaknesses and feelings  (b) only knowing other people  (c) working without help  (d) following orders
    Ans: (a) knowing your own strengths, weaknesses and feelings. It is the first step of self-management.

M8. The useful stress that pushes you to perform better is called: (a) eustress  (b) distress  (c) burnout  (d) depression
    Ans: (a) eustress. Distress is the harmful kind that lowers performance and health.
