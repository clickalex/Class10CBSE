A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] List four steps of evacuation in the correct order.
    Ans: Hear the alarm → leave via stairs (not lift) → go to the assembly point → answer the headcount and wait for the all-clear.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Why report a near-miss?
    Ans: A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is an emergency, and what should a workplace emergency plan contain?
    Ans: An emergency is an unexpected event that threatens life, health or property, such as a fire, an earthquake, a chemical spill or a sudden illness. A plan should name the escape routes and the assembly point, the person responsible for each task, the emergency telephone numbers, the location of first-aid kits and extinguishers, and how often evacuation drills will be held.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is the difference between an accident and a near-miss? Why must both be reported?
    Ans: An accident is an event that causes harm — for example, a shock from a damaged cable. A near-miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not — for example, spotting the same damaged cable before anyone touched it. Both must be reported because the near-miss reveals the hazard while there is still time to fix it.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] The fire alarm rings. A student runs back for a USB and takes the lift. Two wrong actions and the correct ones.
    Ans: Going back for belongings and using the lift. Correct: leave immediately by stairs, USB can be replaced, assemble for headcount.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] A student says that toothpaste or ice should be put on a minor burn. Correct this and give the right first aid.
    Ans:
      Toothpaste, butter and ice must not be put on a burn; they can damage the skin or cause infection.
      Hold the burn under cool running water for about 10–20 minutes.
      Then cover it loosely with a clean, non-sticky dressing, do not burst any blisters, and get medical help if the burn is large or deep.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the first aid for a minor burn and for a person who has fainted.
    Ans:
      For a minor burn: hold the area under cool running water for about ten minutes, remove rings or tight clothing near it, cover it with a clean, non-sticky cloth, and do not apply ice, oil or ointment and do not break blisters.
      For fainting: lay the person flat on their back, raise the legs above the level of the heart, loosen tight clothing and keep the airway clear.
      Do not crowd around, do not throw water on the face and do not give anything to drink until the person is fully conscious.
      Call for medical help if the person does not recover within a minute or if breathing is not normal.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] What should a workplace emergency plan contain? List its essential parts with one line on each.
    Ans:
      Alarm and exits — how the alarm sounds and which stairs and exits to use (never the lift).
      Assembly and headcount — where to gather outside and who checks that everyone is out.
      First aid and numbers — where the kit is and the emergency number 112 for fire, police and ambulance.
      Roles and reporting — who leads the evacuation and how every incident and near-miss is written up afterwards.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain first aid for (i) a small cut (ii) a minor burn (iii) suspected electric shock.
    Ans: Cut: wash, pressure, clean dressing. Burn: cool with running water 10 minutes, loose sterile cover, no toothpaste/ice. Shock: do not touch until power is off; then separate with a dry non-conductor, call help, monitor breathing.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    During a practical a student gets a shock from a damaged mouse cable, laughs it off, and no one writes it down.
    
    (a) Immediate first actions.
    (b) Why a report still matters.
    (c) Two prevention steps so it does not recur.
    Ans:
      (a) Switch off power, check breathing, first aid, inform the teacher.
      (b) Near-miss/incident record → cable replaced, others warned, pattern spotted.
      (c) Inspect peripherals, remove damaged devices, remind: report all shocks.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    During a practical, smoke rises from a UPS in the lab.
    
    (a) What should the teacher and students do first?
    (b) Which emergency numbers can be called?
    (c) How should the room be left?
    Ans:
      (a) Raise the alarm and, if it is safe, switch off the main power supply; do not use water on electrical equipment.
      (b) 101 for the fire service, or 112, the national emergency number (100 for police and 102 for an ambulance).
      (c) Everyone leaves calmly by the stairs, not the lift, and gathers at the assembly point, where the teacher takes a head count.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] List five items of a first-aid box and five rules that a first-aider should follow.
    Ans:
      Items: sterile gauze and bandages, adhesive plasters, antiseptic solution, scissors, and disposable gloves (also cotton wool and a burn dressing).
      Rule 1: first make sure the area is safe, for example switch off the power in case of electric shock.
      Rule 2: call for help and the emergency number quickly.
      Rule 3: do not move a seriously injured person unless there is danger, and reassure them.
      Rule 4: wear gloves and wash hands; do not give food or drink to an unconscious person.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. During a fire, do not: (a) use stairs (b) go to the assembly point (c) use the lift (d) follow the teacher
    Ans: (c) use the lift.

M2. For electric shock, first: (a) grab the person (b) switch off power (c) pour water (d) apply toothpaste
    Ans: (b) switch off power.

M3. A near-miss should be: (a) ignored (b) reported (c) punished always (d) posted on social media
    Ans: (b) reported.

M4. A minor burn is cooled with: (a) ice cubes rubbed hard (b) running water (c) toothpaste (d) cotton oil
    Ans: (b) running water.

M5. In India, the single national emergency number is: (a) 108 only (b) 112 (c) 1800 (d) 911
    Ans: (b) 112. 112 is the all-in-one emergency number; 101 is for fire, 100 for police and 102 for ambulance.

M6. During a fire drill, people should leave the building using the: (a) lift (b) windows (c) back of the lab (d) stairs
    Ans: (d) stairs. Lifts may stop working or trap people in a fire, so the stairs are used.

M7. The single all-India emergency number for police, fire and ambulance is: (a) 112  (b) 100  (c) 101  (d) 108
    Ans: (a) 112. 100 is police, 101 is fire and 108 is the ambulance service in most states.

M8. During an evacuation you should: (a) walk quickly to the assembly point without using the lift  (b) run and push past others  (c) use the lift to leave faster  (d) go back for your bag
    Ans: (a) walk quickly to the assembly point without using the lift. Report to the assembly point so that everyone can be counted.
