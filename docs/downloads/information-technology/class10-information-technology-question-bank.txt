CHAPTER 1 · Communication Skills–II
Unit: Part A · Employability Skills
========================================================================

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Enlist the different methods of communication.
    Ans:  (1) Verbal — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report). (2) Non-verbal — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone. (3) Visual — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Which is the easiest method of communication and why?
    Ans: Non-verbal is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally and work across languages.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?
    Ans: Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.
    Ans: (1) Clear — simple, exact message (“Submit the file by Friday 5 pm”). (2) Courteous — polite words (“please”, “thank you”, “sorry for the delay”).

Q5. [Short · 2 marks] Name any four barriers to effective communication.
    Ans: Physical barriers such as noise or distance; language barriers such as jargon or an unfamiliar accent; psychological barriers such as anger, fear or prejudice; and cultural barriers, where the same word or gesture means different things. Poor listening and the absence of feedback make all of them worse.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya messages her team “do it soon”. Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.
    Ans: Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: “Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email.” — now clear, complete and courteous.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] A team leader’s juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?
    Ans: (1) Replace jargon with simple words and show a chart (linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask “any doubts?”, and paraphrase replies (feedback + active listening).

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] What are the 7 Cs of effective communication? Explain any three.
    Ans:
      The seven Cs are clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous.
      Clear: say what you mean in words the listener understands, and avoid jargon.
      Concise: use only as many words as the message needs, so the point is not lost.
      Complete: give all the facts the receiver needs, including who, what, when and where, so that no further questions are needed.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.
    Ans:
      Verbal = communication using words, in two forms.
      Oral (speech, phone, meeting): fast, instant feedback; no permanent record.
      Written (letter, email, report): permanent proof, re-readable, formal; slower, needs literacy.
      Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written when a record is needed.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Explain any four barriers to communication with one example and one measure each.
    Ans:
      (1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic.
      (2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals.
      (3) Cultural — a thumbs-up is rude in some places → learn customs.
      (4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, choose the right time.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says “no questions, I am getting late”. The manager understands nothing.
    
    (a) Identify four communication mistakes.
    (b) Suggest four fixes.
    Ans:
      (a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback).
      (b) Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, one idea each • slow, clear speech • invite questions and summarise.

Q12. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    For the school fest, Class X puts up posters at shops, announces on a loudspeaker, and the principal sends a written notice to parents.
    
    (a) Name the three methods used.
    (b) Why is combining them better than one?
    Ans:
      (a) Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral/verbal; notice = written.
      (b) Visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together they cover more audiences with both speed and a record.

B. MCQs (10)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. Which part of communication converts an idea into words or signs? (a) Receiving (b) Encoding (c) Decoding (d) Feedback
    Ans: (b) Encoding. Encoding prepares the sender's idea as a message; decoding interprets it.

M2. A loud generator prevents a worker from hearing instructions. The barrier is: (a) linguistic (b) cultural (c) physical (d) emotional
    Ans: (c) Physical — environmental noise blocks the message.

M3. Which of the 7 Cs means “to the point, no extra words”? (a) Clear (b) Concise (c) Concrete (d) Courteous
    Ans: (b) Concise.

M4. “An honest boy” takes which article? (a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
    Ans: (b) an — use an before a vowel sound; h is silent in honest.

M5. A student's puzzled expression after an explanation is: (a) written encoding (b) non-verbal feedback (c) a channel (d) a language rule
    Ans: (b) Non-verbal feedback.

M6. Which sentence is complete? (a) Do that work. (b) The form is important. (c) Submit the signed form to Room 4 by Friday noon. (d) Submit it soon.
    Ans: (c) — task, place and deadline are all present.

M7. Paralanguage refers to: (a) body posture only (b) charts and maps (c) tone, pitch and speed of voice (d) grammar rules
    Ans: (c) Tone, pitch and speed of voice.

M8. The easiest method of communication is: (a) written (b) oral (c) non-verbal (d) email
    Ans: (c) Non-verbal — needs no literacy.

M9. The communication process is complete only when: (a) the receiver gives feedback  (b) the message is written  (c) the sender speaks  (d) the channel is chosen
    Ans: (a) the receiver gives feedback. Feedback tells the sender whether the message was understood.

M10. Which method of communication carries no body language or tone of voice? (a) written communication  (b) face-to-face talk  (c) video call  (d) group discussion
    Ans: (a) written communication. That is why written messages must be especially clear.

Unit: Part A · Employability Skills

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] What is self-management? Give two of its benefits.
    Ans: Controlling one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour to reach goals. Benefits: better work quality, less stress, meeting deadlines, less need for supervision.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Differentiate eustress and distress with one example each.
    Ans: Eustress is helpful pressure — a timed practical that makes you focus. Distress is harmful — so much overtime that errors increase and sleep is lost.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between stress and a stressor.
    Ans: Stress is the way the body and mind react to pressure, while a stressor is the event or situation that triggers it. An examination, a deadline, a family problem or a noisy workplace can each be a stressor; the resulting worry, headache or loss of sleep is the stress.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is self-regulation, and how does it differ from self-motivation? Give one example of each.
    Ans:
      Self-regulation is controlling your emotions and behaviour to stay on track — for example, closing social media during study hours despite the urge to scroll.
      Self-motivation is generating your own drive to start and continue — for example, setting a personal target of finishing ten Calc rows before lunch.
      In short: motivation starts the engine, regulation keeps it on the road.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Meera has three tasks: a 5 pm file, a messy desk, and a social-media reel. Using urgent/important, what should she do first and why?
    Ans: The 5 pm file is urgent and important — do it first. The desk is important but not urgent — schedule it. The reel is neither — drop it until the file is in.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Self-Management Skills–II” (Stress, self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation, time and SMART goals). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: SMART → Specific Measurable Achievable Realistic Time-bound.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Suggest three ways of managing stress and building self-motivation.
    Ans:
      Plan the day, break big tasks into small ones and do the hardest first, so that the work never feels endless.
      Look after the body: sleep enough, exercise, breathe deeply and eat regularly, because a tired body exaggerates every problem.
      Set specific goals, track them, reward yourself when a goal is met, and talk to someone you trust instead of keeping worries inside.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Explain the four steps of time management (list → prioritise → one chunk → review) with a study-evening example.
    Ans:
      List — write down everything for the evening: finish the Writer practical, revise ten formulas, pack the bag.
      Prioritise — rank by urgent/important: the practical due tomorrow comes first, packing the bag last.
      One chunk — work on the top task in a single unbroken block instead of switching every ten minutes.
      Review — at the end, tick off what is done and carry only the remainder forward.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain SMART goals with a data-entry example.
    Ans: Specific (which file), Measurable (how many records), Achievable (within skill), Realistic (hours available), Time-bound (by 5 pm). Example: “Enter 80 customer rows in Calc with zero blanks by 4 pm today.”

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    (a) Name the type of stress.
    (b) List four management steps.

      (a) Distress — it is harming performance.
      (b) Breakfast, 4–7 breathing, a 5-minute practice file, a checklist, sleep 7 hours.
      (c) “Complete the Base table of 10 records with primary key set, in 25 minutes, with zero errors, on Friday.”

      (c) Writing SMART expansions in the wrong order.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Self-Management Skills–II” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] What is an operating system? Give two examples.
    Ans: System software that manages hardware and runs programs. Examples: Windows, Linux, Android.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Differentiate a file and a folder with one example each.
    Ans: A file stores data (marks.ods). A folder stores files and other folders (Class10/IT/).

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is an operating system? Name two examples.
    Ans: An operating system is the system software that manages the computer's memory, processor, disks and devices and lets the user run application programs. Examples are Windows, Linux, macOS and Android.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is the Recycle Bin, and how is restoring a file different from deleting it permanently?
    Ans: The Recycle Bin is a holding area where deleted files wait until it is emptied. Restoring returns the file to its original folder, so nothing is lost. Deleting permanently (emptying the Bin, or Shift + Delete) removes the file beyond easy recovery — which is why important files should be backed up before the Bin is emptied.

Q5. [Short · 2 marks] List four basic care rules that protect a computer from physical damage and data loss.
    Ans:
      Keep the machine dust-free and away from food and drink, which damage keyboards and circuits.
      Use a surge protector so voltage spikes do not harm the hardware.
      Shut down through the OS command instead of pulling the plug, which can corrupt files.
      Run updated antivirus software and keep a backup of important work.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Amit eats chips over the keyboard and never shuts the PC down. List three risks and three care steps.
    Ans: Risks: crumbs jam keys, oil damage, overheating, data loss. Care: no food/drink, proper shutdown, dust-free vents, surge protector, backups.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “ICT Skills–II” (Operating system, files and folders, and basic care of a computer). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Calling Writer an operating system. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: File has an extension; a folder does not need one.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Calling Writer an operating system.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] How would you organise files and folders so that your work is always easy to find?
    Ans:
      Make one folder for each subject or project, and inside it one folder per term or per type of work.
      Give every file a short, descriptive name with the date, such as maths_notes_2026-08-12.
      Keep the folder tree shallow — no more than two or three levels — and delete or archive old versions.
      Keep a backup copy on an external drive or in the cloud, and run an antivirus scan on anything you download.

Q9. [Short · 3 marks] Why should files be given clear names and kept in subject-wise folders? Illustrate with a school-project example.
    Ans:
      Clear names and folders make work findable: a file named aaa.odt on a crowded Desktop is easily lost or overwritten.
      Example — a project split as Project/notes.odt, Project/images/, Project/final-report.odt lets any part be found in seconds.
      Good organisation also protects against loss: one folder is easy to back up, scattered files are not.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.
    Ans: A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] State two myths about entrepreneurship and correct them.
    Ans: (1) “Born, not made” — skills can be learned. (2) “Needs huge capital” — many start with a small idea and grow.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] List any four qualities an entrepreneur needs.
    Ans: Self-confidence, the willingness to take calculated risks, initiative and perseverance, the ability to plan and organise, and the readiness to learn from failure and try again.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a social entrepreneur? How does the aim differ from that of a business entrepreneur?
    Ans: A social entrepreneur starts an enterprise mainly to solve a social problem — for example, cheap solar lamps for villages — rather than to maximise profit. A business entrepreneur also creates value but measures success primarily in profit and growth. Both take risks and organise resources; they differ in what counts as the return.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Neha notices students waiting 40 minutes for a shared printer. She starts a paid print-and-bind desk. Which entrepreneurial qualities is she using?
    Ans: Opportunity-spotting, initiative, risk-taking (she invests in a printer), problem-solving and customer focus.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Entrepreneurial Skills–II” (Who an entrepreneur is, qualities, functions, myths and role in society). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Calling every shopkeeper an entrepreneur without the risk/innovation idea. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Quality vs function: quality is a trait (perseverance); function is a job (arrange capital).
      Why the mistake costs marks: Calling every shopkeeper an entrepreneur without the risk/innovation idea.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What functions does an entrepreneur perform in running an enterprise?
    Ans:
      Spotting an opportunity and turning it into a product or service people will pay for.
      Arranging the money, buying raw materials, machines and premises, and hiring and training workers.
      Organising production, keeping accounts and marketing the product.
      Bearing the risk of loss, and taking the decisions that keep the enterprise alive.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Explain the role of entrepreneurs in society with reference to jobs, products and local resources.
    Ans:
      Jobs — every new enterprise hires people, from a bakery's helpers to a startup's designers.
      Products — entrepreneurs turn needs into goods and services, such as affordable school stationery or repair shops.
      Local resources — they use nearby material and talent, for example fruit from local orchards in a jam unit, keeping wealth in the area.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain four functions of an entrepreneur with a bakery example.
    Ans: Identify demand for fresh bread; arrange shop, oven and staff; take the risk of unsold stock; market via samples and a board; keep accounts. Each function maps to one quality: opportunity-spotting, organising, risk-taking, selling.

CHAPTER 5 · Green Skills–II
Unit: Part A · Employability Skills
========================================================================

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 1 mark] Define sustainable development.
    Ans: Development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] What are green skills? Give two examples.
    Ans: Skills that help a person live and work in an environmentally friendly way. Examples: waste segregation, saving electricity, water conservation.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is sustainable development?
    Ans: It is development that meets the needs of the present without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs — using resources carefully, keeping pollution low and protecting ecosystems while the economy grows.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a green economy? State its three features.
    Ans: A green economy is one that improves human well-being while reducing environmental risks. Its three features are: low carbon emissions, efficient use of resources, and inclusive growth that does not leave the poor behind.

Q5. [Short · 2 marks] Arrange Reduce, Reuse, Recycle in order of preference and justify the order with one example each.
    Ans:
      First Reduce — preventing waste is best: carry a cloth bag instead of taking a plastic one each time.
      Then Reuse — using again costs nothing extra: refill the same water bottle for months.
      Last Recycle — reprocessing still needs energy: send worn-out notebooks for paper recycling only when reuse is over.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] A computer lab leaves 20 PCs on overnight. Suggest three green practices and one green skill the lab assistant needs.
    Ans: Shut down PCs, use sleep mode, switch off AC/lights, enable duplex printing. Skill: energy-conservation habit / e-waste handling.

Q7. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Green Skills–II” (Sustainable development, green economy and green jobs). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Defining sustainable development as “using no resources”. It is wise use, not zero use. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] What is Goal Seek in Calc?
    Ans: A what-if tool: you set the result you want in a formula cell and Calc finds the input value that produces it.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] What is a scenario?
    Ans: A named set of values for specified cells so you can switch between cases (best/worst) without retyping.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is the difference between a scenario and Goal Seek?
    Ans: A scenario is a named set of input values that you save and switch between to compare outcomes, such as best case, worst case and expected case. Goal Seek works the other way: you state the result you want and Calc finds the single input value that produces it.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is Solver in Calc, and how does it differ from Goal Seek?
    Ans: Solver finds input values that produce a target result, like Goal Seek — but it can change many cells at once and respect constraints (for example, quantities that must stay whole numbers). Goal Seek changes exactly one cell with no constraints. Use Goal Seek for one unknown, Solver for many.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A shop wants profit of ₹8,000. Profit is =B2*B3-B4 (price × qty − cost). Which tool, and which cell is “By changing” if qty and cost are fixed?
    Ans:
      Use Goal Seek (Tools → Goal Seek). Set cell = the profit cell, To value = 8000, and By changing cell = the price cell B2.
      Goal Seek works backwards: instead of asking what the profit is, it asks what input would produce the profit you want, and it tries values until the formula cell reaches 8000.
      Only one input cell can be changed, so the quantity and the cost must stay fixed while the price is adjusted.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Scenarios and Goal Seek (Calc)” (Data consolidation, subtotals, what-if scenarios, Goal Seek and Solver). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Mixing up Goal Seek (one input) with Solver (many inputs + constraints). Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Goal Seek needs a formula in the set cell. Typing a number there will fail.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Mixing up Goal Seek (one input) with Solver (many inputs + constraints).
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Describe how to use Goal Seek in Calc, with an example.
    Ans:
      Build the sheet so that the result cell contains a formula, for example the EMI for a loan in B5.
      Choose Tools → Goal Seek and fill in the dialog: Formula Cell B5, Target Value 8000, Variable Cell B2 (the interest rate or the loan amount).
      Click OK; Calc changes the variable cell step by step until the formula gives the target, and reports whether it succeeded.
      This is quicker than trial and error when only one input is unknown.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] What is the ‘Group’ feature in Calc? Explain with a monthly-expense sheet example.
    Ans:
      Group (Data → Define Range → Group) bundles rows or columns so they can be collapsed to a single line or expanded again with one click.
      Example — a yearly sheet with twelve monthly blocks: group each month's daily rows, collapse all twelve, and only the monthly totals stay visible for review.
      Unlike hiding, grouping shows a clear plus/minus control, so hidden detail is never forgotten.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Differentiate Consolidate and Subtotals.
    Ans: Consolidate combines several ranges/sheets into one summary (often another sheet). Subtotals insert summary rows inside one sorted list (sum of each group). Consolidate is across blocks; Subtotals is inside a table.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Differentiate data and information with an example.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] What is a primary key? Give one example.
    Ans: A field (or set of fields) that uniquely identifies each record and cannot be empty. Example: AdmissionNo in a Student table.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] Distinguish between data and information with an example.
    Ans: Data are raw, unprocessed facts, such as the marks obtained by each student in each subject. Information is data that has been processed and given meaning, such as the class average, the highest mark or a list of students who passed. A database stores data; queries and reports turn it into information.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a foreign key? Explain with a Student–Library example how it links two tables.
    Ans: A foreign key is a field in one table that refers to the primary key of another table. Example — the Issue table stores RollNo as a foreign key pointing to the Student table's RollNo primary key, so each issued book is tied to exactly one student without retyping the student's name and class.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A school stores students in one sheet with teacher phone numbers typed against every student. Two problems, and the DBMS idea that fixes them.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] List any four advantages of a DBMS over a simple file system.
    Ans:

    Ans:

      Table — the full set of records of one kind, such as the Student table holding every student's row.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] List four advantages of a DBMS over a paper register or a single Calc sheet.
    Ans: Less duplication; one correction updates all uses; password/security; backup and recovery; many users; validation (integrity). A Calc sheet is easy to duplicate and hard to relate as tables grow.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] What is a one-to-many relationship? Give an example.
    Ans: One record in table A matches many in table B. One student, many library issues. The Issue table stores AdmNo as FK.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] What is referential integrity?
    Ans: A rule that every foreign-key value must match an existing primary key, so you cannot create orphan records.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is referential integrity and why is it useful?
    Ans: Referential integrity is the rule that every value of a foreign key must match a value of the primary key in the related table, or be empty. It prevents ‘orphan' records — for example, a fee record belonging to a student who is not in the student table — and keeps the database reliable.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a many-to-many relationship? Explain why it needs a junction table, with an example.
    Ans: Many-to-many means rows on both sides link to many rows on the other — for example, each student borrows many books and each book is borrowed by many students. Neither table can hold the other's key directly, so a junction table (Issue: IssueID, RollNo, BookID, Date) stores each pairing as its own record.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A shop has Customer and Order tables. Where does CustomerID go, and what relationship is this?
    Ans: CustomerID is PK in Customer and FK in Order. One-to-many (one customer, many orders).

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Relationships and Multiple Tables” (One-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many; referential integrity). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Drawing the relationship backwards (PK on the many side). Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: FK type must match PK type (both Integer, or both VARCHAR).
      Why the mistake costs marks: Drawing the relationship backwards (PK on the many side).
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Explain the three types of relationship with an example of each.
    Ans:
      One-to-one: one record in the first table matches one in the second, for example one student and one library card.
      One-to-many: one record in the first table matches many in the second, for example one class has many students.
      Many-to-many: records on both sides match many on the other, for example students and subjects; this is usually split into two one-to-many relationships through a third table.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] What is ‘cascade delete’? Describe one situation where it is useful and one where it is dangerous.
    Ans:
      Cascade delete automatically deletes child records when their parent is deleted.
      Useful — removing a passed-out batch's students together with their old fee rows in one step.
      Dangerous — deleting one supplier and silently wiping years of purchase history that accounts still needs. Prefer blocking the delete (referential integrity) unless you are certain the children are disposable.

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Differentiate a hazard and an accident with an IT-lab example.
    Ans: A hazard is a potential source of harm (a loose cable across the aisle). An accident is the event (a student trips and the monitor falls).

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] List four electrical safety rules for a computer lab.
    Ans: Dry hands; earthed plugs; no overloaded boards; don’t open the power supply; report frayed wires; use a surge protector.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is a hazard? Give two examples from a computer laboratory.
    Ans: A hazard is anything in the workplace that can cause injury or ill health. In a computer laboratory the hazards include a trailing cable that can trip someone, a socket or adapter that is overloaded and can start a fire, a wet floor, and a chair with a broken back that can cause a fall.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is the difference between a hazard and a risk? Give one IT-lab example of each.
    Ans:
      A hazard is anything that can cause harm — for example, a trailing cable across the lab floor.
      Risk is the chance that the harm actually happens — for example, the high risk that someone hurrying between PCs trips over that cable.
      Safety work removes hazards first, then reduces the remaining risk with guards, rules and training.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] A student pours water on a sparking socket. Two mistakes and the correct actions.
    Ans: Water on electrical fire; staying next to a live spark. Instead: don’t touch, switch off mains if safe, alert staff, use CO2/powder extinguisher, evacuate.

    Ans:

      Why the mistake costs marks: Writing “be careful” with no concrete precaution.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] List four fire safety rules for a workplace.
    Ans:
      Keep every fire exit and aisle clear of boxes, cables and furniture, and make sure the exit signs are lit.
      Check that fire extinguishers are in place, visible and within their expiry date, and know which type to use for an electrical fire.
      Switch off and unplug equipment at the end of the day, and never overload a socket with adapters.
      If a fire starts, raise the alarm, cut off the power if it is safe to do so, leave by the marked route without using the lift, and go to the assembly point.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] List four data-security measures for a school computer lab and explain the purpose of each.
    Ans:
      Unique passwords per user — so every action can be traced to its author.
      Authorised access only — outsiders cannot reach student records or exam files.
      Regular backups — a failed disk or ransomware cannot destroy the only copy of practical files.
      Locked server and storeroom — physical theft of drives is blocked at the door.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain four types of workplace hazard with one IT-lab example each.
    Ans: Physical — trailing cables; electrical — open socket; ergonomic — low screen causing neck pain; psychosocial — long hours without a break. Control each: cable covers, close sockets, raise the monitor, timetable breaks.

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] What is ergonomics? Give two workstation rules.
    Ans: Designing work to fit the person. Rules: screen at eye level, feet flat, back supported, wrists straight, mouse beside the keyboard.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Expand 5S.
    Ans: 5S = Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardise, Sustain. Sort removes what is not needed; Set in order gives everything a fixed place; Shine means cleaning as you go; Standardise writes the method down so anyone can follow it; Sustain makes it a daily habit. The first three clean the workplace, the last two keep it clean.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is ergonomics and why does it matter when working at a computer?
    Ans: Ergonomics is the science of designing the workplace to fit the person. At a computer it keeps the body comfortable and prevents backache, neck and shoulder pain, eye strain and repetitive strain injury caused by hours of sitting in a wrong posture with badly placed equipment.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What are the four features of quality work in IT? Illustrate with a data-entry task.
    Ans:
      Accurate — every figure matches the source; for example, marks typed without a single digit error.
      Complete — nothing is missing; every student's row is filled.
      On time — the file is ready before the deadline, not after.
      Checked — a second pass (spell-check, totals re-added) proves the first three.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Amit’s laptop is on a high table, he looks up, and his wrists bend on the edge. Two injuries this invites and two fixes.
    Ans: Neck strain and wrist RSI. Fixes: raise the chair or lower the desk, laptop stand + external keyboard, wrist straight, breaks.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Workplace Quality and Ergonomics” (Quality of work, 5S, housekeeping, ergonomic posture and workstation setup). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Drawing the user hunched over a laptop as “correct posture”. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Screen top at eye level, not looking down at a laptop on the lap.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Drawing the user hunched over a laptop as “correct posture”.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Describe a correct workstation arrangement and the working habit that prevents strain.
    Ans:
      Sit back in a chair that supports the lower back, with both feet flat on the floor and knees at about a right angle.
      Keep the top of the screen at or just below eye level, about an arm's length away, with the keyboard and mouse close enough to keep the forearms horizontal and the wrists straight.
      Place the document you are copying beside the screen rather than flat on the desk, so the neck does not keep turning.
      Follow the 20-20-20 habit: every 20 minutes look 20 feet away for 20 seconds, and take a short break and stretch every hour.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Explain the 5S method with one computer-lab example for each S.
    Ans:
      Sort — remove what is not needed: discard dried pens and broken CDs from the lab drawers.
      Set in order — give everything a place: label each shelf, cable and software CD.
      Shine — clean regularly: wipe keyboards and screens on a fixed weekly roster.
      Standardise — make it a rule: the same layout and cleaning checklist in every lab.
      Sustain — keep it going: a monthly audit so the lab never slides back.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Describe a correct computer workstation and two quality checks before submitting a practical file.
    Ans: Chair and desk so elbows and knees are about 90°, screen arm’s length, top at eye level, no glare. Quality: proof-read, spell-check, file named as asked, print preview, backup copy.

A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] List four steps of evacuation in the correct order.
    Ans: Hear the alarm → leave via stairs (not lift) → go to the assembly point → answer the headcount and wait for the all-clear.

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] Why report a near-miss?
    Ans: A near-miss shows a hazard that almost caused harm. Reporting it lets the school fix the condition before someone is actually injured.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] What is an emergency, and what should a workplace emergency plan contain?
    Ans: An emergency is an unexpected event that threatens life, health or property, such as a fire, an earthquake, a chemical spill or a sudden illness. A plan should name the escape routes and the assembly point, the person responsible for each task, the emergency telephone numbers, the location of first-aid kits and extinguishers, and how often evacuation drills will be held.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is the difference between an accident and a near-miss? Why must both be reported?
    Ans: An accident is an event that causes harm — for example, a shock from a damaged cable. A near-miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not — for example, spotting the same damaged cable before anyone touched it. Both must be reported because the near-miss reveals the hazard while there is still time to fix it.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] The fire alarm rings. A student runs back for a USB and takes the lift. Two wrong actions and the correct ones.
    Ans: Going back for belongings and using the lift. Correct: leave immediately by stairs, USB can be replaced, assemble for headcount.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Prevent Accidents and Emergencies” (Causes of accidents, evacuation, first aid, emergency numbers and incident reporting). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Power off before touch is the electric-shock line examiners want.
      Why the mistake costs marks: Applying toothpaste or ice directly on a burn.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the first aid for a minor burn and for a person who has fainted.
    Ans:
      For a minor burn: hold the area under cool running water for about ten minutes, remove rings or tight clothing near it, cover it with a clean, non-sticky cloth, and do not apply ice, oil or ointment and do not break blisters.
      For fainting: lay the person flat on their back, raise the legs above the level of the heart, loosen tight clothing and keep the airway clear.
      Do not crowd around, do not throw water on the face and do not give anything to drink until the person is fully conscious.
      Call for medical help if the person does not recover within a minute or if breathing is not normal.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] What should a workplace emergency plan contain? List its essential parts with one line on each.
    Ans:
      Alarm and exits — how the alarm sounds and which stairs and exits to use (never the lift).
      Assembly and headcount — where to gather outside and who checks that everyone is out.
      First aid and numbers — where the kit is and the emergency number 112 for fire, police and ambulance.
      Roles and reporting — who leads the evacuation and how every incident and near-miss is written up afterwards.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain first aid for (i) a small cut (ii) a minor burn (iii) suspected electric shock.
    Ans: Cut: wash, pressure, clean dressing. Burn: cool with running water 10 minutes, loose sterile cover, no toothpaste/ice. Shock: do not touch until power is off; then separate with a dry non-conductor, call help, monitor breathing.
