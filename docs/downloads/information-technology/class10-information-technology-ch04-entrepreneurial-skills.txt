A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.
    Ans: A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] State two myths about entrepreneurship and correct them.
    Ans: (1) “Born, not made” — skills can be learned. (2) “Needs huge capital” — many start with a small idea and grow.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] List any four qualities an entrepreneur needs.
    Ans: Self-confidence, the willingness to take calculated risks, initiative and perseverance, the ability to plan and organise, and the readiness to learn from failure and try again.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a social entrepreneur? How does the aim differ from that of a business entrepreneur?
    Ans: A social entrepreneur starts an enterprise mainly to solve a social problem — for example, cheap solar lamps for villages — rather than to maximise profit. A business entrepreneur also creates value but measures success primarily in profit and growth. Both take risks and organise resources; they differ in what counts as the return.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Neha notices students waiting 40 minutes for a shared printer. She starts a paid print-and-bind desk. Which entrepreneurial qualities is she using?
    Ans: Opportunity-spotting, initiative, risk-taking (she invests in a printer), problem-solving and customer focus.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] Riya is revising “Entrepreneurial Skills–II” (Who an entrepreneur is, qualities, functions, myths and role in society). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Calling every shopkeeper an entrepreneur without the risk/innovation idea. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.
    Ans:
      Correct method: Quality vs function: quality is a trait (perseverance); function is a job (arrange capital).
      Why the mistake costs marks: Calling every shopkeeper an entrepreneur without the risk/innovation idea.
      Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What functions does an entrepreneur perform in running an enterprise?
    Ans:
      Spotting an opportunity and turning it into a product or service people will pay for.
      Arranging the money, buying raw materials, machines and premises, and hiring and training workers.
      Organising production, keeping accounts and marketing the product.
      Bearing the risk of loss, and taking the decisions that keep the enterprise alive.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Explain the role of entrepreneurs in society with reference to jobs, products and local resources.
    Ans:
      Jobs — every new enterprise hires people, from a bakery's helpers to a startup's designers.
      Products — entrepreneurs turn needs into goods and services, such as affordable school stationery or repair shops.
      Local resources — they use nearby material and talent, for example fruit from local orchards in a jam unit, keeping wealth in the area.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain four functions of an entrepreneur with a bakery example.
    Ans: Identify demand for fresh bread; arrange shop, oven and staff; take the risk of unsold stock; market via samples and a board; keep accounts. Each function maps to one quality: opportunity-spotting, organising, risk-taking, selling.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:
    
    A poster says “Only rich, highly educated people can start a business.”
    
    (a) Name the myth.
    (b) Give two counter-examples from daily life.
    (c) State two roles of entrepreneurship in society.
    Ans:
      (a) Myth of huge capital / only the highly educated.
      (b) A tiffin service from a home kitchen; a tuition class in a garage.
      (c) Jobs, new products, local resources used, local problems solved.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Discuss “Entrepreneurial Skills–II” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.
    Ans:

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. An entrepreneur primarily: (a) avoids all risk (b) takes calculated risk to create value (c) only works for a salary (d) copies without a customer
    Ans: (b).

M2. Which is a quality, not a function? (a) arranging capital (b) perseverance (c) hiring staff (d) marketing
    Ans: (b) perseverance.

M3. A social entrepreneur mainly aims to: (a) ignore people (b) solve a social problem, not only maximise profit (c) avoid accounts (d) work without a product
    Ans: (b).

M4. “Entrepreneurs are born, not made” is: (a) a fact (b) a myth (c) a 7 C (d) an OS function
    Ans: (b) a myth.

M5. The most exam-ready way to revise “Entrepreneurial Skills–II” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Who an entrepreneur is, qualities, functions, myths and role in society.

M6. A 3-mark answer on “Entrepreneurial Skills–II” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M7. A person who starts a business, organises it and bears its risks is a/an: (a) entrepreneur  (b) manager  (c) employee  (d) customer
    Ans: (a) entrepreneur. A manager runs someone else's business for a salary.

M8. Which of these is a myth about entrepreneurs? (a) they are born, not made  (b) they take risks  (c) they work hard  (d) they need capital
    Ans: (a) they are born, not made. Entrepreneurial skills such as planning, selling and record-keeping can be learned.
