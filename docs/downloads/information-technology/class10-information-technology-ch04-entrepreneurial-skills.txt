A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Who is an entrepreneur? Give two qualities.
    Ans: A person who spots a need, takes a risk and organises resources to start a venture. Qualities: initiative and perseverance (or creativity, self-confidence).

Q2. [Short · 2 marks] State two myths about entrepreneurship and correct them.
    Ans: (1) “Born, not made” — skills can be learned. (2) “Needs huge capital” — many start with a small idea and grow.

Q3. [Short · 2 marks] List any four qualities an entrepreneur needs.
    Ans: Self-confidence, the willingness to take calculated risks, initiative and perseverance, the ability to plan and organise, and the readiness to learn from failure and try again.

Q4. [Short · 2 marks] What is a social entrepreneur? How does the aim differ from that of a business entrepreneur?
    Ans: A social entrepreneur starts an enterprise mainly to solve a social problem — for example, cheap solar lamps for villages — rather than to maximise profit. A business entrepreneur also creates value but measures success primarily in profit and growth. Both take risks and organise resources; they differ in what counts as the return.

Q5. [Application · 3 marks] Neha notices students waiting 40 minutes for a shared printer. She starts a paid print-and-bind desk. Which entrepreneurial qualities is she using?
    Ans: Opportunity-spotting, initiative, risk-taking (she invests in a printer), problem-solving and customer focus.

Q6. [Application · 3 marks] A student says, “Every shopkeeper is an entrepreneur.” Correct this using the ideas of risk and innovation.
    Ans:
      An entrepreneur starts a new venture, brings something new (a product, service or way of working), organises the resources and bears the risk of loss.
      A shopkeeper who only resells goods in the usual way, with no new idea, is a trader or a small business owner.
      A shopkeeper who starts a new kind of service, such as home delivery that no one offered before, takes a risk and innovates, and so acts as an entrepreneur.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] What functions does an entrepreneur perform in running an enterprise?
    Ans:
      Spotting an opportunity and turning it into a product or service people will pay for.
      Arranging the money, buying raw materials, machines and premises, and hiring and training workers.
      Organising production, keeping accounts and marketing the product.
      Bearing the risk of loss, and taking the decisions that keep the enterprise alive.

Q8. [Long · 3 marks] Explain the role of entrepreneurs in society with reference to jobs, products and local resources.
    Ans:
      Jobs — every new enterprise hires people, from a bakery's helpers to a startup's designers.
      Products — entrepreneurs turn needs into goods and services, such as affordable school stationery or repair shops.
      Local resources — they use nearby material and talent, for example fruit from local orchards in a jam unit, keeping wealth in the area.

Q9. [Long · 4 marks] Explain four functions of an entrepreneur with a bakery example.
    Ans: Identify demand for fresh bread; arrange shop, oven and staff; take the risk of unsold stock; market via samples and a board; keep accounts. Each function maps to one quality: opportunity-spotting, organising, risk-taking, selling.

Q10. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    A poster says “Only rich, highly educated people can start a business.”

    (a) Name the myth.
    (b) Give two counter-examples from daily life.
    (c) State two roles of entrepreneurship in society.
    Ans:
      (a) Myth of huge capital / only the highly educated.
      (b) A tiffin service from a home kitchen; a tuition class in a garage.
      (c) Jobs, new products, local resources used, local problems solved.

Q11. [Competency · 4 marks] Read and answer:

    Amit notices that farmers near his village lose money because vegetables spoil on the way to the city. He buys a van with a cooling unit, collects vegetables directly from farmers, sells them to city shops and hires two local youths.

    (a) Which entrepreneurial qualities does Amit show?
    (b) Name two functions he performs as an entrepreneur.
    (c) How does his enterprise help society?
    Ans:
      (a) He spots a need (opportunity recognition), is innovative in finding a solution and is willing to take a financial risk.
      (b) He organises resources (the van, workers, suppliers) and bears the risk of the business.
      (c) It creates jobs, reduces food wastage and increases the farmers’ income.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Describe five steps in setting up a small enterprise.
    Ans:
      Step 1: generate and evaluate a business idea that meets a real need.
      Step 2: study the market: who the customers are, what they will pay and who the competitors are.
      Step 3: prepare a business plan covering the product, costs, expected sales and profit.
      Step 4: arrange the money (capital) from savings, family or a loan, and choose a suitable location.
      Step 5: complete legal formalities such as registration and licences, then start and review the business regularly.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. An entrepreneur primarily: (a) avoids all risk (b) only works for a salary (c) takes calculated risk to create value (d) copies without a customer
    Ans: (c).

M2. Which is a quality, not a function? (a) perseverance (b) arranging capital (c) hiring staff (d) marketing
    Ans: (a) perseverance.

M3. A social entrepreneur mainly aims to: (a) ignore people (b) avoid accounts (c) work without a product (d) solve a social problem, not only maximise profit
    Ans: (d).

M4. “Entrepreneurs are born, not made” is: (a) a fact (b) a 7 C (c) a myth (d) an OS function
    Ans: (c) a myth.

M5. The first step in starting an enterprise is usually to: (a) register the business (b) find and evaluate a business idea (c) hire employees (d) print the visiting cards
    Ans: (b) find and evaluate a business idea. Everything else depends on having an idea that meets a need and can make a profit.

M6. The money needed to start and run a business is called: (a) capital (b) profit (c) revenue (d) tax
    Ans: (a) capital. Capital is the funds an entrepreneur invests in the enterprise; profit is what remains after costs.

M7. A person who starts a business, organises it and bears its risks is a/an: (a) manager  (b) employee  (c) customer  (d) entrepreneur
    Ans: (d) entrepreneur. A manager runs someone else's business for a salary.

M8. Which of these is a myth about entrepreneurs? (a) they take risks  (b) they are born, not made  (c) they work hard  (d) they need capital
    Ans: (b) they are born, not made. Entrepreneurial skills such as planning, selling and record-keeping can be learned.
