A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (13)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] List the main functions of money.
    Ans: Money works as a medium of exchange, a unit of account (a common measure of value) and a store of value; it also allows deferred payments. Because it removes the need for a double coincidence of wants, it makes trade, specialisation and economic growth possible.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why is money called a medium of exchange?
    Ans: Because it acts as an intermediate step in the exchange of goods and services. In a barter system, exchange requires a double coincidence of wants — each person must want what the other has. Money removes this difficulty: a person can sell his goods for money and use that money to buy whatever he wants, so it mediates between the two parties.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What is collateral and why do banks ask for it?
    Ans: Collateral is an asset that the borrower owns — such as land, a building, a vehicle, livestock or deposits with banks — and uses as a guarantee to a lender until the loan is repaid. Banks ask for it so that if the borrower fails to repay, the bank can sell the asset to recover its money. Because poor households often have no collateral, they are excluded from formal credit.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] Why do most poor households in India still depend on informal sources of credit?
    Ans: Because banks and cooperatives demand collateral and documentation which poor households do not possess, banks are not present in all rural areas, and small borrowers are treated as risky. Moneylenders and traders, by contrast, lend without collateral and are available in the village, even though they charge much higher rates of interest.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] What is the role of the Reserve Bank of India in supervising banks?
    Ans: The RBI supervises the functioning of formal sources of loans. It ensures that banks maintain a minimum cash balance, that they lend not just to profit-making businesses but also to small cultivators, small-scale industries and small borrowers, and that they submit periodical information to the RBI on how much they are lending, to whom and at what interest rate.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Explain how the Reserve Bank of India supervises banks and why this supervision is needed.
    Ans:
      The RBI is the apex bank: it issues currency and keeps the cash reserves of the commercial banks.
      It decides how much of their deposits banks must keep as reserves and how much they may lend, and it sets the rules for interest rates and priority-sector lending.
      It inspects banks, licenses them and can penalise them if they break the rules.
      This supervision is needed because banks hold other people's savings; without it they could lend recklessly and collapse, wiping out depositors' money and unsettling the whole economy.

Q11. [Long · 4 marks] Explain the terms of credit and how they decide whether a loan helps or harms the borrower.
    Ans:
      The terms of credit are the interest rate, the collateral or security, the documents required and the mode and time of repayment.
      A loan helps when the borrower can repay it out of the extra income it creates — a farmer irrigating land to grow a second crop, or a weaver buying yarn.
      It harms when the activity does not earn enough to repay, when the interest rate is high, or when the borrower loses the collateral and has to borrow again to repay the first loan — a debt trap.
      This is why formal credit from banks and cooperatives at lower rates, with supervision and advice, is better than borrowing from an informal moneylender.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] Explain with an example how credit can either help or harm the borrower.
    Ans:
      Where credit helps — Salim: Salim, a shoe manufacturer in Delhi, received a large order for 30,000 pairs of shoes to be delivered within a month. He took a loan from a trader, bought the raw material, completed the order on time, made a profit and repaid the loan. Here credit enabled him to expand production and earn more.
      Where credit harms — Swapna: Swapna, a small farmer in Andhra Pradesh, borrowed ₹ 2,500 from a moneylender at a high rate of interest to cultivate groundnut. The crop failed because of pest attack, and to repay the first loan she had to take another. Over three years her debt grew, and she finally had to sell a part of her land to repay the loan. This is a debt trap — a situation where credit pushes the borrower into a cycle from which she cannot escape.
      Conclusion: whether credit improves a person's situation depends on the risk involved and on whether there is support if something goes wrong. Credit is therefore essential but must be available on fair terms.

Q13. [Long · 5 marks] What are self-help groups and how do they help poor borrowers?
    Ans:
      A self-help group is a group of about 15–20 members, usually women belonging to one neighbourhood, who meet regularly and save, each contributing anything from ₹ 25 to ₹ 100 or more.
      Internal lending: members can take small loans from the group itself, at a rate of interest decided by the group but lower than that charged by moneylenders.
      Bank loans: after a year or two of regular savings, the group becomes eligible for a loan from a bank. The loan is sanctioned in the name of the group, and the group is responsible for repayment, so members keep track of each other's borrowing.
      Why it helps: it overcomes the problem of lack of collateral, provides credit at a reasonable rate, makes women financially self-reliant, and creates a platform for women to discuss health, nutrition, domestic violence and education. The Grameen Bank of Bangladesh, started by Muhammad Yunus, demonstrated that poor women are reliable borrowers; today more than 9 crore women in India belong to self-help groups.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The institution that issues currency notes in India on behalf of the Central Government is: (a) SBI (b) NABARD (c) the Reserve Bank of India (d) SEBI
    Ans: (c) the Reserve Bank of India — no other individual or organisation is allowed to issue currency.

M2. The Grameen Bank, which demonstrated that poor women are reliable borrowers, was set up in: (a) India (b) Bangladesh (c) Sri Lanka (d) Nepal
    Ans: (b) Bangladesh, by Muhammad Yunus in the 1970s.

M3. Collateral is best defined as: (a) the interest on a loan (b) an asset used as a guarantee until the loan is repaid (c) the principal amount (d) the bank's profit margin
    Ans: (b) — an asset the borrower owns, such as land, a vehicle, livestock or deposits, pledged until the loan is repaid.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Money and Credit” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Modern money, banks, credit terms, SHGs and debt traps.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Money and Credit” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Which of these is a function of the Reserve Bank of India? (a) accepting deposits from the public  (b) issuing currency notes  (c) lending directly to farmers  (d) selling insurance policies
    Ans: (b) issuing currency notes. It also supervises the commercial banks and acts as the government's banker.

M7. A self-help group generally consists of about: (a) 5 members  (b) 15 to 20 members  (c) 100 members  (d) 500 members
    Ans: (b) 15 to 20 members. Members save regularly, lend to each other at low rates and then qualify for bank loans.

M8. Cheap and affordable credit matters because it: (a) increases dependence on moneylenders  (b) lets borrowers invest and raise their income  (c) raises taxes  (d) reduces savings
    Ans: (b) lets borrowers invest and raise their income. Formal credit at low interest helps poor households escape the debt trap.
