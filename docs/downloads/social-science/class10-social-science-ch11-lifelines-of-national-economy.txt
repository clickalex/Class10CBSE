A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Why is the density of roads higher in the northern plains than in the Himalayan region?
    Ans: The northern plains are level, densely populated and rich in agriculture and industry, so roads are cheap to build and carry heavy traffic. The Himalayas are rugged, prone to landslides and sparsely populated, so construction costs are high and the volume of traffic is low.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] Why are roadways considered more efficient than railways in India?
    Ans: Because the cost of constructing roads is much lower than that of railway lines; roads can traverse more dissected and undulating topography and negotiate higher gradients, such as in the Himalayas; they provide door-to-door service, which reduces the loading and unloading cost; and they act as feeders to other modes of transport, connecting railway stations, airports and sea ports.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] Why are means of transport and communication called the lifelines of a nation and its economy?
    Ans: Because goods and services cannot move from supply locations to demand locations without them. A dense and efficient transport and communication network is a prerequisite for local, national and international trade, it enables industries to reach their markets and raw materials, it creates employment, and it has made the world a global village by allowing information to move instantly.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] What are the advantages of pipeline transport?
    Ans: Pipelines can transport crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas from the fields to refineries, fertiliser plants and thermal power stations, and solids can be moved as slurry. Once laid, they have minimal running cost, they eliminate transhipment losses and delays, and they can pass through difficult terrain including rivers and hills, so they provide a continuous and reliable supply. Their only major drawback is the high initial cost of laying the line.

Q5. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the importance of waterways in India.
    Ans: Waterways are the cheapest means of transport and are best suited for carrying heavy and bulky goods. They are a fuel-efficient and environment-friendly mode of transport. India has about 14,500 km of inland waterways, of which six stretches have been declared National Waterways, including the Ganga between Allahabad and Haldia and the Brahmaputra between Sadiya and Dhubri. These waterways also support tourism and connect remote regions to the national market.

Q8. [Short · 3 marks] Explain the importance of railways in India and any two problems they face.
    Ans:
      Railways carry a huge number of passengers and bulky goods over long distances cheaply, link markets, ports and pilgrim centres, and promote national integration.
      Problem 1: a large backlog of investment — tracks, bridges and rolling stock are old and the network is congested on trunk routes.
      Problem 2: losses on passenger services, theft and damage to railway property, and the slow pace of gauge conversion and electrification.

Q11. [Long · 4 marks] Describe the importance of means of communication in India's economy.
    Ans:
      The Indian postal network, the largest in the world, reaches even remote villages through nearly 1.55 lakh post offices and provides mail, savings, money-order and insurance services.
      Telecom has grown rapidly: STD, ISD, mobile phones and satellite communication connect people across the country and the world.
      The internet and mobile telephony have made e-banking, e-commerce, e-governance, telemedicine and online education possible.
      Together, transport and communication link producers with markets, buyers with sellers, and citizens with the government, which is why they are called the lifelines of the economy.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. The Golden Quadrilateral superhighway connects: (a) Srinagar and Kanyakumari (b) Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai (c) Silchar and Porbandar (d) Kandla and Haldia
    Ans: (b) Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai — it is a six-lane highway linking the four major metropolitan cities.

M2. The first port developed soon after Independence to ease the volume of trade on Mumbai port was: (a) Kandla (b) Marmagao (c) Tuticorin (d) Kochi
    Ans: (a) Kandla in Kachchh, developed to serve the rich agricultural and industrial hinterland of the north-west.

M3. Air transport in India was nationalised in: (a) 1947 (b) 1953 (c) 1965 (d) 1972
    Ans: (b) 1953.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Lifelines of National Economy” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Roadways, railways, pipelines, waterways, airways and trade.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Lifelines of National Economy” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. The longest national highway in India is: (a) NH 44  (b) NH 7  (c) NH 1  (d) NH 2
    Ans: (a) NH 44. It runs from Srinagar to Kanyakumari; the old NH 7 from Varanasi to Kanyakumari is now part of it.

M7. National Waterway 1 lies on the: (a) Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly  (b) Brahmaputra  (c) Godavari  (d) Narmada
    Ans: (a) Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly. It runs from Prayagraj to Haldia; NW 2 is on the Brahmaputra and NW 3 is the West Coast Canal.

M8. The Konkan Railway was built mainly to: (a) link Delhi and Mumbai  (b) provide a fast route along the western coast through difficult terrain  (c) carry coal  (d) connect Kolkata and Chennai
    Ans: (b) provide a fast route along the western coast through difficult terrain. It runs from Roha near Mumbai to Mangalore.
