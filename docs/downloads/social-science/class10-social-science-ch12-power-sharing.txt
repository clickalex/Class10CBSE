A. WRITTEN QUESTIONS (12)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. [Short · 2 marks] Why is power sharing desirable in a democracy?
    Ans: It ensures that no single community or group can dominate the others, reduces the fear of oppression and the chances of conflict, respects the claims of minorities and regions, and makes the government more stable, accountable and legitimate in the eyes of the people.

Q2. [Short · 3 marks] What is the difference between prudential and moral reasons for power sharing?
    Ans: Prudential reasons stress the good consequences of power sharing — it reduces conflict between social groups, ensures the stability of political order and prevents violence and political instability. Moral reasons stress the intrinsic worth of power sharing — that people have a right to be consulted on how they are to be governed, and that a legitimate government is one in which citizens acquire a stake in the system through participation. Prudential reasons are about outcomes; moral reasons are about principles.

Q3. [Short · 3 marks] What are the different forms of power sharing in modern democracies? Give one example of each.
    Ans: Horizontal distribution among organs of government — legislature, executive and judiciary, as in India, where the judiciary can review the acts of the legislature. Vertical distribution among governments at different levels — Union, state and local governments in India. Distribution among social groups — the community government in Belgium and reserved constituencies for weaker sections and women in India. Distribution among political parties — coalition governments formed by two or more parties when no single party gains a clear majority.

Q4. [Short · 3 marks] What is a system of checks and balances?
    Ans: It is the arrangement in which the organs of government — legislature, executive and judiciary — are placed at the same level but each exercises a check on the others. The legislature makes laws, the executive implements them and the judiciary can declare a law invalid if it violates the constitution. Because of these checks no organ can exercise unlimited power, and this horizontal distribution of power is a basic feature of democracy.

Q7. [Short · 3 marks] Describe the ethnic composition of Belgium and Sri Lanka and how it shaped their politics.
    Ans:
      In Belgium about 59% of the people speak Dutch (Flemish), 40% speak French (Walloon) and about 1% speak German; the French-speakers were historically richer and more powerful although fewer in number, which caused tension.
      In Brussels the situation is reversed, with a French-speaking majority among a Dutch-speaking region.
      In Sri Lanka the Sinhala-speakers are about 74% and the Tamil-speakers about 18%; the Sinhala-majority government imposed majoritarian measures such as making Sinhala the only official language (1956), which alienated the Tamils and led to a long civil war.

Q10. [Long · 4 marks] Describe the different forms of power sharing in modern democracies, with an example of each.
    Ans:
      Horizontal distribution among the legislature, executive and judiciary, so that each organ checks the others — for example, the Indian Parliament makes laws while the courts can declare them unconstitutional.
      Vertical division between the Union, States and local governments — federalism, as in India, Belgium and the USA.
      Sharing among different social groups, such as the community government in Belgium, where Dutch-, French- and German-speaking communities have equal representation on some matters.
      Sharing among political parties, pressure groups and movements through coalitions and competitive politics, so that power is not concentrated in one party.

Q11. [Long · 5 marks] Describe the major steps taken towards power sharing in Belgium.
    Ans:
      Equal representation in the central government: the constitution prescribes an equal number of Dutch-speaking and French-speaking ministers, so no single community can take decisions unilaterally.
      Devolution to the regions: many powers of the central government were given to the state governments of the Flemish and Walloon regions, and these regional governments are not subordinate to the central government.
      Brussels: the capital was given a separate government with equal representation for both the French- and Dutch-speaking groups.
      Community government: a third kind of government was set up, elected by people belonging to one language group — Dutch, French or German-speaking — irrespective of where they live, with power over cultural, educational and language-related issues.
      This arrangement, worked out between 1932 and 1968 through four constitutional amendments, prevented civic strife and the division of the country on linguistic lines.

Q12. [Long · 5 marks] What were the majoritarian measures adopted by the Sri Lankan government? What were their consequences?
    Ans:
      In 1956 an act was passed to recognise Sinhala as the only official language, disregarding Tamil.
      Successive governments followed preferential policies that favoured Sinhala applicants for university positions and government employment.
      The new constitution stipulated that the state shall protect and foster Buddhism.
      Consequences: the Sri Lankan Tamils felt that none of the major political parties, led by the Buddhist Sinhala leaders, was sensitive to their language and culture. Their demand for recognition of Tamil as an official language, regional autonomy and equal opportunity was repeatedly denied, and the demand for a separate Tamil Eelam grew. This led to a civil war that lasted until 2009, in which thousands of people were killed and the social, cultural and economic life of the country was badly affected.

B. MCQs (8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

M1. In Belgium, the community government is elected by: (a) all citizens of the country (b) people belonging to one language group, wherever they live (c) the residents of Brussels only (d) the Flemish region only
    Ans: (b) — people belonging to one language group, Dutch, French or German-speaking, irrespective of where they live; it has power over culture, education and language.

M2. The 1956 act in Sri Lanka recognised which language as the only official language? (a) Tamil (b) English (c) Sinhala (d) Sinhala and Tamil
    Ans: (c) Sinhala, disregarding Tamil and setting off a long conflict.

M3. Power sharing among the legislature, executive and judiciary is an example of: (a) vertical power sharing (b) horizontal power sharing (c) community power sharing (d) coalition government
    Ans: (b) horizontal power sharing, since the organs are placed at the same level and each checks the others.

M4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Power Sharing” is: (a) read the heading only  (b) learn the key idea plus one example  (c) skip written practice  (d) memorise a random extra topic
    Ans: (b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Belgium and Sri Lanka, and the four forms of power sharing.

M5. A 3-mark answer on “Power Sharing” should: (a) be one sentence  (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method  (c) copy the whole chapter  (d) leave the example out
    Ans: (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

M6. Which of these was a majoritarian measure in Sri Lanka? (a) making Sinhala the only official language in 1956  (b) power sharing  (c) federalism  (d) proportional representation
    Ans: (a) making Sinhala the only official language in 1956. It disregarded the Tamil-speaking minority and deepened the conflict.

M7. In Belgium's central government, the number of Dutch-speaking and French-speaking ministers must be: (a) unequal  (b) equal  (c) decided by the King  (d) decided by the majority
    Ans: (b) equal. This special arrangement balances the two main linguistic communities.

M8. Horizontal distribution of power means sharing power among: (a) different levels of government  (b) the legislature, executive and judiciary  (c) social groups  (d) political parties only
    Ans: (b) the legislature, executive and judiciary. It creates a system of checks and balances between the organs of government.
