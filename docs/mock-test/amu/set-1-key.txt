Q1. (b) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q2. (c) passes undeviated. Refraction changes the speed but there is no bending when the ray is along the normal, because sin 0° = 0 on both sides of Snell's law.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (b) concave, focal length 50 cm. f = 1/P = 1/(−2) m = −0.5 m; the negative sign means a concave (diverging) lens, which corrects myopia.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q4. (b) dispersion. Different wavelengths bend by different amounts inside a prism, so the colours separate.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q5. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q7. (a) 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q8. (b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q9. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q10. (a). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q11. (c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q12. (b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q13. (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q14. (b) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q15. (b) silver. Silver conducts best, then copper; silver is too costly for wiring, so copper is used.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q16. (b) copper and zinc. Bronze is copper and tin, solder is lead and tin, and an amalgam contains mercury.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q17. (d) C₂H₄. Only an unsaturated hydrocarbon (ethene, with a C=C double bond) can add hydrogen; the other three are saturated alkanes.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q18. (b) C₂H₄. Ethene is unsaturated; only double or triple bonds add on and decolourise the reagent.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q19. (b) chloroplast. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q20. (c) green plant. It prepares its own food by photosynthesis; Cuscuta is a parasite and fungi are saprotrophs.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q21. (d) abscisic acid — it inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves and the closing of stomata.

Q22. (b) spinal cord. Because the brain is not involved in the decision, the response is quick and automatic.

Q23. (b) testes. They are formed in the seminiferous tubules, where the temperature is about 2–3 °C below body temperature.

Q24. (b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts. Vasectomy in males and tubectomy in females prevent gametes from meeting.
