Q1. (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q2. (b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q4. (b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q5. (c) kilowatt-hour (kWh), equal to 3.6 × 10⁶ J, and commonly called a 'unit'.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q6. (a) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q7. (b) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q8. (c) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q9. (a) electromagnetic induction. Faraday's discovery — the basis of the electric generator; the induced current direction is given by Fleming's right-hand rule.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q10. (c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q11. (b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q12. (b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q13. (d) 10. Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution, whose pH is above 7.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q14. (c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q15. (b) Mg > Zn > Cu. In the reactivity series magnesium lies above zinc, and copper lies below hydrogen, so it is the least reactive of the three.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q16. (c) 7 — six C–H bonds plus one C–C bond.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q17. (c) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q18. (c) CO₂. 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂; the gas turns lime water milky.

Q19. (c) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.

Q20. (c). Absorption of digested food occurs mainly in the small intestine through the villi.

Q21. (b) cerebellum. It also makes voluntary movements precise; alcohol affects it, which is why a drunk person staggers.

Q22. (a) budding in yeast. Asexual modes include budding, binary fission, fragmentation, spore formation and vegetative propagation.

Q23. (b) the father's sperm. Eggs always carry X; a sperm carrying Y produces a boy and one carrying X produces a girl.

Q24. (c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.

Q25. (b) 100 J. Grass 10,000 J → deer 1,000 J → lion 100 J (10% at each transfer).
