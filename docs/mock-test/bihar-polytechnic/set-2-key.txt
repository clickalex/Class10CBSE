Q1. (a) less than one. A convex mirror always forms a diminished, virtual and erect image.

Q2. (b) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.33.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q4. (c) passes undeviated. Refraction changes the speed but there is no bending when the ray is along the normal, because sin 0° = 0 on both sides of Snell's law.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q5. (b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q7. (b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules. Fine particles scatter blue light far more than red, so scattered blue reaches the eye from every direction.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q8. (b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.

Q9. (b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.

Q10. (a) in series. It has very low resistance so it does not change the current; a voltmeter is connected in parallel.

Q11. (b) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q12. (b) 100 times per second — the direction reverses twice in each cycle.

Q13. (a) electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator does the reverse conversion.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q14. (b) decreases as you move away from the wire. The concentric field lines get farther apart with distance; the field is stronger for a larger current and closer to the wire.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q15. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q16. (b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q17. (a). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).

Q23. (b) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.
