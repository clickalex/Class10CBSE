Q1. (b) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q2. (b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (a) +2 D. P = 1/f in metres = 1/0.5 = +2 D; a converging lens always has positive power.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q4. (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q5. (b) concave, focal length 50 cm. f = 1/P = 1/(−2) m = −0.5 m; the negative sign means a concave (diverging) lens, which corrects myopia.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (b) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q7. (b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q8. (b) dispersion. Different wavelengths bend by different amounts inside a prism, so the colours separate.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q9. (b) less resistance, since R = ρl/A and a larger area of cross-section reduces the resistance.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q10. (c) kilowatt-hour (kWh), equal to 3.6 × 10⁶ J, and commonly called a 'unit'.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q11. (a) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q12. (c) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q13. (c). The slip rings of an AC generator allow the output to reverse, while the split-ring commutator of a DC generator keeps the external current in one direction.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q14. (a) electromagnetic induction. Faraday's discovery — the basis of the electric generator; the induced current direction is given by Fleming's right-hand rule.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q15. (b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q16. (c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q17. (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q18. (b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q19. (a) Cu and CuO. Copper is reddish-brown and forms black copper(II) oxide on heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.

Q21. (b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.

Q22. (b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.

Q23. (b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.
