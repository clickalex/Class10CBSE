Explanations

Q1. (a) less than one. A convex mirror always forms a diminished, virtual and erect image.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q2. (b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q4. (b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q5. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (b) 25 cm. This is the near point; the far point of a normal eye is at infinity.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q7. (b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q8. (b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q9. (a) electromagnetic induction. Faraday's discovery — the basis of the electric generator; the induced current direction is given by Fleming's right-hand rule.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q10. (b) decreases as you move away from the wire. The concentric field lines get farther apart with distance; the field is stronger for a larger current and closer to the wire.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q11. (c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q12. (a). Copper forms basic copper carbonate CuCO3·Cu(OH)2 (green); silver forms silver sulphide Ag2S (black).
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q13. (c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q14. (a) dry slaked lime. Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O. Quicklime is CaO, limestone is CaCO₃, gypsum is a sulphate.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q15. (b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q16. (b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q17. (b) Al2O3 and ZnO — they react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q18. (c) 7 — six C–H bonds plus one C–C bond.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q19. (c) C2H4 (ethene). Only unsaturated compounds with a double bond undergo addition with bromine.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q20. (b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q21. (c) 9/40. 40 = 2³ × 5, of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ. The others have denominators with other primes.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Real Numbers (Mathematics)

Q22. (b) Proportional. That is the SSS similarity criterion.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Triangles (Mathematics)

Q23. (c) 0.

Q24. (b) 1. tan and cot are reciprocals.

Q31. (c) green plant. It prepares its own food by photosynthesis; Cuscuta is a parasite and fungi are saprotrophs.
