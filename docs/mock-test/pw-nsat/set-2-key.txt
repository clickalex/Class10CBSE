Q1. (b) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.33.

Q2. (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (c) ciliary muscles — they change the curvature of the crystalline lens, giving the eye its power of accommodation.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q4. (b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules. Fine particles scatter blue light far more than red, so scattered blue reaches the eye from every direction.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q5. (b) less resistance, since R = ρl/A and a larger area of cross-section reduces the resistance.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q6. (a) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q7. (a) 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q8. (c) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q9. (b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q10. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q11. (b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q12. (a) NO₂. 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂; nitrogen dioxide is brown, the residue lead oxide is yellow.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q13. (b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q14. (b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q15. (b) hydrogen. Metal + dilute acid → salt + H₂, which burns with a pop sound.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q16. (c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q17. (b) silver. Silver conducts best, then copper; silver is too costly for wiring, so copper is used.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q18. (b) copper and zinc. Bronze is copper and tin, solder is lead and tin, and an amalgam contains mercury.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q19. (a) 5–8% ethanoic acid in water. Pure (glacial) acetic acid freezes at 290 K, which is why it is not used directly in kitchens.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q20. (b) C₂H₄. Ethene is unsaturated; only double or triple bonds add on and decolourise the reagent.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q21. (c) cubic. Degree 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic and 4 is biquadratic.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Polynomials (Mathematics)

Q22. (c) 6. Coincident lines need 1/3 = 2/k = 5/15, and 2/k = 1/3 gives k = 6.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (Mathematics)

Q23. (b) Real and equal.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Quadratic Equations (Mathematics)

Q24. (c) 55. n(n+1)/2 = 10 × 11/2.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Arithmetic Progressions (Mathematics)

Q25. (a) equal. Equiangular triangles are similar — the AAA similarity criterion.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Triangles (Mathematics)

Q26. (a) a. Distance = √(a²cos²θ + a²sin²θ) = √(a²) = a.

Q27. (c) 45°. sin 45° = cos 45° = 1/√2.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Introduction to Trigonometry (Mathematics)

Q28. (b) 6 m. By Pythagoras, √(100 − 64) = √36 = 6 m.

Q29. (b) 1.

Q30. (b) 154 cm². 2πr = 44 gives r = 7 cm, so the area is πr² = 154 cm².
      Topic: Ch 11 · Areas Related to Circles (Mathematics)

Q31. (b) chloroplast. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q32. (b) cerebellum. It also makes voluntary movements precise; alcohol affects it, which is why a drunk person staggers.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q33. (a) budding in yeast. Asexual modes include budding, binary fission, fragmentation, spore formation and vegetative propagation.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q34. (b) 3 tall : 1 dwarf. 1:2:1 is the genotypic ratio of the same cross.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q35. (b) 100 J. Grass 10,000 J → deer 1,000 J → lion 100 J (10% at each transfer).
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)

Q36. (c) 36. These are the squares 1², 2², 3², 4², 5²; the next is 6² = 36.
      Topic: Number series (Mental Ability)

Q37. (b) U. The gaps grow by one each time: +2, +3, +4, +5, so the next gap is +6. O is the 15th letter; 15 + 6 = 21 = U.

Q38. (a) NPR. Each letter moves one step forward: M → N, O → P, Q → R.
      Topic: Analogy (Mental Ability)

Q39. (b) 75°. The minute hand is at 180°; the hour hand is at 3 × 30° + 30 × 0.5° = 105°. The difference is 75°.
      Topic: Clock and calendar (Mental Ability)

Q40. (d) 20. Ten times in the units place (7, 17, …, 97) and ten times in the tens place (70–79); 77 counts twice.
      Topic: Ranking, arrangement and puzzles (Mental Ability)
