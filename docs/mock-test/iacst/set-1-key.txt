Q1. (a) +2 D. P = 1/f in metres = 1/0.5 = +2 D; a converging lens always has positive power.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q2. (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (b) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q4. (b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q5. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q7. (a) its length is doubled. R ∝ l/A; doubling the area halves R, doubling the diameter reduces R to a quarter.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q8. (a) in series. It has very low resistance so it does not change the current; a voltmeter is connected in parallel.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q9. (b) 100 times per second — the direction reverses twice in each cycle.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q10. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q11. (c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q12. (c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q13. (a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q14. (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q15. (b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q16. (c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q17. (c) zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference to the iron even if the coating is scratched.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q18. (c) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q19. (b) propanal. Three carbons (prop-) with an aldehyde group –CHO (-al). Propanone is a ketone, propanol an alcohol, propanoic acid a carboxylic acid.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q20. (b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q21. (c) 9/40. 40 = 2³ × 5, of the form 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ. The others have denominators with other primes.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Real Numbers (Mathematics)

Q22. (c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Polynomials (Mathematics)

Q23. (a) x = 9, y = 5. Adding the equations gives 2x = 18, so x = 9 and then y = 5.
