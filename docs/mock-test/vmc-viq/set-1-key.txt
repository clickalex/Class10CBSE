Q1. (b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.

      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q3. (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q4. (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q5. (b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q6. (b) concave, focal length 50 cm. f = 1/P = 1/(−2) m = −0.5 m; the negative sign means a concave (diverging) lens, which corrects myopia.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q7. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q8. (b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules. Fine particles scatter blue light far more than red, so scattered blue reaches the eye from every direction.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)
