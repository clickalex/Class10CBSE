Explanations
------------

Q1. (c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q2. (a) NO₂. 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂; nitrogen dioxide is brown, the residue lead oxide is yellow.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q3. (a) Cu and CuO. Copper is reddish-brown and forms black copper(II) oxide on heating: 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q4. (c) NaOH. Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases and ionise completely; the others are weak.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q5. (a) less than 7. The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid, which keeps the gastric juice acidic (roughly pH 1.2–3) so that pepsin can act.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q6. (b) more reactive than silver. Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag; the blue colour is due to Cu2+ ions.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q7. (c) zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes in preference to the iron even if the coating is scratched.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q8. (c) 7 — six C–H bonds plus one C–C bond.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q9. (d) C₂H₄. Only an unsaturated hydrocarbon (ethene, with a C=C double bond) can add hydrogen; the other three are saturated alkanes.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q10. (b) C₂H₄. Ethene is unsaturated; only double or triple bonds add on and decolourise the reagent.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q11. (c) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q12. (c). Absorption of digested food occurs mainly in the small intestine through the villi.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q13. (b) cerebellum. It also makes voluntary movements precise; alcohol affects it, which is why a drunk person staggers.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q14. (b) spinal cord. Because the brain is not involved in the decision, the response is quick and automatic.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q15. (a) budding in yeast. Asexual modes include budding, binary fission, fragmentation, spore formation and vegetative propagation.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q16. (b) testes. They are formed in the seminiferous tubules, where the temperature is about 2–3 °C below body temperature.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q17. (c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q18. (b) 46. Each gamete carries 23 chromosomes and their fusion restores the 23 pairs.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q19. (b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q20. (b) bends away from the normal. Moving from a denser to a rarer medium, the ray speeds up and bends away from the normal.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q21. (b) myopia. Short-sightedness is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q22. (b) dispersion. Different wavelengths bend by different amounts inside a prism, so the colours separate.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q23. (b) 25 cm. This is the near point; the far point of a normal eye is at infinity.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q24. (b) less than 1 Ω. Connecting all four in parallel gives an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest individual resistance, 1 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q25. (b) less resistance, since R = ρl/A and a larger area of cross-section reduces the resistance.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q26. (a) in series. It has very low resistance so it does not change the current; a voltmeter is connected in parallel.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q27. (b) parallel straight lines. The field inside a solenoid is nearly uniform, like the field inside a bar magnet.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q28. (c) green. Live is red or brown, neutral is black or blue, and earth is green or green-yellow.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q29. (b) 100 J. Grass 10,000 J → deer 1,000 J → lion 100 J (10% at each transfer).
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)

Q30. (a) energy. Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is stored in the next; the rest is lost as heat and in life processes.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)
