Q1. (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q2. (b) adding antioxidants and flushing with nitrogen. Both slow down the oxidation of fats that causes the stale smell and taste.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q3. (c) 4. 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂ — three iron atoms need four oxygen atoms, which come from four water molecules.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q4. (b) HCl. Egg-shell is CaCO3; CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 turns lime water milky.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q5. (b) CaSO₄·½H₂O. Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated to 373 K loses three-quarters of its water of crystallisation to give the hemihydrate.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q6. (c) electrolytic reduction. Sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce it; the molten chloride is electrolysed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q7. (b) copper and zinc. Bronze is copper and tin, solder is lead and tin, and an amalgam contains mercury.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q8. (c) C2H4 (ethene). Only unsaturated compounds with a double bond undergo addition with bromine.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q9. (a) 5–8% ethanoic acid in water. Pure (glacial) acetic acid freezes at 290 K, which is why it is not used directly in kitchens.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q10. (c) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q11. (b) chloroplast. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q12. (b) urea. It is filtered out of blood in the nephrons along with water and salts to form urine.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q13. (d) abscisic acid — it inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves and the closing of stomata.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q14. (b) dendrite. Dendrites receive signals; the axon carries the impulse away towards the next neuron.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q15. (b) nucleus. The nucleus divides first, followed by the division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q16. (c) condom. It physically prevents the sperm from meeting the egg and also reduces the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q17. (b) 3 tall : 1 dwarf. 1:2:1 is the genotypic ratio of the same cross.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q18. (b) the father's sperm. Eggs always carry X; a sperm carrying Y produces a boy and one carrying X produces a girl.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q19. (a) less than one. A convex mirror always forms a diminished, virtual and erect image.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q20. (c) convex. Its image is always virtual, erect and smaller, which is why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q21. (c) ciliary muscles — they change the curvature of the crystalline lens, giving the eye its power of accommodation.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q22. (b) is scattered the least — being of the longest wavelength, so it can be seen from a greater distance through fog or smoke.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q23. (b) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules. Fine particles scatter blue light far more than red, so scattered blue reaches the eye from every direction.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q24. (b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q25. (c) kilowatt-hour (kWh), equal to 3.6 × 10⁶ J, and commonly called a 'unit'.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q26. (a) 3.6 × 10⁶ J. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q27. (b) 100 times per second — the direction reverses twice in each cycle.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q28. (b) decreases as you move away from the wire. The concentric field lines get farther apart with distance; the field is stronger for a larger current and closer to the wire.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q29. (b) CFCs — chlorofluorocarbons from refrigerators, air conditioners and fire extinguishers destroy ozone molecules.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)

Q30. (b) — all three are broken down by the action of micro-organisms.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)
