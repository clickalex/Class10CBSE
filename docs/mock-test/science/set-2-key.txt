Explanations
------------

Q1. (c). Decomposition always needs energy, so heating ferrous sulphate is endothermic. The other three release heat.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q2. (b) photochemical decomposition. Light, not heat, splits silver bromide into silver and bromine; the grey silver darkens the film.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q3. (d) 10. Phenolphthalein turns pink only in a basic solution, whose pH is above 7.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q4. (b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q5. (b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q6. (b) Al2O3 and ZnO — they react with both acids and bases to form salt and water.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q7. (b) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q8. (b) silver. Silver conducts best, then copper; silver is too costly for wiring, so copper is used.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q9. (b) propanal. Three carbons (prop-) with an aldehyde group –CHO (-al). Propanone is a ketone, propanol an alcohol, propanoic acid a carboxylic acid.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q10. (b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q11. (b) mitochondria. Glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate) occurs in the cytoplasm; the further oxidation of pyruvate is mitochondrial.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q12. (c) green plant. It prepares its own food by photosynthesis; Cuscuta is a parasite and fungi are saprotrophs.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q13. (b) synapse — the junction where a neurotransmitter carries the signal across.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q14. (b) thyroxine. Secreted by the thyroid gland; iodine is needed to make it, and its deficiency causes goitre.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q15. (d) pollen grains. The anther is the male reproductive part; ovules are inside the ovary.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q16. (b) surgically blocking the gamete ducts. Vasectomy in males and tubectomy in females prevent gametes from meeting.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q17. (b) 3:1 — 25% of F2 are homozygous dwarf (tt).
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q18. (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Mendel obtained this for two independently assorting traits, such as seed shape and seed colour.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q19. (b) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q20. (b) 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. v = c/n = (3 × 10⁸)/1.33.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q21. (b) 20 cm in front of the mirror. The object is at C (2f = 20 cm), so the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size. Mirror formula: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/20 = −1/20, v = −20 cm.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q22. (b) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q23. (a) atmospheric refraction. Moving layers of air of changing density bend the starlight by varying amounts, so its apparent position shifts rapidly.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q24. (b) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q25. (c) 2 Ω. 1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6, so R = 2 Ω — equal resistors in parallel give R/n.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q26. (c) the right-hand thumb rule — hold the wire in the right hand with the thumb along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the field lines.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q27. (a) electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator does the reverse conversion.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q28. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q29. (c) sunlight. Coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels that take millions of years to form.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)

Q30. (a) CFCs. Chlorofluorocarbons from refrigerants and aerosol sprays release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone molecules.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)
