Explanations

Q1. (c) 39. The differences are 3, 5, 7, 9 (consecutive odd numbers), so add 11: 28 + 11 = 39.
      Topic: Number series (Mental Ability)

Q2. (b) F. The positions double: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Position 32 wraps around the 26-letter alphabet to 32 − 26 = 6, which is F.
      Topic: Letter series (Mental Ability)

Q3. (c) 56. 30 = 5 × 6, i.e. n × (n + 1). So 7 × 8 = 56.
      Topic: Analogy (Mental Ability)

Q4. (c) 21. 13, 17 and 23 are prime; 21 = 3 × 7 is not.
      Topic: Odd one out (Mental Ability)

Q5. (b) 40. B (2) + L (12) + U (21) + E (5) = 40. Check: R (18) + E (5) + D (4) = 27.
      Topic: Coding–decoding (Mental Ability)

Q6. (b) 1. The first statement says 1 = 5, so 5 = 1. The powers of 5 are a distraction.
      Topic: Coding–decoding (Mental Ability)

Q7. (a) South-East. Every direction is turned 135° anticlockwise (SE → N, NE → W). West turned 135° anticlockwise is South-East.
      Topic: Direction sense (Mental Ability)

Q8. (b) Wife. D is the father of A and B (they are siblings), so C, the other parent of B, is D's wife.
      Topic: Blood relations (Mental Ability)

Q9. (b) Wednesday. 100 = 14 × 7 + 2, so the day moves two places forward: Monday → Wednesday.
      Topic: Clock and calendar (Mental Ability)

Q10. (c) 5 minutes. Each machine makes one toy in 5 minutes, so 100 machines make 100 toys in the same 5 minutes.
      Topic: Ranking, arrangement and puzzles (Mental Ability)

Q11. (b) at 2F — the image forms at 2F on the other side, real, inverted and of the same size.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q12. (b) virtual, erect and diminished. A diverging lens forms the image between F₁ and the optical centre on the same side as the object, whatever the object distance.
      Topic: Ch 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction (Science)

Q13. (c) ciliary muscles — they change the curvature of the crystalline lens, giving the eye its power of accommodation.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q14. (b) hypermetropia — difficulty in seeing near objects clearly, corrected with a convex lens.
      Topic: Ch 10 · The Human Eye and the Colourful World (Science)

Q15. (b) 30 kWh. Energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h × 30 = 30 kWh, i.e. 30 units.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q16. (b) 6 Ω. Ohm's law: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω.
      Topic: Ch 11 · Electricity (Science)

Q17. (a) electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator does the reverse conversion.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q18. (c) force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force (motor). The right-hand rule is for generators.
      Topic: Ch 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (Science)

Q19. (c) displacement. Aluminium, being more reactive, displaces iron from its oxide. It is also a redox and a highly exothermic (thermite) reaction.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q20. (a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. Two reactants combine to form a single product; (b) is decomposition, (c) displacement and (d) double displacement.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations (Science)

Q21. (b) sodium carbonate. Washing soda precipitates out the dissolved calcium and magnesium salts that cause hardness.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q22. (b) NaHCO₃. Sodium hydrogencarbonate; it releases CO₂ on heating or with acid, which makes cakes rise and antacids fizz.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts (Science)

Q23. (b) MgCl₂. Magnesium loses two electrons to become Mg²⁺; each chlorine atom gains one, so two Cl⁻ ions are needed.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q24. (c) sodium. It reacts violently with cold water; iron and zinc react only with steam, and copper does not react at all.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Metals and Non-metals (Science)

Q25. (c) –OH. –CHO is aldehyde, –COOH is carboxylic acid and –CO– is a ketone group.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q26. (b) C₂H₆ and C₃H₈. Both are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) differing by one –CH₂– unit. The other pairs have different functional groups or bond types.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Carbon and its Compounds (Science)

Q27. (b) 4. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3; the lowest power of the common prime 2 is 2² = 4.
      Topic: Ch 1 · Real Numbers (Mathematics)

Q28. (c) 2 zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes, so degree 2 gives at most two.
      Topic: Ch 2 · Polynomials (Mathematics)

Q29. (c) 6. Coincident lines need 1/3 = 2/k = 5/15, and 2/k = 1/3 gives k = 6.
      Topic: Ch 3 · Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (Mathematics)

Q30. (c) x³ − x² + 1 = 0. Its degree is 3, so it is a cubic; the others reduce to degree 2.
      Topic: Ch 4 · Quadratic Equations (Mathematics)

Q31. (a) The 5th term. That is what aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ means.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Arithmetic Progressions (Mathematics)

Q32. (a) Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean. Equivalently, 3 Median = Mode + 2 Mean.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Statistics (Mathematics)

Q33. (b) 12. Jacks, queens and kings of four suits.
      Topic: Ch 14 · Probability (Mathematics)

Q34. (c) excretion — they remove nitrogenous waste (urea and uric acid) from the blood.
      Topic: Ch 5 · Life Processes (Science)

Q35. (b) thyroxine. Secreted by the thyroid gland; iodine is needed to make it, and its deficiency causes goitre.
      Topic: Ch 6 · Control and Coordination (Science)

Q36. (c) condom. It physically prevents the sperm from meeting the egg and also reduces the risk of sexually transmitted diseases.
      Topic: Ch 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce? (Science)

Q37. (b) 3:1 — 25% of F2 are homozygous dwarf (tt).
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q38. (c) eye colour. Only changes in the DNA of reproductive cells are passed to the next generation; acquired changes in body cells are not.
      Topic: Ch 8 · Heredity (Science)

Q39. (c) sunlight. Coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels that take millions of years to form.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)

Q40. (a) CFCs. Chlorofluorocarbons from refrigerants and aerosol sprays release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone molecules.
      Topic: Ch 13 · Our Environment (Science)
