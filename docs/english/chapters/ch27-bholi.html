Chapter 27 · Bholi

Chapter 27 of 32. The transformation from Bholi to Sulekha. The rejection of the dowry is the turning point.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Short answers and a value-based question on how education changed her, and on the dowry.
Bholi / Sulekha, the pock marks, Bishamber Nath, and the teacher are the key names.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. Bholi, whose real name was Sulekha, was the fourth daughter of Ramlal, the village numberdar. She fell off the cot as a child and was disfigured on one side of her face, and later an attack of smallpox left black pock marks all over her body. She was neglected, given coarse clothes, and never sent to school. She was slow to speak and stammered, so the other children mocked her, and she became silent. Then the tehsildar asked Ramlal, as a revenue official, to set an example by sending his daughters to school, so Bholi was sent to the village school. There her teacher was kind and encouraging: she told Bholi that if she came to school every day she would learn more than anyone in the village, and would speak without stammering. Bholi began to read, and her confidence grew. When she was of marriageable age, Bishamber Nath, a prosperous grocer from another village — lame, with a grown-up family — came to see her. He agreed to the match, but at the wedding ceremony the veil slipped from Bholi's face and he saw her pock-marked face; he demanded five thousand rupees as compensation. Ramlal, humiliated, begged, but Bishamber Nath refused to lower the demand. At that moment Bholi, who had been silent throughout, spoke clearly and without a stammer: she threw the garland of yellow marigolds into the fire and refused to marry such a mean, greedy and contemptible coward. She then said she would serve her parents in their old age and teach in the same school where she had learnt so much.
2. Characters. Bholi (Sulekha) — neglected and mocked, transformed by kindness and education into a confident young woman. The teacher — the turning figure; her encouragement gives Bholi a voice. Ramlal and his wife — parents who neglected her, though her father is not cruel. Bishamber Nath — a lame, prosperous, greedy grocer who demands dowry.
3. Themes. The transforming power of education and kindness; the evils of dowry; and the rights of girls, especially those who are differently abled or considered unattractive.

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Describe Bholi's start: the fourth daughter, pock-marked, a stammerer, and considered a burden.
  2. Give the school and the teacher — Bholi refuses to go, then stays because the mistress is kind, and slowly finds her voice.
  3. Show the marriage bargain — Bishamber demands six thousand rupees because he is marrying the 'backward' girl.
  4. Close with Bholi's refusal: she throws the garland into the fire and chooses to serve her father and teach in the village school.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Two names: Bholi and Sulekha. The shift from one to the other is the story.
  • She rejects the marriage by speaking clearly — no stammer. That detail is the proof of her transformation.
  • The garland of yellow marigolds thrown into the fire is the most quoted image.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Bholi's parents hated her. They neglected her; her father was not cruel.
  • Saying Bishamber Nath was handsome and young. He is lame and has grown-up children.
  • Missing the ending: she decides to teach in the same school.

1. How did the teacher transform Bholi's life?

Show Answer

Ans:

The teacher was the first person to treat Bholi with kindness and respect. She spoke to her in a soft, soothing voice, told her not to be afraid, and promised that if she came to school every day she would learn more than anyone else in the village and would soon speak without a stammer. She encouraged Bholi to read, praised her progress, and gave her confidence. Education gave Bholi a voice, self-respect and the courage to refuse a humiliating marriage.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why did Bholi refuse to marry Bishamber Nath?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because at the wedding, when her veil slipped and he saw her pock-marked face, he demanded five thousand rupees as compensation and refused to marry her without it. He had already agreed to the match when he thought she was fair, so his demand showed that he was mean, greedy and contemptible. Bholi, who had been silent until then, spoke clearly and without a stammer and refused to marry such a coward.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What does the story say about the value of girls' education?

Show Answer

Ans:

It shows that education can transform a neglected, mocked and silent girl into a confident person with self-respect. Before school, Bholi was slow to speak, was laughed at and had no self-worth. After school, she could read, spoke without a stammer, and was able to stand up against a greedy bridegroom and his dowry demand. She finally decides to serve her parents and teach in the school where she learnt so much — so education gives her both independence and a purpose.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why is Bholi called a simpleton, and how does she change?

Show Answer

Ans:

As a child she fell and damaged part of her brain, so she was slow, stammered and was mocked as ‘Bholi, the simpleton'.
She was neglected, given old clothes and little food, and kept out of school for years.
The village primary school and then the city school gave her a teacher's kindness, confidence and the ability to speak without stammering.
By the end she is able to refuse an unequal marriage and to speak up for her father, her teacher and herself.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Why was Bholi sent to school when her parents did not value girls' education?

Show Answer

Ans:

The tehsildar had ordered Ramlal, the numberdar, to set an example by sending his daughters to school, and Ramlal dared not disobey. Since Bholi — pock-marked, stammering, considered a simpleton — was the least valued child and unlikely to marry, her parents sacrificed her to the school while keeping her sisters at home. Neglect, ironically, gave her the chance that changed her life.

LONGL104 marks

10. How does education give Bholi a voice?

Show Answer

Ans:

Her teacher's encouragement makes her believe she can learn; she is given books, a clean dress and, above all, attention.
Education gives her self-respect: she learns to read, write and do sums, and she stops stammering in front of others.
When Bishamber Nath demands five thousand rupees as dowry, she understands that she is being bought and tears up the wedding garland.
She finally declares that she will serve her parents and teach at the same school, showing that education has turned a frightened child into a person with a will of her own.

LONGL114 marks

11. Bholi throws the wedding garland into the fire. Why is this gesture the climax of the story?

Show Answer

Ans:

The garland stands for the marriage her parents arranged at any price — even a 5,000-rupee dowry to a middle-aged widower.
By throwing it into the fire, Bholi burns not just one wedding but the whole bargain: dowry, obedience and the idea that a girl is a burden.
It is the climax because the stammering simpleton now speaks clearly and chooses for herself — education has given her a voice, and she uses it.
