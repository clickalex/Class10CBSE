Chapter 20 · A Triumph of Surgery

Chapter 20 of 33. A vet's story about a dog made ill by kindness. The lesson is for the owner, not the animal.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Short answers of 30–40 words and one 50–60 word value-based question.
Know Tricki the Pomeranian, Mrs Pumphrey, Hodgkin, and the vet's decision not to use any medicine.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. The narrator, a veterinary surgeon, is asked by Mrs Pumphrey to look at her dog Tricki, a small Pomeranian. The dog is hugely fat, with bloodshot, rheumy eyes and a lolling tongue, and Mrs Pumphrey worries that he is suffering from malnutrition. The vet finds the dog is simply overfed and spoilt: Mrs Pumphrey gives him cream cakes, chocolates, sweets and cod-liver oil, and never lets him exercise. The vet takes Tricki to his surgery and keeps him there for a fortnight. He gives him no medicine at all — only water, plenty of exercise and no food for the first two days. Tricki recovers completely, and when Mrs Pumphrey comes to collect him she says it was a triumph of surgery. Her friends are so impressed that they begin to bring their own dogs to the vet, and the surgery fills with luxury items sent by the grateful mistress.
2. Characters. Tricki — a small, spoilt Pomeranian, made ill by overfeeding. Mrs Pumphrey — a rich, doting mistress who mistakes overindulgence for love. The vet (James Herriot) — practical, kind and shrewd; he understands that the dog's illness is a social problem, not a medical one.
3. Theme. Excessive love without discipline harms; and the wisest treatment is sometimes to remove the cause rather than to prescribe medicine.

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Begin with the problem: Mrs Pumphrey'sTricki, overfed and listless, 'like a bloated sausage'.
  2. Give Dr Herriot's diagnosis and plan — no medicine, only discipline, company and a strict diet at the surgery.
  3. Show the change in order: two days nothing, then Tricki joins the other dogs, then he is racing about with them.
  4. Close with Mrs Pumphrey's tearful reunion and her mistaken 'triumph of surgery' — the surgery was diet and exercise, not an operation.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • The vet gives no medicine — water, fasting and exercise only. That is the point of the story.
  • 'A triumph of surgery' is Mrs Pumphrey's phrase, and it is ironic: no surgery was done.
  • Mrs Pumphrey's gifts (eggs, wine, brandy) end up feeding the vet's staff — a comic detail examiners like.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying the vet operated on Tricki. There was no surgery at all.
  • Describing Tricki as a large dog. He is a small Pomeranian made fat by overfeeding.
  • Missing the irony in the title.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Tricki become so ill?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because Mrs Pumphrey overfed him. She gave him cream cakes, chocolates, sweets and cod-liver oil, and never allowed him any exercise. The result was that he became hugely fat, with bloodshot, rheumy eyes and a lolling tongue, and could hardly walk. His illness was caused by indulgence, not by disease.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What treatment did the vet give Tricki, and why was it successful?

Show Answer

Ans:

The vet gave no medicine at all. He kept Tricki in his surgery, gave him only water for the first two days, and then plenty of exercise and normal food. The treatment worked because the cause of the illness was overfeeding and lack of exercise, so removing the cause was enough to restore the dog's health.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Do you think Mrs Pumphrey was a wise owner? Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. She confused love with indulgence. She fed Tricki cream cakes, chocolates and sweets, and never let him exercise, which is why he became dangerously fat and ill. She also ignored the vet's advice about diet and exercise, and continued to send luxury food even after the dog had recovered. A wise owner would have shown affection through discipline and regular activity rather than through food.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What was wrong with Tricki and how had Mrs Pumphrey treated him?

Show Answer

Ans:

Tricki was hugely overfed and listless: Mrs Pumphrey gave him malt, cod-liver oil, Horlicks, cream cakes, chocolates, and even a whole box of eggs with wine and brandy between meals. He had become a bloated, panting little creature with no exercise, so at the surgery James Herriot diagnosed nothing more serious than overeating and lack of discipline.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Why did Mrs Pumphrey think Tricki's recovery was ‘a triumph of surgery’? What is ironical about this phrase?

Show Answer

Ans:

Mrs Pumphrey believed the vet had performed some difficult medical feat, so she called the recovery a triumph of surgery. The irony is that there was no surgery and no medicine at all — only water, fasting, exercise and normal food. The real cure was removing the cause (overfeeding), not prescribing anything.

LONGL104 marks

10. How does James Herriot cure Tricki, and what does the story say about love and discipline?

Show Answer

Ans:

He takes Tricki away from Mrs Pumphrey, keeps him in the surgery with the other dogs and, for the first two days, gives him no food at all — only water.
On the third day the dog begins to take interest in his bowl, and he is gradually given small amounts of food.
He recovers quickly, learns to play and run with the other dogs, and soon ‘the cure was complete'.
The story shows that love without discipline harms its object: Mrs Pumphrey's affection nearly killed Tricki, while Herriot's firmness restored his health.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. The vet shares Mrs Pumphrey's food hampers with his partner while Tricki fasts. Does this make him dishonest, or is it part of the humour? Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is part of the humour, not real dishonesty — the hampers of eggs, wine and brandy were meant to tempt Tricki, and giving them to a fasting dog would have undone the cure.
The vet and his partner enjoying the food while Tricki slimmed down on water is the story's running joke: the rich food fattens everyone except the patient.
What matters is that Tricki recovered fully; the vet's method, if not his manners, was exactly right.
