Chapter 8 · The Sermon at Benares

Chapter 8 of 33. A story about grief. The mustard-seed test is the heart of the answer.

🎯 Marks lens

Extracts from Kisa Gotami's search and from the Buddha's sermon; a 5-mark answer on the Buddha's teaching about death.
Know the mustard-seed condition, why Kisa Gotami failed, and the words about the world being afflicted with decay and death.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The Buddha's early life. Born a prince named Siddhartha Gautama, he was married young and had a son. At about the age of twenty-five, while out hunting, he came upon a sick man, then an aged man, then a funeral procession, and finally a monk begging for alms. These four sights so moved him that he left the palace at once and went out in search of enlightenment. He wandered for seven years and finally sat under a fig (Bo) tree, where he was enlightened. He then began to teach, and the tree was renamed the Bo tree. His first sermon was given at Sarnath, near Benares, on the banks of the Ganges, and has been preserved and is the subject of this chapter.
2. Kisa Gotami's story. Kisa Gotami had an only son, who died. Carrying the dead child, she went from house to house asking for medicine to cure him, and people thought she had lost her senses. At last a man directed her to the Buddha. The Buddha asked her to bring a handful of mustard seed, and when she promised to do so, he added the condition: the mustard seed must be taken from a house where no one has died — where no son, husband, parent or servant has been lost. Kisa Gotami went from door to door, and in every house someone had died: a father, a daughter, a servant, a slave. By evening she was weary and hopeless, and she sat down and watched the lights of the city flicker and go out; the darkness fell and spread. She understood at last the selfishness of her grief: she had been thinking only of her own loss, while death is common to all. She took the body of her child to the forest, laid it down, and returned to the Buddha.
3. The sermon. The Buddha said: 'Not from weeping nor from grief will anyone obtain peace of mind; on the contrary, his pain will be the greater and his body will suffer. He will make himself sick and pale, and yet the dead are not saved by his lamentation.' Those who seek peace of mind should draw out the arrow of lamentation, complaint and grief; he who has drawn out the arrow and composed himself will obtain peace. The world is afflicted with death and decay, therefore the wise do not grieve, having realised the truth of the world. As a ripe fruit is always in danger of falling, so mortals, once born, are always in danger of dying. As vessels made by the potter end by being broken, so is the life of mortals. The wise, understanding the world, do not grieve. Grieving will not bring peace; it will only increase suffering.
4. Themes. The universality of death — every family has lost someone. The futility of excessive grief — it harms the living and cannot save the dead. Enlightenment through experience — the Buddha does not lecture Kisa Gotami; he makes her discover the truth herself. Detachment and peace of mind.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • The Buddha's method is a test, not a lecture: the mustard-seed condition makes Kisa Gotami find the answer herself.
  • 'Draw out the arrow of lamentation' is the key image — quote it in any answer about the sermon.
  • Potter's vessels break; ripe fruit falls. Two similes that carry a full mark each.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying the Buddha refused to help Kisa Gotami. He gave her a condition, not a refusal.
  • Forgetting that the mustard seed had to come from a house where no one had died — the condition is the whole point.
  • Writing that the sermon was delivered at Bodh Gaya. It was at Sarnath, near Benares.

LONGL35 marks

3. How did the Buddha make Kisa Gotami understand the nature of death without preaching to her?

Show Answer

Ans:

He did not tell her the truth directly. He asked her to bring him a handful of mustard seed — a simple request that gave her hope.
He then added one condition: the mustard seed must be taken from a house where no one had died, where no son, husband, parent or servant had been lost.
Kisa Gotami went from door to door with confidence, but in the first house she was told the father had died; in the next, a daughter; then a servant, then a slave. There was no house untouched by death.
By evening she was tired and hopeless. She sat and watched the lights of the city being lit and then going out until darkness covered everything, and she realised the selfishness of her grief — she had been absorbed in her own loss while death is common to all.
So the Buddha's method was to let her experience the truth rather than hear it, which is why it changed her completely. She took her child's body to the forest and returned to him ready to listen.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Explain the meaning of 'the world is afflicted with death and decay, therefore the wise do not grieve'.

Show Answer

Ans:

It means that death and change are built into the world, so no amount of sorrow can prevent them. The wise understand this truth and accept it, so they do not grieve; they realise that lamentation cannot bring back the dead and only harms the living. Accepting the nature of the world is what brings peace of mind.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What were the four sights that led the Buddha to leave the palace?

Show Answer

Ans:

While out hunting at the age of about twenty-five, the prince came upon a sick man, then an aged man, then a funeral procession, and finally a monk begging for alms. These four sights showed him sickness, old age, death and renunciation, and moved him so deeply that he left the palace at once in search of enlightenment.
