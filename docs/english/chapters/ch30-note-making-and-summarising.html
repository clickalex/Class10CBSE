Chapter 30 · Note Making and Summarising

🎯 Marks lens

About 5 marks, usually as a note-making task based on a passage, followed by a short summary.
Marks are split between content, format and the summary — learn the format exactly.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The format. Write a title in the centre at the top. Use a numbering system: main headings 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.2 and so on, indented to show the level. Main headings carry the key idea; sub-headings carry the details. Every heading is a phrase, not a sentence. End with a conclusion line if the passage has one. Do not write in sentences, do not add your own ideas, and do not exceed the recommended number of headings.
2. Rules. Use only what is in the passage. Keep headings short — usually four to six words. Use standard abbreviations, and give a key to abbreviations at the end. Do not copy whole sentences. Indentation must be consistent, because the examiner reads the structure, not the prose.
3. The summary. The summary is usually limited to about 80 words and must be written in your own words, in complete sentences, covering the main idea and the two or three supporting points. Do not add anything new, do not give your opinion, and do not exceed the word limit — the limit is checked.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Headings are phrases, not sentences. That is the single most common loss of format marks.
  • 1, 1.1, 1.1.1 — the decimal numbering shows the examiner your structure at a glance.
  • Always write the key to abbreviations — it is a separate mark.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing notes in full sentences.
  • Exceeding the number of headings asked for.
  • Forgetting the key to abbreviations.
  • Exceeding the 80-word limit in the summary, or copying sentences from the passage.

❓ Exam Q&A

3 marks Describe the format required for note making.

Write a title in the centre at the top. Use decimal numbering for the headings — 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.2 — with consistent indentation to show the levels. Each heading must be a short phrase, not a sentence, and must contain only what is in the passage. Standard abbreviations may be used, and a key to abbreviations must be given at the end.

3 marks What are the rules for writing the summary that follows the notes?

The summary must be written in your own words, in complete sentences, and within the word limit, usually about 80 words. It must cover the main idea of the passage and its two or three main supporting points, and it must not include anything new or any personal opinion. Exceeding the word limit or copying sentences from the passage results in a deduction.

3 marks Why is the key to abbreviations important?

Because it is assessed as part of the format, and because abbreviations are meaningless without it. The examiner needs to be able to see that the abbreviations used in the headings are standard and have been explained. Omitting the key costs a mark even if the notes themselves are correct.

✍️ Hands-on task

Take any newspaper article of about 400 words and make notes on it in eight minutes, then write the 80-word summary. Check your headings: if any of them is a full sentence, rewrite it as a phrase. Do this five times and the format becomes automatic.
