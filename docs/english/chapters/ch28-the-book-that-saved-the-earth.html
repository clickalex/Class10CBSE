Chapter 28 · The Book That Saved the Earth

Chapter 28 of 33. A comic science-fiction play. Learn the three Martians and the nursery book.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Short answers; know Think-Tank, Captain Omega, Noodle, Sergeant Oop, and Mother Goose.
The twenty-fifth century is called the Era of the Book.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. The play is set in the twenty-fifth century, described as the Era of the Book, when books were used for everything. The Historian tells how, in the year 2040, the people of Mars had planned to invade Earth, but the invasion was called off because of a book. The Martian Think-Tank, the Commander-in-Chief of the Space Probe fleet, is vain and stupid, and believes he is the most intelligent creature in the universe. He sends Captain Omega, Lieutenant Iota and Sergeant Oop to Earth to make contact. They land in a library and find books. Think-Tank, examining the book from the control room, mistakes it for an earth sandwich and orders them to eat it; when they cannot, he decides that the books are communication devices and that the earthlings use them to communicate by ear. He orders Noodle to give the crew vitamins so that they can hear the books. The crew then reads Mother Goose aloud. When Sergeant Oop reads 'Mistress Mary, quite contrary, how does your garden grow?' with silver bells and cockle shells, Think-Tank concludes that the earthlings grow metals and explosives in their gardens. When he hears 'Hey diddle diddle, the cat and the fiddle, the cow jumped over the moon' and the picture of a cow, he panics, believing that the earthlings have taught their animals a high culture of music and space techniques, and that they are planning to attack Mars Central Control. Terrified, he orders the crew to leave Earth at once, and later flees to Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away. The Historian ends by saying that this is how the book saved the Earth — the Martians abandoned the invasion because of their misunderstanding of a nursery rhyme, and afterwards the Martians and the earthlings became friends, and Mars is now called New Earth.
2. Characters. Think-Tank — the vain, stupid, boastful Commander-in-Chief of Mars. Noodle — his clever, modest apprentice, who politely corrects him. Captain Omega, Lieutenant Iota, Sergeant Oop — the crew of the space probe. The Historian and Miss Twist — the narrators of the twenty-fifth century.
3. Theme. Ignorance and arrogance lead to comic misjudgement; books are powerful; and understanding, not force, brings peace.

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Set the frame: it is a play-within-a-play, with the Historian addressing the audience from the twenty-fifth century.
  2. Describe the Martian invasion — Think-Tank, Noodle, Captain Omega, and the book Mother Goose that is mistaken for a battle plan.
  3. Explain the misreading: 'Mistress Mary, quite contrary' and 'Hey diddle diddle' convince the Martians that humans are preparing tanks and bombs.
  4. Close with the joke — the Martians flee from a nursery rhyme, and the book is marked with a red triangle as a dangerous weapon.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Think-Tank is stupid and Noodle is clever — but Noodle lets Think-Tank take the credit. That contrast is the joke.
  • Three misreadings in order: book = sandwich, book = ear communication device, nursery rhyme = military threat.
  • The Era of the Book is the twenty-fifth century; the invasion was planned for 2040.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying the Martians attacked. They fled, to Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away.
  • Calling Noodle stupid. He is the clever one; Think-Tank is the fool.
  • Forgetting the ending: Mars and Earth become friends, and Mars is renamed New Earth.

SHORTS23 marks

2. How did the book save the Earth?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the Martians misunderstood what they read. When Sergeant Oop read 'Mistress Mary, quite contrary', with its silver bells and cockle shells, Think-Tank concluded that the earthlings grew metals and explosives in their gardens. When he heard about the cow jumping over the moon, he believed that the earthlings had taught their animals a high culture of music and space techniques, and that they planned to attack Mars Central Control. Terrified, he ordered the crew to leave Earth at once and later fled to Alpha Centauri — so the invasion was called off, and the Earth was saved by a nursery rhyme.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Describe Think-Tank's character.

Show Answer

Ans:

He is the Commander-in-Chief of the Space Probe fleet of Mars, and he believes himself to be the most powerful and intelligent creature in the universe. In fact he is vain, boastful and stupid: he mistakes a book for a sandwich, cannot understand a nursery rhyme, and panics at the idea of a cow jumping over the moon. He is also a coward, since he flees to Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away, at the first sign of danger.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How do the Martians misunderstand the nursery rhymes?

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank asks to be shown books, and the crew bring him nursery rhymes from a library.
From ‘Mistress Mary, quite contrary' he decides that humans grow money, and from ‘Mother Goose' he concludes that she is the supreme ruler of Earth.
He takes the rhyme about the cow that jumped over the moon as proof that Earth's creatures can jump between planets.
Finally he hears ‘Humpty Dumpty' and ‘Hey diddle diddle' and decides that Earth is planning to launch an attack on Mars.

SHORTS73 marks

7. How does Noodle's character contrast with Think-Tank's? Why is this contrast important to the play?

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank is vain, boastful and stupid — he mistakes a book for a sandwich and nursery rhymes for threats. Noodle, his apprentice, is clever and modest — he quietly suggests the right ideas (that the book is for reading) and lets his master take the credit.
The contrast drives both the humour and the ending: folly commands, wisdom obeys, until folly flees to Alpha Centauri and Noodle's line — Mars and Earth as friends — wins.

LONGL104 marks

10. What is the humour in the way the Martians abandon their invasion?

Show Answer

Ans:

Their invasion is decided by a commander who is certain he is the greatest intellect in the universe but cannot read a simple rhyme.
The more evidence he gets, the more absurd his conclusions become: library shelves become ‘sandwiches' and books are ‘communication sandwiches'.
He panics, orders the fleet to retreat and abandons the invasion of a planet whose only weapons were a cow, a dish and a spoon.
The joke is that the greatest danger to Earth was never an attack from Mars, but a Martian commander too proud to ask what the words mean.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. ‘Silver bells and cockle shells’ become metals and explosives in Martian eyes. Explain two such misreadings and the panic each causes.

Show Answer

Ans:

First, ‘mistress Mary, quite contrary’ with her silver bells and cockle shells is read as an earthling growing metals and explosives — proof that earthlings farm weapons.
Second, ‘Humpty Dumpty had a great fall’ is read as Earth's plan for Think-Tank himself: ‘he will have a great fall’ sends the commander into panic.
Each rhyme the Martians decode raises the alarm, until Think-Tank orders the invasion abandoned — a book of rhymes defeats an army.
