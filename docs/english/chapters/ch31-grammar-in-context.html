🎯 Marks lens

About 10 marks, usually as a gap-filling or editing task set within a short passage or dialogue.
The syllabus topics are tenses, modals, subject-verb agreement, reported speech and determiners. Clauses, passive voice and prepositions have been removed.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Tenses. The tense must agree with the time marker in the sentence. Since and for take the perfect tenses; yesterday, last year take the simple past; now, at present take the present continuous; tomorrow, next week take the future. In a narrative passage, the tense often shifts only when the time shifts — so keep the tense until a new time marker appears.
2. Modals. Can = ability, could = past ability or polite request, may = permission or possibility, might = weaker possibility, must = necessity or strong probability, should = advice, would = a polite offer or a past habit. In reported speech, will becomes would, can becomes could, and may becomes might.
3. Subject-verb agreement. The verb agrees with the subject, not with the nearest noun. 'The list of items is on the table.' Collective nouns take a singular verb when the group acts as one: 'The team has won.' With either…or / neither…nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Each, every, everyone, nobody take a singular verb.
4. Reported speech. Change the tense back one step: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would, can → could. Change the pronouns and the time and place words: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that. For questions, use asked and change the word order back to statement form: 'Where are you?' becomes He asked where I was. For commands, use ordered, requested or advised followed by the infinitive.
5. Determiners. A/an for a singular countable noun mentioned for the first time; the for something already mentioned or unique. Some in affirmative sentences, any in questions and negatives. Few/a few with countable nouns, little/a little with uncountable. Much with uncountable, many with countable. Each/every with singular nouns, all/both with plural.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • In a gap-filling passage, look at the other verbs first — the tense of the passage is usually consistent.
  • For subject-verb agreement, cross out everything between the subject and the verb and then decide.
  • In reported speech, count three changes: tense, pronoun, time/place word. If you have only made one, look again.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Using the simple past with 'since'. Since requires a perfect tense.
  • Making the verb agree with the nearest noun in 'the list of items are…'.
  • Forgetting to change the word order in reported questions.
  • Using 'a' before a word beginning with a vowel letter rather than a vowel sound — it is 'a university', 'an hour'.

SHORTS11 mark

1. Fill in the blank: Neither of the two candidates ____ (has/have) replied yet.

Show Answer

Ans:

has. 'Neither of' takes a singular verb, because the subject is 'neither', not 'candidates'.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Change into reported speech: 'Where have you kept my files?' she asked the clerk.

Show Answer

Ans:

She asked the clerk where he had kept her files. Note the three changes: the present perfect becomes the past perfect, the question word order becomes statement order, and 'my' becomes 'her'.

SHORTS31 mark

3. Fill in the blank with a suitable modal: You ____ consult a doctor if the cough persists. (can / should / may)

Show Answer

Ans:

should — the sentence gives advice, which is the use of 'should'. 'Can' expresses ability and 'may' expresses permission or possibility, neither of which fits.

SHORTS41 mark

4. Choose the correct determiner: There isn't ____ milk left in the jug. (some / any / much)

Show Answer

Ans:

any — 'any' is used with uncountable nouns in negative sentences; 'some' is used in affirmative sentences.

✍️ Hands-on task

Take any page of a story book, remove ten function words and verbs at random, and fill them back in from memory. Then check. This is exactly the gap-filling exercise the paper sets, and it trains the pattern recognition the questions need.
