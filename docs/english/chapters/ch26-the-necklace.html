Chapter 26 · The Necklace

🎯 Marks lens

Short answers and a value-based question on whether Matilda's suffering was deserved.
Matilda Loisel, the Ministry ball, and the necklace worth 36,000 francs replaced at 40,000.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. Matilda Loisel was a pretty and charming girl born into a family of clerks, married to a clerk at the Board of Education. She was unhappy, believing she was born for all the delicacies and luxuries of life, and she suffered from the poverty of her home. Her husband brought home an invitation to a reception at the Ministry of Public Instruction. Instead of being delighted she was irritated, because she had no suitable dress and no jewels. Her husband gave her four hundred francs he had saved for a gun, and she borrowed a superb diamond necklace from her rich friend Madame Forestier. At the ball she was the prettiest of all, elegant, smiling and quite above herself with happiness. On the way home she found the necklace was lost. The Loisels searched everywhere, then bought a new one for forty thousand francs, eighteen thousand of which her husband had inherited and the rest borrowed at ruinous interest. They spent ten years repaying the debt: they dismissed their maid, changed their lodgings, and Matilda did the heavy housework while her husband worked evenings and did copying at twopence-halfpenny a page. After ten years everything was paid. Matilda, now old and hard-looking, met Madame Forestier in the Champs-Élysées and told her the whole story. Madame Forestier, moved, took her hands and said: 'Oh, my poor Matilda! But mine was only paste! It was worth at most five hundred francs!'
2. Characters. Matilda — pretty, discontented, vain, and ultimately hardworking and honest in paying the debt. M. Loisel — kind, contented, self-sacrificing; he gives up his gun money and works for ten years to pay her debt. Madame Forestier — rich, generous, and the unknowing cause of the disaster.
3. Theme. Discontent and vanity can cost a whole life; and appearances are deceptive — what looked like a diamond necklace was only paste.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Two figures: 36,000 francs for the necklace and 40,000 to replace it; ten years to repay.
  • The final line — 'mine was only paste' — is the irony. Always quote it.
  • Matilda is not simply a villain: she pays the debt honestly for ten years. Say both halves.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Matilda stole the necklace. It was lost, not stolen.
  • Giving the replacement cost as 36,000 francs. The new necklace cost 40,000.
  • Forgetting M. Loisel's sacrifice — he gave up his gun and worked for ten years.

❓ Exam Q&A

3 marks Why was Matilda unhappy with her life?

Because she believed she was born for all the delicacies and luxuries of life, and her actual life was poor and plain. She suffered from the poverty of her home, from the shabby walls, the worn chairs and the ugly curtains, and she dreamed of silent antechambers, tall bronze candelabra and expensive dinners. Her dissatisfaction came from comparing herself with the rich, so she could not enjoy what she had.

3 marks What was the effect of losing the necklace on the Loisels' lives?

They had to replace it, and a similar necklace cost forty thousand francs. Her husband used the eighteen thousand francs his father had left him and borrowed the rest at ruinous interest. For ten years they lived in poverty: they dismissed their maid, moved to a garret, and Matilda did all the heavy housework while her husband worked evenings and did copying at twopence-halfpenny a page. Matilda grew old, rough and hard-looking, and they lost their youth and their comfort to pay a debt they need never have had.

5 marks Was Matilda's suffering deserved? Give reasons for your answer.

The case that it was deserved: her suffering came from her own vanity and discontent. She had a kind husband who loved her and a comfortable enough life, but she could not enjoy either because she compared herself with the rich. She was embarrassed to borrow a dress, insisted on jewels for the ball, and was so anxious to escape quickly that she did not check the necklace. Her vanity caused the loss.
The case that it was not deserved: she never stole anything, and she behaved honourably afterwards. Rather than admit the loss to her friend, she and her husband replaced the necklace honestly and spent ten years repaying a ruinous debt. She did the heavy housework and gave up her looks and her youth. A mistake of vanity, however foolish, does not deserve ten years of punishment.
Conclusion: the punishment was far greater than the fault, and that disproportion is what makes the ending so bitter. The real tragedy is that the necklace was only paste, so the ten years of suffering were wasted entirely.

✍️ Hands-on task

Write the plot in eight sentences with the two key figures in them, then two sentences arguing whether her suffering was deserved. Ten sentences is a complete 5-mark answer.
