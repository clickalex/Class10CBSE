Chapter 4 · From the Diary of Anne Frank

Chapter 4 of 33. Anne Frank's own words. Two essays and one teacher are the whole plot — but the voice is what is examined.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Reference-to-context extracts, usually from the opening passage about why she keeps a diary, or from the essay 'Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox'.
Know why she began the diary, why she needed a friend, and what Mr Keesing asked her to write about.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. Thirteen-year-old Anne Frank begins her diary, which she names Kitty, on her thirteenth birthday. She explains that 'paper has more patience than people': she has a loving family, an adoring father and mother, a sixteen-year-old sister Margot, about thirty friends and many admirers, but no true friend to whom she can confide her innermost thoughts. Her grandmother, whom she loved very much, died in January 1942; Anne's entry of June 1942 is followed by her family going into hiding in July. She is a student in a Jewish secondary school in Amsterdam.
2. The essays. Her mathematics teacher, Mr Keesing, is annoyed by her talking in class and assigns her an essay titled 'A Chatterbox'. Anne writes three pages and argues that talking is a student's trait and that she would try to keep it under control, but that she would never be able to cure herself of the habit since her mother talks as much as she does — and there is little one can do about inherited traits. Mr Keesing takes the joke in the right spirit. The next time she talks he assigns 'An Incorrigible Chatterbox'. The third time he sets 'Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox' as punishment, this time to be written in verse. Anne's friend Sanne helps, and Anne writes the poem about a mother duck and a father swan whose three ducklings are bitten to death because they quacked too much. Mr Keesing reads it to his own class and to another class, adds his own comments, and from then on allows Anne to talk in class and never gives her any extra homework. To Anne he is 'the nicest teacher' she has ever met.
3. Themes. The need for a friend and for a private voice; the gap between the self one shows and the self one feels; humour as a defence; and the shadow of the Jewish experience under Nazi rule, which the diary entries only hint at.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 'Paper has more patience than people' — the single most quoted line from this chapter; learn it exactly.
  • Three essays in order: A Chatterbox → An Incorrigible Chatterbox → Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox.
  • Anne argues her talking is an inherited trait from her mother — that argument is what wins Mr Keesing over.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Anne had no friends at all. She had about thirty friends and admirers, but no true friend to confide in.
  • Naming the diary 'Anne'. She named it Kitty.
  • Describing Mr Keesing as strict and unkind throughout. He turns out to be, in Anne's words, the nicest teacher.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Anne Frank feel the need for a diary?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because although she had a loving family, friends and admirers, she had no true friend — nobody to whom she could confide her innermost thoughts. She wrote that paper has more patience than people, so she began the diary as a substitute for a real friend, and named it Kitty.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What argument did Anne make in her first essay, 'A Chatterbox'?

Show Answer

Ans:

She argued that talking is a student's trait and that she would try to keep it under control, but that she would never be able to cure herself completely of the habit, because her mother talks as much as she does. She added that there is little one can do about inherited traits — an argument which Mr Keesing took in the right spirit.

LONGL35 marks

3. Describe Mr Keesing and how his attitude towards Anne changed.

Show Answer

Ans:

Mr Keesing was Anne's mathematics teacher, an old-fashioned teacher who was annoyed by her constant talking in class. He punished her by assigning an essay titled 'A Chatterbox'.
Anne's response: she wrote three pages, admitting the habit but arguing that talking is a student's trait and that her mother talks as much as she does, so it is an inherited trait and there is little one can do about it. Mr Keesing took the joke in the right spirit.
The second and third essays: when she talked again he assigned 'An Incorrigible Chatterbox', and the third time he set 'Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox' — this time in verse. With the help of her friend Sanne, Anne wrote a comic poem about a mother duck and a father swan whose three ducklings are bitten to death by the swan for quacking too much.
The change: Mr Keesing read the poem to his own class and then to another class, adding his own comments, and laughed at it. From then on he allowed Anne to talk in class and never gave her extra homework again. Anne calls him the nicest teacher she ever met.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What does Anne write about her grandmother?

Show Answer

Ans:

Anne writes that nobody knows how often she thinks of her grandmother and still loves her. Her grandmother was born in 1888 and died in January 1942, and Anne says that she wrote the diary partly so that this love would not be lost. She adds that this whole diary was written in the thought of her grandmother.

SHORTS103 marks

10. Why did Anne Frank decide to keep a diary?

Show Answer

Ans:

She had no real friend in whom she could confide; although she had many acquaintances, she could not discuss her innermost thoughts with them.
A diary cannot talk back, and she felt that paper has more patience than people.
She wanted to write out her deepest thoughts and feelings, and the diary became the friend she called Kitty.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. Describe Mr Keesing's punishment and how Anne used it to her advantage.

Show Answer

Ans:

Anne was caught talking in class, so the maths teacher Mr Keesing gave her extra homework: first an essay on ‘A Chatterbox', then ‘An Incorrigible Chatterbox', and then ‘Quack Quack Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox'.
Anne wrote the first essay arguing that talking is a student's trait which she inherited from her mother and could never cure.
For the second she argued that she could not stop talking in a maths class because it was impossible to keep quiet.
For the third she wrote a poem about a swan with three ducklings that bit their father to death, and illustrated it; Mr Keesing was amused, read the poem to the class and stopped punishing her.

✍️ Hands-on task

Write a one-page diary entry of your own in Anne's voice — starting with why you are writing it, as she does. The exercise forces you to notice that her opening is an argument, not an introduction, which is exactly what the extract question tests.
