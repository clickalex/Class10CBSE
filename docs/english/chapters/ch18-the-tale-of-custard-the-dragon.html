Chapter 18 · The Tale of Custard the Dragon

Chapter 18 of 33. A comic ballad with a clear moral. Know the four characters, their names, and who actually fought.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🛠️ Steps 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

Reference-to-context extracts; a 5-mark answer on the moral and on why Custard cried for a nice safe cage.
Belinda and her pets: Ink (a little black kitten), Blink (a little grey mouse), Mustard (a little yellow dog), and Custard the dragon.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The poem. Belinda lived in a little white house with a little black kitten, a little grey mouse, a little yellow dog, and a little pet dragon named Custard. Belinda was as brave as a barrel full of bears; Ink and Blink could chase lions down the stairs; Mustard was as brave as a tiger in a rage. But Custard cried for a nice safe cage. Then a pirate climbed in through the window, with a pistol in his hand and two cutlasses, and a black beard and one wooden leg. Mustard fled with a terrified cry; Ink trickled down to the bottom of the house; Blink disappeared into his mouse hole. Only Custard jumped up, snorting like an engine, clashing his tail like iron in a dungeon, and went at the pirate like a robin at a worm — and he ate the pirate, every bit. Belinda embraced him, Mustard licked him, and nobody wept for the pirate. Afterwards they all went back to boasting: Mustard said he would have been twice as brave without being flustered, Ink and Blink said they would have been three times as brave, and Belinda said she would have been as brave as the whole of them combined. Custard agreed that everyone was braver than he, and said he would still prefer a nice safe cage — and they all laughed, and the poem ends with the boast repeated.
2. Central idea. The poem is a humorous look at bravery and cowardice. Those who boast are the first to run away, and the one who admits his fear is the one who acts when it matters. But the poem is not a simple moral lesson — Custard himself goes back to wanting a safe cage, so the poet is also saying that being a coward is not a fault to be corrected.
3. Poetic devices. Ballad form — a story told in verse, with a regular rhythm and rhyme (aabb). Simile — 'brave as a barrel full of bears', 'as brave as a tiger in a rage', 'like a robin at a worm', 'snorting like an engine'. Repetition — the repeated boast at the end. Humour and irony — the boastful animals run away; the coward saves them.
Onomatopoeia — the sounds of the pirate and the dragon.

🛠️ Method, step by step

  1. Identify the form — a ballad, written in light, sing-song stanzas with a mock-heroic tone.
  2. Introduce the household: Belinda, Ink, Blink, Mustard and Custard, the dragon who is teased for wanting a nice safe cage.
  3. Recount the pirate episode — the others hide while Custard 'snorts like an engine' and eats the pirate.
  4. Close with the turn: Belinda embraces Custard and the others claim they would have been braver, while 'Custard the dragon went on wanting a nice safe cage' — the humour undercuts the heroics.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Four pets, four names: Ink, Blink, Mustard, Custard. Learn which animal is which colour.
  • The irony is the whole poem: the boasters flee, the coward fights.
  • Custard still wants a safe cage at the end — that is not a failure of the moral, it is the joke.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Making Mustard the grey mouse. Ink is the black kitten, Blink is the grey mouse, Mustard is the yellow dog.
  • Saying Custard killed the pirate with fire. He ate him, every bit.
  • Saying the other pets were grateful afterwards. They went straight back to boasting.

SHORTS53 marks

5. How does Custard prove himself when the pirate appears?

Show Answer

Ans:

While Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all run away in terror, Custard alone stands his ground.
He snorts, claps his sharp teeth, gulps some mustard at the pirate and charges at him like a robin at a worm.
The pirate fires two bullets that miss, and Custard swallows him in one bite, clashing his tail like irons in a dungeon.
The ‘cowardly' dragon turns out to be the only one with real courage, which is the joke of the poem.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How do Belinda and the other pets behave after Custard kills the pirate? What does their behaviour show?

Show Answer

Ans:

Belinda embraces Custard and Mustard licks him — but within a page they go back to boasting: Mustard says he would have been twice as brave, Ink and Blink say they would have been three times as brave, and Custard agrees with all of them.
Their behaviour shows that cowards mock true courage and then claim the credit afterwards — while the truly brave Custard keeps quiet and asks again for his nice safe cage.

LONGL94 marks

9. What makes ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon' a ballad of humour?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is written in the form of a ballad, with regular quatrains, a strong rhythm and a repeating refrain about the little pet dragon.
The names are comic — Belinda, Ink, Blink, Mustard and Custard — and the rhyme is deliberately playful, as in ‘realio, trulio, little pet dragon'.
The situation is upside down: the fierce-looking pets flee while the teased dragon fights, and the pirate is described as a dastardly coward.
The ending is comic too: once the danger is over, everyone goes back to boasting, and Custard meekly wishes for his nice safe cage again.

LONGL104 marks

10. ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon’ is called a ballad. Which features of a ballad do you find in it?

Show Answer

Ans:

A story in verse — the ballad narrates a complete tale with a beginning (the household), a crisis (the pirate) and an ending (the kill).
Music and repetition — the aabb rhyme, the rolling rhythm and repeated refrains like ‘coward, and she called him Custard’ make it singable.
A dramatic climax — the pirate's attack and Custard's sudden bravery give it the ballad's single moment of high action.

LONGL115 marks

11. What is the central idea of the poem? Is Custard a coward?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem is a humorous story about bravery and boasting. Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all boast about how brave they are, while Custard openly admits his fear and asks for a nice safe cage.
When the pirate actually appears, the boasters run away: Mustard flees with a terrified cry, Ink trickles to the bottom of the house, and Blink disappears into his mouse hole. Only Custard faces the pirate and eats him up, every bit.
The moral is that those who boast are not necessarily brave, and the one who admits his fear may be the bravest of all when action is needed. Bravery is shown in deeds, not in words.
Is Custard a coward? The poet leaves this open. Custard himself says he is a coward and would still prefer a nice safe cage, and the others all agree they are braver than he is. The humour lies in the fact that nobody changes their opinion, even after Custard has saved them. So the poem suggests that a person can be genuinely brave in action and still be timid by nature — the two are not contradictory.
