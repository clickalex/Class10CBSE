Chapter 21 · The Thief's Story

🎯 Marks lens

Short answers and a value-based question on why the thief came back.
Anil the struggling writer; the thief is fifteen and takes the name Hari Singh.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. The narrator is a fairly successful thief, still only fifteen but already experienced. He meets Anil, a tall, lean, easy-going young writer at a wrestling match, and decides to rob him. He gives his name as Hari Singh and changes it every month to escape the police. Anil takes him in as a servant, but he pays him irregularly and mostly in food, so there is little to steal. The narrator learns to write his name and to write whole sentences from Anil, and realises that once he can write like an educated man there will be no limit to what he can achieve. One night the thief steals six hundred rupees from under Anil's mattress and runs to the station to catch the Lucknow Express. As the train moves out, he finds he cannot board it — he has no ticket and no one to help him. He walks aimlessly in the rain, finds the stolen notes are wet and ruined, and realises that Anil will not be angry for the loss of money but for the loss of trust. He goes back, puts the damp notes under the mattress, and lies down. In the morning Anil gives him a fifty-rupee note and says he will pay him regularly; the note is still damp, but Anil says nothing. The narrator knows that Anil knew everything, and that he has been given a second chance.
2. Characters. The thief (Hari Singh) — young, experienced in petty theft, but still capable of shame; he is the narrator. Anil — a kind, careless, easy-going writer who trusts people, and whose trust is what reforms the boy.
3. Theme. Trust and kindness can reform a person where punishment cannot; and education opens a better path than crime.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • The thief returns because of loss of trust, not loss of money. That sentence is the standard 3-mark answer.
  • Anil knows the notes were stolen — he says nothing, which is the act of kindness.
  • The name Hari Singh changes every month to dodge the police.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Anil punished him. He says nothing and pays him regularly instead.
  • Saying the notes were safe. They were wet and ruined by the rain.
  • Describing Anil as rich. He is a struggling writer who paid irregularly.

❓ Exam Q&A

3 marks Why did the thief return to Anil?

Because while walking in the rain he realised that Anil would not be grieved by the loss of money — Anil was careless about money — but he would be grieved by the loss of trust. The thief understood that he had destroyed something more valuable than the six hundred rupees, and that realisation, along with the thought of what he was learning from Anil, brought him back.

3 marks What did the thief learn from Anil, and why was it important to him?

Anil taught him to write his name, and then to write whole sentences. The thief realised that once he could write like an educated man there would be no limit to what he could achieve — he could earn respectably instead of stealing small amounts. Education offered him a future that crime could not.

3 marks Did Anil know that the money had been stolen? How can you tell?

Yes, he knew. The fifty-rupee note he gave the thief in the morning was still damp from the rain, and Anil said nothing about it. His silence, and his decision to pay the boy regularly from that day, show that he understood what had happened and chose to give the boy a second chance rather than to accuse him.

✍️ Hands-on task

Write the moment on the platform in four sentences, then two sentences on why he turned back. Six sentences is a complete value-based answer.
