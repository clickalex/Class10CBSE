Chapter 7 · Madam Rides the Bus

Chapter 7 of 33. A child's independence and her first meeting with death — in one bus ride.

🎯 Marks lens

Extracts from the planning of the journey, or from the return trip when Valli sees the dead cow.
Know her age (eight years and some months), the fare (thirty paise each way), the journey time (forty-five minutes), and what she refused on the bus.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. Valliammai, called Valli for short, was an eight-year-old girl — eight years and some months — who lived in a village. Her favourite pastime was standing in the front doorway of her house and watching what was happening in the street outside, because there were no other children of her own age. The sight that filled her with unbelievable desire was the bus that travelled between her village and the nearest town. It was a new bus, painted a gleaming white, with silver overhead bars; when it started, it seemed to be coming towards them, then turned and went back to the town. Day after day she watched it, and she gathered details by listening to conversations: the town was six miles away, the fare was thirty paise one way, and the trip took forty-five minutes. She saved the money by resisting every temptation to buy peppermints, toys and balloons, and by staying away from the merry-go-round at the village fair.
2. The journey. Valli stood up on her seat so that she could see everything: the bus ran along the bank of a canal, past palm trees, grassland, distant mountains and a blue sky. She saw a young cow running in front of the bus; the driver sounded the horn again and again, and the cow turned and looked at them and then ran faster, which made Valli laugh until tears filled her eyes. At the town, the conductor offered her a cold drink, but she refused and asked only to be taken back on the same bus. He called her 'madam' throughout, and she insisted on being treated as an adult.
3. The return. On the way back, Valli saw the same young cow lying dead by the roadside, with her legs spread out, eyes lifeless and a fixed stare, and blood all over. She was horrified and had no desire to laugh any more. She did not want to look out of the window and sat glued to her seat. She reached her village at about 1:45 p.m., and found her mother awake and talking to an aunt. When her aunt asked where she had been, Valli's mother said she had been sleeping and had gone nowhere; Valli smiled to herself, because she had been so many places — and all without anyone knowing.
4. Themes. Growing up and independence — a child who plans and executes a journey alone. Curiosity — learning by listening and observing. The first encounter with death — the dead cow changes her, and the loss of innocence is the point of the ending.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Thirty paise each way, six miles, forty-five minutes. Those three numbers are the most common 1-mark items.
  • The cow appears twice — alive on the way out, dead on the way back. That contrast is the whole point of the story.
  • The conductor calls her 'madam' — hence the title.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Valli took the bus with her mother. She went alone; that is the point.
  • Giving her age as eight years. It is eight years and some months, which she is proud of.
  • Missing the ending: Valli does not tell anyone, and her mother still thinks she was asleep.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why was the bus journey such a fascination for Valli?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because she had never been to the town and had no companions of her own age, so the bus became her window on the world. Every day it passed her house twice, carrying new people each time, and she longed to travel on it. The sight of the gleaming white bus, with its silver overhead bars and the people inside, filled her with an unbelievable desire, and the thought of travelling to the town and back grew stronger and stronger until she saved enough money to do it.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What did Valli see on her way that made her laugh until tears filled her eyes?

Show Answer

Ans:

She saw a young cow whose tail was high in the air, running very fast right in the middle of the road in front of the bus. The driver sounded his horn again and again, but the more he honked the more frightened the cow became and the faster it ran. Valli found it so funny that she laughed and laughed until tears filled her eyes. On the return journey the same cow lay dead by the roadside, which filled her with horror and took away her joy.

LONGL35 marks

3. How did Valli plan her bus journey? What does this tell you about her?

Show Answer

Ans:

Planning: she listened carefully to the conversations of her neighbours and of people who regularly used the bus, and she asked a few discreet questions here and there. From this she learnt that the town was six miles from her village, that the fare was thirty paise one way, and that the trip took forty-five minutes.
Saving: she resisted every temptation to buy peppermints, toys and balloons, and finally she even overcame her urge to ride the merry-go-round at the village annual fair, so that she could save sixty paise in all.
Timing: she chose to travel on the one o'clock afternoon bus, when her mother took her daily nap, so that she could be back before her mother woke.
What it shows: Valli was observant, determined and capable of self-control far beyond her years. She was also proud of being grown up — she resented being treated as a child, and insisted that the conductor call her madam.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why did Valli refuse the cold drink offered by the conductor?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because she had only sixty paise with her and wanted to keep enough for the return journey. Even though she was thirsty, she refused the drink and asked only to be taken back on the same bus. It shows her discipline and her determination to complete the plan she had made.
