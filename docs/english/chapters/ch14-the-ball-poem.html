Chapter 14 · The Ball Poem

Chapter 14 of 33. A child loses a ball, and the poet refuses to comfort him — because loss is the lesson.

🎯 Marks lens

Reference-to-context extracts; a 5-mark answer on the boy's lesson and on the meaning of 'the epistemology of loss'.
Know that the ball cost a dime, that the poet does not offer a new ball, and the phrase 'epistemology of loss'.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The poem. The boy stands rigid, trembling, staring down at the harbour where his ball went. The poet does not intrude on him; he says he would not intrude on the boy, and that a dime, another ball, is worthless. The boy is learning the epistemology of loss — the knowledge of what it is to lose something. The poet says that money is external, and that the boy will learn to stand up, knowing that people will take balls and balls will always be lost. He stands there, rigid, trembling, staring down at where his ball went, and finally shakes himself and stands. The poem ends with the boy learning that he is now a part of the world of possessions that people keep losing.
2. Central idea. Loss is a universal human experience, and the first loss teaches us how to live in a world where things can be taken away. The poet refuses to buy another ball, because a new ball would only postpone the lesson. The boy must learn to stand up and accept loss — that is the first step in growing up.
3. Poetic devices. Free verse — no fixed rhyme or metre, which suits the reflective tone. Alliteration — 'bouncing ball', 'balls will be lost always'. Repetition — 'balls' is repeated to show that the loss is not unique. Symbolism — the ball stands for childhood, and its loss for the first experience of growing up. Enjambment — the sentences run on, mirroring the boy's thoughts.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 'Epistemology of loss' = the knowledge of how to lose things. Explain it in simple words and you get the mark.
  • 'Money is external' — a new ball can be bought, but the feeling of loss cannot.
  • The boy stands 'rigid, trembling, staring down' — three adjectives that describe his shock.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying the poet buys the boy a new ball. He does not, and explains why.
  • Saying the ball was expensive. It cost only a dime — which is the point.
  • Treating the poem as being about the ball. It is about loss and growing up.

1. Why does the poet say he would not intrude on the boy?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the boy is learning something important for the first time — the epistemology of loss, or the knowledge of what it is to lose something. Intruding on him, or offering a new ball, would spoil the lesson. The poet wants the boy to feel the loss fully and to learn to stand up on his own.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What does the poet mean by 'money is external'?

Show Answer

Ans:

He means that money can buy another ball, but it cannot buy back what has been lost or take away the feeling of loss. The material value of the ball is small — a dime — so what matters is not the object but the experience of losing it, which is something outside the reach of money.

LONGL35 marks

3. What is the boy learning, according to the poet? Explain the significance of the first loss.

Show Answer

Ans:

The boy is learning the epistemology of loss — the knowledge of what loss means and how to live with it. He stands rigid and trembling, staring down at the harbour where his ball went, and in that moment he understands that some things, once gone, cannot be brought back.
The poet says a dime, another ball, is worthless, because a new ball would not restore the one that is lost. Money is external, so it cannot help him here.
The boy learns that balls will be lost always — loss is a permanent feature of life, and everyone must learn to accept it. He learns to stand up, which means to accept the loss and go on.
The significance of the first loss is that it is the beginning of maturity. Until this moment the boy lived in a world where everything could be replaced; now he is part of a world of possessions in which things can be taken away. That realisation is painful, but it is also the first step towards becoming an adult who can bear responsibility and disappointment.
