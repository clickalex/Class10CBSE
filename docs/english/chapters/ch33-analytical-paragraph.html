Chapter 33 · Analytical Paragraph

Chapter 33 of 33. Ten marks for describing data you are given. Nothing has to be invented — the answer is in front of you.

Open this chapter’s questions + MCQs →

🎯 Marks lens 📖 Deep concepts 🧠 Tricks ⚠️ Mistakes ❓ Exam Q&A ✍️ Task

🎯 Marks lens

10 marks, 100–120 words. Based on a chart, graph, table, data set, outline or set of clues.
The marks go to accurate description, comparison and a concluding inference — not to opinions.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The structure. First sentence: introduce the data — what the chart shows, what period it covers and what units are used. Middle sentences: describe the main features, comparing and contrasting the significant figures. Last sentence: give a conclusion or a trend inferred from the data. Do not list every number — select the two or three that matter.
2. The language of description. Comparison: higher than, lower than, twice as much as, three times the figure for. Trend: rose steadily, fell sharply, remained constant, fluctuated, peaked in, reached a low of. Proportion: accounted for, made up, constituted, represented. Use these phrases rather than repeating 'it increased'.
3. What not to do. Do not give your opinion or a recommendation unless the question asks for one. Do not invent data that is not in the chart. Do not exceed the word limit. Do not write a story — this is a description, not a narrative.
4. Worked example. Given a table showing the percentage of households with internet access in 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025, a good paragraph would open: 'The table shows the percentage of households with internet access over a fifteen-year period from 2010 to 2025.' It would then compare the figures, noting the sharpest rise and any period of stagnation, and close with the overall trend and its implication.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Open with what the chart shows, then compare, then conclude. Three moves, one paragraph.
  • Pick three figures, not ten. Examiners reward selection, not transcription.
  • Learn six comparison phrases and use them. Repeating 'it increased' loses language marks.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Copying every number from the table.
  • Adding an opinion or a recommendation that was not asked for.
  • Exceeding 120 words.
  • Writing 'the chart is very interesting' — description, not comment, is what is rewarded.

LONGL25 marks

2. Write an analytical paragraph on a bar chart showing the mode of transport used by students: bus 45%, cycle 25%, walk 20%, private car 10%.

Show Answer

Ans:

The bar chart shows how students travel to school, expressed as a percentage of the total number of students.
The most common mode of transport is the bus, used by 45 per cent of students — almost half. Cycling is the second most popular, at 25 per cent, while 20 per cent of students walk to school. Only 10 per cent travel by private car, the least common mode.
Taken together, the figures show that the great majority of students — 90 per cent — use public or non-motorised transport, and that reliance on private cars is minimal. This suggests that improving bus services and cycle lanes would benefit far more students than any measure aimed at car users.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What should an analytical paragraph avoid?

Show Answer

Ans:

It should avoid giving personal opinions or recommendations unless the question asks for them, inventing data that is not in the chart, listing every figure instead of selecting the significant ones, and exceeding the word limit. It should describe and compare the data, not comment on it.
