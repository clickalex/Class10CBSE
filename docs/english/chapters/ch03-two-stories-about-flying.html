Chapter 3 · Two Stories about Flying

Chapter 3 of 33. Two stories, one theme: fear overcome. The seagull is the more frequently examined of the two.

🎯 Marks lens

Extracts come from either story; know both well enough to identify which one is quoted.
His First Flight — Liam O'Flaherty, a young seagull afraid to fly. The Black Aeroplane — Frederick Forsyth, a pilot in a storm helped by a mysterious black aeroplane.

📖 Deep concepts

1. His First Flight — summary. A young seagull is alone on his ledge, afraid to fly, while his two brothers and sister have already flown away the day before. His parents have scolded him and threatened to let him starve on the ledge unless he flies. His siblings taunt him by calling him 'Ga-ga'. After twenty-four hours of hunger and fear, his mother tears a piece of fish and stands on the ledge, and the sight of the food maddens him. He dives at her, his hunger overcomes his fear, and he falls outward into space. The air rushes against his feathers, he flaps his wings and finds himself soaring. His family screams around him, praising him and offering scraps of fish. He then flies with them, sinks tired onto the water, and though afraid at first, learns to float — his first flight is complete.
2. The Black Aeroplane — summary. The narrator, flying an old Dakota aeroplane from France to England to spend a breakfast of bacon and eggs with his family, flies into a storm. The clouds are black, his compass and radio fail, and he has only enough fuel for five or ten minutes. He sees a black aeroplane with no lights on its wings, and the pilot waves for him to follow. He follows it through the storm until he runs out of fuel, and then the black plane disappears. He lands safely and asks the woman in the control centre where the other pilot is. She says the sky was empty — there was no other aeroplane that night, and no other plane on the radar. The narrator looks up and wonders who helped him.
3. Common theme. Both stories are about facing fear and finding the strength to overcome it. The seagull is helped by hunger and by his mother's example; the pilot is helped — or so he believes — by another pilot. Both suggest that the help we need is sometimes already within us.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Twenty-four hours on the ledge is the key number in His First Flight.
  • The seagull's fear breaks when he sees the fish, not when his parents scold him.
  • In The Black Aeroplane, the control-centre woman says there was no other plane — that ambiguity is the point of the ending.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Confusing the two authors: O'Flaherty wrote His First Flight, Forsyth wrote The Black Aeroplane.
  • Saying the seagull flew because his father threatened him. It was hunger and the sight of food.
  • Naming the Dakota a fighter plane. It is an old Dakota carrying the narrator home.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why was the young seagull afraid to fly? What compelled him to finally make the flight?

Show Answer

Ans:

He was afraid because he felt certain that his wings would never support him, and because his siblings had already flown while he was left behind on the ledge. What compelled him to fly was hunger: when his mother tore a piece of fish and stood on the ledge, the sight of the food maddened him, and he dived at it. In that dive he fell outward into space and, flapping his wings, found that he could fly.

Show Answer

Ans:

When his instruments failed and he ran low on fuel, he saw a black aeroplane with no lights on its wings. Its pilot waved to him and led him through the storm until the narrator came out of the clouds and could see the runway. When he landed and asked the woman in the control centre about the other pilot, she told him there had been no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on her radar.

LONGL35 marks

3. 'Both the stories are about overcoming fear.' Justify with reference to the two stories.

Show Answer

Ans:

In His First Flight, the young seagull is paralysed by fear while his brothers and sister fly. His parents scold him and his siblings taunt him, but fear keeps him on the ledge for twenty-four hours. Only extreme hunger, and the sight of his mother with a piece of fish, drive him to dive — and in that desperate moment he discovers that his wings do support him. His fear is overcome by necessity and by the example of his family.
In The Black Aeroplane, the narrator is an experienced pilot, but the storm terrifies him: the compass and radio fail, he cannot see anything, and he has only a few minutes of fuel left. His fear is overcome by following the mysterious black aeroplane, whose pilot leads him out of the storm and vanishes. He never finds out who helped him, which suggests that the courage came from within.
In both stories, fear is conquered only by taking the leap — the seagull by diving, the pilot by following into the dark — and in both the reward is a safe arrival.

SHORTS43 marks

4. How did the seagull's family react when he flew for the first time?

Show Answer

Ans:

They screamed around him, praising him and offering him scraps of fish. They landed ahead of him on the water, beckoning him to join them, and when he finally sank tired onto the sea and floated, they praised him again — his first flight was complete.
