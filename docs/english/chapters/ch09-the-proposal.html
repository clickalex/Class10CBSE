Chapter 9 · The Proposal

Chapter 9 of 33. A play, not a story. Learn who says what, and never lose sight of the fact that the marriage proposal is never actually discussed.

🎯 Marks lens

Extracts with a question on character or on the comedy; the two quarrels are the set 5-mark answer.
Know the three characters, the two disputes (the Oxen Meadows, then Squeezer and Guess), and the ages (Lomov is 35, Natalya is 25).

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. Stepan Stepanovich Chubukov, a wealthy landowner, is sitting in his drawing room in his house clothes. Ivan Vassilevitch Lomov, his neighbour, arrives dressed in evening clothes with gloves. Chubukov assumes he has come to borrow money, and complains that Lomov always comes to him for a loan. Lomov, nervous and stammering, asks permission to speak. Chubukov embraces him, thinking he has come to borrow money. Lomov then says that he has come to trouble him with a request — and Chubukov again thinks of money. Finally Lomov says that he has come to ask for the hand of Natalya Stepanovna, Chubukov's daughter. Chubukov is overjoyed, embraces and kisses him, and calls Natalya in. Before he can propose, Lomov and Natalya fall into a quarrel about the Oxen Meadows: Natalya claims the meadows belong to her, and Lomov insists they are his, saying his aunt's grandmother gave the free use of the meadows in perpetuity to the peasants of Chubukov's grandfather in return for making bricks. The argument grows, Chubukov joins in and insults Lomov, and Lomov leaves in a rage. Chubukov then reveals that Lomov had actually come to propose, and Natalya, realising this, begs her father to bring him back. Lomov returns, half-fainting, and Chubukov hurries the two into marriage. But before the proposal is settled, a second quarrel breaks out about hunting dogs — whether Squeezer (Lomov's dog) or Guess (Chubukov's) is the better dog — and the two men abuse each other again. Lomov collapses, and Chubukov blesses the couple: 'She's willing! Well, I'm sure enough!' The play ends with the proposal achieved and the argument still going on.
2. Characters. Lomov — 35, a hypochondriac who suffers from palpitations, a twitching eyebrow and sleeplessness; timid, easily flustered and argumentative. Chubukov — loud, short-tempered and money-minded, but ready to push his daughter into a marriage. Natalya Stepanovna — 25, spoiled, quarrelsome and more interested in winning an argument than in marriage.
3. The comedy. The humour comes from the contrast between the occasion and the behaviour: a marriage proposal becomes a shouting match about a piece of land and then about a dog. Nobody ever speaks about love. Chekhov is satirising the landowning class, for whom property and possessions matter more than human feeling.
4. Themes. The absurdity of the marriage market; greed and property; petty pride; and the fact that people in a hurry to marry are not necessarily in love.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Two quarrels, one proposal that is never discussed. That is the whole play in one line.
  • Lomov's ailments: palpitations, twitching eyebrow, sleeplessness, numbness in the leg — a hypochondriac.
  • The Oxen Meadows argument is about free use in perpetuity granted by an aunt's grandmother — the absurd chain of ownership is part of the joke.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Calling The Proposal a story by G. L. Fuentes. It is a one-act play by Anton Chekhov.
  • Reversing the order of the quarrels: Oxen Meadows first, then the dogs.
  • Saying Chubukov refuses the match. He is delighted and pushes it through, blessing the pair at the end.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why does Chubukov think Lomov has come to him?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because Lomov had come to him the previous year to borrow money, and Chubukov expects the same again. He even complains that Lomov is always coming to him for a loan, and when he hears that Lomov has a request, he assumes it is for money. Only when Lomov asks for Natalya's hand does Chubukov understand.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is the Oxen Meadows quarrel about?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is about who owns the Oxen Meadows, which lie between Lomov's Burnt Marsh and Chubukov's birchwood. Natalya insists the meadows are hers; Lomov says they are his, arguing that his aunt's grandmother gave the peasants of Chubukov's grandfather the free use of the meadows in perpetuity in return for their making bricks for her. The peasants had used them for forty years and regarded them as their own, but neither side will give way, and the argument turns into an exchange of insults.

LONGL35 marks

3. How does Chekhov use the two quarrels to create comedy and to criticise the characters?

Show Answer

Ans:

The first quarrel, about the Oxen Meadows, begins before Lomov has even made his proposal. Natalya claims the meadows are hers, Lomov insists they are his, and both become abusive, calling each other a land grabber and an intriguess. Chubukov joins in, calls Lomov an old rat and a Jesuit, and orders him out of the house. The absurdity is that a marriage proposal has become a land dispute, and that Chubukov does not know what the proposal was until after the quarrel.
The second quarrel, about the dogs, follows immediately. Lomov praises his dog Squeezer; Chubukov insists his own Guess is better, and they argue over whether Squeezer's muzzle is short, whether Guess is purebred, and whether Squeezer's legs are crooked. The two men become hysterical and Lomov collapses.
The criticism: Chekhov shows that for these landowners, property and possessions matter far more than human relationships. Neither Lomov nor Natalya says a word about love or about each other's feelings, and the marriage is arranged in the middle of an argument. The comedy exposes a class that treats marriage as a transaction.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Describe Lomov's character.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lomov is a thirty-five-year-old landowner who is timid, nervous and easily flustered. He is a hypochondriac: he complains of palpitations, a twitching right eyebrow, sleeplessness and numbness in his leg. He is also stubborn and quick to quarrel, which is why a marriage proposal turns into two arguments. Yet he is sincere in his wish to settle down, and he finally gets what he came for.

SHORTS53 marks

5. How does the play end? Comment on the ending.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lomov collapses after the argument about the dogs, and Chubukov, without waiting for anything to be settled, blesses the couple and cries that Natalya is willing, calling for champagne. Natalya does not object, and the proposal is agreed. The ending is comic because the marriage is decided in the middle of a quarrel that neither side has stopped fighting — nobody has spoken a word about love, and the audience is left to see how absurd the whole arrangement is.
