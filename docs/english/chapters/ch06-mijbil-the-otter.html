Chapter 6 · Mijbil the Otter

Chapter 6 of 33. A true account of keeping an otter as a pet. The details — the water, the suitcase, the plane — are what is asked.

🎯 Marks lens

Reference-to-context extracts and a long answer on Maxwell's feelings towards Mijbil or on the otter's habits.
Know where Maxwell was (Basra, Iraq), why he wanted an otter, what Mijbil did on the aeroplane, and what the Londoners thought he was.

📖 Deep concepts

1. Summary. In 1956 the author, Gavin Maxwell, travelled to Basra in Iraq to the Consulate-General to collect and answer his mail from Europe. His pet otter had died, and his friend suggested he get an otter from the Tigris marshes. Two Arabs brought him a small otter in a sack — a creature he had never seen before, resembling most of all a small, medievally-conceived dragon. He named it Mijbil, and scientists later classified it as a previously unrecognised species, Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli, or Maxwell's otter. Mijbil spent most of his time playing in the bathroom water, plunging and rolling in it, and shuffling on the tiled floor. When they went to London, Mijbil escaped from the box on the aeroplane, ran along the aircraft, and tore the lining to shreds, so Maxwell had to catch him — Mijbil came and sat on his knee and nuzzled his face. In London, Mijbil invented games with marbles, ping-pong balls and rubber fruit, and would play with them for hours. He learned to walk on a lead and developed compulsive habits, such as jumping over a low wall and running along the boundary wall of a school, where the children made noise. Londoners, who had never seen an otter, guessed that it was 'a baby seal', 'a squirrel', 'a walrus', 'a hippo', 'a beaver', 'a bear cub', 'a leopard' or even 'a brontosaurus'. Maxwell reflects that Mijbil seemed to have certain characteristics: he was, like a child, both restless and full of joy, and he had a strong need for the company and affection of human beings.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Water is everything: Mijbil's joy was plunging and rolling in water — 'he would screw the tap up and then play in the trickle'.
  • The London guesses list is a favourite 1-mark MCQ: seal, squirrel, walrus, hippo, beaver, bear cub, leopard, brontosaurus.
  • Maxwell's own conclusion: Mijbil was like a child — restless, joyful and needing company.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying Maxwell found Mijbil in the wild himself. Two Arabs brought him the otter in a sack.
  • Placing the story in Kerala, because of the Tigris marshes confusion. It begins in Basra, Iraq.
  • Forgetting that the species was named after Maxwell — Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli.

❓ Exam Q&A

3 marks What things does Mijbil do which tell you that he is an intelligent, friendly and fun-loving animal who needs love?

He invented games with marbles, ping-pong balls and rubber fruit, and could keep himself amused for hours. He learned to walk on a lead and to respond to his name. He escaped from his box on the aeroplane, but instead of running away he came back and sat on Maxwell's knee and nuzzled his face — showing affection rather than fear. He developed compulsive rituals of his own, like the wall-jumping routine he repeated every day on the way to school, which shows both intelligence and a need for company.

3 marks Why did Maxwell think the air-hostess was 'the very queen of her kind'?

Because when he told her about Mijbil and the trouble he expected on the flight, she was extremely friendly and helpful. She suggested that he keep the box on his knee, and her kindness made a difficult journey possible — so he called her the very queen of her kind.

5 marks Describe the journey from Basra to London and the trouble Mijbil caused on the aeroplane.

Maxwell had to travel to London with Mijbil, and the airline insisted that the otter be packed in a box not more than eighteen inches square, to be carried on the floor near his feet.
Thirty minutes before the flight, the box was lined with metal and Mijbil was put inside. When the plane took off, Mijbil tore the lining to shreds and escaped. He ran at high speed down the aircraft, and passengers cried out; a girl screamed 'A rat! A rat!'
Maxwell dived across the legs of other passengers to catch him, but Mijbil disappeared. When he returned to his seat he found the air-hostess with the otter on her knee. She had caught him and held him.
Mijbil then came back to Maxwell, sat on his knee and began to nuzzle his face and his neck, so the journey ended with the otter calm again, though the box was in tatters.

3 marks What guesses did the Londoners make about what Mijbil was?

A Londoner who had never seen an otter made wild guesses: that it was a baby seal, a squirrel, a walrus, a hippo, a beaver, a bear cub, a leopard — one even said it was a brontosaurus. None of them recognised the animal, because otters were unknown in London.

✍️ Hands-on task

List six things Mijbil does that show intelligence and six that show affection, in two columns. Then write one sentence linking both to Maxwell's conclusion that he was like a child — that single sentence is worth two marks in the exam.
