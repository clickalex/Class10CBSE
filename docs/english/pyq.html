CBSE • CLASS 10 • ENGLISH

English Language & Literature — PYQ trends

Where past papers keep returning, chapter by chapter. Use it to decide revision order — not to guess the paper.

Trend notes below are an original reading of publicly available past papers and sample papers. They are not official CBSE predictions.

ChapterTitleWhat past papers ask
Ch 1A Letter to GodThe two ironies and the 'bunch of crooks' line are asked in almost every paper; the postmaster's character is the recurring short answer.
Ch 2Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to FreedomThe meaning of freedom and how it changed with age is the set 5-mark answer; the twin obligations and the definition of courage recur as short answers.
Ch 3Two Stories about FlyingExtracts from His First Flight are more frequent; the comparison of the two stories as studies in overcoming fear is the standard 5-mark answer.
Ch 4From the Diary of Anne FrankThe reason Anne began the diary and Mr Keesing's three essays alternate as the set questions; the 'paper has more patience than people' line recurs as a short extract.
Ch 5Glimpses of IndiaOne of the three parts is examined each year; the Coorg extract is the most frequent, with the Goan baker's role in family celebrations close behind.
Ch 6Mijbil the OtterThe aeroplane episode and the Londoners' guesses alternate as the set extract; Mijbil's play with water and marbles is the recurring 3-mark answer.
Ch 7Madam Rides the BusThe planning of the journey and the return trip with the dead cow alternate as the set questions; Valli's self-control in saving the fare recurs as a value-based question.
Ch 8The Sermon at BenaresKisa Gotami's search for the mustard seed and the Buddha's teaching on grief alternate as the set 5-mark answer; the four sights and the two similes recur as short answers.
Ch 9The ProposalThe two quarrels and their comic effect is the set 5-mark answer; Chubukov's character and the absurdity of the ending recur as short questions.
Ch 10Dust of SnowThe central idea and the significance of the crow and hemlock tree are asked almost every year; the poem is short, so the extract is usually two to four lines.
Ch 11Fire and IceThe symbolism of fire and ice is asked in nearly every paper; the rhyme scheme is the usual 1-mark item.
Ch 12A Tiger in the ZooThe contrast between the cage and the forest, and the significance of the last stanza, alternate as the set questions; 'quiet rage' recurs as a 1-mark explanation.
Ch 13How to Tell Wild AnimalsOne stanza is chosen as an extract each year — most often the Bengal tiger or the bear — and the humour of the poem is the standard 3-mark question.
Ch 14The Ball PoemThe meaning of 'the epistemology of loss' and why the poet does not offer a new ball alternate as the set questions.
Ch 15Amanda!Whether Amanda is an orphan, why she wants to be Rapunzel, and the theme of parental control alternate as the set questions.
Ch 16The TreesThe message about deforestation and the comparison of the boughs to discharged patients alternate as the set questions; the empty forest recurs as a short extract.
Ch 17FogThe poem is short, so the question is almost always on the central metaphor and the figure of speech; it is a reliable two to three marks.
Ch 18The Tale of Custard the DragonThe central idea and whether Custard is a coward alternate as the set 5-mark answers; the pirate's description and the animals' boasting recur as extracts.
Ch 19For Anne GregoryThe metaphor of the ramparts and the central question about loving a person for themselves alone alternate as the set questions.
Ch 20A Triumph of SurgeryThe cause of Tricki's illness and the vet's treatment are asked almost every year; the value-based question on Mrs Pumphrey's wisdom recurs.
Ch 21The Thief's StoryWhy the thief came back, and whether Anil knew, alternate as the set questions; the value of education to the thief recurs.
Ch 22The Midnight VisitorHow Ausable got rid of Max and the difference between Ausable and the usual spy image alternate as the set questions.
Ch 23A Question of TrustHow the woman tricked Horace and why he was arrested alternate as the set questions; the value-based question on whether she was right recurs.
Ch 24Footprints without FeetHow Griffin became invisible and the events at the Iping inn alternate as the set questions; the brilliant-versus-lawless judgement is the standard 5-mark answer.
Ch 25The Making of a ScientistThe role of Ebright's mother, the gold-spot discovery, and the three qualities of a scientist rotate as the set questions.
Ch 26The NecklaceWhy Matilda was unhappy, the effect of the loss, and whether her suffering was deserved rotate as the set questions; the ironic ending is quoted in almost every answer.
Ch 27BholiThe teacher's role and Bholi's refusal of the marriage alternate as the set questions; the value-based question on girls' education or dowry recurs.
Ch 28The Book That Saved the EarthHow the book saved the Earth and Think-Tank's character alternate as the set questions; the three misreadings recur as short answers.
Ch 29Reading: Unseen and Case-based PassagesThe pattern is stable: one discursive passage and one case-based passage with visual input, all objective. The skill is speed and elimination, not knowledge.
Ch 30Note Making and SummarisingThe task is stable: a passage, a set of notes and an 80-word summary. The marks lost are almost always format marks, not content marks.
Ch 31Grammar in ContextThe topics are fixed — tenses, modals, subject-verb agreement, reported speech and determiners — and are tested inside a passage or dialogue, so the skill is recognising the pattern in context.
Ch 32Formal Letter WritingOne formal letter is set every year, alternating between a letter to the editor on a civic issue and a letter of order, enquiry or complaint. The marks lost are format marks and word-limit marks.
Ch 33Analytical ParagraphThe task is stable: a chart, graph, table or set of clues, and a 100–120 word paragraph. Marks go to selection, comparison and a concluding inference.
