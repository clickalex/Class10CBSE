Ch 1 · A Letter to God (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. Lencho asked God for: (a) one hundred pesos (b) fifty pesos (c) seventy pesos (d) one hundred and fifty pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) one hundred pesos, to sow his field again and to live until the coming harvest.

MCQM21 mark

2. The letter Lencho received was signed: (a) the postmaster (b) the employees of the post office (c) the President (d) God

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) God — the postmaster signed it that way so that Lencho's faith would not be shaken.

MCQM31 mark

3. The writer of the story 'A Letter to God' is: (a) Nelson Mandela (b) G. L. Fuentes (c) Liam O'Flaherty (d) Betty Renshaw

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) G. L. Fuentes.

MCQM41 mark

4. When the postmaster first read Lencho’s letter, he: (a) tore it up (b) laughed, and then became serious (c) gave it to the police (d) ignored it

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) laughed, and then became serious. He laughed at the idea of a letter to God, but Lencho’s faith moved him and he decided to answer it.

MCQM51 mark

5. In his second letter, Lencho described the post-office employees as: (a) kind-hearted men (b) God’s messengers (c) a bunch of crooks (d) his relatives

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) a bunch of crooks. He believed they had kept the missing thirty pesos, though they had actually collected the seventy.

MCQM61 mark

6. Lencho asked God for: (a) 30 pesos (b) 70 pesos (c) 100 pesos (d) 1000 pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 100 pesos. He asked for a hundred pesos to sow his field again and live until the harvest.

MCQM71 mark

7. The postmaster and his staff managed to collect: (a) 70 pesos (b) 100 pesos (c) 30 pesos (d) 700 pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 70 pesos. They contributed from their own salaries, and the postmaster put in the rest of his own money.

MCQM81 mark

8. The postmaster collected the seventy pesos by: (a) asking the government (b) borrowing from a bank (c) selling his watch (d) giving part of his salary and asking employees and friends

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) giving part of his salary and asking employees and friends. The money was charity collected peso by peso.

Ch 2 · Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. Mandela was sworn in as South Africa's first black President on: (a) 10 May 1994 (b) 10 May 1995 (c) 26 April 1994 (d) 27 April 1993

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 10 May 1994, at the Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria.

MCQM21 mark

2. How many national anthems were sung at the inauguration? (a) one (b) two (c) four (d) three

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) three — 'Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika' and 'Morena' by the blacks, 'Die Stem' the old anthem, and the new combined anthem.

MCQM31 mark

3. According to Mandela, courage is: (a) the absence of fear (b) the triumph over fear (c) the denial of pain (d) the love of danger

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) the triumph over fear — the brave man conquers fear rather than not feeling it.

MCQM41 mark

4. Mandela’s inauguration took place in the amphitheatre formed by the: (a) Union Buildings in Pretoria (b) Parliament House in Cape Town (c) Robben Island prison (d) Johannesburg Stadium

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Union Buildings in Pretoria. The ceremony was held at the Union Buildings, the seat of government in Pretoria.

MCQM51 mark

5. According to Mandela, courage is: (a) the absence of fear (b) anger at one’s enemies (c) the triumph over fear, not the absence of fear (d) a gift that few people are born with

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) the triumph over fear, not the absence of fear. He says the brave man is not the one who does not feel afraid but the one who conquers that fear.

MCQM61 mark

6. The policy of racial discrimination followed in South Africa was called: (a) satyagraha (b) apartheid (c) segregation by choice (d) democracy

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) apartheid. It kept the black majority out of government, jobs, land and even public places.

MCQM71 mark

7. Mandela's autobiography is titled: (a) The Struggle Is My Life (b) The Prison Diary (c) Long Walk to Freedom (d) Invictus

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Long Walk to Freedom. The extract in the textbook is taken from it.

MCQM81 mark

8. Mandela says the oppressor is not free because: (a) he is in prison (b) he is poor (c) he is uneducated (d) he is a prisoner of hatred and prejudice

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) he is a prisoner of hatred and prejudice. The oppressor is robbed of his humanity as surely as the oppressed.

Ch 3 · Two Stories about Flying (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. 'His First Flight' is written by: (a) Frederick Forsyth (b) Liam O'Flaherty (c) G. L. Fuentes (d) Gavin Maxwell

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Liam O'Flaherty.

MCQM21 mark

2. How long was the young seagull left alone on his ledge before he flew? (a) twelve hours (b) two days (c) twenty-four hours (d) three days

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) twenty-four hours, during which he was hungry and afraid while his siblings flew.

MCQM31 mark

3. In 'The Black Aeroplane', the woman in the control centre told the narrator that: (a) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night (b) the other pilot had landed elsewhere (c) the black plane was a military aircraft (d) the radar had failed

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on the radar.

MCQM41 mark

4. “The Black Aeroplane” was written by: (a) Liam O’Flaherty (b) Anton Chekhov (c) Frederick Forsyth (d) Gavin Maxwell

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Frederick Forsyth. Frederick Forsyth wrote The Black Aeroplane; Liam O’Flaherty wrote His First Flight.

MCQM51 mark

5. The young seagull finally flew when he: (a) was pushed off the ledge by his father (b) saw a storm approaching (c) followed another bird over the sea (d) dived at the piece of fish his mother held

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) dived at the piece of fish his mother held. Hunger overcame his fear, and in diving for the fish he found that he could fly.

MCQM61 mark

6. In ‘His First Flight', the young seagull's mother tempted him with: (a) a piece of fish (b) a worm (c) a leaf (d) her call alone

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) a piece of fish. Hunger overcame his fear and he dived for it.

MCQM71 mark

7. The narrator of ‘The Black Aeroplane' was flying his Dakota from: (a) London to Paris (b) Paris to London (c) New York to Paris (d) Dakar to Paris

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Paris to London. He wanted to reach England for breakfast with his family.

MCQM81 mark

8. The young seagull was afraid to fly because: (a) he hated his family (b) he was ill (c) the weather was bad (d) he felt his wings would not support him over the great depth below

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) he felt his wings would not support him over the great depth below. Fear of falling kept him on the ledge for twenty-four hours.

Ans:

(b) Kitty — she treated the diary as the true friend she did not have.

Ans:

(d) Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox, and this time it had to be written in verse.

3. The line 'paper has more patience than people' means that: (a) a diary will listen without judging or tiring (b) paper lasts longer than people (c) people are impatient with writing (d) diaries are more valuable than friends

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Kitty. She addressed her diary as Kitty, the friend she did not have.

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) “Quack, Quack, Quack, Said Mistress Chatterbox”. The third essay was a humorous poem about a mother duck and her ducklings, which won Mr Keesing over.

MCQM61 mark

6. Anne Frank named her diary: (a) Kitty (b) Margot (c) Lies (d) Sanne

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Kitty. She addressed all her entries to this imaginary friend.

MCQM71 mark

7. Anne and her family went into hiding in: (a) Berlin (b) Paris (c) Frankfurt (d) Amsterdam

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Amsterdam. They hid in the secret annexe behind her father's office on the Prinsengracht.

MCQM81 mark

8. Anne's grandmother died in: (a) July 1942 (b) January 1942 (c) June 1941 (d) December 1942

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) January 1942. Anne writes that no one could understand her serious illness, and her birthday candles were lit in her memory.

(b) Kuppia — its resemblance to the kuffia of the Arabs and Kurds supports the Arab-origin theory.

MCQM21 mark

2. According to the chapter, how many cups of tea are drunk every day throughout the world? (a) eight crore (b) eight hundred crore (c) eight lakh (d) eighty crore

(d) eighty crore cups a day.

3. In Goa, the sweet bread given as a marriage gift is called: (a) pao (b) bolinhas (c) bol (d) kabai

Ans:

4. The sweet bread that is made in Goa for weddings is called: (a) bolinhas (b) pao (c) bol (d) kabai

5. Coorg lies midway between which two cities? (a) Mysore and Mangalore (b) Bengaluru and Chennai (c) Mumbai and Pune (d) Kochi and Madurai

Show Answer

(a) Mysore and Mangalore. Coorg, or Kodagu, is a hilly district of Karnataka between Mysore and the coastal town of Mangalore.

Ans:

(b) coffee plantations and oranges. The Coorgis are also known for their hospitality and their Kodava martial traditions.

MCQM81 mark

8. The finest tea is picked during: (a) the second flush, from May to July (b) the first flush (c) winter (d) the monsoon

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the second flush, from May to July. This is when the best Assam tea comes to the market.

Ch 6 · Mijbil the Otter (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. Mijbil was scientifically classified as: (a) Lutra lutra (b) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli (c) Aonyx cinereus (d) Enhydra lutris

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli — a previously unrecognised species, named Maxwell's otter after Gavin Maxwell.

MCQM21 mark

2. Where did the author collect Mijbil from? (a) the Tigris marshes near Basra (b) Kerala (c) the Nile delta (d) Assam

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the Tigris marshes near Basra in Iraq, where two Arabs brought him the otter in a sack.

MCQM31 mark

3. On the aeroplane Mijbil: (a) slept throughout (b) escaped from his box and tore the lining to shreds (c) bit the air-hostess (d) refused to eat

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) — he escaped, ran down the aircraft and tore the box lining; later he returned to sit on Maxwell's knee.

MCQM41 mark

4. Mijbil was brought to Maxwell at: (a) London in England (b) Kochi in Kerala (c) Basra in Iraq (d) Cairo in Egypt

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Basra in Iraq. The otter came from the Tigris marshes and was handed to Maxwell at Basra.

MCQM51 mark

5. The subspecies of otter that Maxwell’s Mijbil belonged to was named: (a) Lutra lutra mijbili (b) Aonyx cinerea basrae (c) Enhydra lutris maxwelli (d) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli. Zoologists named the new subspecies after Maxwell, who had brought it to light.

MCQM61 mark

6. Mijbil was an otter of the species found in: (a) the Amazon (b) the Himalayas (c) Africa (d) the marshes of southern Iraq

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) the marshes of southern Iraq. Maxwell named him Mijbil and trained him to obey a lead.

MCQM71 mark

7. The author of ‘Mijbil the Otter' is: (a) Gavin Maxwell (b) Gerald Durrell (c) Ruskin Bond (d) James Herriot

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Gavin Maxwell. The extract is from his book Ring of Bright Water.

MCQM81 mark

8. Mijbil loved to play with: (a) sticks and stones (b) paper boats (c) marbles and ping-pong balls (d) balloons

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) marbles and ping-pong balls. He juggled them lying on his back and invented compulsive games with them.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) eight years and some months — a detail she is very conscious of.

MCQM21 mark

2. The fare from Valli's village to the town was: (a) twenty paise (b) thirty paise (c) fifty paise (d) one rupee

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) thirty paise one way, and the journey took forty-five minutes over six miles.

MCQM31 mark

3. The conductor called Valli: (a) child (b) sister (c) little girl (d) madam

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) madam — hence the title of the story.

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) her mother was taking an afternoon nap. She waited for her mother’s afternoon sleep so that she could go without being noticed.

MCQM51 mark

5. On the return journey Valli saw the cow: (a) grazing in a field (b) standing on the bus (c) being driven home (d) lying dead by the roadside

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) lying dead by the roadside. The cow that had made her laugh had been killed by a fast vehicle.

MCQM61 mark

6. Valli was: (a) ten (b) twelve (c) eight years old (d) six

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) eight years old. For all her smallness she was a determined and careful planner.

MCQM71 mark

7. Valli saved what amount for her bus journey? (a) 60 paise (b) 30 paise (c) one rupee (d) two rupees

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 60 paise. Thirty paise each way, for the round trip from her village to the town and back.

MCQM81 mark

8. Valli travelled to the town on the: (a) morning bus (b) one o'clock bus (c) evening bus (d) night bus

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) one o'clock bus. She timed it so that she would be back by about a quarter to four, unseen.

Ch 8 · The Sermon at Benares (8)

MCQM11 mark

1. The Buddha asked Kisa Gotami to bring mustard seed from a house where: (a) no one had died (b) there was no poverty (c) everyone was happy (d) there were no children

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) no one had died — a condition no house could meet.

MCQM21 mark

2. The Buddha gave his first sermon at: (a) Bodh Gaya (b) Kushinagar (c) Kapilavastu (d) Sarnath, near Benares

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Sarnath, near Benares, on the banks of the Ganges.

MCQM31 mark

3. The prince Siddhartha Gautama was enlightened under a: (a) fig (Bo) tree (b) sal tree (c) banyan tree (d) mango tree

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) fig (Bo) tree, which was afterwards renamed the Bo tree.

MCQM41 mark

4. The Buddha asked Kisa Gotami to bring mustard seed from a house in which: (a) no one had ever been sick (b) there was no child (c) no one had ever died (d) there was a physician

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) no one had ever died. The condition revealed to her that death visits every house.

MCQM51 mark

5. According to the Buddha’s sermon, the lives of mortals are like: (a) a river that flows to the sea (b) a potter’s earthen vessels, which end by being broken (c) a flame that never dies (d) a tree that keeps growing

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) a potter’s earthen vessels, which end by being broken. He compares human life to clay vessels, which are all eventually broken.

MCQM61 mark

6. Kisa Gotami was asked to bring mustard seed from a house: (a) where a child had been born (b) where a wedding was held (c) that belonged to a king (d) where no one had died

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) where no one had died. She could not find one, and that was the lesson.

MCQM71 mark

7. The Buddha delivered his first sermon at: (a) Kushinagar (b) Lumbini (c) Sarnath near Benares (d) Bodh Gaya

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Sarnath near Benares. The deer park there is also called Rishipattan.

MCQM81 mark

8. The Buddha compared mortal life to: (a) a river (b) ripe fruits that may fall and earthen vessels fashioned by the potter that must all end broken (c) a mountain (d) a lamp

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) ripe fruits that may fall and earthen vessels fashioned by the potter that must all end broken. Both similes teach that death is the common end.

Ch 9 · The Proposal (8)

(a) Oxen Meadows and the dogs Squeezer and Guess. The first quarrel is over who owns Oxen Meadows and the second over whose dog is better.

MCQM71 mark

(c) she is an excellent housekeeper and he is thirty-five and needs a settled life. His reasons are practical, not romantic, which is the joke.

MCQM81 mark

8. The first quarrel in the play is about: (a) the two hunting dogs (b) the Oxen Meadows (c) the proposal itself (d) money

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) the Oxen Meadows. The disputed strip of land between the two estates starts the first explosion.
