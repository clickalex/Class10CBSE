Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How does the poet contrast the tiger in the cage with the tiger in the wild?

Show Answer

Ans:

In the cage the tiger stalks only the few steps of his enclosure, in quiet rage, with his strength locked behind bars and his attention turned away from the visitors. In the wild he would lurk in shadow, slide through the long grass near the watering hole where plump deer pass, and snarl around houses at the jungle's edge, baring his white fangs and claws and terrorising the village. The contrast is between useless confinement and natural power.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Explain the meaning of 'quiet rage'.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is an oxymoron. The tiger's rage is intense but silent, because in the cage there is nothing he can do about it. He cannot hunt, cannot roam and cannot express his anger, so the rage stays inside him and becomes quiet. The phrase captures the whole tragedy of captivity in two words.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What is the significance of the last stanza?

Show Answer

Ans:

The tiger is locked in a concrete cell, his strength behind bars, and he stalks the length of his cage ignoring visitors. At night he stares with his brilliant eyes at the brilliant stars. The repetition of 'brilliant' links the tiger to the vast, free sky that he can only look at. The stanza shows that captivity has taken away everything except the longing for freedom.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “A Tiger in the Zoo” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem. The tiger in the zoo stalks the few steps of his cage in quiet rage; his strength is locked in a cage, so he ignores visitors. The poet then imagines where the tiger should be — lurking in the shadow, sliding through long grass near the watering hole where plump deer pass. He should be lurking in shadow, sliding through long grass near the water hole, where plump deer pass. In the last stanza the tiger is l.
Marks lens: Reference-to-context extracts, usually the first stanza (the cage) or the last (the stars).
Method / memory trick: Two settings, two lists. Cage: few steps, concrete cell, bars, visitors, stare at the stars. Forest: shadow, long grass, watering hole, plump deer, terrorise the village.
Do not: Saying the tiger attacks the visitors. He ignores them.

SHORTS93 marks

9. Contrast the tiger in the cage with the tiger in its natural habitat.

Show Answer

Ans:

In the cage he paces a few steps along the length of his cage, ignores visitors, looks at the brilliant stars and is described as being ‘locked in concrete cell'.
His strength is behind bars; his voice is ‘the few steps of his cage' — reduced and silent.
In the forest he would be lurking in shadow, sliding through long grass near the water hole, snarling around houses at the jungle's edge.
There he would terrorise villages, show his white fangs and claws, and be free; the contrast is between helplessness and wild power.

LONGL104 marks

10. What does ‘A Tiger in the Zoo' say about freedom and captivity?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem shows that a wild animal in a zoo is not living its life, only existing within a few feet of concrete.
Freedom is not a luxury for such a creature: it is the condition in which its strength, hunting and instincts have any meaning.
The tiger's quiet rage and his staring at the stars suggest an awareness of what he has lost.
By ending with the tiger asleep behind bars while the forest world goes on, Norris quietly criticises the human habit of caging wild animals for amusement.
