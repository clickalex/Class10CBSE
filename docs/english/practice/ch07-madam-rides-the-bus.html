MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Valli's age at the time of the story was: (a) eight years and some months (b) six years (c) ten years (d) twelve years

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) eight years and some months — a detail she is very conscious of.

MCQM21 mark

2. The fare from Valli's village to the town was: (a) twenty paise (b) thirty paise (c) fifty paise (d) one rupee

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) thirty paise one way, and the journey took forty-five minutes over six miles.

MCQM31 mark

3. The conductor called Valli: (a) child (b) sister (c) little girl (d) madam

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) madam — hence the title of the story.

MCQM41 mark

4. Valli slipped out of the house to catch the bus when: (a) her mother was at the market (b) her father took her (c) her mother was taking an afternoon nap (d) it was dark

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) her mother was taking an afternoon nap. She waited for her mother’s afternoon sleep so that she could go without being noticed.

MCQM51 mark

5. On the return journey Valli saw the cow: (a) grazing in a field (b) standing on the bus (c) being driven home (d) lying dead by the roadside

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) lying dead by the roadside. The cow that had made her laugh had been killed by a fast vehicle.

MCQM61 mark

6. Valli was: (a) ten (b) twelve (c) eight years old (d) six

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) eight years old. For all her smallness she was a determined and careful planner.

MCQM71 mark

7. Valli saved what amount for her bus journey? (a) 60 paise (b) 30 paise (c) one rupee (d) two rupees

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 60 paise. Thirty paise each way, for the round trip from her village to the town and back.

MCQM81 mark

8. Valli travelled to the town on the: (a) morning bus (b) one o'clock bus (c) evening bus (d) night bus

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) one o'clock bus. She timed it so that she would be back by about a quarter to four, unseen.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why was the bus journey such a fascination for Valli?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because she had never been to the town and had no companions of her own age, so the bus became her window on the world. Every day it passed her house twice, carrying new people each time, and she longed to travel on it. The sight of the gleaming white bus, with its silver overhead bars and the people inside, filled her with an unbelievable desire, and the thought of travelling to the town and back grew stronger and stronger until she saved enough money to do it.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What did Valli see on her way that made her laugh until tears filled her eyes?

Show Answer

Ans:

She saw a young cow whose tail was high in the air, running very fast right in the middle of the road in front of the bus. The driver sounded his horn again and again, but the more he honked the more frightened the cow became and the faster it ran. Valli found it so funny that she laughed and laughed until tears filled her eyes. On the return journey the same cow lay dead by the roadside, which filled her with horror and took away her joy.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why did Valli refuse the cold drink offered by the conductor?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because she had only sixty paise with her and wanted to keep enough for the return journey. Even though she was thirsty, she refused the drink and asked only to be taken back on the same bus. It shows her discipline and her determination to complete the plan she had made.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. A student writes: “Valli, a girl of eight, took the bus with her mother.” What is wrong with this? Correct it.

Show Answer

Ans:

Valli went alone, without telling anyone; that is the point of the story.
She slipped out of the house while her mother was taking an afternoon nap.
She was proud of being eight years and some months old, and calling herself “madam” to the conductor showed her sense of being grown-up.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Valli saved her coins for weeks for one journey. Give two lessons that readers can learn from her planning and saving.

Show Answer

Ans:

A goal needs planning: she found out the fare, the time and the distance before she set out.
It needs self-control: she resisted peppermints, toys and the merry-go-round to save sixty paise.
Determination brings results, though she also learns that a journey can teach unexpected lessons.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How did Valli plan her first bus journey?

Show Answer

Ans:

Valli was eight and longed to ride the bus that ran between her village and the town six miles away.
She listened carefully to the passengers' talk and to her neighbour to learn the fare, the timings and what the town was like.
She saved every coin she could, resisting the temptation of peppermints and fair-ground toys, and collected sixty paise for the round trip.
She then waited for her mother's afternoon nap, slipped out of the house and boarded the bus as an ordinary passenger, calling herself ‘madam' to the conductor.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. The cow is alive on the way out and dead on the way back. What does this episode teach Valli, and why does she tell no one about it?

Show Answer

Ans:

The dead cow is Valli's first meeting with death — she learns that life can vanish suddenly, and her joyful mood turns thoughtful for the rest of the ride.
She tells no one because the journey was her secret and the experience too private to share; like all growing up, this lesson is hers alone.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: Valli saved sixty paise for her round trip. On the bus, the conductor offered her a cold drink, but she refused. (a) What was the fare for each way? (b) Why did Valli refuse the drink? (c) What quality of hers does this show?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Thirty paise one way, so sixty paise for the round trip.
(b) She had no money to spare; she wanted to keep enough for the return journey.
(c) Self-discipline and determination to carry out her plan.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: On the way to town, Valli laughed at a young cow running in front of the bus. On the way back the same cow lay by the roadside. (a) What was the cow doing when Valli first saw it? (b) What did she see on the return journey? (c) How did her mood change?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) It was running fast in the middle of the road with its tail in the air, and honking only frightened it more.
(b) The cow lay dead and mangled, struck by a fast vehicle.
(c) Her joy turned to silence and horror; she met death for the first time and became thoughtful.

LONGL104 marks

10. Describe what Valli saw on her ride and how her mood changed.

Show Answer

Ans:

From her window she watched the canal, the palm trees, the green fields, the distant hills and the blue sky, which filled her with joy.
She saw a young cow running in front of the bus, frisking and refusing to move, and laughed aloud at it.
On the return journey her mood changed: the same cow lay dead and mangled by the roadside, struck by a fast vehicle.
The sight silenced her happiness; she understood that the world held ugliness and death as well as beauty, which is the knowledge she brings home.

LONGL115 marks

11. How did Valli plan her bus journey? What does this tell you about her?

Show Answer

Ans:

Planning: she listened carefully to the conversations of her neighbours and of people who regularly used the bus, and she asked a few discreet questions here and there. From this she learnt that the town was six miles from her village, that the fare was thirty paise one way, and that the trip took forty-five minutes.
Saving: she resisted every temptation to buy peppermints, toys and balloons, and finally she even overcame her urge to ride the merry-go-round at the village annual fair, so that she could save sixty paise in all.
Timing: she chose to travel on the one o'clock afternoon bus, when her mother took her daily nap, so that she could be back before her mother woke.
What it shows: Valli was observant, determined and capable of self-control far beyond her years. She was also proud of being grown up — she resented being treated as a child, and insisted that the conductor call her madam.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does Valli change in the course of the story? Show her growth from fascination to understanding.

Show Answer

Ans:

At the start she is fascinated by the bus and eager to see the town; she has no companions of her own age.
She shows initiative and courage by planning and saving, then going alone.
During the ride she is joyful and proud, calling herself “madam”.
The dead cow brings her face to face with the cruelty of the world, and her mood turns serious.
She keeps her secret and returns home quietly, a little more grown-up than she left.
