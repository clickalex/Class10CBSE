MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Bholi's real name was: (a) Sunita (b) Kamla (c) Sulekha (d) Rukmini

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Sulekha — Bholi was the name she was given because she was considered simple.

MCQM21 mark

2. Bholi's face was pock-marked because of: (a) an attack of smallpox (b) a fall from the cot (c) a burn (d) a skin disease

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) an attack of smallpox, which left black pock marks all over her body.

MCQM31 mark

3. Bishamber Nath demanded five thousand rupees because: (a) he was poor (b) he saw Bholi's pock-marked face (c) her father refused (d) the priest demanded it

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) he saw Bholi's pock-marked face when her veil slipped at the wedding.

MCQM41 mark

4. What quality in Bholi's teacher helps her begin to speak with confidence? (a) Threats and punishment (b) Patience and encouragement (c) Silence about her progress (d) A promise of marriage

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Patience and encouragement. The teacher's kindness helps Bholi feel safe enough to learn.

MCQM51 mark

5. What does Bholi choose to do after refusing Bishamber? (a) Leave the village alone (b) Return to the wedding ceremony (c) Stop learning (d) Teach at the school

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Teach at the school. She chooses useful work and independence rather than the dowry marriage.

MCQM61 mark

6. Bholi's real name is: (a) Sulekha (b) Bholi (c) Mangla (d) Radha

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Sulekha. She was called Bholi, meaning the simpleton, because of her looks and her slowness.

MCQM71 mark

7. Bholi refuses to marry Bishamber Nath because he: (a) is poor (b) is uneducated (c) demands dowry from her father after the garlands are exchanged (d) is a widower

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) demands dowry from her father after the garlands are exchanged. She throws the garland into the fire.

MCQM81 mark

8. Bishamber Nath, the bridegroom Ramlal finds for Bholi, was: (a) a young schoolteacher (b) a rich landlord (c) a doctor (d) a middle-aged grocer and widower from another village

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) a middle-aged grocer and widower from another village. He limped, had grown-up children — and demanded five thousand rupees on seeing Bholi's face.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How did the teacher transform Bholi's life?

Show Answer

Ans:

The teacher was the first person to treat Bholi with kindness and respect. She spoke to her in a soft, soothing voice, told her not to be afraid, and promised that if she came to school every day she would learn more than anyone else in the village and would soon speak without a stammer. She encouraged Bholi to read, praised her progress, and gave her confidence. Education gave Bholi a voice, self-respect and the courage to refuse a humiliating marriage.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why did Bholi refuse to marry Bishamber Nath?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because at the wedding, when her veil slipped and he saw her pock-marked face, he demanded five thousand rupees as compensation and refused to marry her without it. He had already agreed to the match when he thought she was fair, so his demand showed that he was mean, greedy and contemptible. Bholi, who had been silent until then, spoke clearly and without a stammer and refused to marry such a coward.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What does the story say about the value of girls' education?

Show Answer

Ans:

It shows that education can transform a neglected, mocked and silent girl into a confident person with self-respect. Before school, Bholi was slow to speak, was laughed at and had no self-worth. After school, she could read, spoke without a stammer, and was able to stand up against a greedy bridegroom and his dowry demand. She finally decides to serve her parents and teach in the school where she learnt so much — so education gives her both independence and a purpose.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Bholi is mocked for stammering and marked by smallpox, but she ultimately refuses a humiliating marriage. Explain how education helps her make that choice.

Show Answer

Ans:

Her teacher treats her kindly and encourages her to speak, so school gives Bholi confidence as well as lessons.
By the wedding, she can recognise Bishamber's demand for dowry as an insult and injustice, not a fate she must accept.
She refuses the marriage and chooses to support herself by teaching, showing how education gives her a voice and independence.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Ramlal sends Bholi to school because the tehsildar asks the village officials to set an example. Explain how this unexpected start changes Bholi's future.

Show Answer

Ans:

Bholi begins school reluctantly, but her teacher's patience helps her overcome silence and stammering.
Education gives her confidence to reject Bishamber when he demands dowry, and she chooses a life of dignity rather than an unjust marriage.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why is Bholi called a simpleton, and how does she change?

Show Answer

Ans:

As a child she fell and damaged part of her brain, so she was slow, stammered and was mocked as ‘Bholi, the simpleton'.
She was neglected, given old clothes and little food, and kept out of school for years.
The village primary school and then the city school gave her a teacher's kindness, confidence and the ability to speak without stammering.
By the end she is able to refuse an unequal marriage and to speak up for her father, her teacher and herself.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Why was Bholi sent to school when her parents did not value girls' education?

Show Answer

Ans:

The tehsildar had ordered Ramlal, the numberdar, to set an example by sending his daughters to school, and Ramlal dared not disobey. Since Bholi — pock-marked, stammering, considered a simpleton — was the least valued child and unlikely to marry, her parents sacrificed her to the school while keeping her sisters at home. Neglect, ironically, gave her the chance that changed her life.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: At school, Bholi is frightened and stammers. Her teacher does not mock her; she speaks gently and encourages Bholi to try again. Years later, at her wedding, Bishamber demands money after seeing her face, and Bholi refuses to marry him. (a) What does the teacher's response give Bholi? (b) What prompts Bholi's refusal at the wedding? (c) How has Bholi changed?

Show Answer

Ans:

The teacher gives her patience, encouragement and a safe place to speak without being ridiculed.
Bishamber demands dowry after seeing her pock-marked face; Bholi rejects his greed and the disrespect shown to her family.
She grows from a frightened, silent girl into a confident young woman able to speak for herself and choose her future.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Bholi's parents once think that she is unlikely to marry and do not expect much from her. At the end, she refuses Bishamber's dowry demand and tells her father that she will teach at the school. (a) What does Bholi's final decision reject? (b) What future does she choose? (c) Why is this ending important to the story's message?

Show Answer

Ans:

She rejects the idea that she must accept a disrespectful marriage because of her appearance or the family's fear of social shame.
She chooses to earn a living and become a teacher at the school that helped her.
Her education has given her a voice and agency; the ending challenges dowry and the neglect of girls while affirming her right to decide.

LONGL104 marks

10. How does education give Bholi a voice?

Show Answer

Ans:

Her teacher's encouragement makes her believe she can learn; she is given books, a clean dress and, above all, attention.
Education gives her self-respect: she learns to read, write and do sums, and she stops stammering in front of others.
When Bishamber Nath demands five thousand rupees as dowry, she understands that she is being bought and tears up the wedding garland.
She finally declares that she will serve her parents and teach at the same school, showing that education has turned a frightened child into a person with a will of her own.

LONGL114 marks

11. Bholi throws the wedding garland into the fire. Why is this gesture the climax of the story?

Show Answer

Ans:

The garland stands for the marriage her parents arranged at any price — even a 5,000-rupee dowry to a middle-aged widower.
By throwing it into the fire, Bholi burns not just one wedding but the whole bargain: dowry, obedience and the idea that a girl is a burden.
It is the climax because the stammering simpleton now speaks clearly and chooses for herself — education has given her a voice, and she uses it.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does Bholi's journey from a fearful child to an independent young woman challenge the way her family and society judge her?

Show Answer

Ans:

Her family treats Bholi as a burden because she is shy, stammers and has smallpox marks, while others mock her as a simpleton.
At school, her teacher offers kindness and patient encouragement. Bholi gradually gains confidence and learns to speak without fear.
At the wedding, Bishamber demands dowry after noticing her face. Bholi refuses to let the demand or her family's anxiety decide her worth.
By choosing to teach, she becomes independent and uses her voice. The story argues that education can transform opportunity and that a girl's dignity is not measured by appearance or marriage prospects.
