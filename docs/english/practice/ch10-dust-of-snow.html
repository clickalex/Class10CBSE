MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. 'Dust of Snow' is written by: (a) W. W. E. Ross (b) Leslie Norris (c) Robert Frost (d) Carolyn Wells

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Robert Frost.

MCQM21 mark

2. The bird in the poem is a: (a) sparrow (b) robin (c) cuckoo (d) crow

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) crow, perched on a hemlock tree.

MCQM31 mark

3. The word 'rued' in the poem means: (a) regretted (b) enjoyed (c) remembered (d) forgot

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) regretted — the poet had rued the day before the snow fell.

MCQM41 mark

4. How is the poem arranged? (a) One eight-line stanza (b) Two four-line stanzas (c) Four couplets (d) Three three-line stanzas

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Two four-line stanzas. The eight lines are divided into two quatrains.

MCQM51 mark

5. What does the word “dust” suggest about the snow in the title? (a) A fine, light amount (b) A heavy avalanche (c) A frozen river (d) A snowstorm lasting all day

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) A fine, light amount. The small fall of snow matches the brief, ordinary event that changes the speaker's mood.

MCQM61 mark

6. The dust of snow fell on the poet from a: (a) oak tree (b) pine tree (c) maple tree (d) hemlock tree

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) hemlock tree. The tree is poisonous, which matches the poet's dark mood.

MCQM71 mark

7. The poem says the day was saved because the incident: (a) warmed him (b) made him laugh (c) gave his heart a change of mood (d) brought him money

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) gave his heart a change of mood. The rest of the day was rued by the change.

MCQM81 mark

8. The rhyme scheme of ‘Dust of Snow’ is: (a) aabb ccdd (b) abab cdcd (c) abcb abcb (d) aba abc

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) abab cdcd. Two neat quatrains carry the eight lines of the poem.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. What change of mood does the poet describe and what caused it?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poet was in a depressed or sorrowful mood and had regretted the day. The change came when a crow shook down a dust of snow on him from a hemlock tree. This small, unexpected event lifted his spirits and saved some part of the day that he had rued.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why is it significant that the bird is a crow and the tree a hemlock?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because both are traditionally associated with sorrow and death — the crow is considered an ill omen, and the hemlock is a poisonous plant. The poet deliberately chooses them to show that joy can come from the most unlikely and unfavourable sources, and that our associations should not blind us to the good in things.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Explain the central idea of the poem.

Show Answer

Ans:

The central idea is that a small, natural event can transform a person's outlook. The poet is gloomy and regretting his day, but the dust of snow shaken down by a crow gives him a sudden change of mood and saves part of the day. Frost suggests that we should stay open to such small joys, because they have the power to alter our whole perspective.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. A classmate calls “Dust of Snow” a wholly gloomy poem because both the crow and the hemlock are associated with ill fortune. Correct the reading using the poem's turning point.

Show Answer

Ans:

The crow shakes a light fall of snow from the hemlock onto the speaker; the incident changes his mood.
The crow and hemlock may suggest gloom, but here they unexpectedly bring relief rather than deepen it.
The ending therefore resists judging an event only by its usual associations: a small moment can rescue part of a bad day.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. The poem moves from a small physical event to a change of feeling. Identify the event, explain the change, and say why that scale matters to the poem's message.

Show Answer

Ans:

A crow shakes dust-like snow from a hemlock tree onto the speaker.
The unexpected contact lifts his spirits; he says it saves part of the day he had regretted.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How does ‘Dust of Snow' bring about a change of mood?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poet is in a bitter, unhappy mood, suggested by the hemlock tree — a poisonous plant — under which he sits.
A crow, another conventionally unpleasant bird, shakes down a shower of dust of snow onto him.
Instead of annoying him, the small event is taken as a good omen: it gives his heart a change of mood.
He says he has rued many a day that day; the rest of the day is saved, so the poem is about how a tiny natural incident can lift depression.

SHORTS73 marks

7. What does the phrase ‘saved some part of a day I had rued’ tell us about the poet's day before the incident?

Show Answer

Ans:

‘Rued’ means regretted, so the poet had been having a gloomy, regret-filled day — part of it already wasted in a sad mood. The falling dust of snow changes his mood, and the part of the day that remains is rescued from regret. The smallness of the incident and the size of its effect is the poem's whole point.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: A speaker begins a winter day in regret. A crow settles on a hemlock and shakes a fine fall of snow onto him. After this brief incident, he feels that some part of the day has been saved. (a) What causes the speaker's mood to change? (b) Why is the choice of crow and hemlock surprising? (c) What broader idea can a reader infer?

Show Answer

Ans:

The crow's movement shakes the snow down onto the speaker; the tiny event interrupts his regret.
Both images are commonly linked with gloom, so it is unexpected that they become the source of cheer.
A small encounter can alter a person's outlook, and an object's usual associations do not determine what it can mean in a particular moment.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Frost builds “Dust of Snow” from two compact four-line stanzas. The first presents the snow falling from the tree; the second tells how the moment affects the speaker's day. The rhyme links the lines within each stanza. (a) How many lines are in the poem? (b) What changes between the two stanzas? (c) How does the short structure suit the incident?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem has eight lines in all, arranged as two quatrains.
The first gives the physical incident; the second makes explicit its emotional effect on the speaker.
Its brief, balanced form mirrors the smallness of the event, while the clear turn makes its large effect on the day easy to see.

LONGL104 marks

10. Why does Frost choose a crow and a hemlock tree rather than a nightingale and a rose?

Show Answer

Ans:

Romantic poets used beautiful or musical images; Frost deliberately chooses the ugly and the poisonous — the crow and the hemlock.
This makes the change of mood more surprising and honest: consolation comes from ordinary, even unpleasant, nature.
It also suits Frost's view that nature is indifferent and that meaning is made by the mind that receives it.
The poem thus becomes modern rather than romantic: nature does not flatter us, and yet it can still save a ruined day.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. Give an example from your own life of a small incident that changed your mood the way the dust of snow changed the poet's. What does this tell us about the poem's message?

Show Answer

Ans:

Example — reaching school upset after an argument at home, then a friend sharing a joke in the corridor, after which the whole day felt lighter.
Like the crow and the hemlock, the trigger was ordinary and even gloomy, yet it broke the chain of regret.
The message is that healing does not need grand events; nature and small kindnesses can save a day we had rued.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does Frost use the contrast between the poem's sombre images and its hopeful ending to suggest that a small event can change a person's outlook?

Show Answer

Ans:

At first the crow and hemlock seem suited to the speaker's regret: the bird carries an ill-omened association and the tree is poisonous.
Yet a crow's movement releases only a dusting of snow, an ordinary winter event rather than a grand intervention.
The speaker's response is the important change: his heart lifts and part of the day he had rued is saved.
Frost overturns the images' expected associations, showing that a moment's effect depends on how it is experienced, not simply on its reputation.
