MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Griffin became invisible by: (a) an accident (b) swallowing certain rare drugs (c) a magic spell (d) a laboratory explosion

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) swallowing certain rare drugs after many experiments.

MCQM21 mark

2. The inn where Griffin stayed was at: (a) Drury Lane (b) London Bridge (c) Iping (d) Shotover

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Iping, where Mr and Mrs Hall kept the inn.

MCQM31 mark

3. The village constable who tried to arrest Griffin was: (a) Mr Jaffers (b) Mr Hall (c) Mr Griffin (d) Mr Kemp

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Mr Jaffers, who was knocked unconscious by a man he could not see.

MCQM41 mark

4. What does Griffin do to his landlord's house before leaving? (a) Sets it on fire (b) Turns it into an inn (c) Builds a laboratory in it (d) Sells its furniture

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Sets it on fire. Griffin's reckless actions put other people and their property in danger.

MCQM51 mark

5. Why does Griffin take clothes after becoming invisible? (a) To disguise himself as a policeman (b) To sell them to a shopkeeper (c) To build a laboratory (d) To keep warm and move about in public

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) To keep warm and move about in public. Invisibility leaves him exposed to the weather and unable to appear normally in public.

MCQM61 mark

6. Griffin was a scientist who discovered: (a) how to fly (b) a new gas (c) how to make the human body transparent (d) a cure for blindness

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) how to make the human body transparent. He could not reverse the process, which ruined him.

MCQM71 mark

7. Mrs Hall, the innkeeper, first grew suspicious when she saw: (a) footprints in the snow (b) a broken window (c) money missing from the till (d) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes moving

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes. The bandages hid the fact that he was invisible.

MCQM81 mark

8. Griffin first betrayed his presence as: (a) a shadow on the wall (b) footprints appearing in the snow (c) a voice in the dark (d) smoke in the air

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) footprints appearing in the snow. Two boys saw fresh footprints of bare feet where no man was visible.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How did Griffin become invisible?

Show Answer

Ans:

He was a brilliant scientist who carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could be made invisible, and at last he swallowed certain rare drugs. His body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though still as hard to see. To avoid being seen he also had to remove his clothes, which left him naked, homeless and penniless in the middle of winter.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Describe what happened at the inn in Iping.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin booked two rooms and paid Mr Hall in advance, but behaved strangely, refusing company and shutting himself in. When Mrs Hall went to his room she found the door open and the room empty, with the clothes he had been wearing scattered about. Suddenly a hat hopped off the bedpost and struck her face, and then a chair became alive, pushed both her and her husband out of the room and slammed and locked the door behind them. Mrs Hall was convinced the room was haunted by spirits.

APPLICATIONA33 marks

3. Griffin is a brilliant scientist, but the story presents him as a threat to the people around him. Explain why invisibility does not make him admirable.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin's discovery of invisibility is scientifically remarkable, but he uses it for selfish and unlawful acts.
He sets fire to his landlord's house, steals clothes and money, and harms people who try to stop him.
The story separates scientific ability from character: knowledge without responsibility can make someone dangerous.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. A student claims that an invisible person could move through a town without leaving evidence. Use Griffin's footprints at Iping to correct this claim.

Show Answer

Ans:

Invisibility hides Griffin's body, but it does not remove the physical marks his feet make in wet mud.
The moving footprints alert the villagers that someone is there, showing that an invisible person can still leave evidence.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why does Griffin fail as an invisible man?

Show Answer

Ans:

He discovers how to make the human body transparent, but he does not discover how to make it visible again, and he cannot reverse his experiment.
Invisibility leaves him without clothes, food and shelter; he is hungry, cold and hunted everywhere.
His behavior turns violent: he sets fire to his landlord's house, assaults people who get in his way and steals to survive.
Finally he is attacked by the crowd in the inn, and his cloak is torn off, revealing an empty space where a man should be.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why did Griffin burn his landlord's house? What does this first crime tell us about him?

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin burnt the house in revenge because his landlord tried to eject him. This first crime tells us that Griffin answers every obstacle with destruction — he has brilliance without conscience. The pattern never changes: robbery, assault and finally beating Constable Jaffers follow from the same lawless temper.

COMPETENCYC74 marks

7. Read and answer: Griffin's body cannot be seen, yet in muddy streets at Iping a line of footprints appears to move without a visible person. The villagers follow the marks and become alarmed. (a) What do the footprints reveal? (b) Why do the people find them frightening? (c) What limit of Griffin's experiment do they expose?

Show Answer

Ans:

They reveal Griffin's passage even though the rest of him is invisible.
The marks seem to move on their own because no body can be seen making them.
Invisibility does not erase every physical trace: his feet still press into wet ground and leave tracks.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: Griffin removes his clothes to become invisible, but he still needs food, shelter and warmth. In Iping he takes clothes and money and frightens the people who try to question him. (a) Why must Griffin obtain clothes after becoming invisible? (b) What does his conduct at Iping show about him? (c) What central conflict does the story raise?

Show Answer

Ans:

Without clothes, an invisible person is exposed to cold and cannot pass unnoticed in ordinary weather or surroundings.
Griffin acts selfishly and violently, using his discovery to intimidate people instead of helping them.
The story asks what happens when scientific power is separated from ethical responsibility and regard for others.

LONGL94 marks

9. Describe Griffin's character and what the story says about the misuse of science.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin is a brilliant scientist, but he is selfish, violent and dishonest: he uses his discovery to rob, to revenge himself and to terrify ordinary people.
He is disowned by his father, who had given him the money, and he is prepared to hurt anyone who obstructs him.
The story suggests that science without moral responsibility destroys the scientist himself: Griffin ends up hunted, hungry and alone.
Wells thus warns that a discovery used for private gain instead of public good turns its maker into a criminal and a victim.

APPLICATIONA104 marks

10. Invisibility should have made Griffin powerful, but it made him helpless. Explain this paradox with two examples from the story.

Show Answer

Ans:

First, at the inn in Iping, invisibility strands him: without clothes he is naked and freezing, with clothes he is visible and suspicious — the ‘headless’ reveal terrifies everyone and brings the police.
Second, he cannot eat, sleep or move unseen in a watched village; every theft leaves footprints, literally and figuratively.
The paradox is that invisibility removes his disguise but not his needs — science without planning is a trap, not a power.

LONGL115 marks

11. Was Griffin a brilliant scientist or a lawless person? Justify your answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

He was certainly a brilliant scientist. He carried out experiment after experiment until he proved that the human body could be made invisible, and succeeded in swallowing drugs that made his body as transparent as glass. It is a discovery of extraordinary difficulty, and it shows real scientific ability and persistence.
But he was also lawless. His first use of the discovery was revenge: he set fire to his landlord's house because the man disliked him and was trying to eject him. He then robbed a shop in London, stole clothes from a big store, robbed a shopkeeper at Drury Lane, and attacked the village constable Mr Jaffers at Iping.
Conclusion: scientific brilliance without moral responsibility is dangerous. Griffin used a remarkable discovery for theft, revenge and violence, and ended up a hunted, homeless wanderer with no friends. So while his ability as a scientist cannot be denied, he was, in his behaviour, a lawless person.

LONGL125 marks

12. Is Griffin's invisibility a triumph of science or a source of danger in the story? Give a balanced answer using his discovery and his conduct.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin's experiments show unusual scientific skill: he discovers how to make his body invisible, a remarkable achievement.
But the discovery does not make him wise or responsible. He burns his landlord's house and later steals clothes, food and money to survive.
At Iping, unexplained footprints and disturbances frighten people, while Griffin's temper and violence make the situation worse.
The story's balance is clear: scientific power can be impressive, but without self-control and concern for others it becomes a danger to society.
