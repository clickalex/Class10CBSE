MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Griffin became invisible by: (a) an accident (b) swallowing certain rare drugs (c) a magic spell (d) a laboratory explosion

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) swallowing certain rare drugs after many experiments.

MCQM21 mark

2. The inn where Griffin stayed was at: (a) Drury Lane (b) Iping (c) London Bridge (d) Shotover

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Iping, where Mr and Mrs Hall kept the inn.

MCQM31 mark

3. The village constable who tried to arrest Griffin was: (a) Mr Hall (b) Mr Jaffers (c) Mr Griffin (d) Mr Kemp

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Mr Jaffers, who was knocked unconscious by a man he could not see.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Footprints without Feet” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is H. G. Wells: Griffin the invisible scientist becomes a hunted lawless man..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Footprints without Feet” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. Griffin was a scientist who discovered: (a) how to make the human body transparent (b) how to fly (c) a new gas (d) a cure for blindness

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) how to make the human body transparent. He could not reverse the process, which ruined him.

MCQM71 mark

7. Mrs Hall, the innkeeper, first grew suspicious when she saw: (a) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes moving (b) footprints in the snow (c) a broken window (d) money missing from the till

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the stranger's bandaged head and his empty clothes. The bandages hid the fact that he was invisible.

MCQM81 mark

8. Griffin first betrayed his presence as: (a) footprints appearing in the snow (b) a shadow on the wall (c) a voice in the dark (d) smoke in the air

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) footprints appearing in the snow. Two boys saw fresh footprints of bare feet where no man was visible.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How did Griffin become invisible?

Show Answer

Ans:

He was a brilliant scientist who carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could be made invisible, and at last he swallowed certain rare drugs. His body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though still as hard to see. To avoid being seen he also had to remove his clothes, which left him naked, homeless and penniless in the middle of winter.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Describe what happened at the inn in Iping.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin booked two rooms and paid Mr Hall in advance, but behaved strangely, refusing company and shutting himself in. When Mrs Hall went to his room she found the door open and the room empty, with the clothes he had been wearing scattered about. Suddenly a hat hopped off the bedpost and struck her face, and then a chair became alive, pushed both her and her husband out of the room and slammed and locked the door behind them. Mrs Hall was convinced the room was haunted by spirits.

APPLICATIONA33 marks

3. Riya is revising “Footprints without Feet” (H. G. Wells: Griffin the invisible scientist becomes a hunted lawless man.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Saying Griffin was invisible by birth. He made himself invisible by swallowing rare drugs. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Two words describe him: brilliant and lawless. Every long answer on Griffin needs both.
Why the mistake costs marks: Saying Griffin was invisible by birth. He made himself invisible by swallowing rare drugs.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Footprints without Feet”: “How did Griffin become invisible?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: H. G. Wells: Griffin the invisible scientist becomes a hunted lawless man..
He was a brilliant scientist who carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could be made invisible, and at last he swallowed certain rare drugs. His body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though still as hard to see. To avoid being seen he also had to remove his clothes, which left him naked, homeless and penniless in the middle of winter.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why does Griffin fail as an invisible man?

Show Answer

Ans:

He discovers how to make the human body transparent, but he does not discover how to make it visible again, and he cannot reverse his experiment.
Invisibility leaves him without clothes, food and shelter; he is hungry, cold and hunted everywhere.
His behavior turns violent: he sets fire to his landlord's house, assaults people who get in his way and steals to survive.
Finally he is attacked by the crowd in the inn, and his cloak is torn off, revealing an empty space where a man should be.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Why did Griffin burn his landlord's house? What does this first crime tell us about him?

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin burnt the house in revenge because his landlord tried to eject him. This first crime tells us that Griffin answers every obstacle with destruction — he has brilliance without conscience. The pattern never changes: robbery, assault and finally beating Constable Jaffers follow from the same lawless temper.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: Two students attempt: “How did Griffin become invisible?” Student A writes one line. Student B writes points, an example and a check. (a) Which answer frame should you use for full marks? (b) Write the model points. (c) Add one memory trick you would use on exam morning.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Statement → working or reasons in points → example or check. One-line answers lose step marks.
(b) He was a brilliant scientist who carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could be made invisible, and at last he swallowed certain rare drugs. His body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though still as hard to see. To avoid being seen he also had to remove his clothes, which left him naked, homeless and penniless in the middle of winter.
(c) Two words describe him: brilliant and lawless. Every long answer on Griffin needs both.

LONGL94 marks

9. Describe Griffin's character and what the story says about the misuse of science.

Show Answer

Ans:

Griffin is a brilliant scientist, but he is selfish, violent and dishonest: he uses his discovery to rob, to revenge himself and to terrify ordinary people.
He is disowned by his father, who had given him the money, and he is prepared to hurt anyone who obstructs him.
The story suggests that science without moral responsibility destroys the scientist himself: Griffin ends up hunted, hungry and alone.
Wells thus warns that a discovery used for private gain instead of public good turns its maker into a criminal and a victim.

APPLICATIONA104 marks

10. Invisibility should have made Griffin powerful, but it made him helpless. Explain this paradox with two examples from the story.

Show Answer

Ans:

First, at the inn in Iping, invisibility strands him: without clothes he is naked and freezing, with clothes he is visible and suspicious — the ‘headless’ reveal terrifies everyone and brings the police.
Second, he cannot eat, sleep or move unseen in a watched village; every theft leaves footprints, literally and figuratively.
The paradox is that invisibility removes his disguise but not his needs — science without planning is a trap, not a power.

LONGL115 marks

11. Was Griffin a brilliant scientist or a lawless person? Justify your answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

He was certainly a brilliant scientist. He carried out experiment after experiment until he proved that the human body could be made invisible, and succeeded in swallowing drugs that made his body as transparent as glass. It is a discovery of extraordinary difficulty, and it shows real scientific ability and persistence.
But he was also lawless. His first use of the discovery was revenge: he set fire to his landlord's house because the man disliked him and was trying to eject him. He then robbed a shop in London, stole clothes from a big store, robbed a shopkeeper at Drury Lane, and attacked the village constable Mr Jaffers at Iping.
Conclusion: scientific brilliance without moral responsibility is dangerous. Griffin used a remarkable discovery for theft, revenge and violence, and ended up a hunted, homeless wanderer with no friends. So while his ability as a scientist cannot be denied, he was, in his behaviour, a lawless person.

LONGL125 marks

12. Discuss “Footprints without Feet” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Summary. Griffin was a brilliant scientist who had carried out experiment after experiment to prove that the human body could become invisible, and finally swallowed certain rare drugs, so that his body became as transparent as a sheet of glass, though as hard to see. His landlord disliked him and was trying to eject him, so in revenge Griffin set fire to the house and, to escape without being seen, removed his cloth.
Marks lens: Short answers and a long answer on whether Griffin was a brilliant scientist or a lawless person.
Method / memory trick: Two words describe him: brilliant and lawless. Every long answer on Griffin needs both.
Do not: Saying Griffin was invisible by birth. He made himself invisible by swallowing rare drugs.
