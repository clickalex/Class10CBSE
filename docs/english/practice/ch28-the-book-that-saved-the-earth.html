MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. 'The Book That Saved the Earth' is written by: (a) Guy de Maupassant (b) Claire Boiko (c) Victor Canning (d) H. G. Wells

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Claire Boiko, and it is written as a play.

MCQM21 mark

2. Think-Tank first mistakes the book for: (a) an earth sandwich (b) a mirror (c) a weapon (d) a map

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) an earth sandwich, and he orders the crew to eat it.

MCQM31 mark

3. The book that frightened Think-Tank was: (a) the Bible (b) Mother Goose (c) an atlas (d) a Martian history

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Mother Goose, a book of nursery rhymes.

MCQM41 mark

4. Who is Think-Tank's apprentice and the more perceptive adviser? (a) Noodle (b) Captain Omega (c) Lieutenant Iota (d) Sergeant Oop

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Noodle. He offers careful suggestions that help reveal the crew's misinterpretations.

MCQM51 mark

5. What name is given to Mars after the events of the play? (a) The Era of War (b) Little Earth (c) New Earth (d) The Book World

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) New Earth. The story ends with Mars and Earth becoming friends, and Mars taking the name New Earth.

MCQM61 mark

6. The commander of the Martian invasion force was: (a) Noodle (b) Omega (c) Think-Tank (d) Iota

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Think-Tank. His assistant Noodle is the one who actually understands the books.

MCQM71 mark

7. The Martians decided that Earth was dangerous after reading: (a) a newspaper (b) a scientific journal (c) a history book (d) nursery rhymes

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) nursery rhymes. ‘Humpty Dumpty' and ‘Hey diddle diddle' convinced them that Earth was attacking Mars.

MCQM81 mark

8. The play is set in: (a) the twentieth century (b) the twenty-first century (c) ancient times (d) the twenty-fifth century, the Era of the Book

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) the twenty-fifth century, the Era of the Book. A historian of that age stages the story as a history play.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Think-Tank mistake the book for an earth sandwich?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the Martians had never seen a book. Think-Tank examined it from the control room, saw that it was flat and had pages, and concluded that the earthlings ate it as a sandwich. He ordered the crew to eat it, and when they could not, he changed his mind and decided that the books were communication devices used for listening.

SHORTS23 marks

2. How did the book save the Earth?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the Martians misunderstood what they read. When Sergeant Oop read 'Mistress Mary, quite contrary', with its silver bells and cockle shells, Think-Tank concluded that the earthlings grew metals and explosives in their gardens. When he heard about the cow jumping over the moon, he believed that the earthlings had taught their animals a high culture of music and space techniques, and that they planned to attack Mars Central Control. Terrified, he ordered the crew to leave Earth at once and later fled to Alpha Centauri — so the invasion was called off, and the Earth was saved by a nursery rhyme.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Describe Think-Tank's character.

Show Answer

Ans:

He is the Commander-in-Chief of the Space Probe fleet of Mars, and he believes himself to be the most powerful and intelligent creature in the universe. In fact he is vain, boastful and stupid: he mistakes a book for a sandwich, cannot understand a nursery rhyme, and panics at the idea of a cow jumping over the moon. He is also a coward, since he flees to Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away, at the first sign of danger.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Think-Tank believes the nursery book reveals a dangerous Earth army. Explain how his arrogance turns simple rhymes into a false military report.

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank assumes that his first interpretation must be correct and dismisses the possibility that the book is for children.
He reads nursery images such as “mistress Mary” and “silver bells” as references to weapons, technology or military threats.
His crew's fear grows from his confident but mistaken readings, so the planned invasion is abandoned.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Noodle offers suggestions to Think-Tank without openly challenging him. Explain how this careful manner helps Noodle and the crew.

Show Answer

Ans:

Noodle phrases corrections as respectful possibilities, allowing Think-Tank to accept the advice without admitting he has been wrong.
His quiet intelligence helps the crew interpret the book more sensibly and later helps avert the Martian invasion.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How do the Martians misunderstand the nursery rhymes?

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank asks to be shown books, and the crew bring him nursery rhymes from a library.
From ‘Mistress Mary, quite contrary' he decides that humans grow money, and from ‘Mother Goose' he concludes that she is the supreme ruler of Earth.
He takes the rhyme about the cow that jumped over the moon as proof that Earth's creatures can jump between planets.
Finally he hears ‘Humpty Dumpty' and ‘Hey diddle diddle' and decides that Earth is planning to launch an attack on Mars.

SHORTS73 marks

7. How does Noodle's character contrast with Think-Tank's? Why is this contrast important to the play?

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank is vain, boastful and stupid — he mistakes a book for a sandwich and nursery rhymes for threats. Noodle, his apprentice, is clever and modest — he quietly suggests the right ideas (that the book is for reading) and lets his master take the credit.
The contrast drives both the humour and the ending: folly commands, wisdom obeys, until folly flees to Alpha Centauri and Noodle's line — Mars and Earth as friends — wins.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: The Martian probe studies a book of nursery rhymes. Think-Tank treats ordinary images as evidence of Earth's power; “silver bells” and other harmless words become military equipment in his interpretation. Noodle makes careful suggestions, but Think-Tank remains certain of his own brilliance. (a) What has Think-Tank misunderstood? (b) How does his confidence affect the crew? (c) What does Noodle's manner reveal about him?

Show Answer

Ans:

He mistakes a children's book and its playful rhymes for a literal account of Earth's weapons and military strength.
The crew accepts his claims and becomes frightened, even though the evidence is being misread.
Noodle is intelligent and tactful: he offers a better interpretation indirectly so that the arrogant commander can accept it.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: The invasion force abandons its plan after Think-Tank interprets Mother Goose rhymes as dangerous instructions. The crew has not tested his readings against the book's purpose; Noodle's modest comments are the only gentle challenge. (a) Why do the Martians retreat? (b) What reasoning error causes their fear? (c) How does the play use this mistake for comedy?

Show Answer

Ans:

They believe Earth has powerful weapons and that an invasion would be unsafe, so they retreat to Mars.
They take figurative, childlike rhymes literally and accept Think-Tank's guesses without checking them.
The audience recognises the harmless nursery references while the Martians imagine a terrifying arsenal; the gap between the two readings creates dramatic irony.

LONGL104 marks

10. What is the humour in the way the Martians abandon their invasion?

Show Answer

Ans:

Their invasion is decided by a commander who is certain he is the greatest intellect in the universe but cannot read a simple rhyme.
The more evidence he gets, the more absurd his conclusions become: library shelves become ‘sandwiches' and books are ‘communication sandwiches'.
He panics, orders the fleet to retreat and abandons the invasion of a planet whose only weapons were a cow, a dish and a spoon.
The joke is that the greatest danger to Earth was never an attack from Mars, but a Martian commander too proud to ask what the words mean.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. ‘Silver bells and cockle shells’ become metals and explosives in Martian eyes. Explain two such misreadings and the panic each causes.

Show Answer

Ans:

First, ‘mistress Mary, quite contrary’ with her silver bells and cockle shells is read as an earthling growing metals and explosives — proof that earthlings farm weapons.
Second, ‘Humpty Dumpty had a great fall’ is read as Earth's plan for Think-Tank himself: ‘he will have a great fall’ sends the commander into panic.
Each rhyme the Martians decode raises the alarm, until Think-Tank orders the invasion abandoned — a book of rhymes defeats an army.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does the play make a nursery-rhyme book powerful enough to stop an invasion, and what does it suggest about leadership?

Show Answer

Ans:

Think-Tank's crew finds a book of nursery rhymes, but the arrogant commander does not understand its purpose or language.
He interprets ordinary images as evidence of advanced weapons. The crew trusts his confidence, so a string of mistaken readings becomes a threat in their minds.
Noodle is the more perceptive leader: he suggests corrections politely, while Think-Tank's vanity prevents him from checking his assumptions.
The Martians retreat from a danger that exists only in their interpretation. The comedy warns that authority without humility or evidence can turn a harmless text into a crisis.
