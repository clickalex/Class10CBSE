MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Lencho asked God for: (a) fifty pesos (b) seventy pesos (c) one hundred pesos (d) one hundred and fifty pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) one hundred pesos, to sow his field again and to live until the coming harvest.

MCQM21 mark

2. The letter Lencho received was signed: (a) the postmaster (b) God (c) the employees of the post office (d) the President

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) God — the postmaster signed it that way so that Lencho's faith would not be shaken.

MCQM31 mark

(a) G. L. Fuentes.

MCQM51 mark

MCQM61 mark

6. Lencho asked God for: (a) 30 pesos (b) 70 pesos (c) 100 pesos (d) 1000 pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 100 pesos. He asked for a hundred pesos to sow his field again and live until the harvest.

MCQM71 mark

7. The postmaster and his staff managed to collect: (a) 100 pesos (b) 70 pesos (c) 30 pesos (d) 700 pesos

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 70 pesos. They contributed from their own salaries, and the postmaster put in the rest of his own money.

MCQM81 mark

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) giving part of his salary and asking employees and friends. The money was charity collected peso by peso.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Lencho say the post office employees were a bunch of crooks?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because he had asked God for one hundred pesos and received only seventy. His faith was so absolute that he could not believe God had made a mistake or had sent less, so he concluded that the post office employees must have taken the remaining thirty pesos. The irony is that these were the very men who had collected the money for him out of charity.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What made the postmaster decide to help Lencho?

Show Answer

Ans:

He was moved by the farmer's unshakable faith in God. He did not want to shake that faith, so he collected money from his employees and friends, gave part of his own salary, and sent it to Lencho in a letter signed 'God'.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Was Lencho surprised to find a letter with money in it? What made him angry?

Show Answer

Ans:

No, he was not surprised at all — he had expected an answer from God, and his confidence in God was so complete that receiving a reply seemed natural. What made him angry was that the envelope contained only seventy pesos instead of the hundred he had asked for. He was certain that God could not have made a mistake, so he concluded that the post office employees had taken the rest.

SHORTS63 marks

6. Bring out the irony in the ending of ‘A Letter to God'.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lencho is certain that God sent him the money and cannot imagine that human beings could be so generous. He writes a second letter to complain and calls the post office employees ‘a bunch of crooks' because thirty pesos were missing. The irony is that the very people who helped him are the ones he distrusts: his faith in God survives, while his faith in human beings is destroyed.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. “The postmaster was a fat, jolly fellow.” How does his character contrast with Lencho's, and why does the contrast matter to the story?

Show Answer

Ans:

Lencho is intense, single-minded and absolute in his faith; the postmaster is warm, practical and humane — he helps without needing to be believed in.
The contrast creates the story's deepest irony: the believer cannot recognise human kindness, while the kind man asks for no recognition at all.
Without this contrast the ending would be merely sad; with it, it becomes a comment on faith that blinds as well as sustains.

LONGL104 marks

10. Describe Lencho's character and say what his faith tells us about him.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lencho is a poor but hard-working farmer who knows his field like a child and never stops hoping for a good crop.
His faith is complete and childlike: after the hailstorm destroys everything, he writes to God for a hundred pesos with the same confidence with which he sows his field.
The same faith makes him naive — he cannot imagine that the postmaster and his staff collected the money themselves.
The story uses this innocence to show both the strength of faith and the loneliness of the poor, who trust God more than the people around them.

LONGL115 marks

11. 'The story is built around two ironies.' Explain with reference to the text.

Show Answer

Ans:

The first irony is in the weather: Lencho's field needed rain, and rain did come — but it turned into a hailstorm that destroyed the entire crop. The very thing that promised a good harvest took it away.
The second irony is in the ending: the post office employees, moved by Lencho's faith, collected seventy pesos and sent them to him. Lencho, finding the amount short, wrote back accusing those same men of stealing the money and calling them 'a bunch of crooks'. The helpers become the accused thieves, and the man who never doubted God doubts the honesty of human beings.
