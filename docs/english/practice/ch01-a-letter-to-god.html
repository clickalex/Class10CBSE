Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Lencho say the post office employees were a bunch of crooks?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because he had asked God for one hundred pesos and received only seventy. His faith was so absolute that he could not believe God had made a mistake or had sent less, so he concluded that the post office employees must have taken the remaining thirty pesos. The irony is that these were the very men who had collected the money for him out of charity.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What made the postmaster decide to help Lencho?

Show Answer

Ans:

He was moved by the farmer's unshakable faith in God. He did not want to shake that faith, so he collected money from his employees and friends, gave part of his own salary, and sent it to Lencho in a letter signed 'God'.

LONGL35 marks

3. 'The story is built around two ironies.' Explain with reference to the text.

Show Answer

Ans:

The first irony is in the weather: Lencho's field needed rain, and rain did come — but it turned into a hailstorm that destroyed the entire crop. The very thing that promised a good harvest took it away.
The second irony is in the ending: the post office employees, moved by Lencho's faith, collected seventy pesos and sent them to him. Lencho, finding the amount short, wrote back accusing those same men of stealing the money and calling them 'a bunch of crooks'. The helpers become the accused thieves, and the man who never doubted God doubts the honesty of human beings.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Was Lencho surprised to find a letter with money in it? What made him angry?

Show Answer

Ans:

No, he was not surprised at all — he had expected an answer from God, and his confidence in God was so complete that receiving a reply seemed natural. What made him angry was that the envelope contained only seventy pesos instead of the hundred he had asked for. He was certain that God could not have made a mistake, so he concluded that the post office employees had taken the rest.

LONGL55 marks

5. Discuss “A Letter to God” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Summary. Lencho, a simple hardworking farmer, lives on the crest of a low hill in a Latin American valley, with his cornfield ripening and dotted with flowers. Rain comes — but it turns into a hailstorm that destroys the crop completely. Lencho's only hope is God, so he writes a letter asking for one hundred pesos to sow his field again and to live until the next harvest. The postmaster, a fat, amiable fellow, is mov.
Marks lens: Reference-to-context extracts and one 5-mark value-based long answer. The irony is always the question.
Method / memory trick: The word 'crooks' in the last line is the exam favourite. It means dishonest people — and the irony is that Lencho says it about the men who gave him money.
Do not: Saying Lencho received one hundred pesos. He received seventy.
