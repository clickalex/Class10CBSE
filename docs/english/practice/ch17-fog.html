Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. What does the poet compare the fog to? Does he actually say that the fog is a cat?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poet compares the fog to a cat. He says it comes on little cat feet, sits on silent haunches looking over the harbour and city, and then moves on — all the quiet, soft, unhurried movements of a cat. He never says directly that the fog is a cat, and he never uses the word 'like' or 'as', so the comparison is a metaphor rather than a simile.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is the central idea of the poem?

Show Answer

Ans:

The central idea is that fog, like a cat, arrives silently and without warning, covers the city and the harbour, looks down on them, and then moves away. The comparison brings out the mysterious, gentle and unobtrusive nature of fog, and shows how a simple natural event can be described vividly in a few lines.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Identify the figure of speech used in 'The fog comes on little cat feet'.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is a metaphor, since the fog is directly compared to a cat without the use of 'like' or 'as'. There is also personification, because the fog is given the cat's action of walking on little feet and sitting on silent haunches.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “Fog” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem. The fog comes / on little cat feet. / It sits looking / over harbour and city / on silent haunches / and then moves on. Six lines, no rhyme.
Marks lens: Reference-to-context extracts; a 3-mark answer on the central metaphor and on whether the poet actually says the fog is a cat.
Method / memory trick: It is a metaphor, not a simile. The poet never uses 'like' or 'as' — that distinction is a marked point.
Do not: Saying the poet says the fog is a cat. He compares it to a cat; the fog is not literally a cat.
