MCQs

In the poem, 'ice' symbolises: (a) death (b) hatred (c) sorrow (d) winter

(b) hatred — fire symbolises desire.

The rhyme scheme of 'Fire and Ice' is: (a) abab cdcd (b) aba abc bcb (c) aabb ccdd (d) abcb abcb

(b) aba abc bcb, an unusual interlocking scheme.

The poet says that for destruction ice is: (a) not sufficient (b) also great and would suffice (c) less dangerous than fire (d) impossible

(b) also great and would suffice — hatred alone could destroy the world.

Written questions

3 marks What do 'fire' and 'ice' stand for in the poem?

Fire stands for desire or passion, and ice stands for hatred. The poet says that from what he has tasted of desire he agrees with those who say the world will end in fire, but he knows enough of hate to say that for destruction ice is also great and would suffice. Both emotions are destructive enough to end the world.

3 marks What is the central idea of the poem?

The central idea is that the world may be destroyed by two opposite but equally powerful human emotions — desire, which is like fire, and hatred, which is like ice. The poet does not say which is worse; he says that both would suffice. Desire consumes quickly and passionately, while hatred works slowly and coldly, but the result is the same destruction.

3 marks Comment on the rhyme scheme of the poem.

The rhyme scheme is aba abc bcb, an interlocking pattern rather than a simple alternating one. It links the two ideas of fire and ice across the stanzas, and it allows the poet to repeat the sound of 'fire' and 'desire' and of 'ice', 'suffice' and 'twice', so the two symbols keep echoing each other.
