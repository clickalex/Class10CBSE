Ch 3 · Two Stories about Flying — Practice

MCQs

'His First Flight' is written by: (a) Frederick Forsyth (b) Liam O'Flaherty (c) G. L. Fuentes (d) Gavin Maxwell

(b) Liam O'Flaherty.

How long was the young seagull left alone on his ledge before he flew? (a) twelve hours (b) twenty-four hours (c) two days (d) three days

(b) twenty-four hours, during which he was hungry and afraid while his siblings flew.

In 'The Black Aeroplane', the woman in the control centre told the narrator that: (a) the other pilot had landed elsewhere (b) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night (c) the black plane was a military aircraft (d) the radar had failed

(b) there was no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on the radar.

Written questions

3 marks Why was the young seagull afraid to fly? What compelled him to finally make the flight?

He was afraid because he felt certain that his wings would never support him, and because his siblings had already flown while he was left behind on the ledge. What compelled him to fly was hunger: when his mother tore a piece of fish and stood on the ledge, the sight of the food maddened him, and he dived at it. In that dive he fell outward into space and, flapping his wings, found that he could fly.

3 marks Describe the help the pilot received during the storm in 'The Black Aeroplane'.

When his instruments failed and he ran low on fuel, he saw a black aeroplane with no lights on its wings. Its pilot waved to him and led him through the storm until the narrator came out of the clouds and could see the runway. When he landed and asked the woman in the control centre about the other pilot, she told him there had been no other aeroplane in the sky that night, and no other plane on her radar.

5 marks 'Both the stories are about overcoming fear.' Justify with reference to the two stories.

In His First Flight, the young seagull is paralysed by fear while his brothers and sister fly. His parents scold him and his siblings taunt him, but fear keeps him on the ledge for twenty-four hours. Only extreme hunger, and the sight of his mother with a piece of fish, drive him to dive — and in that desperate moment he discovers that his wings do support him. His fear is overcome by necessity and by the example of his family.
In The Black Aeroplane, the narrator is an experienced pilot, but the storm terrifies him: the compass and radio fail, he cannot see anything, and he has only a few minutes of fuel left. His fear is overcome by following the mysterious black aeroplane, whose pilot leads him out of the storm and vanishes. He never finds out who helped him, which suggests that the courage came from within.
In both stories, fear is conquered only by taking the leap — the seagull by diving, the pilot by following into the dark — and in both the reward is a safe arrival.

3 marks How did the seagull's family react when he flew for the first time?

They screamed around him, praising him and offering him scraps of fish. They landed ahead of him on the water, beckoning him to join them, and when he finally sank tired onto the sea and floated, they praised him again — his first flight was complete.
