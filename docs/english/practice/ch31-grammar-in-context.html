MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Choose the correct sentence: (a) The committee have taken their seats. (b) The committee has taken its seat. (c) The committee have taken its seat. (d) The committee has taken their seats.

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) — when the collective noun acts as a single unit, the verb and the pronoun are both singular.

MCQM21 mark

2. Report the sentence: He said, 'I am leaving tomorrow.' (a) He said that he was leaving the next day. (b) He said that he is leaving tomorrow. (c) He said that he left tomorrow. (d) He said that he will leave the next day.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) — the tense moves back one step, and 'tomorrow' becomes 'the next day'.

MCQM31 mark

3. Fill in the blank: She has been waiting ____ two hours. (a) for (b) since (c) from (d) during

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) for — 'for' is used with a period of time; 'since' is used with a point in time.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS11 mark

1. Fill in the blank: Neither of the two candidates ____ (has/have) replied yet.

Show Answer

Ans:

has. 'Neither of' takes a singular verb, because the subject is 'neither', not 'candidates'.

SHORTS22 marks

2. Change into reported speech: 'Where have you kept my files?' she asked the clerk.

Show Answer

Ans:

She asked the clerk where he had kept her files. Note the three changes: the present perfect becomes the past perfect, the question word order becomes statement order, and 'my' becomes 'her'.

SHORTS31 mark

3. Fill in the blank with a suitable modal: You ____ consult a doctor if the cough persists. (can / should / may)

Show Answer

Ans:

should — the sentence gives advice, which is the use of 'should'. 'Can' expresses ability and 'may' expresses permission or possibility, neither of which fits.

SHORTS41 mark

4. Choose the correct determiner: There isn't ____ milk left in the jug. (some / any / much)

Show Answer

Ans:

any — 'any' is used with uncountable nouns in negative sentences; 'some' is used in affirmative sentences.

LONGL55 marks

5. Discuss “Grammar in Context” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Tenses. The tense must agree with the time marker in the sentence. Since and for take the perfect tenses; yesterday, last year take the simple past; now, at present take the present continuous; tomorrow, next week take the future. In a narrative passage, the tense often shifts only when the time shifts — so keep the tense until a new time marker appears.
Marks lens: About 10 marks, usually as a gap-filling or editing task set within a short passage or dialogue.
Method / memory trick: In a gap-filling passage, look at the other verbs first — the tense of the passage is usually consistent.
Do not: Using the simple past with 'since'. Since requires a perfect tense.
