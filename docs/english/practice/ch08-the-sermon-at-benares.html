MCQM81 mark

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Kisa Gotami go from house to house, and what did she discover?

Show Answer

Ans:

Her only son had died, and she went from house to house asking for medicine to cure him. The Buddha told her to bring a handful of mustard seed, but on the condition that it be taken from a house where no one had died. In every house she entered, someone had died — a father, a daughter, a servant — so she could not find a single such house. She discovered that death is common to all, and that her grief had made her blind to the suffering of others.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What did the Buddha say about grieving?

Show Answer

Ans:

He said that no one obtains peace of mind from weeping or from grief; on the contrary, the pain will be the greater and the body will suffer, and the person will make himself sick and pale, yet the dead are not saved by his lamentation. Those who seek peace of mind should draw out the arrow of lamentation, complaint and grief, and having drawn it out, they will obtain peace.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Explain the meaning of 'the world is afflicted with death and decay, therefore the wise do not grieve'.

Show Answer

Ans:

It means that death and change are built into the world, so no amount of sorrow can prevent them. The wise understand this truth and accept it, so they do not grieve; they realise that lamentation cannot bring back the dead and only harms the living. Accepting the nature of the world is what brings peace of mind.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What were the four sights that led the Buddha to leave the palace?

Show Answer

Ans:

While out hunting at the age of about twenty-five, the prince came upon a sick man, then an aged man, then a funeral procession, and finally a monk begging for alms. These four sights showed him sickness, old age, death and renunciation, and moved him so deeply that he left the palace at once in search of enlightenment.

SHORTS73 marks

7. What did Kisa Gotami learn from the handful of mustard seed?

Show Answer

Ans:

Carrying her dead child from neighbour to neighbour, Kisa Gotami was told to bring mustard seed from a house where no one had ever died.
She went from house to house and everyone was willing to give her seed, but no household was free of death.
She saw that there was not a single house where some beloved one had not died, and understood that death is common to all.
She accepted the truth, stopped grieving, and took her child's body to the cremation ground before returning to the Buddha to become his follower.

LONGL104 marks

10. Describe the Buddha's sermon on death and its message.

Show Answer

Ans:

The Buddha told Kisa Gotami that the life of mortals in this world is troubled and brief and combined with pain; there is no means by which those who are born can avoid dying.
He said that a man is like a potter's earthen vessel: the vessels end in being broken, and so does mortal life.
Both young and adult, both those who are fools and those who are wise, all fall into the power of death; the only escape is to accept it and to live wisely.
He asked her not to grieve, because weeping and complainers gain nothing; only a man who does not tremble at death and does not grieve finds peace.

LONGL115 marks

11. How did the Buddha make Kisa Gotami understand the nature of death without preaching to her?

Show Answer

Ans:

He did not tell her the truth directly. He asked her to bring him a handful of mustard seed — a simple request that gave her hope.
He then added one condition: the mustard seed must be taken from a house where no one had died, where no son, husband, parent or servant had been lost.
Kisa Gotami went from door to door with confidence, but in the first house she was told the father had died; in the next, a daughter; then a servant, then a slave. There was no house untouched by death.
By evening she was tired and hopeless. She sat and watched the lights of the city being lit and then going out until darkness covered everything, and she realised the selfishness of her grief — she had been absorbed in her own loss while death is common to all.
So the Buddha's method was to let her experience the truth rather than hear it, which is why it changed her completely. She took her child's body to the forest and returned to him ready to listen.
