Ch 13 · How to Tell Wild Animals — Practice

MCQs

'How to Tell Wild Animals' is written by: (a) Carolyn Wells (b) Leslie Norris (c) Vicky Tree (d) Walt Whitman

(a) Carolyn Wells.

The animal that 'hugs you very, very hard' is the: (a) bear (b) hyena (c) leopard (d) chameleon

(a) the bear — a bear hug is the popular image the poet plays on.

The leopard's hide has: (a) black stripes on a yellow ground (b) spots that pepper it (c) no markings (d) brown patches

(b) spots that pepper it; black stripes belong to the Bengal tiger.

Written questions

3 marks How does the poet suggest you can tell a leopard from a tiger?

The Bengal tiger has a hide with black stripes on a yellow ground and announces itself with a roar. The leopard has a hide with spots that pepper it, and it does not roar — it simply leaps at you, and it will do no good to roar with pain, because it will only leap again.

3 marks What is the humour in the poem?

The humour comes from the poet's pretended practical advice: the only reliable way to identify a wild animal is to be attacked by it, and by the time you are certain, you are being eaten. The advice is useless to the reader, who is meant to be the bait. The poet also plays with the animals' characteristics — the bear's hug, the crocodile's tears, the hyena's smile — turning well-known sayings into a comic method of identification.

3 marks Explain the lines about the crocodile and the hyena.

The poet plays on two popular beliefs. A crocodile is said to weep before it eats its prey, hence 'crocodile tears'; so if a creature weeps, it is a crocodile. A hyena is supposed to laugh, so the hyena 'comes with merry smiles'. By contrasting weeping with smiling, the poet gives the reader an amusing rule for telling the two apart — a rule that would only be tested at the cost of one's life.
