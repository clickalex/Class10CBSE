MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. 'The Tale of Custard the Dragon' is written by: (a) Carl Sandburg (b) Ogden Nash (c) Robin Klein (d) Adrienne Rich

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Ogden Nash.

MCQM21 mark

2. Blink in the poem is: (a) a little black kitten (b) a little yellow dog (c) a little grey mouse (d) a little pet dragon

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) a little grey mouse. Ink is the black kitten and Mustard is the yellow dog.

MCQM31 mark

3. When the pirate appeared, Mustard: (a) attacked him (b) hid behind Belinda (c) barked at him (d) fled with a terrified cry

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) fled with a terrified cry — despite having boasted that he was as brave as a tiger in a rage.

MCQM41 mark

4. What does Custard do when the pirate threatens the household? (a) He confronts and defeats him (b) He hides with Mustard (c) He leaves the house (d) He asks Ink to fight

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) He confronts and defeats him. Custard is the one who faces the danger.

MCQM51 mark

5. What do the other pets do after the pirate has been defeated? (a) They return to boasting about their bravery (b) They leave the house forever (c) They give Custard a crown (d) They refuse to speak to Belinda

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) They return to boasting about their bravery. Their confidence returns once the danger is gone.

MCQM61 mark

6. Custard is teased by the others because he: (a) cannot fly (b) has no teeth (c) is too small (d) is cowardly and cries for a nice safe cage

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) is cowardly and cries for a nice safe cage. His cry for safety is the refrain of the poem.

MCQM71 mark

7. Belinda's other pets are: (a) a cow and a goat (b) a kitten, a mouse and a dog (c) a parrot and a crow (d) a horse and a hen

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) a kitten, a mouse and a dog. Ink is the kitten, Blink the mouse and Mustard the dog.

MCQM81 mark

8. The rhyme scheme of the poem is: (a) abab (b) abcb (c) aabb (d) free verse

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) aabb. The paired rhymes give the ballad its rolling, song-like rhythm.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why did Custard cry for a nice safe cage?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because he was a coward, or at least thought of himself as one. While Belinda and the other pets boasted of their bravery, Custard admitted his fear and asked for a nice safe cage. His cry is honest, and it is what makes his later action so surprising — he is the only one who did not pretend to be brave.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Describe the pirate and how Custard dealt with him.

Show Answer

Ans:

The pirate climbed in through the window with a pistol in his hand and two cutlasses shining, a black beard and one wooden leg, and he was clearly bad. Mustard fled with a terrified cry, Ink trickled down to the bottom of the house, and Blink disappeared into his mouse hole. Custard jumped up, snorting like an engine, clashing his tail like iron in a dungeon, and went at the pirate like a robin at a worm — and he ate him, every bit.

APPLICATIONA33 marks

3. Before the pirate arrives, Custard is called a coward because he asks for a safe cage. Explain how his actions during the danger complicate that label.

Show Answer

Ans:

Custard openly admits fear by asking for a safe cage, while the other pets boast of their bravery.
When the pirate appears, the boastful pets flee; Custard faces the danger and defeats him.
The contrast shows that loud claims do not prove courage; Custard acts when action matters, even though he still prefers safety afterwards.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Imagine you are directing the pirate scene for a class performance. How would you stage the contrast between the pets' boasting and Custard's response?

Show Answer

Ans:

Before the danger, let Belinda, Mustard, Ink and Blink appear confident as they boast, while Custard asks for safety.
When the pirate enters, show the others running away and Custard confronting him; that reversal creates the poem's irony.

SHORTS53 marks

5. How does Custard prove himself when the pirate appears?

Show Answer

Ans:

While Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all run away in terror, Custard alone stands his ground.
He snorts, claps his sharp teeth, gulps some mustard at the pirate and charges at him like a robin at a worm.
The pirate fires two bullets that miss, and Custard swallows him in one bite, clashing his tail like irons in a dungeon.
The ‘cowardly' dragon turns out to be the only one with real courage, which is the joke of the poem.

SHORTS63 marks

6. How do Belinda and the other pets behave after Custard kills the pirate? What does their behaviour show?

Show Answer

Ans:

Belinda embraces Custard and Mustard licks him — but within a page they go back to boasting: Mustard says he would have been twice as brave, Ink and Blink say they would have been three times as brave, and Custard agrees with all of them.
Their behaviour shows that cowards mock true courage and then claim the credit afterwards — while the truly brave Custard keeps quiet and asks again for his nice safe cage.

COMPETENCYC74 marks

7. Read and answer: Belinda and the pets have been praised for bravery, while Custard is teased for wanting a safe cage. When the pirate enters with weapons, the others run away and Custard attacks him. (a) What changes when the pirate appears? (b) How does Custard respond? (c) What contrast creates the humour?

Show Answer

Ans:

The real danger tests the pets' earlier claims; the scene reveals who will act rather than merely boast.
Custard faces the pirate and defeats him, protecting the household.
The ones who called themselves brave flee, while the pet who admitted fear proves courageous in the moment.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: After Custard defeats the pirate, the household is safe again. The other pets soon return to praising their own bravery, while Custard again asks for a safe cage. (a) What does the pets' return to boasting show? (b) What does Custard's request show about him? (c) Why is the ending more complex than a simple “cowards are bad” lesson?

Show Answer

Ans:

Their claims of courage are self-congratulation; they did not stand their ground when the danger arrived.
Custard is still cautious and prefers safety, even though he proved capable of acting bravely.
The poem distinguishes fear from behaviour: feeling afraid does not prevent someone from acting well, and being cautious is not itself a moral fault.

LONGL94 marks

9. What makes ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon' a ballad of humour?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is written in the form of a ballad, with regular quatrains, a strong rhythm and a repeating refrain about the little pet dragon.
The names are comic — Belinda, Ink, Blink, Mustard and Custard — and the rhyme is deliberately playful, as in ‘realio, trulio, little pet dragon'.
The situation is upside down: the fierce-looking pets flee while the teased dragon fights, and the pirate is described as a dastardly coward.
The ending is comic too: once the danger is over, everyone goes back to boasting, and Custard meekly wishes for his nice safe cage again.

LONGL104 marks

10. ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon’ is called a ballad. Which features of a ballad do you find in it?

Show Answer

Ans:

A story in verse — the ballad narrates a complete tale with a beginning (the household), a crisis (the pirate) and an ending (the kill).
Music and repetition — the aabb rhyme, the rolling rhythm and repeated refrains like ‘coward, and she called him Custard’ make it singable.
A dramatic climax — the pirate's attack and Custard's sudden bravery give it the ballad's single moment of high action.

LONGL115 marks

11. What is the central idea of the poem? Is Custard a coward?

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem is a humorous story about bravery and boasting. Belinda, Ink, Blink and Mustard all boast about how brave they are, while Custard openly admits his fear and asks for a nice safe cage.
When the pirate actually appears, the boasters run away: Mustard flees with a terrified cry, Ink trickles to the bottom of the house, and Blink disappears into his mouse hole. Only Custard faces the pirate and eats him up, every bit.
The moral is that those who boast are not necessarily brave, and the one who admits his fear may be the bravest of all when action is needed. Bravery is shown in deeds, not in words.
Is Custard a coward? The poet leaves this open. Custard himself says he is a coward and would still prefer a nice safe cage, and the others all agree they are braver than he is. The humour lies in the fact that nobody changes their opinion, even after Custard has saved them. So the poem suggests that a person can be genuinely brave in action and still be timid by nature — the two are not contradictory.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does Ogden Nash use the pirate episode to challenge the difference between appearing brave and acting bravely?

Show Answer

Ans:

Before the pirate arrives, Belinda and the other pets boast of their courage while Custard is mocked for wanting a safe cage.
The danger reverses their roles: the boastful pets run away, but Custard confronts the pirate and saves them.
The comic contrast makes action, not talk, the test of courage. Custard does not need to pretend that he feels no fear.
Afterward he returns to asking for a cage, which keeps the poem from becoming a simple lesson that caution is shameful; a frightened person can still act with courage.
