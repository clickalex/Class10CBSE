Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. What is the difference between a discursive and a case-based passage?

Show Answer

Ans:

A discursive passage is 400–450 words and argues or discusses an idea, so the questions test inference, interpretation and evaluation. A case-based factual passage is 200–250 words and is supported by visual input such as statistical data or a chart, so the questions test your ability to read the data and connect it to the text.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Describe a method for attempting the reading section in the time available.

Show Answer

Ans:

Read the questions first, so that you know what to look for. Then read the passage once quickly for the main idea. Go question by question, locate the relevant line and underline it. Eliminate the two options that are clearly wrong, then choose between the remaining two by returning to the text. Keep to about ninety seconds per question and mark any you cannot decide, returning to them at the end.

SHORTS33 marks

3. How should you answer a vocabulary-in-context question?

Show Answer

Ans:

Find the sentence containing the word, cover the word, and decide for yourself what word would fit in its place. Then look at the options and choose the one closest to your own word. Do not choose a meaning you have met elsewhere, because the meaning must fit the sentence in front of you.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

What is tested. The questions test inference (what is implied but not stated), analysis (how an argument is built), interpretation (what a word or phrase means in context), evaluation (whether a claim is supported), and vocabulary. Because the questions are multiple choice, the answer is always in the passage — never bring in outside knowledge.
Marks lens: About 15 marks. Two passages: a discursive passage of 400–450 words and a case-based factual passage of 200–250 words with visual input such as statistical data or a chart.
Method / memory trick: Questions first, then passage. It saves two or three minutes on a fifteen-mark section.
Do not: Answering from your own knowledge instead of from the passage.
