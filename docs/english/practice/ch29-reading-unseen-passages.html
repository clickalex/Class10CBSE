MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The length of the discursive passage in the reading section is: (a) 200–250 words (b) 300–350 words (c) 400–450 words (d) 500–550 words

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) 400–450 words. The case-based factual passage is 200–250 words.

MCQM21 mark

2. Objective type questions in the reading section assess: (a) memorisation (b) inference, analysis, interpretation, evaluation and vocabulary (c) handwriting (d) grammar rules

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) — they test higher-order reading skills, not recall.

MCQM31 mark

3. The best first step in attempting an unseen passage is to: (a) read the passage three times (b) read the questions first (c) answer from general knowledge (d) begin with the hardest question

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) read the questions first, so that you know what information to look for.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most exam-ready way to revise “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages” is: (a) read the heading only (b) learn the key idea plus one example (c) skip written practice (d) memorise a random extra topic

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) learn the key idea plus one example. The chapter is Discursive and factual passages, MCQ-based, with a speed strategy..

MCQM51 mark

5. A 3-mark answer on “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages” should: (a) be one sentence (b) start with the idea, then a reason or method (c) copy the whole chapter (d) leave the example out

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) start with the idea, then a reason or method. Step marks follow that frame.

MCQM61 mark

6. Before writing the answers to an unseen passage you should: (a) read the questions, then read the passage (b) start writing immediately (c) copy lines from the passage (d) skip the passage

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) read the questions, then read the passage. It saves time and keeps your reading focused.

MCQM71 mark

7. Answers to an unseen passage should be: (a) in your own words and within the word limit (b) as long as possible (c) copied from the passage (d) written as bullet points only

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) in your own words and within the word limit. Copying whole sentences loses marks.

MCQM81 mark

8. In a case-based factual passage, the visual input is usually: (a) a chart, table or graph (b) a photograph (c) a map only (d) a cartoon

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) a chart, table or graph. The questions test whether you can read data, not just prose.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. What is the difference between a discursive and a case-based passage?

Show Answer

Ans:

A discursive passage is 400–450 words and argues or discusses an idea, so the questions test inference, interpretation and evaluation. A case-based factual passage is 200–250 words and is supported by visual input such as statistical data or a chart, so the questions test your ability to read the data and connect it to the text.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Describe a method for attempting the reading section in the time available.

Show Answer

Ans:

Read the questions first, so that you know what to look for. Then read the passage once quickly for the main idea. Go question by question, locate the relevant line and underline it. Eliminate the two options that are clearly wrong, then choose between the remaining two by returning to the text. Keep to about ninety seconds per question and mark any you cannot decide, returning to them at the end.

SHORTS33 marks

3. How should you answer a vocabulary-in-context question?

Show Answer

Ans:

Find the sentence containing the word, cover the word, and decide for yourself what word would fit in its place. Then look at the options and choose the one closest to your own word. Do not choose a meaning you have met elsewhere, because the meaning must fit the sentence in front of you.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. Riya is revising “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages” (Discursive and factual passages, MCQ-based, with a speed strategy.). A classmate lost marks by doing this: Answering from your own knowledge instead of from the passage. Apply the correct method and rewrite the scoring answer.

Show Answer

Ans:

Correct method: Questions first, then passage. It saves two or three minutes on a fifteen-mark section.
Why the mistake costs marks: Answering from your own knowledge instead of from the passage.
Write the definition or formula first, then the working or example.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. Amit is given this exam-style task from “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages”: “What is the difference between a discursive and a case-based passage?” Treat it as an application question. Identify the idea, then write the working in points.

Show Answer

Ans:

Idea: Discursive and factual passages, MCQ-based, with a speed strategy..
A discursive passage is 400–450 words and argues or discusses an idea, so the questions test inference, interpretation and evaluation. A case-based factual passage is 200–250 words and is supported by visual input such as statistical data or a chart, so the questions test your ability to read the data and connect it to the text.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is the right way to attempt an unseen passage in the board paper?

Show Answer

Ans:

Read the questions first, so that you know what to look for while reading.
Then read the passage once quickly for the sense and a second time to locate the answers, underlining key words.
Answer in complete sentences and, wherever asked, in your own words; do not copy long phrases from the passage.
Stick to the word limit — usually 30 to 40 words for a two-mark question — and number the answers exactly as the questions are numbered.

SHORTS73 marks

7. What is the ‘eliminate two options’ rule, and why does it work in the reading section?

Show Answer

Ans:

In every reading MCQ, two options can usually be ruled out at once — one contradicts the passage and one is not mentioned in it at all.
That leaves a fifty-fifty choice between two close options, which you settle by re-reading the exact lines the question points to.
It works because boards design one distractor as opposite and one as out-of-scope; eliminating both is faster and safer than hunting directly for the answer.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: Two students attempt: “What is the difference between a discursive and a case-based passage?” Student A writes one line. Student B writes points, an example and a check. (a) Which answer frame should you use for full marks? (b) Write the model points. (c) Add one memory trick you would use on exam morning.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Statement → working or reasons in points → example or check. One-line answers lose step marks.
(b) A discursive passage is 400–450 words and argues or discusses an idea, so the questions test inference, interpretation and evaluation. A case-based factual passage is 200–250 words and is supported by visual input such as statistical data or a chart, so the questions test your ability to read the data and connect it to the text.
(c) Questions first, then passage. It saves two or three minutes on a fifteen-mark section.

APPLICATIONA104 marks

10. How should you answer a case-based factual passage with MCQ and short items?

Show Answer

Ans:

Read the whole case once, including any table, chart or data given with it, before looking at the questions.
For the MCQ, choose the option that is supported by the passage and cannot be contradicted by any line in it; do not bring in outside knowledge.
For short items, write one clear sentence with the fact taken from the passage and the number or unit it gives.
Keep every answer brief and grammatical, and do not repeat the question in the answer.

APPLICATIONA114 marks

11. You have ninety seconds per question and a 450-word passage. Plan your time across reading, locating and answering.

Show Answer

Ans:

First minute — read the questions, not the passage, so you know what to hunt for.
Next three to four minutes — one fast read of the passage, underlining names, figures and opinion words.
Then about a minute per question — locate the exact line, eliminate two options, mark the answer, and move on without re-reading the whole passage.

LONGL125 marks

12. Discuss “Reading: Unseen and Case-based Passages” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

What is tested. The questions test inference (what is implied but not stated), analysis (how an argument is built), interpretation (what a word or phrase means in context), evaluation (whether a claim is supported), and vocabulary. Because the questions are multiple choice, the answer is always in the passage — never bring in outside knowledge.
Marks lens: About 15 marks. Two passages: a discursive passage of 400–450 words and a case-based factual passage of 200–250 words with visual input such as statistical data or a chart.
Method / memory trick: Questions first, then passage. It saves two or three minutes on a fifteen-mark section.
Do not: Answering from your own knowledge instead of from the passage.
