MCQM11 mark

1. Horace Danby robbed a safe: (a) every month (b) once a year (c) whenever he needed money (d) only from the rich and the poor alike

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) once a year, planning each robbery carefully so that the money lasted twelve months.

MCQM21 mark

2. The safe that Horace planned to rob was at: (a) Shotover Grange (b) Shotover House (c) a London bank (d) a jeweller's shop

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Shotover Grange, which contained about fifteen thousand pounds' worth of jewels.

MCQM31 mark

(b) he left his fingerprints all over the room — he had forgotten to wear gloves.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How did the young woman manage to get the jewels from Horace?

Show Answer

Ans:

She appeared while he was working on the safe and spoke to him as if she were the lady of the house, saying she had arrived home just in time. She told him that the jewels were needed that evening for a party, that she had forgotten the number of the safe, and that if he did not open it for her she would have to call the police. Afraid of being sent to prison, Horace opened the safe and handed her the jewels, and she let him go. She was herself a burglar, who had been waiting for her chance.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why was Horace arrested?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because he had left his fingerprints all over the room. When the young woman told him her story and asked him to open the safe, he was so relieved and so eager to escape that he forgot to put on his gloves. Two days later the police arrested him, using the fingerprints as evidence.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Do you think the young lady was right in tricking Horace? Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. She used another person's crime to cover her own, and she let an innocent-seeming man take the blame. Although Horace was himself a thief, she could have simply left the house instead of framing him. Her action shows that 'honour among thieves' does not exist — she was willing to send him to prison to protect herself and keep the jewels.
