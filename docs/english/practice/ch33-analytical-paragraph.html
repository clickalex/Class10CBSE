Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Describe the structure of an analytical paragraph.

Show Answer

Ans:

The paragraph opens with a sentence introducing the data — what it shows, the period it covers and the units used. The middle sentences describe the main features, selecting the significant figures and comparing them. The final sentence gives a conclusion or a trend inferred from the data. The whole paragraph is within the word limit and contains no opinion.

LONGL25 marks

2. Write an analytical paragraph on a bar chart showing the mode of transport used by students: bus 45%, cycle 25%, walk 20%, private car 10%.

Show Answer

Ans:

The bar chart shows how students travel to school, expressed as a percentage of the total number of students.
The most common mode of transport is the bus, used by 45 per cent of students — almost half. Cycling is the second most popular, at 25 per cent, while 20 per cent of students walk to school. Only 10 per cent travel by private car, the least common mode.
Taken together, the figures show that the great majority of students — 90 per cent — use public or non-motorised transport, and that reliance on private cars is minimal. This suggests that improving bus services and cycle lanes would benefit far more students than any measure aimed at car users.

SHORTS33 marks

3. What should an analytical paragraph avoid?

Show Answer

Ans:

It should avoid giving personal opinions or recommendations unless the question asks for them, inventing data that is not in the chart, listing every figure instead of selecting the significant ones, and exceeding the word limit. It should describe and compare the data, not comment on it.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “Analytical Paragraph” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The structure. First sentence: introduce the data — what the chart shows, what period it covers and what units are used. Middle sentences: describe the main features, comparing and contrasting the significant figures. Last sentence: give a conclusion or a trend inferred from the data. Do not list every number — select the two or three that matter.
Marks lens: marks, 100–120 words. Based on a chart, graph, table, data set, outline or set of clues.
Method / memory trick: Open with what the chart shows, then compare, then conclude. Three moves, one paragraph.
Do not: Copying every number from the table.
