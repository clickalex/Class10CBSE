MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The people of Coorg wear a long black coat with an embroidered waist-belt called: (a) kabai (b) Kuppia (c) kuffia (d) bol

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Kuppia — its resemblance to the kuffia of the Arabs and Kurds supports the Arab-origin theory.

MCQM21 mark

2. According to the chapter, how many cups of tea are drunk every day throughout the world? (a) eight crore (b) eight hundred crore (c) eight lakh (d) eighty crore

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) eighty crore cups a day.

MCQM31 mark

3. In Goa, the sweet bread given as a marriage gift is called: (a) pao (b) bolinhas (c) bol (d) kabai

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) bol — bolinhas are the coconut cookies served at Christmas.

MCQM41 mark

4. The sweet bread that is made in Goa for weddings is called: (a) bolinhas (b) pao (c) bol (d) kabai

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) bol. Bol is the marriage bread; bolinhas are coconut cookies for Christmas.

MCQM51 mark

5. Coorg lies midway between which two cities? (a) Mysore and Mangalore (b) Bengaluru and Chennai (c) Mumbai and Pune (d) Kochi and Madurai

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Mysore and Mangalore. Coorg, or Kodagu, is a hilly district of Karnataka between Mysore and the coastal town of Mangalore.

MCQM61 mark

6. The traditional bakers of Goa are called: (a) pao wallahs (b) bakers (c) lohars (d) paders

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) paders. Their ovens are called ‘pods' and the marriage gift bread is called ‘bol'.

MCQM71 mark

7. Coorg is famous for: (a) tea only (b) coffee plantations and oranges (c) spices only (d) wheat

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) coffee plantations and oranges. The Coorgis are also known for their hospitality and their Kodava martial traditions.

MCQM81 mark

8. The finest tea is picked during: (a) the second flush, from May to July (b) the first flush (c) winter (d) the monsoon

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the second flush, from May to July. This is when the best Assam tea comes to the market.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why is the baker called a friend, companion and guide in Goa?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because he was woven into the daily life of the village. His arrival, announced by the jangling of his bamboo staff, was a fixed event twice a day, and children ran to meet him to choose their bread bangles. He was present at every celebration — bol for marriages, bolinhas and coconut cookies for Christmas, sandwiches for engagements — so his presence marked the family's important occasions.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What do you know about the people of Coorg?

Show Answer

Ans:

They are a proud race of martial men, beautiful women and wild creatures. They are perhaps the most hospitable people in India and are the only people in India permitted to carry firearms without a licence. One theory says they are descended from a part of Alexander's army that settled there and married among the locals; another says they are of Arab origin, because the Kuppia, the long black coat with an embroidered waist-belt worn by the Kodavus, resembles the kuffia worn by Arabs and Kurds.

SHORTS33 marks

3. How does the legend of tea connect China and India?

Show Answer

Ans:

According to a Chinese legend, tea was first drunk in China around 2700 BC, when a few leaves from a twig burning under a vessel of boiling water fell into it and gave a delicious drink. An Indian legend says that Bodhidharma, an ancient Buddhist ascetic, cut off his eyelids because he felt sleepy during meditation, and ten tea plants grew out of the eyelids; the leaves of these plants, when put in hot water and drunk, banished sleep. Tea came to Europe only in the sixteenth century, and was drunk more as medicine than as a beverage.

APPLICATIONA43 marks

4. A student writes that Coorg is in Kerala and that the Chinese legend of tea goes back to 2700 AD. Correct both statements.

Show Answer

Ans:

Coorg (Kodagu) is in Karnataka; it is the smallest district of the state and lies midway between Mysore and Mangalore.
According to the Chinese legend, tea was first drunk around 2700 BC, when leaves from a burning twig fell into a vessel of boiling water.
(An Indian legend adds that tea plants grew from the eyelids of the monk Bodhidharma.)

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. The Goan baker is part of village life. Name one person from your own locality, such as a milkman or a postman, who is similarly part of community life, and explain how in about three lines.

Show Answer

Ans:

Any suitable example, for instance the milkman who comes every morning.
He is part of daily routine: people know the sound or time of his arrival and plan around it.
Like the baker, he is present in families’ regular life and sometimes in special occasions, so people think of him as a friend and not only a seller.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is a ‘pader' and how does Goa's Portuguese inheritance survive in its bread?

Show Answer

Ans:

A pader is the traditional Goan baker; the descendants of these bakers still run the bread business in Goa.
The Portuguese introduced the leavened bread called pao, which is still the staple of every Goan meal alongside rice, fish, curry and vegetables.
The bread is baked in the old-fashioned ‘pod' or mud ovens, and the baker's bamboo staff thumping on the floor is still heard at dawn.
Marriage gifts called ‘bol' and the sweet bread of special occasions keep the tradition alive.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. The chapter covers Goa, Coorg and Assam. What does each place contribute to the ‘glimpse of India’?

Show Answer

Ans:

Goa contributes living history — the Portuguese pader, the kabai and the bol show how a colonial past survives in daily bread.
Coorg contributes landscape and valour — coffee-scented hills, the martial Kodavus and the Kaveri's origin.
Assam contributes a global story — tea drunk eighty crore cups a day, linking Chinese legend and Indian gardens. Together they show India's variety.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: In Goa, the baker announces himself with the jingling of his bamboo staff, and the children rush to him to pick their bread bangles. (a) What announces the baker’s arrival? (b) Name the breads or cookies made for a wedding and for Christmas. (c) What does the baker’s role show about Goan culture?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) The jingling of the bamboo staff that he thumps on the floor.
(b) Bol is the sweet bread made for marriages and bolinhas are the coconut cookies of Christmas.
(c) The Portuguese influence lives on in food, and the baker marks every important family occasion.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: The Kodavus of Coorg are described as a proud, martial race. (a) What special privilege do they have? (b) What two theories are given about their origin? (c) What clue supports the Arab theory?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) They are the only people in India allowed to carry firearms without a licence.
(b) That they descended from part of Alexander’s army that settled in Coorg, or that they are of Arab origin.
(c) Their traditional long black coat with an embroidered waist-belt, the kuppia, resembles the kuffia worn by Arabs and Kurds.

LONGL104 marks

10. Describe Rajvir's journey to Assam and what he saw in the tea gardens.

Show Answer

Ans:

Rajvir travelled by train to Dhekiabari with his friend Pranjol, whose father managed a tea estate.
As the train neared the tea country, Rajvir looked out at endless stretches of orderly tea bushes and doll-like women plucking leaves.
Pranjol, who had grown up on a plantation, slept through it, but Rajvir could not sleep with excitement.
He impressed Pranjol with his knowledge: the second flush or sprouting period gives the best tea, and he told the legend of the Chinese emperor who discovered tea when leaves fell into his boiling water.

LONGL115 marks

11. Describe the Coorg landscape and its attractions.

Show Answer

Ans:

Coorg, or Kodagu, is the smallest district of Karnataka, lying midway between Mysore and the coastal town of Mangalore. It is a rolling hillside with a permanent fragrance of fresh coffee, coffee estates and spice plantations, colonial bungalows tucked under treetops, and dense forests.
The best season is from September to March, when the weather is perfect, the air breathes of invigorating coffee, and the rain showers keep the hills fresh.
Wildlife: the Kaveri river, which takes its birth from the hills and forests of Coorg, is home to Mahaseer, a large freshwater fish; kingfishers, squirrels and langurs can be seen, and wild elephants bathe in the river.
Attractions: the climb to the Brahmagiri hills gives a panoramic view of the entire misty landscape of Coorg; a walk into the sixty-acre Buddhist monastery at Bylakutte, hidden among the coffee plantations, is a surprise; and there are rappelling, river rafting, canoeing and rock climbing for the adventurous.

LONGL125 marks

12. Why is “Glimpses of India” a suitable title? Explain with reference to the three sections.

Show Answer

Ans:

Goa: the pader and the Goan bread show how Portuguese history lives on in everyday life.
Coorg: the hills, coffee estates and martial Kodavus show a landscape and a people of great character.
Assam: tea gardens and the legend of tea link India to China and to the whole world.
Each section is only a short glimpse, not a full picture, of one region.
Together they show how varied India is in food, landscape, people and history.
