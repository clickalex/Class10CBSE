SHORTS13 marks

1. Describe the format required for note making.

Show Answer

Ans:

Write a title in the centre at the top. Use decimal numbering for the headings — 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.2 — with consistent indentation to show the levels. Each heading must be a short phrase, not a sentence, and must contain only what is in the passage. Standard abbreviations may be used, and a key to abbreviations must be given at the end.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What are the rules for writing the summary that follows the notes?

Show Answer

Ans:

The summary must be written in your own words, in complete sentences, and within the word limit, usually about 80 words. It must cover the main idea of the passage and its two or three main supporting points, and it must not include anything new or any personal opinion. Exceeding the word limit or copying sentences from the passage results in a deduction.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why is the key to abbreviations important?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because it is assessed as part of the format, and because abbreviations are meaningless without it. The examiner needs to be able to see that the abbreviations used in the headings are standard and have been explained. Omitting the key costs a mark even if the notes themselves are correct.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “Note Making and Summarising” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The format. Write a title in the centre at the top. Use a numbering system: main headings 1, 1.1, 1.1.1, 1.2 and so on, indented to show the level. Main headings carry the key idea; sub-headings carry the details. Every heading is a phrase, not a sentence. End with a conclusion line if the passage has one. Do not write in sentences, do not add your own ideas, and do not exceed the recommended number of headings.
Marks lens: About 5 marks, usually as a note-making task based on a passage, followed by a short summary.
Method / memory trick: Headings are phrases, not sentences. That is the single most common loss of format marks.
Do not: Writing notes in full sentences.
