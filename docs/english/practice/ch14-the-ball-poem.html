Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why does the poet say he would not intrude on the boy?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the boy is learning something important for the first time — the epistemology of loss, or the knowledge of what it is to lose something. Intruding on him, or offering a new ball, would spoil the lesson. The poet wants the boy to feel the loss fully and to learn to stand up on his own.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What does the poet mean by 'money is external'?

Show Answer

Ans:

He means that money can buy another ball, but it cannot buy back what has been lost or take away the feeling of loss. The material value of the ball is small — a dime — so what matters is not the object but the experience of losing it, which is something outside the reach of money.

LONGL35 marks

3. What is the boy learning, according to the poet? Explain the significance of the first loss.

Show Answer

Ans:

The boy is learning the epistemology of loss — the knowledge of what loss means and how to live with it. He stands rigid and trembling, staring down at the harbour where his ball went, and in that moment he understands that some things, once gone, cannot be brought back.
The poet says a dime, another ball, is worthless, because a new ball would not restore the one that is lost. Money is external, so it cannot help him here.
The boy learns that balls will be lost always — loss is a permanent feature of life, and everyone must learn to accept it. He learns to stand up, which means to accept the loss and go on.
The significance of the first loss is that it is the beginning of maturity. Until this moment the boy lived in a world where everything could be replaced; now he is part of a world of possessions in which things can be taken away. That realisation is painful, but it is also the first step towards becoming an adult who can bear responsibility and disappointment.

LONGL45 marks

4. Discuss “The Ball Poem” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

The poem. The boy stands rigid, trembling, staring down at the harbour where his ball went. The poet does not intrude on him; he says he would not intrude on the boy, and that a dime, another ball, is worthless. The boy is learning the epistemology of loss — the knowledge of what it is to lose something. The poet says that money is external, and that the boy will learn to stand up, knowing that people will take balls.
Marks lens: Reference-to-context extracts; a 5-mark answer on the boy's lesson and on the meaning of 'the epistemology of loss'.
Method / memory trick: 'Epistemology of loss' = the knowledge of how to lose things. Explain it in simple words and you get the mark.
Do not: Saying the poet buys the boy a new ball. He does not, and explains why.
