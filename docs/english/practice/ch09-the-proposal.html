MCQs

'The Proposal' is a one-act play written by: (a) Anton Chekhov (b) Betty Renshaw (c) Gavin Maxwell (d) Liam O'Flaherty

(a) Anton Chekhov, the Russian dramatist.

The second quarrel in the play is about: (a) the Oxen Meadows (b) two hunting dogs (c) money (d) Natalya's age

(b) two hunting dogs — Lomov's Squeezer and Chubukov's Guess.

Lomov suffers from: (a) asthma (b) palpitations and a twitching eyebrow (c) a limp (d) poor eyesight

(b) — he is a hypochondriac who complains of palpitations, a twitching eyebrow, sleeplessness and numbness in his leg.

Written questions

3 marks Why does Chubukov think Lomov has come to him?

Because Lomov had come to him the previous year to borrow money, and Chubukov expects the same again. He even complains that Lomov is always coming to him for a loan, and when he hears that Lomov has a request, he assumes it is for money. Only when Lomov asks for Natalya's hand does Chubukov understand.

3 marks What is the Oxen Meadows quarrel about?

It is about who owns the Oxen Meadows, which lie between Lomov's Burnt Marsh and Chubukov's birchwood. Natalya insists the meadows are hers; Lomov says they are his, arguing that his aunt's grandmother gave the peasants of Chubukov's grandfather the free use of the meadows in perpetuity in return for their making bricks for her. The peasants had used them for forty years and regarded them as their own, but neither side will give way, and the argument turns into an exchange of insults.

5 marks How does Chekhov use the two quarrels to create comedy and to criticise the characters?

The first quarrel, about the Oxen Meadows, begins before Lomov has even made his proposal. Natalya claims the meadows are hers, Lomov insists they are his, and both become abusive, calling each other a land grabber and an intriguess. Chubukov joins in, calls Lomov an old rat and a Jesuit, and orders him out of the house. The absurdity is that a marriage proposal has become a land dispute, and that Chubukov does not know what the proposal was until after the quarrel.
The second quarrel, about the dogs, follows immediately. Lomov praises his dog Squeezer; Chubukov insists his own Guess is better, and they argue over whether Squeezer's muzzle is short, whether Guess is purebred, and whether Squeezer's legs are crooked. The two men become hysterical and Lomov collapses.
The criticism: Chekhov shows that for these landowners, property and possessions matter far more than human relationships. Neither Lomov nor Natalya says a word about love or about each other's feelings, and the marriage is arranged in the middle of an argument. The comedy exposes a class that treats marriage as a transaction.

3 marks Describe Lomov's character.

Lomov is a thirty-five-year-old landowner who is timid, nervous and easily flustered. He is a hypochondriac: he complains of palpitations, a twitching right eyebrow, sleeplessness and numbness in his leg. He is also stubborn and quick to quarrel, which is why a marriage proposal turns into two arguments. Yet he is sincere in his wish to settle down, and he finally gets what he came for.

3 marks How does the play end? Comment on the ending.

Lomov collapses after the argument about the dogs, and Chubukov, without waiting for anything to be settled, blesses the couple and cries that Natalya is willing, calling for champagne. Natalya does not object, and the proposal is agreed. The ending is comic because the marriage is decided in the middle of a quarrel that neither side has stopped fighting — nobody has spoken a word about love, and the audience is left to see how absurd the whole arrangement is.
