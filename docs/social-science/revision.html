CBSE • CLASS 10 • SOCIAL SCIENCE

Social Science — Quick revision

One screen per chapter: formulas and the points that actually get asked. Expand what you need, skip what you know.

Ch 1 The Rise of Nationalism in Europe
  • 1789 French Revolution; 1804 Napoleonic Civil Code; 1832 Treaty of Constantinople (Greek independence); 1834 Zollverein.
  • 1848 Frankfurt Parliament, Church of St Paul; crown rejected by Friedrich Wilhelm IV.
  • Germany 1871 (Bismarck, three wars in seven years); Italy 1861 (Mazzini–Cavour–Garibaldi); Britain by Acts of Union 1707 and 1801.
  • Marianne (France) and Germania (oak leaves, Germany); Balkan conflict led to the First World War.
Ch 2 Nationalism in India
  • 1915 Gandhi returns; 1917 Champaran/Kheda; 1918 Ahmedabad; 1919 Rowlatt and Jallianwala Bagh (13 April).
  • 1920 Nagpur session, Non-Cooperation launched; 1922 withdrawn after Chauri Chaura.
  • 1929 Lahore session, Purna Swaraj; 1930 Salt March (12 March, Sabarmati–Dandi, 240 miles).
  • 1931 Gandhi-Irwin Pact, Second Round Table Conference; Poona Pact 1932.
  • Bharat Mata by Abhinindranath Tagore; Vande Mataram by Bankim Chandra; Swaraj flag 1921.
Ch 3 The Making of a Global World
  • Silk Routes carried silk, cotton textiles and spices west, silver east — plus religions and disease.
  • Columbian exchange: potato, maize, tomato, chilli, groundnut, soya, sweet potato from the Americas.
  • Silver from Peru and Mexico financed Europe's trade with Asia.
  • Smallpox, not guns, made the Spanish conquest possible.
  • Only subtopics 1 to 1.3 are examined.
Ch 4 Print Culture and the Modern World
  • Diamond Sutra AD 868 (oldest printed book); printing reached Japan AD 768–770; Korea developed metal type.
  • Gutenberg: adapted the olive press, cast metal type, first printed the Bible; 20 million copies printed in Europe 1450–1550.
  • Luther's 95 Theses (1517); the Index of Prohibited Books from the mid-16th century.
  • India: press with the Portuguese at Goa; first Tamil book 1579; Bengal Gazette 1780 (Hicky).
  • Vernacular Press Act 1878; Tilak's Kesari and Maratha; Sambad Kaumudi 1821.
Ch 5 Resources and Development
  • A resource must be technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable.
  • Classification: origin, exhaustibility, ownership, status of development.
  • Black soil → cotton → Deccan trap; laterite → tea and coffee → leaching in hilly areas.
  • Land degradation: mining (Jharkhand, MP), overgrazing (Rajasthan, Gujarat), over-irrigation (Punjab, Haryana).
  • Agenda 21, Rio 1992; Brundtland Report, Our Common Future, 1987.
Ch 6 Forest and Wildlife Resources
  • India holds about 8% of all recorded species: 47,000 plants, 81,000 animals, 15,000 flowering plants.
  • Categories: normal, endangered, vulnerable, rare, extinct, endemic — one example each.
  • Causes: agriculture, development projects, mining, grazing, fires, poaching, habitat destruction.
  • Protection: Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Project Tiger 1973, national parks and sanctuaries.
  • Forest types: reserved (more than half), protected (about a third), unclassed.
  • Communities: Chipko, Beej Bachao Andolan, JFM 1988 (first in Odisha), sacred groves.
Ch 7 Water Resources
  • Only 2.5% of the earth's water is fresh; India ranks 133rd per person per annum (2010).
  • Multi-purpose projects: irrigation, power, flood control, navigation, fish breeding, recreation.
  • Drawbacks: displacement, sedimentation, waterlogging, inter-state disputes, earthquakes.
  • Rainwater harvesting: tankas (Rajasthan), guls and kuls (Himalayas), inundation canals (WB), bamboo drip (Meghalaya), compulsory rooftop (Tamil Nadu).
Ch 8 Agriculture
  • Kharif: rice, maize, cotton, jute. Rabi: wheat, gram, barley, mustard. Zaid: watermelon, fodder.
  • Rice 100 cm rain; wheat 50–75 cm; jute 150–250 cm; cotton 50–100 cm and 210 frost-free days.
  • Plantation: tea (Assam), coffee (Karnataka), rubber (Kerala).
  • Green Revolution — HYV seeds in Punjab, Haryana, western UP; White Revolution — Operation Flood.
  • Buffer stock via the Food Corporation of India; Kissan Credit Card; minimum support price.
Ch 9 Minerals and Energy Resources
  • Hematite (70% iron) is the finest; magnetite (60–70%) has magnetic properties.
  • Coal ranks: anthracite, bituminous, lignite, peat. Damodar valley = Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro.
  • Petroleum: Bombay High 63%, Gujarat 18%, Assam 16%.
  • Largest wind cluster: Tamil Nadu (Nagercoil–Madurai). Bauxite: Odisha. Mica: Koderma.
  • Minerals are exhaustible and non-renewable — use them in a planned, sustainable way and recycle.
Ch 10 Manufacturing Industries
  • Manufacturing adds value; industry location depends on raw material, labour, power, market and transport.
  • Classification: source, role, capital, ownership (public, private, joint, cooperative), bulk.
  • Cotton textiles: Maharashtra, Gujarat (spinning in Tamil Nadu). Jute: West Bengal on the Hooghly. Sugar: shifting to Maharashtra.
  • Iron and steel: basic, heavy, Chotanagpur; TISCO 1907 Jamshedpur; largest producer of sponge iron.
  • Bengaluru = electronic capital; four types of industrial pollution; effluent treated in three stages.
Ch 11 Lifelines of National Economy
  • Road network about 54 lakh km; Golden Quadrilateral; PMGSY for rural roads; BRO for border roads.
  • Railways: about 7,000 stations; distribution depends on physiography, economy and administration; Konkan Railway along the west coast.
  • Pipelines: high initial cost, minimal running cost; HVJ Hazira–Vijaypur–Jagdishpur.
  • Waterways: cheapest, 14,500 km, NW-1 Ganga (Allahabad–Haldia), NW-2 Brahmaputra.
  • Airways nationalised 1953; postal network the largest in the world.
Ch 12 Power Sharing
  • Belgium: 59% Flemish/Dutch, 40% Walloon/French, 1% German; Brussels 80% French; four amendments 1932–68.
  • Sri Lanka: 74% Sinhala, 18% Tamil; Sinhala the only official language 1956; civil war to 2009.
  • Prudential reasons = stability; moral reasons = the spirit of democracy.
  • Four forms: horizontal, vertical, social groups, political parties and movements.
Ch 13 Federalism
  • Two routes: coming together (USA, Switzerland, Australia); holding together (India, Spain, Belgium).
  • Union List ~100 subjects, State List ~61, Concurrent List ~52; central law prevails in a conflict.
  • Residuary subjects → Union. UTs are run directly by the centre.
  • 1992 amendment: regular elections, SC/ST/OBC reservation, one-third for women, State Election Commission, sharing of powers and revenue.
  • Gram panchayat → panchayat samiti → zilla parishad; municipality headed by a chairperson, corporation by a mayor.
Ch 14 Gender, Religion and Caste
  • The sexual division of labour is social, not biological, and it keeps women in the private sphere.
  • Equal Remuneration Act 1976; women's literacy about 65% against men's about 82% (Census 2011).
  • One-third of local body seats reserved for women; the 106th Amendment (2023) reserves one-third in the Lok Sabha and assemblies.
  • Communalism: the same religion means common interests, different religions means opposed interests — from everyday prejudice to separate political units.
  • Secular state: no official religion, freedom of religion, no discrimination, and the right to intervene for equality.
  • Caste in politics (caste shapes elections) and politics in caste (politics sharpens caste identity). No constituency and no caste votes as one block.
Ch 15 Political Parties
  • A party has three parts: leaders, active members, followers.
  • Functions: contest elections, offer policies, make laws, form government, oppose, shape opinion, provide access.
  • National party: 6% votes in four states + 4 Lok Sabha seats. State party: 6% + 2 Assembly seats.
  • Challenges: internal democracy, dynasty, money and muscle power, no real choice.
  • Reforms: anti-defection law, affidavit, organisational elections, income tax returns, audited accounts.
Ch 16 Outcomes of Democracy
  • Test: accountable, responsive, legitimate.
  • Growth: dictatorships slightly faster, difference small; growth depends on population, global situation and policy.
  • Democracy accommodates diversity by turning competition into agreed procedures.
  • Dignity and freedom of the citizen is the most fundamental outcome.
  • Public criticism of democracy is evidence of its strength.
Ch 17 Development

Formulas

  • Per capita income = National income ÷ Total population
  • BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in metres)²
  • IMR = (infant deaths under 1 year ÷ live births) × 1,000
  • Different people, different developmental goals; income is important but not sufficient.
  • Per capita income = national income ÷ population; rich countries US$ 13,845+, low income US$ 1,045 or less (2019).
  • India: lower-middle-income, US$ 2,110 (2019).
  • Kerala has lower IMR than Haryana despite lower income, because of public health and education facilities.
  • HDI (UNDP) = health + education + income, 0 to 1.
Ch 18 Sectors of the Indian Economy

Formulas

  • GDP = value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year
  • Primary + Secondary + Tertiary = total production; count a good only once, at its final stage
  • Organised sector: registered, regular wages, social security; unorganised: none of these guaranteed
  • Sectoral share today: tertiary largest by output, primary still largest by employment
  • Primary = extract, secondary = manufacture, tertiary = services.
  • Tertiary produces the largest GDP; primary still employs the most people.
  • Disguised unemployment: extra workers, output unchanged; remedy = irrigation, credit, rural construction, MGNREGA.
  • Organised = registration, job security, PF, paid leave, labour laws. Unorganised = 90% of workers, no security.
  • GDP counts final goods only; public sector for welfare, private for profit.
Ch 19 Money and Credit

Formulas

  • Simple interest = (P × R × T) ÷ 100 — the extra money a borrower pays over the principal
  • Total repayment = Principal + Interest; the interest rate is what makes credit cheap or costly
  • Formal credit: banks and cooperatives, supervised by the RBI; informal: moneylenders, traders, landlords
  • Self-help group: about 15–20 members pool savings and lend to one another at their own rate
  • Money is a medium of exchange because it removes the need for a double coincidence of wants.
  • Banks keep about 15% of deposits as cash and lend the rest; the interest difference is their income.
  • Formal credit = banks and cooperatives, RBI supervised, collateral needed. Informal = moneylenders, high rates, 70% of rural poor.
  • SHG: 15–20 women, save ₹25–100, internal lending, then a bank loan in the group's name.
  • Debt trap = borrowing to repay borrowing; Grameen Bank, Bangladesh, Muhammad Yunus.
Ch 20 Globalisation and the Indian Economy

Formulas

  • Globalisation = integration of markets through trade + foreign investment + technology
  • Foreign direct investment: buying an asset and controlling it; portfolio investment buys shares only
  • Trade barrier: a tax (tariff) or a quota that makes imports costlier or scarcer
  • 1991 reforms: devaluation, removal of licensing, lower tariffs — the year the Indian market opened
  • MNC = production controlled in two or more countries besides the home country.
  • MNCs spread production by building new plants, buying local companies, or placing branded orders with small producers.
  • Liberalisation 1991; WTO 1995; SEZs offer a five-year tax holiday.
  • Gains: choice, lower prices, IT and services growth, remittances. Losses: small units shut, contract labour, job insecurity.
  • Fair globalisation = support small producers, implement labour laws, negotiate fairer WTO rules.
