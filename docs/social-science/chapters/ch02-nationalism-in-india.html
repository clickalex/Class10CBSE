🎯 Marks lens

5 marks, often more. Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience are compared every year; the Sense of Collective Belonging is the standard 5-mark answer.
Learn the sequence: 1915 return, 1919 Rowlatt and Jallianwala Bagh, 1920 Nagpur session and Non-Cooperation launched, 1922 Chauri Chaura withdrawal, 1930 Salt March and Civil Disobedience, 1931 Gandhi-Irwin Pact and the Second Round Table Conference, 1934 movement called off.

📖 Deep concepts

1. The First World War, Khilafat and Non-Cooperation. The war created a new economic and political situation: defence expenditure rose, customs duties were raised and income tax introduced, prices doubled between 1913 and 1918, villages were forced to supply soldiers, and crop failure and influenza killed 12–13 million. Gandhiji returned to India in January 1915 from South Africa, where he had developed satyagraha — the power of truth and non-violent resistance. He led early satyagrahas at Champaran (1917) for indigo planters, Kheda (1917) for peasants seeking revenue remission, and Ahmedabad (1918) for cotton mill workers. The Rowlatt Act (1919) allowed detention without trial; on 13 April 1919 General Dyer fired on the crowd at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. The Khilafat Committee was formed in Bombay in 1919 by the Ali brothers, Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali; the Non-Cooperation-Khilafat Movement began in January 1921, formalised at the Nagpur session of the Congress in December 1920.
2. Differing strands within the movement. In the towns, middle-class students left schools and colleges, lawyers gave up practice, foreign cloth was burnt and liquor shops picketed, and foreign cloth imports halved between 1921 and 1922 — but khadi was expensive and there were no Indian institutions to absorb those who left, so many returned to government schools. In the countryside, Awadh peasants led by Baba Ramchandra fought talukdars and landlords over high rents and begar; the Oudh Kisan Sabha was set up in October 1920 by J. L. Nehru and Baba Ramchandra. Tribal peasants in the Gudem Hills of Andhra Pradesh, led by Alluri Sitaram Raju, revolted against the forest laws; Raju claimed special powers, was admired as an incarnation of God, and was executed in 1924. Plantation workers in Assam, restricted by the Inland Emigration Act of 1859, understood swaraj as freedom to move and return to their villages.
3. Towards Civil Disobedience. By 1929 the demand shifted from dominion status to Purna Swaraj; the Lahore session of the Congress in December 1929, under Jawaharlal Nehru, formalised it and fixed 26 January 1930 as Independence Day. The Simon Commission ('Go back Simon') had no Indian member. Gandhiji's letter to Viceroy Irwin listed eleven demands, the most stirring being the abolition of the salt tax. On 12 March 1930 he began the Salt March from Sabarmati to Dandi with 78 volunteers, covering 240 miles, and on 6 April boiled sea water to make salt, breaking the law. This started the Civil Disobedience Movement: foreign cloth was boycotted, liquor shops picketed, peasants refused to pay revenue and chaukidari taxes, and officials resigned. Unlike Non-Cooperation, people were asked not only to refuse cooperation but also to break colonial laws. Women participated in large numbers. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931) released political prisoners and Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference in London, which failed; the movement was revived but lost momentum and was called off by 1934.
4. Limits of Civil Disobedience. The untouchables were called harijan (children of God) by Gandhi, who cleaned toilets to dignify their labour and organised satyagrahas for their entry into temples and access to wells and schools — but many of them, led by Dr B. R. Ambedkar, demanded separate electorates, and at the Second Round Table Conference the dispute was settled by the Poona Pact of September 1932, which gave the Depressed Classes reserved seats in provincial and central councils but voted in by the general electorate. Industrialists such as Purshottamdas Thakurdas and G. D. Birla formed FICCI in 1927 and wanted protection against imports, but withdrew support after the failure of the Round Table Conference.
5. The sense of collective belonging. Nationalism spreads when people begin to believe they are part of the same nation — through history and fiction, folklore and songs, popular prints and images. Bharat Mata was first created by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and painted by Abhinindranath Tagore as an ascetic figure, calm, composed and divine; devotion to this mother figure came to be seen as evidence of one's nationalism. Vande Mataram, written by Bankim Chandra in the 1870s and included in the novel Anandamath, became the hymn of the movement. In Bengal, Natesan Sastri and others collected folk songs; in Punjab, Swadeshi habit and kishan tales circulated. Flags were another vehicle: the Swadeshi flag of 1905 (tricolour with eight lotuses and a crescent moon) and the Swaraj flag designed by Gandhi in 1921 (red, green and white with a spinning wheel, the symbol of self-help). Reinterpretation of history — writing of India's achievements in mathematics, science and culture — gave Indians pride in their past. But this cultural identity also excluded those who did not fit the image, and many communities remained aloof from Congress activities.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Gandhi's satyagraha sequence: C-K-A — Champaran 1917, Kheda 1917, Ahmedabad 1918.
  • Non-Cooperation vs Civil Disobedience in one line: in Non-Cooperation people refused to cooperate; in Civil Disobedience they also broke colonial laws (the salt tax).
  • Two flags: Swadeshi flag 1905 (lotuses + crescent); Swaraj flag 1921 (red-green-white + spinning wheel).

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Giving 1920 as the year the Salt March began. The movement was launched at Nagpur in December 1920; the Salt March was 12 March 1930.
  • Saying the Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn because of the Salt tax. It was withdrawn after the Chauri Chaura incident (February 1922), when a violent mob burnt a police station.
  • Writing that Ambedkar accepted Gandhi's view completely. He demanded separate electorates and only accepted reserved seats under the Poona Pact.
  • Naming Abhinindranath Tagore as the writer of Vande Mataram. Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay wrote it; Abhinindranath Tagore painted Bharat Mata.

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why was the Salt March an effective symbol of resistance against colonialism?

Show Answer

Ans:

Salt was consumed by rich and poor alike and was one of the most essential items of food, yet the tax on it and the government monopoly over its production revealed the most oppressive face of British rule. Choosing it united the whole country behind a single demand, and breaking the salt law by boiling sea water at Dandi was simple, visible and easy for every villager to repeat, so it quickly spread into a nationwide movement.

LONGL25 marks

2. How did the Non-Cooperation Movement differ from the Civil Disobedience Movement?

Show Answer

Ans:

Non-Cooperation (1920–22): people were asked to refuse all cooperation with the British government — students left schools, lawyers gave up practice, foreign cloth was boycotted and burnt. It was launched with the Khilafat issue and withdrawn by Gandhi after the Chauri Chaura violence.
Civil Disobedience (1930–34): people were asked not merely to refuse cooperation but to break colonial laws — the salt law, forest laws, refusal to pay revenue and chaukidari taxes, boycott of foreign cloth and picketing of liquor shops.
Participation: Civil Disobedience saw much larger participation by women, who left their homes to join protest marches and manufacture salt.
Outcome: Non-Cooperation was withdrawn in 1922; Civil Disobedience was suspended by the Gandhi-Irwin Pact in 1931, revived after the failed Round Table Conference, and finally lost momentum by 1934.

LONGL35 marks

3. Explain the idea of the Sense of Collective Belonging with reference to nationalism in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Nationalism develops when people begin to believe that they are part of the same nation, and this feeling is created through history and fiction, folklore and songs, and popular prints.
Images: Bharat Mata, first created by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and painted by Abhinindranath Tagore as a calm, divine ascetic figure, came to symbolise the nation; devotion to her became a sign of nationalism.
Songs: Vande Mataram, written by Bankim Chandra in the 1870s and set in Anandamath, was widely sung during the Swadeshi movement.
Folklore: in Bengal, Natesan Sastri and others collected folk tales and songs, arguing that these were the truest manifestation of national identity.
Flags: the Swadeshi flag of 1905 and the Swaraj flag of 1921, with the spinning wheel, became symbols of defiance; carrying them was itself an act of rebellion.
History: Indians began writing of India's ancient achievements in mathematics, science, law and philosophy, to counter the colonial view of Indians as backward and to instil pride in the past.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Describe the Jallianwala Bagh incident.

Show Answer

Ans:

On 13 April 1919, a large crowd gathered in the enclosed ground of Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar — some to protest against the repressive Rowlatt Act, others to attend the annual Baisakhi fair. Unaware of the martial law imposed in the city, they were trapped when General Dyer entered, blocked the exit points and opened fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds. His object, as he later declared, was to produce a moral effect and create terror in the minds of satyagrahis. News of the massacre spread, and crowds took to the streets in many north Indian towns.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why did Gandhiji decide to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the movement was turning violent. At Chauri Chaura in Gorakhpur, a peaceful demonstration turned into a violent clash and the crowd burnt a police station with policemen inside. Gandhi felt that satyagrahis needed proper training before they could take part in a mass struggle, and that violence would give the British an excuse to crush the movement, so he called it off.
