Chapter 15 · Political Parties

🎯 Marks lens

3–5 marks. The functions of political parties and the challenges they face are asked almost every year.
Recognised national parties and their symbols are a quick, certain 3 marks if learned exactly.

📖 Deep concepts

1. What is a political party? A group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. A party agrees on some policies and programmes for the society with a view to promoting the collective good, decides what policies to pursue once in power, and reflects fundamental political divisions in a society by standing for a particular ideology. A party has three parts: the leaders, the active members and the followers.
2. Why parties are necessary. In a large society representative government needs an agency that gathers a wide range of views and presents them to the government; parties perform this function. Without them, every candidate would be independent, no one could promise any major policy change, and no one would be responsible for how the country is run.
3. Functions of political parties. (a) Contest elections — candidates are selected either by the members and supporters or by the top leaders; in India, top party leaders usually select the candidates. (b) Put forward different policies and programmes — voters get to choose between them. (c) Play a decisive role in making laws — since legislatures have many members, laws are debated and passed along party lines, so the party leadership effectively decides. (d) Form and run the government — the party or coalition that wins forms the government, and the top party leaders become ministers. (e) Play the role of opposition — parties that lose elections voice different views and criticise the government for its failures or wrong policies; they also mobilise opposition to the government. (f) Shape public opinion — parties raise and highlight issues through their members, workers and pressure groups. (g) Provide access to government machinery and welfare schemes — ordinary citizens approach local party leaders for help.
4. National parties of India. A party is recognised as a national party if it secures at least 6% of the total votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in four states and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha. The recognised national parties, with their symbols:

5. State parties. A party that is recognised as a state party if it secures at least 6% of the total votes in the Legislative Assembly of a state and wins at least two seats. Examples include the Samajwadi Party, Rashtriya Janata Dal, Biju Janata Dal, YSR Congress, Shiromani Akali Dal, DMK and AIADMK.
6. Challenges to political parties. (a) Lack of internal democracy — power is concentrated in a few top leaders, there are no organisational elections, no membership registers, and ordinary members do not get enough information. (b) Dynastic succession — top positions are controlled by members of one family, which is unfair to other members and undemocratic. (c) Money and muscle power — parties grow more concerned with winning elections than with principles, so they nominate candidates who can raise money, and sometimes criminals with cases against them. (d) Lack of meaningful choice — parties do not offer significantly different policies or ideologies, and many leaders shift between parties, so voters have little real alternative.
7. Reforms. The Constitution was amended to prevent defection — an elected representative cannot change parties to become a minister or for cash reward; every candidate must file an affidavit giving details of property and pending criminal cases; parties must hold organisational elections and file income tax returns; the Election Commission has ordered parties to give tickets on the basis of merit, to keep accounts and to submit audited statements; and the anti-defection law and the Right to Information have increased transparency. Suggestions also include a law to regulate internal party affairs and reservation for women in party posts.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • National party test: 6% of votes in four states + 4 Lok Sabha seats. State party: 6% + 2 Assembly seats. 'Four for national, two for state.'
  • Seven functions in order: contest, policies, laws, government, opposition, opinion, welfare.
  • Four challenges: internal democracy, dynasty, money and muscle, no real choice — each pairs with a reform.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Giving the BSP symbol as the elephant with a howdah or naming a party that is only a state party. Stick to the EC's recognised national party list.
  • Saying the anti-defection law stops all defection. It bars changing parties to become a minister or for cash reward; splits and mergers are treated differently.
  • Forgetting that candidates must file an affidavit — that is a marked point in the reforms answer.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is a political party and why are parties necessary in a democracy?

Show Answer

Ans:

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in government. Parties are necessary because representatives must be elected, because they offer voters different policies to choose from, because they form and run governments, shape public opinion and give people access to government schemes and welfare measures.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is the criterion for a party to be recognised as a national party?

Show Answer

Ans:

A party is recognised as a national party by the Election Commission if it secures at least six per cent of the total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four states, and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha. State parties need at least six per cent of the votes in the state Assembly elections and at least two seats.

SHORTS33 marks

3. How do political parties shape public opinion?

Show Answer

Ans:

Parties raise and highlight issues through their lakhs of members and workers spread over the country, and through the pressure groups they support. They also use newspapers, meetings and social media to explain their stand. When a party is not able to solve a problem, it highlights the issue and mobilises opposition, which forces the government to respond.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Distinguish between a two-party system and a multi-party system.

Show Answer

Ans:

In a two-party system, such as in the USA or the UK, only two parties are strong enough to win power, and one of them usually wins a majority on its own.
In a multi-party system, such as in India, several parties compete and none usually gets a majority, so they must form coalitions to govern.
A multi-party system represents the diversity of a large and varied society better; a two-party system gives more stable but less representative government.

LONGL104 marks

10. Suggest any four reforms that would strengthen political parties in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

A law to regulate the internal affairs of parties: they should maintain a register of members, hold regular organisational elections and follow their own constitutions.
State funding of elections to reduce the dependence of parties on big donors and on black money, along with the filing of audited accounts and income-tax returns.
Disclosure of the assets and criminal records of candidates, as required by the Supreme Court and the Election Commission, so that voters can make an informed choice.
Reservation for women in the party organisation as well as in the allotment of tickets, and an end to dynastic succession through inner-party democracy.

LONGL115 marks

11. Describe the main functions of political parties in a democracy.

Show Answer

Ans:

Contesting elections: parties select candidates and campaign for them; in India, top leaders usually choose the candidates.
Putting forward policies and programmes: parties present different sets of policies, and voters choose between them.
Making laws: legislatures have many members, so laws are debated and passed along party lines, and the party leadership effectively decides.
Forming and running the government: the winning party or coalition forms the government, and its top leaders become ministers and run the administration.
Playing the opposition: losing parties criticise the government for its failures and wrong policies, mobilise opposition and voice different views.
Shaping public opinion: parties raise and highlight issues through lakhs of members and workers and through pressure groups.
Providing access to government machinery and welfare schemes: people approach local party leaders to get work done or to access government programmes.

LONGL125 marks

12. What are the main challenges faced by political parties in India? Suggest any three reforms.

Show Answer

Ans:

Challenges: (1) Lack of internal democracy — power is concentrated in the hands of a few top leaders, there are no regular organisational elections or membership registers, and ordinary members lack information; (2) Dynastic succession — top posts are controlled by family members of the leaders, which is unfair and undemocratic; (3) Money and muscle power — parties nominate candidates who can raise money and sometimes candidates with criminal cases, because winning matters more than principles; (4) Lack of meaningful choice — parties offer very similar policies and ideologies, and leaders shift freely between parties.
Reforms: the constitution was amended to prevent defection, so elected representatives cannot change parties for ministerial posts or cash; every candidate must file an affidavit declaring property and pending criminal cases, which the Supreme Court made mandatory; parties must hold organisational elections and file income tax returns; and the Election Commission has ordered parties to keep audited accounts and to distribute tickets on merit.
