🎯 Marks lens

3–4 marks. Averages (per capita income, BMI, IMR, literacy) and the limitations of income comparisons are the set questions.
The Human Development Report comparisons between Kerala, Haryana, Bihar and Punjab appear regularly as table-based questions.

📖 Deep concepts

1. What people want from development. Different people have different developmental goals. A landless rural labourer wants more days of work and better wages; a prosperous farmer of Punjab wants cheap labour and higher prices for crops; a rural woman may want the same freedom and opportunity as her brother. What may be development for one may be destructive for another — for example, a dam that gives electricity to industries may submerge the land of a tribal community. People also want things that money cannot buy, such as equal treatment, freedom, security and respect.
2. Income and other goals. In most cases, income is the most important component of development, because people need income to buy goods and services. But income alone cannot determine the quality of life. People look for collective goods too — a non-polluting environment, protection from infectious diseases, good schools and hospitals. These are available only if the community takes collective action, so development is a mix of individual and common goals.
3. National development. It means the improvement in the standard of living and the welfare of the people as a whole. It is decided by the citizens' goals taken together, so it must be fair and just. Different persons can have different notions of what is fair, and development planning has to reconcile them.
4. Comparing states — averages. To compare countries or states we use averages or per capita figures, because total income cannot be used when populations differ. Per capita income = national income ÷ population. According to the World Development Report published by the World Bank, countries with per capita income of US$ 13,845 or more in 2019 are called rich countries, and those with per capita income of US$ 1,045 or less are called low-income countries. India comes in the category of lower-middle-income countries, with a per capita income of US$ 2,110 in 2019.
5. Averages hide disparities. Averages are useful for comparison but they hide differences. If the average income of a country is high, it may still be that a few people are very rich while many are extremely poor. Two states can have the same per capita income but very different income distribution.

6. Kerala's example. Kerala, with lower per capita income than Haryana, has a lower infant mortality rate than Haryana. The reason is the adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities, so that almost all children are born safe, are immunised, and are given nutrition and schooling. Health is not only the absence of disease but overall well-being, and it is not something individuals can buy — it depends on public facilities.
7. Human Development Report. Published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the Human Development Report compares countries on the basis of educational levels of the people, their health status and per capita income. These three are combined into the Human Development Index (HDI), a number between 0 and 1; the higher it is, the more developed the country. India's HDI rank has been improving, and countries are ranked into very high, high, medium and low human development groups. India is in the medium human development group.
8. Sustainability of development. Development should not damage the environment or exhaust resources, because resources are limited. Groundwater is a good example: it is an over-exploited resource, and in many regions of India, such as the western part of Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, the water table is falling. Overuse of groundwater is not sustainable, since it takes a long time to recharge. The same applies to crude oil — reserves will last only a limited number of years at current rates of extraction. Development must therefore be sustainable, using resources in a way that leaves enough for future generations.

🧮 Formulas to memorise

  • Per capita income = National income ÷ Total population
  • BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in metres)²
  • IMR = (infant deaths under 1 year ÷ live births) × 1,000

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Averages are useful but hide disparities. Every 'limitation of per capita income' answer starts with this sentence.
  • Kerala beats Haryana on health with lower income — because of public facilities. Name health and education facilities to score.
  • HDI = health + education + income. Three components, and the index runs 0 to 1.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying a higher BMI is always better. Below 18.5 is undernourished, above 25 is overweight — the healthy range is between.
  • Confusing the World Development Report (World Bank, income-based) with the Human Development Report (UNDP, HDI-based).
  • Using total income to compare countries. Populations differ, so per capita income must be used.

❓ Exam Q&A

3 marks Why is per capita income used to compare countries instead of total income?

Because countries have different population sizes, so total income does not tell us what an average person earns. Dividing national income by the population gives per capita income, which shows the average income of a person and allows a fair comparison of the standard of living between countries.

3 marks Kerala has a lower per capita income than Haryana but a lower infant mortality rate. Explain.

Because Kerala has an adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities. Almost all children are born safe, are immunised and are given nutrition, and the literacy rate is high, so mothers are better able to care for their children. These facilities are public goods that individuals cannot buy, so a higher income alone does not reduce the infant mortality rate.

5 marks What is the Human Development Report and on what basis does it compare countries?

It is published every year by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). It compares countries on the basis of three things: the educational levels of the people, their health status, and their per capita income.
These three are combined into the Human Development Index (HDI), a figure between 0 and 1; the closer to 1, the higher the level of human development. Countries are grouped as having very high, high, medium or low human development.
The report is important because it recognises that income alone does not measure development — a country may be rich but have poor health and education, in which case its people are not truly developed. India has been improving its rank and is placed in the medium human development group.

3 marks Explain the term sustainability of development with reference to groundwater.

Sustainability of development means using resources in a way that meets present needs without exhausting them, so that future generations can meet their own needs. Groundwater is a renewable resource but it is being over-exploited in many parts of India, such as western Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, where the water table is falling. Since it takes a long time to recharge, continued overuse is not sustainable and will leave future generations without water.

3 marks Why do different people have different notions of development?

Because their life situations are different. A landless rural labourer wants more days of work and better wages, a prosperous farmer wants higher prices for his crops and cheap labour, an industrialist wants more electricity and better roads, and a tribal community wants its forest and land protected. What is development for one person may be destructive for another, so development goals are always contested.

✍️ Hands-on task

Calculate your own BMI and interpret it using the 18.5–25 range. Then find the per capita income, IMR and literacy rate of your state and of Kerala from the latest Human Development Report, and write three sentences explaining any difference — that is exactly a 3-mark board answer.
