Chapter 16 · Outcomes of Democracy

Chapter 16 of 21. The most conceptual chapter in Polity. Every answer is a claim plus a reason — practise writing claim-reason pairs.

🎯 Marks lens

3–5 marks. 'Democracy is better than dictatorship because…' and the outcomes on economic growth and dignity are the set questions.
This chapter is also where case-based/source-based questions are most likely — a passage or a statement followed by three questions.

📖 Deep concepts

1. How to assess democracy. People's expectations from democracy are enormous, which makes the assessment difficult. The standard test: a democratic government must be accountable, responsive and legitimate. Accountability means citizens have the right to examine how decisions are made, so democracy must follow norms and procedures and give people a right to information. Responsiveness means the government must attend to the needs and expectations of citizens. Legitimacy means the government is the people's own government, elected by them — which is why even where democracy performs poorly it is still preferred, because it cannot be dismissed as illegitimate.
2. Democracy versus dictatorship on efficiency. A democratic government may be slower, less efficient and not always very attentive to the will of the people, because decisions involve consultation and negotiation among many. But this deliberation reduces the chance of rash or irresponsible decisions, and it makes decisions more acceptable to the people. A dictatorship may take quicker decisions because it does not have to consult anyone, but those decisions are not necessarily better and are often not acceptable.
3. Economic growth and development. Studies of the rate of economic growth between 1950 and 2000 show that dictatorships had a slightly higher rate of growth than democracies overall, but the difference is small. Among the poorer countries, democracies have caught up with dictatorships in recent decades. Economic growth depends on many factors — the country's population size, the global situation, cooperation from other countries and the economic priorities adopted by the ruling party — so democracy cannot be blamed for slower growth. Inequality is a real problem in democracies: a small number of ultra-rich enjoy a disproportionate share of wealth, and those at the bottom find it very difficult to meet basic needs. But democracies at least make it possible to raise the issue, and governments can and do act on it, whereas a dictatorship hides the gap.
4. Reduction of inequality and poverty. Democracy has not been able to eliminate poverty, and in some countries poverty has grown. Even so, democratic governments in India have introduced a range of schemes for the poor, and the fact that poverty can be openly discussed and campaigned about is itself a democratic outcome. In non-democratic states, inequality may be lower on paper but the reasons are not debated and the poor have no voice.
5. Accommodation of social diversity. Democracy is the best form of government to accommodate social diversity because it develops procedures to conduct competition among different groups, so that competition does not become explosive. It gives due representation to different castes, religions and linguistic groups, and it recognises that the interests of the majority are not always the same as those of a particular community. Democracy requires the majority to work with the minority so that any group can form a majority; it therefore respects differences and provides a mechanism to negotiate differences. Democracies worldwide have shown that they can develop procedures to accommodate diversity — although there is much more to be done in India, where demands of caste and religious groups still lead to conflict.
6. Dignity and freedom of citizens. This is the most fundamental outcome. Democracy is based on the recognition of the equal worth of all citizens, so it treats everyone with dignity and respect. Women, who were treated as subordinate, have gained the legal right to equality and have won their way into public life. The claims of disadvantaged and discriminated castes for equal status and equal opportunity are strengthened by democracy. Democracy stands much superior in promoting the dignity and freedom of the citizen — and the passion for respect and freedom is the basis of democracy, which is why people will not accept being ruled by unelected leaders.
7. Democracy's own examination. Democracy allows people to examine its functioning and criticise it, and this criticism is itself a sign of its strength. The fact that people keep complaining that democracy is not working shows that people have become more aware and expect more from democracy — which is exactly what a healthy democracy needs.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Three words for assessment: accountable, responsive, legitimate. Any 5-mark answer can be organised around them.
  • Growth question = 'dictatorships grew slightly faster, but the difference is small and growth depends on other factors'. Give both halves.
  • Dignity is the most fundamental outcome. If a question asks which outcome is basic, that is the answer.

⚠️ Mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying democracies always grow faster. The evidence is that dictatorships had a slightly higher average rate; the difference is small.
  • Writing that democracy has eliminated inequality. It has not — the honest answer is that it makes inequality visible and actionable.
  • Treating criticism of democracy as a weakness. It is a strength: only democracy can be publicly criticised.

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is meant by a legitimate government?

Show Answer

Ans:

A legitimate government is one whose right to rule is accepted by the people. In a democracy this legitimacy comes from free and fair elections, an open and consultative process of decision-making, and the consent of the governed, so people are willing to accept even decisions they disagree with.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why is a democratic government regarded as legitimate?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because it is the people's own government, chosen by them through regular, free and fair elections. Even where democracy performs poorly or is slow, it cannot be dismissed as illegitimate, because the authority to govern comes from the consent of the governed. A dictatorship may be more efficient, but it is never the people's own government.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why does democracy stand superior to other forms of government in promoting the dignity and freedom of citizens?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because democracy is based on the recognition of the equal worth of all citizens. It gives legal equality to women and to disadvantaged and discriminated castes, and it allows people to claim respect and freedom openly. In a non-democratic state, the claims of women and of disadvantaged groups cannot be raised publicly, so their dignity is not legally protected.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why is it wrong to blame democracy for slower economic growth?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the rate of economic growth depends on many factors other than the form of government — the country's population size, the global economic situation, cooperation from other countries and the economic priorities chosen by the ruling party. Studies show that the difference in growth rates between democracies and dictatorships is small, and among poorer countries democracies have caught up with dictatorships in recent decades.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Explain the argument that democracy improves the quality of decision-making.

Show Answer

Ans:

Democracy is based on consultation and discussion, so a decision passes through several stages of debate in the legislature, the media and public opinion.
Because the opposition and the people can criticise, mistakes become visible and can be corrected.
This makes the process slower than in a dictatorship, but it reduces the chances of rash and arbitrary decisions and produces outcomes that people are more willing to accept.

LONGL104 marks

10. Democracy has not removed economic inequality in India. Does that mean democracy has failed? Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

No. Democracy's main promise is political equality and the right to change the government, not an instant economic transformation; economic outcomes depend on policies and on how actively citizens use democratic institutions.
Economic inequality persists because the poor are not organised enough to press their demands, wealthier groups resist redistribution, and governments have often shaped economic policy in favour of powerful interests.
Democracy has, however, given the poor the vote, the right to information, the right to employment under MGNREGA and a voice in public debate, which no dictatorship guarantees.
The remedy for unmet expectations is deeper democracy — better organised and informed citizens, greater transparency and accountability — not less of it.

LONGL115 marks

11. 'Democracy is a better form of government than dictatorship.' Support the statement with five arguments.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is accountable and responsive — citizens can examine how decisions are made, and the government must attend to their needs.
It improves the quality of decision-making — consultation and negotiation among many people reduce the chance of rash or irresponsible decisions.
It provides a method to deal with differences and conflicts — competition is conducted through agreed procedures rather than through force, so social diversity can be accommodated.
It enhances the dignity of citizens — it recognises the equal worth of all, and gives women and disadvantaged castes legal equality and a voice.
It allows its own mistakes to be corrected — a democratic government can be replaced at the next election, and public criticism of its functioning is allowed; a dictatorship hides its errors.

LONGL125 marks

12. How does democracy produce an accountable, responsive and legitimate government?

Show Answer

Ans:

Accountable: citizens have the right to examine the process of decision-making and to hold the government to account; democracy follows norms and procedures, and citizens have legal rights such as the right to information.
Responsive: the government must attend to the needs and expectations of the people, and if it fails to do so it can be voted out; regular elections force governments to respond.
Legitimate: the government is elected by the people and is therefore the people's own government, so even a slow or inefficient democratic government is preferred to a dictatorship that is not answerable to anyone.
Supporting evidence: despite many problems, democracies around the world have survived and people have continued to prefer them, which shows that legitimacy is what ultimately sustains a government.
