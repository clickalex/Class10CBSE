CBSE • CLASS 10 • SOCIAL SCIENCE

Social Science — PYQ trends

Where past papers keep returning, chapter by chapter. Use it to decide revision order — not to guess the paper.

Trend notes below are an original reading of publicly available past papers and sample papers. They are not official CBSE predictions.

ChapterTitleWhat past papers ask
Ch 1The Rise of Nationalism in EuropeThe unification of Italy (usually as a 5-mark note on Mazzini, Cavour and Garibaldi) and the Frankfurt Parliament of 1848 alternate as the set long answer; allegories of Marianne and Germania recur as short questions.
Ch 2Nationalism in IndiaNon-Cooperation vs Civil Disobedience and the Sense of Collective Belonging are the two set 5-mark answers and one of them appears in almost every paper; Jallianwala Bagh, the Salt March and the Poona Pact recur as 3-mark questions.
Ch 3The Making of a Global WorldOnly subtopics 1 to 1.3 are assessed, so questions come from the pre-modern exchanges, the Silk Routes and the Columbian food trades — usually one 3-mark question per paper.
Ch 4Print Culture and the Modern WorldPrint in colonial India — especially the vernacular press, the Vernacular Press Act of 1878 and print's role in nationalism — is the recurring 5-mark answer; Gutenberg and the woodblock tradition in China and Japan cover the 3-mark slot.
Ch 5Resources and DevelopmentSoil types with their distribution and crops is asked almost every year; sustainable development with Agenda 21 and land degradation with remedies are the recurring short answers.
Ch 6Forest and Wildlife ResourcesOne 3-mark question on the causes of depletion, or a 5-mark question combining the categories of species with conservation steps. National parks and wildlife sanctuaries from this chapter are regular map-pointing items, and community conservation — Chipko, JFM, sacred groves — turns up in case-based questions.
Ch 7Water ResourcesThe advantages and drawbacks of multi-purpose river projects is the set 5-mark answer; rainwater harvesting methods with regional names appear frequently as 3-mark questions.
Ch 8AgricultureThe geographical conditions of rice, wheat, cotton, tea or coffee is asked in nearly every paper; kharif vs rabi and the impact of globalisation on Indian agriculture are the recurring 3–5 mark answers.
Ch 9Minerals and Energy ResourcesThe difference between conventional and non-conventional sources and the distribution of one major mineral (iron ore, coal or petroleum) alternate as the set question; oil fields, coal fields and power plants recur as map items.
Ch 10Manufacturing IndustriesThe cotton textile or iron and steel industry with location factors is asked almost every year; industrial pollution with control measures is the other recurring 5-mark answer.
Ch 11Lifelines of National EconomyThis chapter is assessed mainly through map pointing — ports, airports, national highways and pipelines — with an occasional 3-mark question on roadways versus railways or on the importance of trade.
Ch 12Power SharingThe Belgium–Sri Lanka comparison and the four forms of power sharing alternate as the set long answer; prudential versus moral reasons is a frequent 3-mark question.
Ch 13FederalismThe three lists with subjects and the 1992 decentralisation amendment are the two set 5-mark answers; coming together versus holding together is a frequent 3-mark question.
Ch 14Gender, Religion and CasteOne 3-mark or 5-mark question from one of the three sections — the definition of communalism, the two faces of the caste–politics relationship, or the reasons for women's low representation. Answers lifted almost word for word from the book score full marks here, so keep the wording of communalism and of 'caste in politics' and 'politics in caste' exact.
Ch 15Political PartiesThe functions of political parties and the challenges with reforms alternate as the set 5-mark answer; recognised national parties with symbols and the anti-defection/affidavit reforms recur as short questions.
Ch 16Outcomes of DemocracyOne 5-mark question on the outcomes of democracy is virtually certain, and this chapter is the usual source of case-based or source-based questions with three short parts.
Ch 17DevelopmentPer capita income and the limitations of averages, the Kerala-versus-Haryana comparison, and the HDI components recur every year; sustainability with groundwater or crude oil is the common 3-mark question.
Ch 18Sectors of the Indian EconomyOrganised versus unorganised sector, disguised unemployment with remedies, and why the tertiary sector has become the most important are the three set 5-mark answers.
Ch 19Money and CreditThe Salim–Swapna contrast, formal versus informal credit with reasons, and self-help groups rotate through the 3–5 mark slot every year.
Ch 20Globalisation and the Indian EconomyMNCs and how they control production, liberalisation of 1991, the WTO and its criticism, and the impact of globalisation on small producers and workers rotate through the 3–5 mark slot.
Ch 21Consumer RightsAssessed mainly as project work, but the consumer's rights, the three-tier machinery under COPRA and the duties of a consumer recur as 3-mark questions.
