Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of rice.

Show Answer

Ans:

Rice is a kharif crop that requires a temperature above 25 °C and high humidity, with annual rainfall above 100 cm. It grows best in the plains of north and north-eastern India, the coastal areas and the deltaic regions. In areas of less rainfall, such as Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh, it is grown with the help of irrigation and canal networks.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Distinguish between kharif and rabi crops with two examples of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Kharif crops are sown with the onset of the monsoon in June–July and harvested in September–October — for example paddy, maize, cotton and jute. Rabi crops are sown in winter from October to December and harvested in summer from April to June — for example wheat, barley, peas, gram and mustard.

LONGL35 marks

3. Describe the impact of globalisation on Indian agriculture.

Show Answer

Ans:

Indian agriculture has faced the impact of globalisation since colonial times — indigo was grown for export and forced upon farmers, leading to the Champaran revolt.
Today, Indian farmers face competition from highly subsidised agriculture in developed countries, so the prices of agricultural produce are unstable and many small farmers find cultivation unviable.
Liberalisation has removed restrictions on imports, so cheaper imports of edible oils and grains have reduced demand for Indian produce.
Positive side: improved technology, HYV and genetically modified seeds, better irrigation and access to information have raised productivity in some regions, and organic farming is opening new markets.
Result: a large number of small and marginal farmers have withdrawn from farming, and agriculture is no longer a rewarding occupation for them.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is plantation agriculture? Give one example.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is a type of commercial farming in which a single crop is grown on a large area, using capital-intensive inputs and migrant labour, and the produce is used as raw material in industries. Tea in Assam and North Bengal, coffee in Karnataka, rubber in Kerala and banana in southern India are examples; the production of tea in Assam was developed by the British with capital, technology and indentured labour.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is buffer stock and why is it created?

Show Answer

Ans:

Buffer stock is a stock of foodgrains, mainly wheat and rice, procured by the government through the Food Corporation of India. It is created to distribute foodgrains in deficit areas and among the poorer strata of society at a price lower than the market price, and to meet the shortage during a bad harvest or a calamity.

LONGL65 marks

6. Discuss “Agriculture” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Agriculture in India. About two-thirds of India's population is engaged in agriculture, which provides most of the food consumed, produces raw material for industries, and earns foreign exchange through exports of tea, coffee and spices. It is the backbone of the economy.
Marks lens: –4 marks. The geographical conditions of rice/wheat/tea/coffee and the three cropping seasons are asked almost every year.
Method / memory trick: Kharif = monsoon (June–July to Sept–Oct); Rabi = winter (Oct–Dec to April–June); Zaid = summer. 'Kharif rain, Rabi grain, Zaid melon.'
Do not: Mixing up kharif and rabi crops. Wheat, gram, mustard and barley are rabi; rice, cotton, jute and maize are kharif.
