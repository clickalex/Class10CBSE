MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The Human Development Report is published by: (a) the World Bank (b) the IMF (c) the UNDP (d) the RBI

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), which combines health, education and income into the HDI.

MCQM21 mark

2. A person with a BMI of 17 is: (a) overweight (b) undernourished (c) normal (d) obese

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) undernourished — a BMI below 18.5 indicates undernourishment; above 25 indicates overweight.

MCQM31 mark

3. Which criterion does the World Bank use to classify countries? (a) literacy rate (b) HDI (c) per capita income (d) infant mortality rate

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) per capita income, as reported in the World Development Report.

MCQM41 mark

4. Body Mass Index (BMI) is calculated as: (a) height in metres divided by weight in kilograms (b) weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres (c) weight in kilograms multiplied by height in metres (d) weight in kilograms divided by height in metres

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres. BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²); for example 60 kg and 1.5 m gives about 26.7.

MCQM51 mark

5. The infant mortality rate is the number of children who die before the age of one year per: (a) 100 live births (b) 10,000 people (c) 1 lakh live births (d) 1000 live births

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 1000 live births. Infant mortality is measured as deaths of babies under one year of age per thousand live births in a given year.

MCQM61 mark

6. Which of these is included in the Human Development Index? (a) life expectancy at birth (b) the number of cars (c) military spending (d) the area of the country

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) life expectancy at birth. Along with education levels and per capita income.

MCQM71 mark

7. A country with a high per capita income but poor health and education would be regarded as: (a) fully developed (b) socialist (c) underdeveloped by definition (d) not necessarily developed

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) not necessarily developed. Development covers health, education and equality along with income.

MCQM81 mark

8. The literacy rate is measured for the population aged: (a) 7 years and above (b) 18 years and above (c) 60 years and above (d) 5 years and above

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) 7 years and above. It is the percentage of people in that age group who can read and write with understanding.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why is per capita income not an adequate indicator of development?

Show Answer

Ans:

Average income hides inequality: two countries with the same average can differ greatly in how income is distributed. It also counts only goods and services bought for money and ignores health, education, freedom, dignity, security and equality. This is why the Human Development Report uses several indicators together.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why is per capita income used to compare countries instead of total income?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because countries have different population sizes, so total income does not tell us what an average person earns. Dividing national income by the population gives per capita income, which shows the average income of a person and allows a fair comparison of the standard of living between countries.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Kerala has a lower per capita income than Haryana but a lower infant mortality rate. Explain.

Show Answer

Ans:

Because Kerala has an adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities. Almost all children are born safe, are immunised and are given nutrition, and the literacy rate is high, so mothers are better able to care for their children. These facilities are public goods that individuals cannot buy, so a higher income alone does not reduce the infant mortality rate.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Explain the term sustainability of development with reference to groundwater.

Show Answer

Ans:

Sustainability of development means using resources in a way that meets present needs without exhausting them, so that future generations can meet their own needs. Groundwater is a renewable resource but it is being over-exploited in many parts of India, such as western Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Punjab and Haryana, where the water table is falling. Since it takes a long time to recharge, continued overuse is not sustainable and will leave future generations without water.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Why do different people have different notions of development?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because their life situations are different. A landless rural labourer wants more days of work and better wages, a prosperous farmer wants higher prices for his crops and cheap labour, an industrialist wants more electricity and better roads, and a tribal community wants its forest and land protected. What is development for one person may be destructive for another, so development goals are always contested.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student writes: “A higher BMI always means a healthier person.” Correct the statement, using the BMI ranges.

Show Answer

Ans:

BMI (Body Mass Index) = weight in kg ÷ (height in metres)². It compares weight with height.
A BMI below 18.5 means the person is undernourished; above 25 means overweight, with a higher risk of illness.
The healthy range lies between 18.5 and 25, so neither a very low nor a very high BMI is better.

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Country A has a total income of Rs 4,00,000 crore and a population of 20 crore. Country B has a total income of Rs 6,00,000 crore and a population of 40 crore. Which country is better off on average? Show your working.

Show Answer

Ans:

Compare per capita income (total income ÷ population), because the populations differ.
Country A: 4,00,000 ÷ 20 = Rs 20,000 crore per crore people, i.e. Rs 20,000 per person. Country B: 6,00,000 ÷ 40 = Rs 15,000 per person.
Country A is better off on average even though B has the larger total income. (An average, however, hides differences between people.)

SHORTS83 marks

8. What does the Human Development Index measure?

Show Answer

Ans:

The HDI is a composite index published annually by the UNDP in the Human Development Report.
It combines three dimensions: a long and healthy life, measured by life expectancy at birth.
Knowledge, measured by mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling.
A decent standard of living, measured by gross national income per capita. Countries are ranked on a scale between 0 and 1, so development is compared by human outcomes and not by income alone.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: The table gives illustrative figures for two states. State X: per capita income Rs 2,20,000, literacy rate 78%, infant mortality rate 35 per 1000. State Y: per capita income Rs 1,40,000, literacy rate 94%, infant mortality rate 10 per 1000. (a) Which state has the higher per capita income? (b) Which state does better on health and education? (c) Which state is more developed? Explain.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) State X.
(b) State Y: its literacy rate is higher and its infant mortality rate is lower.
(c) State Y is likely to be more developed. Development is not income alone: better health and education give its people a longer, better life, and the Human Development Index counts all three.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: In a village, the government school gives free education and a midday meal, and the ration shop sells rice at a low price. A private clinic in the nearby town charges high fees, so poor families cannot use it. (a) What are public facilities? (b) Why are they important for the poor? (c) Give two more examples of public facilities that help development.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Facilities such as schools, hospitals, water supply and roads that the government provides for everyone, often at low or no cost.
(b) Poor families cannot afford private services, so public facilities give them access to education, food and health care that they would otherwise lack.
(c) Public hospitals and primary health centres; safe drinking water and sanitation (or roads and public transport).

LONGL114 marks

11. Explain the importance of public facilities and the idea of sustainable development with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

Public facilities such as schools, health centres, safe drinking water, sanitation, public transport and cheap food through the public distribution system benefit the whole community rather than one buyer, and only the government can provide them to people who cannot pay.
Without them, even a person with money cannot be healthy or educated, since they need schools, hospitals and clean water shared by all.
Sustainable development means using resources so that they remain available for future generations.
Example: the Green Revolution made India self-sufficient in food, but in Punjab the water table is falling because of over-irrigation, so the present generation is borrowing from the future.

LONGL125 marks

12. What is the Human Development Report and on what basis does it compare countries?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is published every year by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). It compares countries on the basis of three things: the educational levels of the people, their health status, and their per capita income.
These three are combined into the Human Development Index (HDI), a figure between 0 and 1; the closer to 1, the higher the level of human development. Countries are grouped as having very high, high, medium or low human development.
The report is important because it recognises that income alone does not measure development — a country may be rich but have poor health and education, in which case its people are not truly developed. India has been improving its rank and is placed in the medium human development group.

LONGL135 marks

13. The table shows illustrative figures for three countries. A: per capita income Rs 5,00,000, literacy 70%, life expectancy 65 years. B: Rs 2,00,000, literacy 98%, life expectancy 80 years. C: Rs 1,00,000, literacy 60%, life expectancy 62 years. Compare the countries and say which has the highest human development. Why does high income not guarantee it?

Show Answer

Ans:

By income: A > B > C. By education (literacy): B (98%) > A (70%) > C (60%). By health (life expectancy): B (80) > A (65) > C (62).
The Human Development Index combines income, education and health, not income alone.
So country B has the highest human development: it is ahead of A on two of the three measures.
Income is only a means: A’s high average income does not by itself give its people better schooling or longer lives.
An average also hides inequality, so a rich country can still have many poorly educated or unhealthy people.
