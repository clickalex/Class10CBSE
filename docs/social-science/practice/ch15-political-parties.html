MCQM21 mark

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is a political party and why are parties necessary in a democracy?

Show Answer

Ans:

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in government. Parties are necessary because representatives must be elected, because they offer voters different policies to choose from, because they form and run governments, shape public opinion and give people access to government schemes and welfare measures.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is the criterion for a party to be recognised as a national party?

Show Answer

Ans:

A party is recognised as a national party by the Election Commission if it secures at least six per cent of the total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four states, and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha. State parties need at least six per cent of the votes in the state Assembly elections and at least two seats.

SHORTS33 marks

3. How do political parties shape public opinion?

Show Answer

Ans:

Parties raise and highlight issues through their lakhs of members and workers spread over the country, and through the pressure groups they support. They also use newspapers, meetings and social media to explain their stand. When a party is not able to solve a problem, it highlights the issue and mobilises opposition, which forces the government to respond.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Name any three national parties of India with their symbols and year of founding.

Show Answer

Ans:

Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, symbol hand; Bharatiya Janata Party, founded in 1980, symbol lotus; Bahujan Samaj Party, founded in 1984, symbol elephant. (CPI-M, 1964 — hammer, sickle and star; CPI, 1925 — ears of corn and sickle; NCP, 1999 — clock.)

SHORTS73 marks

7. Distinguish between a two-party system and a multi-party system.

Show Answer

Ans:

In a two-party system, such as in the USA or the UK, only two parties are strong enough to win power, and one of them usually wins a majority on its own.
In a multi-party system, such as in India, several parties compete and none usually gets a majority, so they must form coalitions to govern.
A multi-party system represents the diversity of a large and varied society better; a two-party system gives more stable but less representative government.

LONGL104 marks

10. Suggest any four reforms that would strengthen political parties in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

A law to regulate the internal affairs of parties: they should maintain a register of members, hold regular organisational elections and follow their own constitutions.
State funding of elections to reduce the dependence of parties on big donors and on black money, along with the filing of audited accounts and income-tax returns.
Disclosure of the assets and criminal records of candidates, as required by the Supreme Court and the Election Commission, so that voters can make an informed choice.
Reservation for women in the party organisation as well as in the allotment of tickets, and an end to dynastic succession through inner-party democracy.

LONGL115 marks

11. Describe the main functions of political parties in a democracy.

Show Answer

Ans:

Contesting elections: parties select candidates and campaign for them; in India, top leaders usually choose the candidates.
Putting forward policies and programmes: parties present different sets of policies, and voters choose between them.
Making laws: legislatures have many members, so laws are debated and passed along party lines, and the party leadership effectively decides.
Forming and running the government: the winning party or coalition forms the government, and its top leaders become ministers and run the administration.
Playing the opposition: losing parties criticise the government for its failures and wrong policies, mobilise opposition and voice different views.
Shaping public opinion: parties raise and highlight issues through lakhs of members and workers and through pressure groups.
Providing access to government machinery and welfare schemes: people approach local party leaders to get work done or to access government programmes.

LONGL125 marks

12. What are the main challenges faced by political parties in India? Suggest any three reforms.

Show Answer

Ans:

Challenges: (1) Lack of internal democracy — power is concentrated in the hands of a few top leaders, there are no regular organisational elections or membership registers, and ordinary members lack information; (2) Dynastic succession — top posts are controlled by family members of the leaders, which is unfair and undemocratic; (3) Money and muscle power — parties nominate candidates who can raise money and sometimes candidates with criminal cases, because winning matters more than principles; (4) Lack of meaningful choice — parties offer very similar policies and ideologies, and leaders shift freely between parties.
Reforms: the constitution was amended to prevent defection, so elected representatives cannot change parties for ministerial posts or cash; every candidate must file an affidavit declaring property and pending criminal cases, which the Supreme Court made mandatory; parties must hold organisational elections and file income tax returns; and the Election Commission has ordered parties to keep audited accounts and to distribute tickets on merit.
