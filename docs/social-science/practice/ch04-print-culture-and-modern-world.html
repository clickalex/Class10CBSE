Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. How did Gutenberg develop the printing press?

Show Answer

Ans:

Gutenberg grew up on a large agricultural estate and from childhood had seen wine and olive presses. By the 1430s he had perfected the printing press by adapting the olive press, and he developed a metal mould for casting types. His press could print far more copies than hand copying — his first book, the Bible, was printed in about three years, producing 180 copies.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why did Martin Luther's ideas spread so rapidly across Europe?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because his writings were printed. When he posted his Ninety-Five Theses on the church door at Wittenberg in 1517, printers reproduced them in vast numbers within days, and they were read widely and discussed everywhere. His German translation of the New Testament sold 5,000 copies within a few weeks — something impossible in a manuscript culture.

LONGL35 marks

3. How did print culture help the growth of nationalism in India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Newspapers carried nationalist criticism. Vernacular newspapers such as Tilak's Kesari and Maratha reported colonial misrule and supported the Punjab revolutionaries; Tilak's imprisonment in 1908 provoked widespread protest.
Religious and social reform debates were printed. Raja Rammohun Roy's Sambad Kaumudi, the Mirat-ul-Akhbar, and the fatwas of the Deoband seminary spread reformist and religious ideas to a wide readership.
Cheap books and libraries made knowledge available to the poor and to workers, and novels and pamphlets carried political ideas in an easily readable form.
Writings by and about women — Tarabai Shinde's Stripurushtulna, Pandita Ramabai on widowhood — created a new public debate on social questions.
Censorship itself proved the point. The Vernacular Press Act of 1878, which allowed the government to seize presses, showed how powerful print had become, and made the press a recognised weapon of the national movement.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why were manuscripts not widely used in everyday life in pre-colonial India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because they were written on palm leaf or fragile handmade paper, were expensive to produce, and could not be handled or read easily. They were also copied by hand, so copies were few and easily damaged, and the script was written in a variety of styles that made reading difficult. Knowledge was therefore largely transmitted orally — texts were recited and memorised rather than read.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What was the Vernacular Press Act of 1878?

Show Answer

Ans:

It was a law modelled on Irish press laws that gave the government extensive powers to censor reports and editorials in the vernacular press. If a newspaper published anything judged seditious and did not heed an initial warning, its press could be confiscated, including its printing machinery. It was widely resented and became a symbol of colonial repression of the Indian press.
