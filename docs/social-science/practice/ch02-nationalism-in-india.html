MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The Lahore session of the Congress (December 1929), presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, demanded: (a) Purna Swaraj (b) dominion status (c) separate electorates (d) abolition of zamindari

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Purna Swaraj — complete independence — and 26 January 1930 was fixed as Independence Day.

MCQM21 mark

2. The Swaraj flag designed by Gandhiji in 1921 had in its centre: (a) a lotus (b) a crescent moon (c) a spinning wheel (d) the Ashoka chakra

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) a spinning wheel, representing Gandhi's idea of self-help.

MCQM31 mark

3. The Poona Pact of September 1932 was signed between Gandhiji and: (a) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Sardar Patel (d) Dr B. R. Ambedkar

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Dr B. R. Ambedkar. It gave the Depressed Classes reserved seats in provincial and central legislative councils, but they were to be voted in by the general electorate.

MCQM41 mark

4. Who wrote the song “Vande Mataram”? (a) Rabindranath Tagore (b) Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (c) Abanindranath Tagore (d) Muhammad Iqbal

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay. Bankim Chandra wrote it in the 1870s; Abanindranath Tagore painted the image of Bharat Mata.

MCQM51 mark

5. General Dyer ordered his troops to fire on a peaceful gathering on 13 April 1919 at: (a) Chauri Chaura, Gorakhpur (b) Dandi, Gujarat (c) Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar (d) Lahore

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. The firing at Jallianwala Bagh killed hundreds of people and shocked the country.

MCQM61 mark

6. The Dandi March began on: (a) 6 April 1919 (b) 26 January 1930 (c) 8 August 1942 (d) 12 March 1930

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) 12 March 1930. Gandhiji walked from Sabarmati to Dandi and broke the salt law there.

MCQM71 mark

7. The Simon Commission was boycotted because: (a) it recommended partition (b) it had no Indian member (c) it raised land revenue (d) it banned the Congress

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) it had no Indian member. All seven members were British, so the cry was ‘Go back Simon'.

MCQM81 mark

8. The Non-Cooperation Movement was called off after: (a) the Chauri Chaura incident (b) the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (c) the Rowlatt Act (d) the Simon Commission

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the Chauri Chaura incident. In February 1922 an angry crowd burnt a police station, killing 22 policemen, and Gandhiji withdrew the movement.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. What was the Rowlatt Act of 1919 and why was it opposed?

Show Answer

Ans:

It allowed the colonial government to imprison any person suspected of sedition without trial for up to two years. Indians saw it as a betrayal after the war and Gandhiji called for a hartal on 6 April 1919 against what he called the ‘black act'.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why was the Salt March an effective symbol of resistance against colonialism?

Show Answer

Ans:

Salt was consumed by rich and poor alike and was one of the most essential items of food, yet the tax on it and the government monopoly over its production revealed the most oppressive face of British rule. Choosing it united the whole country behind a single demand, and breaking the salt law by boiling sea water at Dandi was simple, visible and easy for every villager to repeat, so it quickly spread into a nationwide movement.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Describe the Jallianwala Bagh incident.

Show Answer

Ans:

On 13 April 1919, a large crowd gathered in the enclosed ground of Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar — some to protest against the repressive Rowlatt Act, others to attend the annual Baisakhi fair. Unaware of the martial law imposed in the city, they were trapped when General Dyer entered, blocked the exit points and opened fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds. His object, as he later declared, was to produce a moral effect and create terror in the minds of satyagrahis. News of the massacre spread, and crowds took to the streets in many north Indian towns.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Why did Gandhiji decide to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the movement was turning violent. At Chauri Chaura in Gorakhpur, a peaceful demonstration turned into a violent clash and the crowd burnt a police station with policemen inside. Gandhi felt that satyagrahis needed proper training before they could take part in a mass struggle, and that violence would give the British an excuse to crush the movement, so he called it off.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student writes that the Salt March began in 1920 and that Gandhiji ended the Non-Cooperation Movement because of the salt tax. Correct both statements.

Show Answer

Ans:

The Salt (Dandi) March began on 12 March 1930, from Sabarmati Ashram, and reached Dandi on 6 April, where Gandhiji broke the salt law; it started the Civil Disobedience Movement.
The Non-Cooperation Movement was begun at the end of 1920 and was withdrawn in February 1922, after the violent incident at Chauri Chaura, where a mob set a police station on fire and killed policemen.
The salt tax had nothing to do with calling off Non-Cooperation.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. How did the peasants of Awadh and the tribal people of the Gudem Hills each understand Gandhiji’s call for swaraj?

Show Answer

Ans:

Awadh: led by Baba Ramchandra, peasants fought talukdars and landlords who demanded high rents and forced labour (begar). For them swaraj meant lower revenue, an end to begar and a social boycott of oppressive landlords.
Gudem Hills: tribal people, led by Alluri Sitaram Raju, were angered by colonial rules that restricted entry into the forests. For them swaraj meant the freedom to move freely in the forests and use forest produce.
In both cases people gave the call their own local meaning, and some, like Raju, took to militant methods that Gandhiji did not approve.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe the participation of women and students in the national movement.

Show Answer

Ans:

Women joined processions, picketed shops selling liquor and foreign cloth, and went to jail; several, such as Sarojini Naidu, became national leaders.
Students left government schools and colleges during the Non-Cooperation Movement and joined national schools and colleges.
Their participation widened the social base of the movement, turning it from a middle-class agitation into a mass struggle.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: In December 1929, the Congress session at Lahore, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, took a major decision. Earlier, the Simon Commission, which had no Indian member, had been greeted with “Go back, Simon!” slogans. (a) What goal did the Lahore session adopt? (b) On which date was “Independence Day” to be observed? (c) Why did the Congress turn more radical after the Simon Commission?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Purna Swaraj — complete independence from British rule.
(b) 26 January 1930.
(c) The Commission, set up to review the constitutional system, had no Indian members, so it was boycotted; the Viceroy’s vague offer of dominion status satisfied no one, and the demand moved to full independence.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: During the Civil Disobedience Movement, Dr B. R. Ambedkar and Mahatma Gandhi disagreed over how the “depressed classes” should be represented. (a) What did Dr Ambedkar demand? (b) Why did Gandhiji oppose it, and what did he do? (c) What was the outcome in September 1932?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Separate electorates for the depressed classes.
(b) Gandhiji believed separate electorates would slow their integration into society, and he began a fast unto death.
(c) The Poona Pact: Ambedkar accepted reserved seats for the depressed classes in the provincial and central councils, to be voted for by the general electorate.

LONGL104 marks

10. Describe the course of the Civil Disobedience Movement and explain why it was withdrawn.

Show Answer

Ans:

It began with the Salt March: on 12 March 1930 Gandhiji set out from Sabarmati with 78 followers and broke the salt law at Dandi on 6 April.
People refused to pay taxes, boycotted foreign cloth and liquor, and peasants and tribals agitated against forest laws and land revenue.
Gandhiji suspended it after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931, revived it in 1932, and finally withdrew it in April 1934 when mass support had faded and the government had crushed the movement.

LONGL115 marks

11. How did the Non-Cooperation Movement differ from the Civil Disobedience Movement?

Show Answer

Ans:

Non-Cooperation (1920–22): people were asked to refuse all cooperation with the British government — students left schools, lawyers gave up practice, foreign cloth was boycotted and burnt. It was launched with the Khilafat issue and withdrawn by Gandhi after the Chauri Chaura violence.
Civil Disobedience (1930–34): people were asked not merely to refuse cooperation but to break colonial laws — the salt law, forest laws, refusal to pay revenue and chaukidari taxes, boycott of foreign cloth and picketing of liquor shops.
Participation: Civil Disobedience saw much larger participation by women, who left their homes to join protest marches and manufacture salt.
Outcome: Non-Cooperation was withdrawn in 1922; Civil Disobedience was suspended by the Gandhi-Irwin Pact in 1931, revived after the failed Round Table Conference, and finally lost momentum by 1934.

LONGL125 marks

12. Explain the idea of the Sense of Collective Belonging with reference to nationalism in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Nationalism develops when people begin to believe that they are part of the same nation, and this feeling is created through history and fiction, folklore and songs, and popular prints.
Images: Bharat Mata, first created by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and painted by Abhinindranath Tagore as a calm, divine ascetic figure, came to symbolise the nation; devotion to her became a sign of nationalism.
Songs: Vande Mataram, written by Bankim Chandra in the 1870s and set in Anandamath, was widely sung during the Swadeshi movement.
Folklore: in Bengal, Natesan Sastri and others collected folk tales and songs, arguing that these were the truest manifestation of national identity.
Flags: the Swadeshi flag of 1905 and the Swaraj flag of 1921, with the spinning wheel, became symbols of defiance; carrying them was itself an act of rebellion.
History: Indians began writing of India's ancient achievements in mathematics, science, law and philosophy, to counter the colonial view of Indians as backward and to instil pride in the past.
