MCQs

The Lahore session of the Congress (December 1929), presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, demanded: (a) dominion status (b) Purna Swaraj (c) separate electorates (d) abolition of zamindari

(b) Purna Swaraj — complete independence — and 26 January 1930 was fixed as Independence Day.

The Swaraj flag designed by Gandhiji in 1921 had in its centre: (a) a lotus (b) a crescent moon (c) a spinning wheel (d) the Ashoka chakra

(c) a spinning wheel, representing Gandhi's idea of self-help.

The Poona Pact of September 1932 was signed between Gandhiji and: (a) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (b) Dr B. R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Patel

(b) Dr B. R. Ambedkar. It gave the Depressed Classes reserved seats in provincial and central legislative councils, but they were to be voted in by the general electorate.

Written questions

3 marks Why was the Salt March an effective symbol of resistance against colonialism?

Salt was consumed by rich and poor alike and was one of the most essential items of food, yet the tax on it and the government monopoly over its production revealed the most oppressive face of British rule. Choosing it united the whole country behind a single demand, and breaking the salt law by boiling sea water at Dandi was simple, visible and easy for every villager to repeat, so it quickly spread into a nationwide movement.

5 marks How did the Non-Cooperation Movement differ from the Civil Disobedience Movement?

Non-Cooperation (1920–22): people were asked to refuse all cooperation with the British government — students left schools, lawyers gave up practice, foreign cloth was boycotted and burnt. It was launched with the Khilafat issue and withdrawn by Gandhi after the Chauri Chaura violence.
Civil Disobedience (1930–34): people were asked not merely to refuse cooperation but to break colonial laws — the salt law, forest laws, refusal to pay revenue and chaukidari taxes, boycott of foreign cloth and picketing of liquor shops.
Participation: Civil Disobedience saw much larger participation by women, who left their homes to join protest marches and manufacture salt.
Outcome: Non-Cooperation was withdrawn in 1922; Civil Disobedience was suspended by the Gandhi-Irwin Pact in 1931, revived after the failed Round Table Conference, and finally lost momentum by 1934.

5 marks Explain the idea of the Sense of Collective Belonging with reference to nationalism in India.

Nationalism develops when people begin to believe that they are part of the same nation, and this feeling is created through history and fiction, folklore and songs, and popular prints.
Images: Bharat Mata, first created by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and painted by Abhinindranath Tagore as a calm, divine ascetic figure, came to symbolise the nation; devotion to her became a sign of nationalism.
Songs: Vande Mataram, written by Bankim Chandra in the 1870s and set in Anandamath, was widely sung during the Swadeshi movement.
Folklore: in Bengal, Natesan Sastri and others collected folk tales and songs, arguing that these were the truest manifestation of national identity.
Flags: the Swadeshi flag of 1905 and the Swaraj flag of 1921, with the spinning wheel, became symbols of defiance; carrying them was itself an act of rebellion.
History: Indians began writing of India's ancient achievements in mathematics, science, law and philosophy, to counter the colonial view of Indians as backward and to instil pride in the past.

3 marks Describe the Jallianwala Bagh incident.

On 13 April 1919, a large crowd gathered in the enclosed ground of Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar — some to protest against the repressive Rowlatt Act, others to attend the annual Baisakhi fair. Unaware of the martial law imposed in the city, they were trapped when General Dyer entered, blocked the exit points and opened fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds. His object, as he later declared, was to produce a moral effect and create terror in the minds of satyagrahis. News of the massacre spread, and crowds took to the streets in many north Indian towns.

3 marks Why did Gandhiji decide to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922?

Because the movement was turning violent. At Chauri Chaura in Gorakhpur, a peaceful demonstration turned into a violent clash and the crowd burnt a police station with policemen inside. Gandhi felt that satyagrahis needed proper training before they could take part in a mass struggle, and that violence would give the British an excuse to crush the movement, so he called it off.
