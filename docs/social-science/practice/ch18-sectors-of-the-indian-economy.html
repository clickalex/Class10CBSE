Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Distinguish between the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors with one example of each.

Show Answer

Ans:

The primary sector includes activities that use natural resources directly, such as agriculture, dairy, fishing and mining. The secondary sector includes activities in which natural products are changed into other forms through manufacturing, such as turning sugarcane into sugar or cotton into cloth. The tertiary sector includes activities that support the primary and secondary sectors without producing goods, such as transport, banking, trade, education and health.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is disguised unemployment? Give an example from rural India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Disguised or hidden unemployment is a situation in which more people are employed in a job than are actually required, so that withdrawing some of them does not reduce production. In rural India, a family of five may all work on a small plot of land although three would be enough; if the extra two members leave, output does not fall. They appear employed but are in fact surplus.

LONGL35 marks

3. Compare the working conditions in the organised and unorganised sectors.

Show Answer

Ans:

Organised sector: enterprises are registered with the government and must follow rules on working hours, overtime, leave and holidays. Workers get regular employment, job security, paid leave, provident fund, medical benefits and other facilities, and are protected by laws such as the Factories Act and the Minimum Wages Act.
Unorganised sector: small, scattered units largely outside government control. Work is irregular and low paid, there is no job security, no paid leave, no provident fund and no medical benefits, and working conditions are often unsafe.
Scale: about 90% of the workers in India are in the unorganised sector — small farmers, landless labourers, artisans, street vendors, construction workers and domestic workers.
What is needed: protection of wages, safety at the workplace, and social security such as health cover and old-age support.

LONGL45 marks

4. Why has the tertiary sector become the most important sector in India? Give five reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

Basic services: services such as hospitals, educational institutions, postal and telegraph services, police stations, courts, banks and insurance companies are required by every citizen and by the government, and their expansion is essential for development.
Growing demand with rising income: as incomes rise, people demand more services such as eating out, tourism, shopping, private hospitals and private schools.
Support to agriculture and industry: the development of the primary and secondary sectors has led to the development of services such as transport, storage and trade.
New services: information and communication technology has made new services essential, and India has become a major provider of software and business process services.
Employment and GDP: the sector now contributes the largest share of GDP and has created a wide range of jobs, from call centres to consultancy, making it the largest producing sector in India.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What is GDP and why are only final goods counted in it?

Show Answer

Ans:

Gross Domestic Product is the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year. Only final goods are counted because the value of intermediate goods is already included in the value of the final product — the value of wheat is included in the flour, and of the flour in the bread. Counting both would amount to double counting.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is MGNREGA 2005 and how does it help to reduce unemployment?

Show Answer

Ans:

The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 guarantees 100 days of wage employment in a year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work. If the government fails to provide work within fifteen days, the applicant is entitled to an unemployment allowance. It creates a legal right to work, raises rural incomes, and builds rural assets such as ponds, roads and irrigation channels.
