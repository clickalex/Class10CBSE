Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. What is a multinational corporation? How do MNCs spread their production?

Show Answer

Ans:

A multinational corporation is a corporation that owns or controls production of goods or services in two or more countries other than its home country. MNCs set up factories in regions where they can get cheap labour, resources and other factors of production, and they keep the large profits with themselves. They spread production by setting up new factories, by buying or acquiring a local company and expanding it, or by placing orders with small producers which are then sold under the MNC's brand name.

SHORTS23 marks

2. What is liberalisation and why did the Indian government introduce it in 1991?

Show Answer

Ans:

Liberalisation means removing barriers or restrictions set by the government on trade and on foreign investment. The government introduced it in 1991 because it felt that Indian producers needed to improve the quality of what they produced, and that foreign competition would improve the performance of producers in India, since they would have to improve quality to compete with imports.

LONGL35 marks

3. How has globalisation affected small producers and workers in India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Small producers: many small industries producing items such as batteries, capacitors, plastics, toys, tyres, dairy products, vegetable oil, groundnut oil and matchboxes could not compete with cheap imports and had to shut down, leaving lakhs of workers unemployed. Small farmers have also been affected by cheap agricultural imports from developed countries.
Workers: globalisation has brought pressure on employers to be 'flexible', so workers are increasingly hired on temporary contracts for short periods, with low wages, long hours and no job security or benefits such as provident fund and medical cover. Labour laws are often not implemented.
The result: globalisation has made the rich richer and has increased the insecurity of the poor, so many workers' organisations and people's movements have been demanding fairer rules.
But: in some sectors, such as information technology and services, globalisation has created new employment opportunities, and consumers have benefited from lower prices and wider choice.

LONGL45 marks

4. What is the WTO and what criticism is made of it?

Show Answer

Ans:

The World Trade Organization was started in 1995 at the insistence of developed countries. It sets the rules for international trade, and all decisions are taken by consensus among its member countries, of which there are about 164.
Aim: to liberalise international trade, and to ensure that countries remove trade barriers such as taxes on imports, since it is believed that this will allow countries to specialise and grow.
Criticism: the rules of the WTO are often biased in favour of developed countries. Developed countries have kept some trade barriers, particularly in agriculture, and have supported their own farmers with subsidies, while asking developing countries to remove their barriers. Developing countries have had to comply with WTO rules, but the benefits have not been shared equally. Hence the demand for fair globalisation.

SHORTS53 marks

5. What are Special Economic Zones and what facilities do they offer?

Show Answer

Ans:

Special Economic Zones are industrial areas set up by the government with world-class facilities such as electricity, water, roads, transport, storage, recreational and educational facilities. Companies that set up production in an SEZ do not have to pay tax for an initial period of five years, and labour laws are applied flexibly. SEZs are located at places such as Noida, Gandhidham, Falta, Cochin, Chennai and Kandla, with the aim of attracting foreign investment.

SHORTS63 marks

6. What is fair globalisation and how can it be achieved?

Show Answer

Ans:

Fair globalisation would create opportunities for all and ensure that the benefits of globalisation are shared better, rather than concentrating them in the hands of the rich. It can be achieved by the government supporting small producers with credit, technology and training until they are strong enough to compete, by implementing labour laws properly and protecting workers' rights, by negotiating with other developing countries at the WTO for fairer rules, and by supporting people's movements that campaign against unfair trade rules.
