Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Interpret the significance of the allegory of Marianne and Germania.

Show Answer

Ans:

Both were female allegories used to give the abstract idea of the nation a concrete visual form. Marianne, with her red cap, tricolour and cockade, represented the ideals of liberty and the French Republic, and statues of her were installed in public squares to remind citizens of national unity. Germania, wearing a crown of oak leaves, symbolised heroism and the German nation. Allegories made it easy for people to identify with the nation.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Briefly trace the process of German unification.

Show Answer

Ans:

Middle-class Germans tried to unite the German Confederation through a parliament elected in 1848, but the Frankfurt assembly failed when Friedrich Wilhelm IV rejected the offered crown. Prussia then took over the leadership, and its Chief Minister Otto von Bismarck used the army and the bureaucracy to fight three wars over seven years against Austria, Denmark and France. Prussian victories ended in the proclamation of the German Empire at Versailles in January 1871, with Kaiser Wilhelm I as emperor.

LONGL35 marks

3. Write a note on the role of Giuseppe Mazzini, Count Cavour and Giuseppe Garibaldi in the unification of Italy.

Show Answer

Ans:

Giuseppe Mazzini — a member of the secret society Carbonari, he founded Young Italy in Marseilles to work for a unitary Italian republic. He provided the ideological inspiration and the programme.
Count Cavour — Chief Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont, he led the unification movement through diplomacy rather than revolution, and with the help of France and the army defeated Austria in 1859.
Giuseppe Garibaldi — he led the armed volunteers known as the Red Shirts, marched into the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in 1860, won the support of the local peasants and drove out the Spanish rulers.
In 1861 Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed king of united Italy, with Cavour as his Chief Minister. Much of the peasant population, however, knew nothing of the ideals of liberal nationalism.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What steps did the French revolutionaries take to create a sense of collective identity among the French people?

Show Answer

Ans:

They introduced the ideas of la patrie (the fatherland) and le citoyen (the citizen), emphasising a united community with equal rights under a constitution. A new tricolour replaced the royal standard, the Estates General was renamed the National Assembly and elected by active citizens, a uniform system of weights and measures was adopted, internal customs duties were abolished, and French as spoken in Paris became the common language. Oaths of loyalty to the constitution and commemoration of martyrs were also used.

SHORTS53 marks

5. How did culture play an important role in creating the idea of the nation in Europe?

Show Answer

Ans:

Romantic artists and poets criticised the glorification of reason and science and turned to emotion, intuition and mysticism, using folk songs, folk poetry and folk dances to reach the people. Language too was used as a weapon of national resistance — in Poland, for example, the use of Polish in schools and church gatherings became a symbol of struggle against Russian dominance. Operas and music carried nationalist feelings to a largely illiterate audience.

On the ground lie the shattered remains of the symbols of absolutist institutions — the age of nationalism had begun.
