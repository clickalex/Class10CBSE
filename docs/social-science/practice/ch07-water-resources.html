Written questions · Write

SHORTS13 marks

1. Why is water scarcity increasing in India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because of a growing population, which raises the demand for water for drinking and for growing more food and cash crops; rapid urbanisation and changing lifestyles that consume more water; over-exploitation of groundwater by commercial farming; and unequal access between social groups. In many regions water is abundant but polluted by domestic and industrial waste and by pesticides and fertilisers, so it cannot be used without treatment.

LONGL25 marks

2. Multi-purpose river projects have several advantages but also several drawbacks. Explain.

Show Answer

Ans:

Advantages: they provide water for irrigation, generate hydroelectricity, control floods, help inland navigation, supply water to industries and cities, and provide fish breeding, recreation and landscape value. Nehru proudly called dams the temples of modern India.
Drawbacks: (i) large dams submerge forests and agricultural land and displace local communities, who are often not properly rehabilitated — the Narmada Bachao Andolan and the Tehri Dam Andolan grew out of this; (ii) they disturb the natural flow of rivers, causing poor sediment flow and excessive sedimentation at the bottom of the reservoir, which destroys habitats; (iii) they cause waterlogging and soil salinity in the command area; (iv) they trigger inter-state disputes over the sharing of costs and benefits, as between Karnataka, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh over the Krishna-Godavari waters; (v) they have induced earthquakes and, in the case of the Sardar Sarovar project, sparked protests when urban water supply was given priority over irrigation for farmers.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Describe the traditional rainwater harvesting system of Rajasthan.

Show Answer

Ans:

In the semi-arid regions of Rajasthan, particularly in Bikaner, Phalodi and Barmer, most houses traditionally had underground tanks or tankas for storing drinking water. The tanks were built inside the house or the courtyard and were connected to the sloping roofs. The first spell of rain was not collected, as it cleans the roof and the pipes; from the second shower onward the water was stored. The stored water lasted through the dry season and was treated as pure enough for religious use.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What is rooftop rainwater harvesting and where has it been made compulsory?

Show Answer

Ans:

It is a method in which rainwater falling on rooftops is collected through pipes and stored in underground tanks or used to recharge groundwater. It is common in Meghalaya, where bamboo pipes are used, and in Tamil Nadu it has been made compulsory for all houses, with legal provisions to punish defaulters.

LONGL55 marks

5. Discuss “Water Resources” in detail. Include the idea that carries marks, one worked example or answer frame, and why students lose marks.

Show Answer

Ans:

Water resources. About three-fourths of the earth's surface is covered with water, but only about 2.5% is fresh water, and nearly 70% of that is frozen in ice sheets; almost all the rest is groundwater. India ranks 133rd in the world in terms of water availability per person per annum (2010 data). Renewable water resources amount to about 1,869 cubic km. About 22% of the total water used is in the industrial sector;.
Marks lens: marks. Multi-purpose river projects with their benefits and drawbacks is the set question; rainwater harvesting methods come second.
Method / memory trick: Dams: give three benefits and three drawbacks in every answer — irrigation, power, flood control against displacement, sedimentation and conflict. Full marks need both sides.
Do not: Writing only the benefits of dams. The question almost always asks for drawbacks too.
