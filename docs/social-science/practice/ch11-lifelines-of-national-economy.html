MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The Golden Quadrilateral superhighway connects: (a) Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai (b) Srinagar and Kanyakumari (c) Silchar and Porbandar (d) Kandla and Haldia

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai — it is a six-lane highway linking the four major metropolitan cities.

MCQM21 mark

2. The first port developed soon after Independence to ease the volume of trade on Mumbai port was: (a) Marmagao (b) Kandla (c) Tuticorin (d) Kochi

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Kandla in Kachchh, developed to serve the rich agricultural and industrial hinterland of the north-west.

MCQM31 mark

3. Air transport in India was nationalised in: (a) 1947 (b) 1953 (c) 1965 (d) 1972

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) 1953.

MCQM41 mark

4. The Golden Quadrilateral Superhighways project is being implemented by the: (a) Border Roads Organisation (b) Public Works Department (c) National Highways Authority of India (d) Indian Railways Board

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) National Highways Authority of India. NHAI builds and maintains the national highways, including the Golden Quadrilateral and the corridors.

MCQM51 mark

5. The Border Roads Organisation, set up in 1960, builds roads mainly in: (a) the coastal plains (b) the Deccan Plateau (c) the northern and north-eastern border areas (d) metropolitan cities

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) the northern and north-eastern border areas. BRO builds and maintains roads in difficult border regions for defence and for the development of remote areas.

MCQM61 mark

6. The longest national highway in India is: (a) NH 7 (b) NH 1 (c) NH 2 (d) NH 44

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) NH 44. It runs from Srinagar to Kanyakumari; the old NH 7 from Varanasi to Kanyakumari is now part of it.

MCQM71 mark

7. National Waterway 1 lies on the: (a) Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly (b) Brahmaputra (c) Godavari (d) Narmada

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly. It runs from Prayagraj to Haldia; NW 2 is on the Brahmaputra and NW 3 is the West Coast Canal.

MCQM81 mark

8. The Konkan Railway was built mainly to: (a) link Delhi and Mumbai (b) carry coal (c) connect Kolkata and Chennai (d) provide a fast route along the western coast through difficult terrain

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) provide a fast route along the western coast through difficult terrain. It runs from Roha near Mumbai to Mangalore.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why is the density of roads higher in the northern plains than in the Himalayan region?

Show Answer

Ans:

The northern plains are level, densely populated and rich in agriculture and industry, so roads are cheap to build and carry heavy traffic. The Himalayas are rugged, prone to landslides and sparsely populated, so construction costs are high and the volume of traffic is low.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Why are roadways considered more efficient than railways in India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the cost of constructing roads is much lower than that of railway lines; roads can traverse more dissected and undulating topography and negotiate higher gradients, such as in the Himalayas; they provide door-to-door service, which reduces the loading and unloading cost; and they act as feeders to other modes of transport, connecting railway stations, airports and sea ports.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why are means of transport and communication called the lifelines of a nation and its economy?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because goods and services cannot move from supply locations to demand locations without them. A dense and efficient transport and communication network is a prerequisite for local, national and international trade, it enables industries to reach their markets and raw materials, it creates employment, and it has made the world a global village by allowing information to move instantly.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What are the advantages of pipeline transport?

Show Answer

Ans:

Pipelines can transport crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas from the fields to refineries, fertiliser plants and thermal power stations, and solids can be moved as slurry. Once laid, they have minimal running cost, they eliminate transhipment losses and delays, and they can pass through difficult terrain including rivers and hills, so they provide a continuous and reliable supply. Their only major drawback is the high initial cost of laying the line.

SHORTS53 marks

5. Describe the importance of waterways in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Waterways are the cheapest means of transport and are best suited for carrying heavy and bulky goods. They are a fuel-efficient and environment-friendly mode of transport. India has about 14,500 km of inland waterways, of which six stretches have been declared National Waterways, including the Ganga between Allahabad and Haldia and the Brahmaputra between Sadiya and Dhubri. These waterways also support tourism and connect remote regions to the national market.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. A student writes that pipelines are cheap to set up but expensive to run. Correct this and give two advantages of pipeline transport.

Show Answer

Ans:

Pipelines have a high initial cost but a very low running cost, not the other way round.
Advantage 1: they carry crude oil, natural gas and refined products continuously over long distances, even through rugged terrain, with no loading or trans-shipment losses.
Advantage 2: they reduce the need for road and rail tankers, so traffic and delays are less (solids can also be moved as slurry).

APPLICATIONA73 marks

7. Suggest the most suitable mode of transport for (i) a large quantity of coal for a power plant 1,500 km away, (ii) fresh flowers for a city 1,500 km away and (iii) goods for a village that has no railway station. Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) Railways (or waterways/pipelines where available): cheapest for bulky goods over long distances.
(ii) Airways: fastest, so perishable and high-value goods arrive fresh, though costly.
(iii) Roadways: they give door-to-door service and can reach villages and remote areas that rail cannot.

SHORTS83 marks

8. Explain the importance of railways in India and any two problems they face.

Show Answer

Ans:

Railways carry a huge number of passengers and bulky goods over long distances cheaply, link markets, ports and pilgrim centres, and promote national integration.
Problem 1: a large backlog of investment — tracks, bridges and rolling stock are old and the network is congested on trunk routes.
Problem 2: losses on passenger services, theft and damage to railway property, and the slow pace of gauge conversion and electrification.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: India has built a network of superhighways to link its major cities and ports. (a) Which four metropolitan cities does the Golden Quadrilateral connect? (b) Name the end points of the North–South and East–West Corridors. (c) How do such superhighways help economic development?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata.
(b) The North–South Corridor runs from Srinagar to Kanyakumari; the East–West Corridor from Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat).
(c) They cut travel time and transport costs, link markets, ports and industries, and encourage trade and investment.

COMPETENCYC104 marks

10. Read and answer: After Independence, the ports of Mumbai and Kolkata became overcrowded. (a) Which ports were developed to ease the load on each? (b) Why was Kandla the first port developed after Independence? (c) Which is the deepest landlocked, well-protected port on the east coast?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Jawaharlal Nehru Port (Nhava Sheva) eased the load on Mumbai; Haldia eased the load on Kolkata.
(b) Karachi port was lost to Pakistan at Partition, so Kandla in Kachchh was developed to handle trade and ease the pressure on Mumbai.
(c) Visakhapatnam.

LONGL114 marks

11. Describe the importance of means of communication in India's economy.

Show Answer

Ans:

The Indian postal network, the largest in the world, reaches even remote villages through nearly 1.55 lakh post offices and provides mail, savings, money-order and insurance services.
Telecom has grown rapidly: STD, ISD, mobile phones and satellite communication connect people across the country and the world.
The internet and mobile telephony have made e-banking, e-commerce, e-governance, telemedicine and online education possible.
Together, transport and communication link producers with markets, buyers with sellers, and citizens with the government, which is why they are called the lifelines of the economy.

LONGL125 marks

12. Why is international trade called the economic barometer of a country? Name four major items each of India’s exports and imports.

Show Answer

Ans:

A barometer shows the weather; similarly, the level and growth of a country’s international trade shows how prosperous its economy is.
Trade lets countries exchange the goods they produce best for those they lack, and brings in technology and raw materials.
India’s exports include gems and jewellery, chemicals and related products, engineering goods and agricultural products such as rice and spices.
India’s imports include petroleum crude and products, pearls and precious stones, machinery and fertilisers.
A healthy balance between exports and imports is needed so that foreign exchange is not drained.
