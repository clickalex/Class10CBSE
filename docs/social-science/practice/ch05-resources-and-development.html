MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. Resources that are surveyed and their quality and quantity determined for utilisation are called: (a) potential (b) developed (c) stock (d) reserves

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) developed resources — potential resources are those expected to be available in the future; stock are those not yet technologically accessible; reserves are the subset that can be put into use with existing technology.

MCQM21 mark

2. Black soil is mainly found in: (a) the Deccan trap region (b) the Gangetic plain (c) the Thar desert (d) the eastern coast

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) the Deccan trap region — Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa and parts of MP; it is ideal for cotton.

MCQM31 mark

3. Agenda 21 was adopted at the Earth Summit held at: (a) Rio de Janeiro in 1992 (b) Johannesburg in 2002 (c) Stockholm in 1972 (d) Kyoto in 1997

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Rio de Janeiro in 1992 — the first Earth Summit on environment and development.

MCQM41 mark

4. The most widespread soil in India, formed by deposits of the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers, is: (a) black soil (b) red soil (c) alluvial soil (d) laterite soil

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) alluvial soil. Alluvial soil covers the northern plains and is the most widely spread and important soil group in India.

MCQM51 mark

5. Ploughing along the contour lines of a slope to slow the flow of water is called: (a) strip cropping (b) contour ploughing (c) shelter-belt farming (d) terrace farming

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) contour ploughing. Ploughing across the slope, along contours, acts as a natural barrier to water flowing down and reduces soil erosion.

MCQM61 mark

6. Laterite soil develops mainly because of: (a) glacial deposition (b) volcanic eruption (c) wind deposition (d) heavy leaching

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) heavy leaching. Heavy rain washes out lime and silica, leaving iron and aluminium oxides which give the soil its red colour.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which of these is a non-renewable resource? (a) solar energy (b) wind (c) forests (d) coal

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) coal. It takes millions of years to form, so once used it cannot be replaced in a human lifetime.

MCQM81 mark

8. Planting a line of trees to protect crops from wind is called: (a) terrace farming (b) strip cropping (c) a shelter belt (d) shifting cultivation

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) a shelter belt. It checks wind erosion in the dry and desert regions of India.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. Why is it said that a resource is a function of human wants and technology?

Show Answer

Ans:

A substance becomes a resource only when human beings find a use for it and have the technology and the will to use it. Petroleum was just a black liquid until refining technology made it valuable; as technology and wants change, substances move in and out of being resources.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Distinguish between renewable and non-renewable resources with examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

Renewable resources can be renewed or reproduced by physical, chemical or mechanical processes and are inexhaustible in the long run — solar and wind energy, water, forests and wildlife. Non-renewable resources occur over very long geological periods and cannot be replenished once used up — coal, petroleum and natural gas, and metallic minerals such as iron ore.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Explain the main features of black soil.

Show Answer

Ans:

It is also called regur soil and is ideal for growing cotton. It is made of extremely fine clayey material, is well known for its capacity to hold moisture, and is rich in soil nutrients such as calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash and lime, though poor in phosphoric content. It develops deep cracks during hot weather, which helps aeration, and becomes sticky when wet. It is found in the Deccan trap region — Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa and parts of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.

SHORTS43 marks

4. What are the causes of land degradation in India and how can it be controlled?

Show Answer

Ans:

Causes: mining in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, MP and Odisha has left deep scars; overgrazing in Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP and Maharashtra; over-irrigation in Punjab, Haryana and western UP causing waterlogging and increased salinity and alkalinity; and industrial effluents polluting land and water.
Control: large-scale afforestation, proper management of grazing, shelter belts of plants, stabilisation of sand dunes by growing thorny bushes, proper discharge and treatment of industrial effluents, and control on mining.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student writes that land degradation in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh is caused by mining. Correct this, name the real cause and give one remedy.

Show Answer

Ans:

In these states the main cause is over-irrigation, which leads to waterlogging and increases the salinity and alkalinity of the soil.
Mining causes land degradation elsewhere, in states such as Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha.
Remedies include controlled irrigation, drainage of waterlogged fields and growing salt-tolerant crops.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Classify solar energy, iron ore, fish and wind as renewable or non-renewable, and as biotic or abiotic.

Show Answer

Ans:

Solar energy: renewable and abiotic (non-living).
Iron ore: non-renewable and abiotic; it takes millions of years to form.
Fish: renewable and biotic (it comes from the living world, and stocks regenerate if not over-fished).
Wind: renewable and abiotic.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Suggest any three methods of soil conservation used in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Contour ploughing and terracing on hill slopes slow down the flow of water and check soil erosion.
Strip cropping and planting shelterbelts of trees reduce erosion caused by wind, especially in dry regions.
Controlling overgrazing, preventing deforestation and practising crop rotation and gully plugging keep the soil covered and fertile.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: The land use of a state is as follows (illustrative figures, % of reporting area): forests 22, net sown area 44, current fallow 8, other fallow 5, pastures 4, barren and waste land 7, land under non-agricultural uses 10. (a) Which is the largest category? (b) What percentage of the land is unavailable for cultivation (barren, waste and non-agricultural uses)? (c) Suggest one way to improve this land-use pattern.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Net sown area, at 44%.
(b) 7% + 10% = 17%.
(c) Bring fallow land (13%) into use through irrigation and fertilisers, or raise forest cover towards the 33% target set by the National Forest Policy.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: A farmer finds that the soil in his field is reddish, gets its colour from iron compounds, is poor in humus, nitrogen and phosphorus, and gives better yields with fertilisers and irrigation. It is common in the eastern and southern parts of the Deccan Plateau. (a) Name this soil. (b) Why is it red? (c) How can its fertility be improved?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Red (and yellow) soil.
(b) It forms on crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks, and the red colour is due to the diffusion of iron; it looks yellow when it is hydrated.
(c) By adding manure and fertilisers (to supply humus, nitrogen and phosphorus) and by irrigating.

LONGL104 marks

10. Compare alluvial soil and laterite soil — how each is formed, where it is found and what it grows.

Show Answer

Ans:

Alluvial soil is deposited by rivers and is very fertile, rich in potash and lime but poor in nitrogen and humus; it covers the northern plains and the deltas and grows rice, wheat, sugarcane and pulses.
Laterite soil forms in hot, wet areas where heavy rain leaches away lime and silica, leaving iron and aluminium oxides; it is acidic and low in humus.
It is found on the summits of the Western Ghats, eastern Maharashtra, Odisha, parts of Chhattisgarh and the north-east.
With manures and irrigation it grows tea, coffee, rubber, cashew and tapioca.

LONGL115 marks

11. What is sustainable development and why is it important? Refer to Agenda 21.

Show Answer

Ans:

Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It requires that economic growth go hand in hand with environmental protection and social equity.
Why it matters: indiscriminate use of resources has led to depletion, concentration of resources in a few hands, land degradation and global ecological crises such as global warming and ozone depletion.
Agenda 21 was adopted by the first Earth Summit held at Rio de Janeiro in 1992. It is a declaration of global agreement and international cooperation to combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through sustainable development, and it also provides that every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21.

LONGL125 marks

12. What is resource planning? Describe the three steps involved in resource planning in India and explain why it is essential.

Show Answer

Ans:

Resource planning is the widely accepted strategy for the judicious use of resources.
Step 1: identify and make an inventory of resources across the regions, by surveying, mapping and estimating their quality and quantity.
Step 2: evolve a planning structure with suitable technology, skill and institutions to carry out the plans.
Step 3: match the resource development plans with the national development plans.
It is essential because resources are unevenly distributed (for example, minerals in Jharkhand, solar and wind power in Rajasthan) and many are limited, so planning is needed for balanced regional development and to prevent over-use.
