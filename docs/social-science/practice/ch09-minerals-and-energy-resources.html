MCQs · Drill

MCQM11 mark

1. The finest quality of iron ore with about 70% iron content is: (a) magnetite (b) limonite (c) hematite (d) siderite

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) hematite — the most important industrial iron ore in terms of quantity used; magnetite has 60–70% iron and valued magnetic properties.

MCQM21 mark

2. The largest producer of mica in India is the: (a) Ajmer region (b) Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh belt (c) Nellore belt (d) Balaghat belt

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh belt in Jharkhand, which is the largest producer of mica in India and the world.

MCQM31 mark

3. About 63% of India's petroleum production comes from: (a) Assam (b) Gujarat (c) the KG basin (d) Bombay High

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Bombay High. Gujarat contributes about 18% and Assam about 16%.

MCQM41 mark

4. The best quality coal, with the highest carbon content, is: (a) anthracite (b) lignite (c) peat (d) bituminous

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) anthracite. Anthracite is hard, black and contains over 80 per cent carbon, so it is the highest-grade coal.

MCQM51 mark

5. Photovoltaic cells convert: (a) heat into sound (b) sunlight directly into electricity (c) wind into heat (d) water into steam

Show Answer

Ans:

(b) sunlight directly into electricity. Solar photovoltaic cells use sunlight to produce electricity without any moving parts.

MCQM61 mark

6. Neyveli in Tamil Nadu is known for: (a) lignite coal (b) anthracite (c) petroleum (d) mica

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) lignite coal. Lignite is a low-grade brown coal used mainly to generate electricity.

MCQM71 mark

7. Which state has the largest installed wind-power capacity in India? (a) Kerala (b) Bihar (c) Punjab (d) Tamil Nadu

Show Answer

Ans:

(d) Tamil Nadu. Wind farms around Muppandal, Nagercoil and Madurai give it the largest capacity.

MCQM81 mark

8. Petroleum is popularly called: (a) brown diamond (b) white coal (c) black gold (d) green fuel

Show Answer

Ans:

(c) black gold. Because of its high commercial value; ‘white coal' refers to hydel power.

Written questions · Write

SHORTS12 marks

1. What is meant by a conventional source of energy? Give two examples.

Show Answer

Ans:

Conventional sources are those that have been in use for a long time and are non-renewable, so their stock is limited and cannot be replaced within a human lifetime. Examples: coal, petroleum, natural gas and firewood.

SHORTS23 marks

2. Distinguish between conventional and non-conventional sources of energy with two examples each.

Show Answer

Ans:

Conventional sources have been in use for a long time, are largely exhaustible, cause pollution and are costly to obtain — for example coal and petroleum. Non-conventional sources are recently developed, are renewable and eco-friendly, and have lower running costs — for example solar and wind energy.

SHORTS33 marks

3. Why is there a pressing need to use non-conventional sources of energy in India?

Show Answer

Ans:

Because the growing consumption of energy has resulted in a rising dependence on coal and petroleum, whose prices are rising and whose reserves are limited; importing petroleum puts a heavy burden on foreign exchange. Conventional sources also cause environmental degradation and global warming. India has abundant potential for solar, wind, tidal, geothermal and biogas energy, which is renewable, eco-friendly and can be generated locally, so developing it improves energy security and reduces pollution.

SHORTS43 marks

4. Describe the distribution of iron ore in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Iron ore is the basic mineral and the backbone of industrial development. Hematite, the finest quality with up to 70% iron, and magnetite, with 60–70% iron and excellent magnetic properties, are found in four belts: the Odisha-Jharkhand belt (Badampahar and Mayurbhanj in Odisha, Kiriburu in Jharkhand); the Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur belt in Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra, including the famous Bailadila mines whose ore is exported to Japan and South Korea; the Bellary-Chitradurga-Chikkamagaluru-Tumakuru belt in Karnataka with the large Kudremukh mines; and the Maharashtra-Goa belt, whose ore is exported through the Marmagao port.

APPLICATIONA53 marks

5. A student says lignite is the best quality coal because it is mined at Neyveli. Correct this and arrange the four types of coal from best to poorest quality.

Show Answer

Ans:

Lignite is a low-grade brown coal with a lot of moisture, so it is not the best; the best quality is anthracite.
Order from best to poorest: anthracite → bituminous → lignite → peat.
Anthracite has the highest carbon content; bituminous is the most popular in commercial use; peat has the lowest carbon content and heat value.

APPLICATIONA63 marks

6. Which non-conventional source of energy would you recommend for (i) a sunny desert region, (ii) a windy coastal region and (iii) a village with plenty of cattle dung and farm waste? Give reasons.

Show Answer

Ans:

(i) Solar energy: strong sunshine all year can be converted into electricity with photovoltaic cells.
(ii) Wind energy: steady winds can turn turbines; the Nagercoil–Madurai belt of Tamil Nadu is a large wind-farm cluster.
(iii) Biogas: cattle dung and farm waste produce a clean cooking fuel and good manure.

SHORTS73 marks

7. Describe the distribution of coal in India and the types of coal found.

Show Answer

Ans:

Most of India's coal comes from the Gondwana rocks of the Damodar valley: Raniganj, Jharia, Bokaro and Karanpura in Jharkhand and West Bengal.
Other important coalfields are Korba in Chhattisgarh, Talcher in Odisha, Singrauli in Madhya Pradesh and Singareni in Telangana.
Anthracite, the highest-quality coal with over 80% carbon, occurs only in Reasi district of Jammu and Kashmir.
Bituminous coal is the most common and is mined in Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh and West Bengal; lignite (brown coal) is mined at Neyveli in Tamil Nadu, and in Gujarat and Rajasthan.

COMPETENCYC84 marks

8. Read and answer: A mining company discards low-grade ore as waste and uses only rich ore. A geologist warns that, at this rate, the rich deposits will run out within a few decades. (a) Why is the conservation of minerals necessary? (b) Suggest two ways to conserve minerals. (c) State one way in which mining harms miners or the environment.

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) Minerals are non-renewable: they take millions of years to form, while the rate of use is very high.
(b) Develop technology that can use low-grade ores cheaply; recycle metals and use scrap and substitutes.
(c) Dust and fumes cause lung diseases in miners, mine roofs can collapse, and waste and slurry pollute water and degrade land.

COMPETENCYC94 marks

9. Read and answer: State X has fast-flowing rivers and heavy rainfall in hilly country. State Y has large coal fields and a flat terrain. (a) Which state suits hydel power and which suits thermal power? (b) Which of the two sources is renewable? (c) Why must hydel projects be planned carefully?

Show Answer

Ans:

(a) State X suits hydroelectric power; State Y suits thermal power.
(b) Hydroelectric power is renewable (water is renewed by the water cycle); coal is non-renewable.
(c) Dams submerge land and forests, displace people and change the flow of rivers, so the social and environmental costs must be weighed.

LONGL104 marks

10. Explain why non-conventional sources of energy are being promoted, and describe solar and wind energy.

Show Answer

Ans:

Coal and petroleum are limited and their use pollutes the air and adds to global warming, while demand keeps rising; non-conventional sources are renewable and much cleaner.
Solar energy is captured by photovoltaic cells that convert sunlight directly into electricity and by solar thermal plants; India's largest solar parks are in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.
Wind energy uses clusters of windmills in windy areas; the biggest wind farms are in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh.
India is now among the world's leading producers of wind power, and the government supports both through subsidies and renewable purchase obligations.

LONGL115 marks

11. Explain any five non-conventional sources of energy available in India.

Show Answer

Ans:

Solar energy: converted using photovoltaic technology; rapidly becoming popular in rural India, used for lighting, cooking and water heating — the largest solar plants are in Rajasthan (Bhadla) and Tamil Nadu (Neyveli).
Wind energy: India is a superpower in wind power, with the largest cluster of wind farms in Tamil Nadu from Nagercoil to Madurai.
Biogas: produced from farm waste, animal and human waste in gobar gas plants; it has higher thermal efficiency than charcoal and is used for lighting and cooking in villages.
Tidal energy: generated by using floodgate dams across inlets — potential in the Gulf of Khambhat, the Gulf of Kuchchh and the Sundarbans.
Geothermal energy: heat from the earth's interior used to generate power — the Manikaran hot springs in Himachal Pradesh and the Puga Valley in Ladakh are promising; six plants have been set up there.
Atomic energy: produced from uranium and thorium; uranium is found in Jharkhand and the Aravalli range, and thorium in the monazite sands of Kerala.

LONGL125 marks

12. In what ways do minerals occur in nature? Explain with an example for each.

Show Answer

Ans:

In veins and lodes: minerals fill cracks and joints of igneous and metamorphic rocks, for example tin, copper, zinc and lead.
In beds or layers: minerals formed in sedimentary rocks, for example coal, iron ore, gypsum and potash salt.
By decomposition of surface rocks: the soluble parts are removed and a residual mass is left, for example bauxite.
As alluvial deposits (placers): minerals washed down and deposited in valley sands, for example gold, silver and tin.
In ocean waters: common salt, magnesium and bromine are obtained from sea water.
